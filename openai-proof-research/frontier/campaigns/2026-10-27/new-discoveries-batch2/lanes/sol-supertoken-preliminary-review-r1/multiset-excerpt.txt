Multiset resolving sets inherit some interesting applications from metric resolving sets. One example is source localization [ 21 , Section 7.1] . Suppose that at time t = 0 t=0 a single (unknown) vertex v 0 v_{0} of a connected graph G G is infected, and that any uninfected vertex which is adjacent to an infected vertex at time t − 1 t-1 becomes infected at time t t , for each t = 1 , 2 , … t=1,2,\dots . We would like to identify the source vertex v 0 v_{0} by observing the spread of the infection. One strategy for achieving this is to place a sensor at each vertex of some multiset resolving set R R for G G , with each sensor emitting a signal at the first moment its vertex becomes infected. For t = 0 , 1 , 2 , … t=0,1,2,\dots , let N t N_{t} be the number of signals emitted by vertices in R R at time t t . (Note that N t N_{t} is simply the number of vertices in R R at distance t t from v 0 v_{0} .) Then since R R is multiset-resolving for G G , the identity of v 0 v_{0} is completely determined by the sequence { N t } t = 0 diam ​ ( G ) \{N_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\text{diam}(G)} . Metric resolving sets have also been shown to be useful as a tool for certain instances of graph embedding , where the goal is map the vertices of G G to real N N -dimensional space while preserving structural properties of the graph. In applications, it is preferable for N N to be small. For example, in [ 22 ] the authors use small resolving sets to find low-dimensional embeddings of graphical representations of genomic sequences, allowing for the application of a variety of clustering algorithms. The embedding is obtained by mapping each vertex to its metric signature with respect to some small metric resolving set; thus, given a metric resolving set R R , the embedding maps the graph into ℝ | R | \mathbb{R}^{|R|} . Importantly, for the application considered in [ 22 ] , the metric resolving set embedding is nearly an isometry, i.e., the Euclidean distance between the images of any pair of vertices is quite close to the graph distance between that pair.
