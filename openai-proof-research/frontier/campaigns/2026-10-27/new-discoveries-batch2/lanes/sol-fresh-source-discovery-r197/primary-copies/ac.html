[1] arXiv:2610.36964 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Counting the Fixed Points of Binary Belief Propagation: Generic Degree, Syzygies, and Real Solutions
Yusuke Watanabe
Comments: 33 pages (main text and supplementary material SM1-SM6 in one PDF), 1 figure. Lean 4 formalization of the main results: this https URL
Subjects: Commutative Algebra (math.AC); Information Theory (cs.IT); Algebraic Geometry (math.AG); Combinatorics (math.CO)

How many fixed points does belief propagation have on a binary pairwise model? Over complex parameters, the admissible equations of a finite loopless multigraph form an algebraic family whose generic degree we determine: it is a graph state sum over edge sets without vertices of degree one, each vertex of positive degree contributing one less than its degree. We give two geometric realizations of this state sum. The first is the total Betti number of a universal edge-incidence ring constructed without parameters. Its full graded Betti table is the state sum, its Tor algebra is natural for noncontracting graph maps, the alternating numerator recovers the graph polynomial of Watanabe and Fukumizu, and an Artin reduction by a regular sequence realizes the one-variable specialization, corrected for tree components. The second lies in the actual family: at the strong-field boundary each state factor is a ramification index, and at sufficiently strong field the state charts exhaust the admissible complex fiber. For fixed nonzero real couplings, every Eulerian message-sign sector contains a real fixed point at every external field, and strong fields attain this lower bound: one admissible real fixed point per element of the binary cycle space and one positive fixed point. For graphs of maximum degree at most three, this minimum equals the number of minimal module generators of the edge-incidence ring. One state decomposition connects the complex degree, graded syzygies, and an attained minimum real count. The degree, selected ring and resolution statements, and the real counts are formalized in Lean 4.

[2] arXiv:2610.36495 (cross-list from math.AG) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Quartic Keller Maps and Tameness in Three Variables
Jie Wang, Dan Yan
Comments: 31
Subjects: Algebraic Geometry (math.AG); Commutative Algebra (math.AC)

We study invertibility and tameness for polynomial maps of affine three-space of total degree at most four. We prove that every complex Keller map in this degree range is a polynomial automorphism and, independently, that every complex polynomial automorphism of total degree at most four is tame. The invertibility argument combines homogeneous factorization of the leading part, invariant rings of conic pencils, and residue obstructions on cubic fibers. The tameness argument uses a criterion based on a constant second directional derivative, low-degree coordinate classifications, and local tameness of plane polynomial automorphisms over a polynomial parameter ring. The invertibility conclusion extends to every field of characteristic zero.

[3] arXiv:2610.36736 (cross-list from math.AG) [pdf, html, other]
Title: The Legendre potential and the gradient case of the differential Galois criterion for the Jacobian Conjecture
Elżbieta Adamus, Zbigniew Hajto
Subjects: Algebraic Geometry (math.AG); Commutative Algebra (math.AC)

Let $h\in\C[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ have constant nonzero Hessian determinant, let $h^{*}$ be its formal Legendre transform and $g=\vv{X,\grad h}-h$. The Nambu derivations of $\grad h$ satisfy $\delta_i(g)=X_i$ and $\delta^\alpha(g)=(\partial^\alpha h^{*})\circ\grad h$. Consequently, in the gradient case the differential Galois criterion of \cite{ACH19}, consisting of $n$ membership conditions, reduces to a single membership condition. Equivalently, $\grad h$ is a polynomial automorphism if and only if $g\in\C[\partial_1h,\dots,\partial_nh]$. Moreover, $\grad h$ is a polynomial automorphism if and only if $\delta^\alpha(g)=0$ for $|\alpha|=(°h-1)^{n-1}+2$, and then the coefficients $\delta^\alpha(g)(x_0)/\alpha!$ of $h^{*}$ at $\grad h(x_0)$ determine the inverse. If the Hessian determinant of $h$ is merely a nonzero polynomial, then $g$ is still a primitive element of $\C(X)$ over $\C(\partial_1h,\dots,\partial_nh)$. For the Nambu-tower criterion, doubling a Keller map yields no independent conditions. After an affine change of coordinates putting $\grad h$ in the normal form $X+H$, the formal Legendre transform is the sum of the formal Pascal difference series of $g$, and the termination of this series implies polynomial invertibility. In at most three variables the normal form of every gradient Keller map has strongly nilpotent Jacobian part and is therefore Pascal finite, by a theorem of de Bondt, and an explicit gradient automorphism in four variables has a normal form which is not Pascal finite. The five-variable counterexample of Meng and Yang shows that the properties used here do not imply the Hessian conjecture. The results do not settle the open cases $\JC_2$ and $\HC_4$.

[4] arXiv:2608.03766 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: A Fitting criterion for quasi-Gorenstein local rings
Sora Miyashita
Comments: 2 pages, I simplified the proof
Subjects: Commutative Algebra (math.AC)

We prove that a Noetherian local ring $R$ with canonical module $\omega_R$ is quasi-Gorenstein if and only if $\Fitt_1^R(\omega_R) \cong \omega_R$. This settles the expectation of Eisenbud, Ficarra, Herzog, and Moradi on canonical ideals.

[5] arXiv:2609.11219 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Multigraded Local Cohomology Categories: Homological Structure and Canonical Sources
Dancheng Lu
Comments: 34 pages comments are welcome
Subjects: Commutative Algebra (math.AC)

Let $S=\kk[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with its standard $\mathbb N^n$-grading, and let $\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. We study the essential images of the functors $M\mapsto H^i_{\mathfrak m}(M(-\mathbf1))$ on categories of positively determined $S$-modules, where $\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)$. For a fixed determining bound $\mathbf t\in\mathbb N^n$ and $0\le i<n$, we identify the image in degree $i$ with the category of finite-dimensional representations of the subposet of $[\mathbf0,\mathbf t]$ consisting of multidegrees with at least $n-i$ positive coordinates. These images are Serre subcategories of the category of negatively $\mathbf t$-determined modules. The top-degree image consists precisely of second cosyzygies in that category and is extension-closed, but is not abelian when $\mathbf t\ne\mathbf0$. For the non-top image categories, we determine attached primes from projective covers and compute the Bass numbers at $\mathfrak m$ and the injective dimensions over $S$ of projective and injective objects. In every non-top degree, we construct an exact functorial section selecting, for each nonzero target, a source of minimal projective dimension $n-i$ and maximal depth $i$ among all its sources. If $n\ge2$ and $\mathbf t\ge\mathbf1$, no fully faithful section exists in degrees $1$ and $n-1$, even without requiring exactness. In top degree, graded Matlis duality followed by $S$-duality gives a fully faithful functorial section. We also describe the global images obtained by varying the determining bound. The non-top global categories have enough injectives and no nonzero projective objects, while the top category corresponds to second cosyzygies in the category of finite-support representations of $\mathbb N^n$.
