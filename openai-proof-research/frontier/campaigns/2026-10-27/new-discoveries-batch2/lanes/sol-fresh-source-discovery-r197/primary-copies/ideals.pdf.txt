                                                    from the reduction algorithm. Tian and Tran subsequently characterized the

                                                    discriminants ∆ ≤ X, with error O(X −1/4+ε ). For individual real quadratic

                                                                                 1. Introduction
                                              Reduced ideals in real quadratic fields connect continued fractions, binary qua-
                                           dratic forms, ideal classes, and Shanks’s infrastructure; see [3, 9, 16, 18]. In his
                                           analysis of algorithms for Arakelov class groups, Schoof observed that certain re-
                                           duced Arakelov divisors cannot occur as outputs of the reduction algorithm. He
                                           explicitly asked “It would be of interest to know how many such reduced divisors
                                           there may be.” [16, p. 492]. Tian and Tran took up this problem and described
                                           their results as a partial answer to Schoof’s question [19]. In real quadratic fields,
                                           they characterized the possible outputs
                                                                                √ of the reduction algorithm as exactly the
                                           1-reduced ideals. For example, Q( 73) has nine reduced ideals, but only seven
                                           are 1-reduced. The present paper gives a quantitative answer to Schoof’s question

                  4. Equidistribution and individual fields
   In this section we prove Theorems 1.2 and 1.3. The deep input is Duke’s theorem.
The passage from closed geodesics to continued fractions through a cross-section of
the geodesic flow is classical [1, 13, 15, 17]. We include the details because we need
all ideal classes of a fixed discriminant simultaneously, sets with curved boundary
such as X ex , and control of the noncompact part of X .
   For orientation, the geometry below may be summarized as follows. A reduced
pair ξ = (w+ , w− ) ∈ X determines an oriented geodesic in the upper half-plane
with endpoints w− and w+ . We record this geodesic when it crosses the imaginary
axis. Away from the null set of integer forward endpoints, the continued- fraction
reduction map Φ is realized as the return map to this cross-section, with return time
Θ. For a fixed fundamental discriminant, the Φ-orbits are the reduction cycles, one
for each ideal class, and each cycle traces one closed geodesic. Duke’s theorem
equidistributes the union of these closed geodesics; the cross-section then transfers
this equidistribution to the reduced pairs ξI .
