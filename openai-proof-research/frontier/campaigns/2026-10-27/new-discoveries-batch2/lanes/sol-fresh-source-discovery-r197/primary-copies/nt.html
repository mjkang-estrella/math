Title: Counting Markov triples and simple geodesics
Morten S. Risager
Comments: 21 pages, 5 figures. All comments welcome
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Geometric Topology (math.GT)

We give an asymptotic formula for the number of Markov triples with largest entry below $x$. We show that the error term is $o(\log x)$, which improves upon the previous best known results due to McShane and Rivin. We give an explicit error based on classical zero-free regions of the Riemann zeta function, and a sharper estimate under the quasi-Riemann hypothesis. The proof uses the correspondence between Markov triples and simple closed geodesics on the modular punctured torus and a careful analysis of the McShane-Rivin unit ball.

[2] arXiv:2610.36130 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Uniform local models of quasi-unipotent $p$-adic differential equations
Poornima Belvotagi
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Algebraic Geometry (math.AG)

We construct uniform analytic models of quasi-unipotent differential modules over Robba rings. For modules of rank at most $r$ and highest ramification break at most $b$, the inner radius of the model annulus depends only on $r,b$ and the residue characteristic $p$. At each radius, the model admits a basis, possibly depending on the radius, in which the connection matrix is bounded by a constant depending only on $r,b,p$. Both bounds are independent of the coefficient field, and these analytic models require no coefficient extension. A prescribed absolute Frobenius structure extends to such a bounded model with the same radius and connection bound. After a finite coefficient extension, we also realize quasi-constant modules, which carry the finite local monodromy, as differential direct summands of sums of pushforwards of rank-one modules, with uniformly bounded cover degrees and highest breaks of the inducing modules.

[3] arXiv:2610.36132 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Hankel determinants for the $(n+2)$-shifted $q$-metallic numbers
Guo-Niu Han, Emmanuel Pedon
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Combinatorics (math.CO)

Let $\Phi_n(q)$ denote the $q$-deformation (in the sense of Morier-Genoud & Ovsienko) of the metallic number $\phi_n=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^2+4}}{2}$. This is a power series with integer coefficients. We prove that the Hankel continued fraction of the $(n+2)$-shifted series $\Phi_n^{(n+2)}$ is eventually periodic, and that the corresponding Hankel determinant sequence $\Delta^{(n+2)}$ is $2n(n+1)$-periodic if $n$ is even, $2n(n+1)$-antiperiodic if $n$ is odd, and that it takes only the values $-2,-1,0,1,2$. This confirms part of a former conjecture of ours and complements similar results we obtained for all $\ell$-shifts of $\Phi_n$ with $0\le\ell\le n+1$. In addition, we formulate a new conjecture of quasi-polynomiality for higher shifts $\ell\ge n+3$. The determinant values arise as telescoping products of the partial numerators of the Hankel continued fraction. Most of the technically involved and lengthy verifications are carried out by a symbolic computation program, whose code is publicly available on GitHub for independent verification.

Hacène Belbachir, Ravi Ranjan, Shubh N. Singh
Comments: 18 pages , 6 tables
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT)

Ranjan and Singh proved that for every composite $n$ which is not twice an odd prime, $\varphi(n)$ is at least one plus the sum of $\varphi$ over the prime divisors of $n$, with equality exactly for $n=4,12,30$. \new{We study the weighted equation $\varphi(n)=c+\alpha\sum_{p\mid n}\varphi(p)$. [6] arXiv:2610.36284 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Retriangular numbers and affine maps between polygonal numbers
Mateo Matijasevick
Comments: 19 pages, 2 figures
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT)

A positive integer $a$ is called \emph{retriangular} if there is a positive integer $b$ such that $aT+b$ is a triangular number whenever $T$ is. We show that the retriangular numbers are exactly the odd squares greater than $1$, and that for each of them $b$ is unique. The proof rests on a rigidity principle: a non-constant affine map carrying the values of one quadratic sequence into the values of another is induced by an affine map of the plane sending the vertex of one parabola to the vertex of the other. We use it to classify all affine maps between sets of polygonal numbers; the $s$-gonal numbers map affinely into the $t$-gonal numbers if and only if $M_t\mid M_s$, where $M_s=2(s-2)/\gcd(s,4)$ defines a bijection from $\{3,4,5,\dots\}$ onto the positive integers. We also treat sparse data, polynomial maps, figurate numbers of higher degree and sums of two triangular numbers, and we study the monoid of affine self-maps of an arbitrary set of integers. For the $s$-gonal numbers this monoid is an arithmetical congruence monoid, and the factorization of self-maps into irreducible ones is unique only for triangular and square numbers.

[7] arXiv:2610.36291 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Pointwise to global good reduction problems
François Gatine
Comments: 44 pages. Comments are very much welcome!
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Algebraic Geometry (math.AG)

Given a smooth proper family of varieties over some $p$-adic field, if (sufficiently many of) the closed fibers have good reduction, does the family as a whole have good reduction? This question is asked and positively answered in recent work of Cadoret-Tamagawa for curves in general, and abelian varieties if the base field has absolute ramification at most $p-1$. In this paper we define a general framework for studying this question, yielding new positive answers for families of K3 surfaces and cubic fourfoulds, again with ramification at most $p-1$. Our strategy is to study several extension properties of moduli spaces and how they transfer under various morphisms. Of particular importance are integral canonical models of Shimura varieties, and whether or not their extension properties are preserved under ramified scalar extension. Additionally, building on earlier counterexamples by Raynaud-Ogus-Gabber and Vasiu-Zink, we show that the ramification assumption is optimal for the result of Cadoret-Tamagawa on families of abelian varieties. This reflects the fact that the extension property of Siegel Shimura varieties no longer holds over a field of absolute ramification at least $p$.

Title: Metric Diophantine approximation with primes on irrational lines
Zirui Jiang
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT)

Let $c>1$ be irrational. We prove that, for almost every $x>0$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there are infinitely many triples $(p,r,s)$, with $p$ and $r$ prime and $s$ an integer, for which \[ 0<px-r<p^{-1/3+\varepsilon},\qquad 0<pcx-s<p^{-1/3+\varepsilon}. \] This improves the exponent $1/5$ in the homogeneous-line case of a theorem of Baier and Ghosh. The arithmetic input is the quantitative prime-counting lower bound in Matomäki's proof concerning primes in shrinking neighborhoods of an irrational rotation. We obtain a local first-moment lower bound by fixing the prime numerator and applying the prime number theorem to the admissible prime denominators. Two Selberg sieve squares, together with explicit trigonometric interval approximations, give a pointwise upper bound whose error tends to zero in $L^1$ after normalization. A truncation and density-point argument then yields the almost-everywhere conclusion.

Title: Moment representations for generalized Lehmer polynomials
Karin Ikeda, Hideki Murahara
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Probability (math.PR)

We represent normalized generalized Lehmer polynomials as moments of a random variable constructed from two independent negative binomial variables and an independent uniform variable. An integral form of their known exponential generating function yields mixtures of uniform distributions and convergent double series. We recover Eulerian polynomial identities and give a probabilistic interpretation of anti-diagonal sums of poly-Bernoulli numbers. We also prove moment determinacy, characterize the Stieltjes parameter range, derive Hankel total non-negativity and log-convexity, and identify the boundary distributions.

We establish a uniform effective Chebotarev-Sato-Tate estimate for abelian varieties, conditional on the Mumford-Tate conjecture and the standard analytic conjectures for L-functions attached to the irreducible representations of the Chebotarev-Sato-Tate group. Title: Resolvent Components of Completely Normal Elements and the Morgan-Mullen Conjecture
Florian Hellwig
Comments: 49 pages. Lean 4 formalisation and verification code: this https URL
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Logic in Computer Science (cs.LO)

We study completely normal elements of $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, that is, elements that are normal over every intermediate field, through partial Lagrange resolvents. In these coordinates, complete normality splits into independent components, in a way closely related to Hachenberger's decomposition theory. Our main tool is a structure theorem for the components; together with a finite computation, it shows that every component has weight (the sum of the probabilities that its conditions fail) at most $3/4$, uniformly in $n$. Combined with the classical character-sum method, this gives an explicit sufficient criterion for the existence of a primitive completely normal element. We verify the criterion for all $n>q$ except $70$ pairs $(q,n)$, which we settle with explicit elements found by computer. Since the case $n\le q$ is due to Garefalakis and Kapetanakis, this confirms the conjecture of Morgan and Mullen (1996) that every $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ contains a primitive element that is completely normal over $\mathbb{F}_q$. The same estimates give two further results. First, Hachenberger's conjectured lower bound $(q-1)^{n'}q^{(p^a-1)n'}$ for the number of completely normal elements, where $p$ is the characteristic and $n=p^an'$ with $p\nmid n'$, holds for all pairs $(q,n)$; it was known for regular pairs. Second, primitivity and complete normality are asymptotically independent: as $q^n\to\infty$, the number of primitive completely normal elements is $(1+o(1))\,\varphi(q^n-1)/(q^n-1)$ times the number of completely normal elements (for fixed $n$ this was known). Our arguments for $n>q$ and for both further results, including every computer-assisted step, are formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib; the formalisation takes the case $n\le q$ and an explicit bound of Rosser and Schoenfeld as axioms and relies on Lean's compiler and interpreter for the large computations.

Title: Density and Defect Distribution of Reduced Ideals Missed by the Reduction Algorithm in Real Quadratic Fields
Mengzhe Guo, Taro Hayashi, Ryota Sueyosh
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT)

Schoof asked how many reduced Arakelov divisors cannot arise from the reduction algorithm. Tian and Tran subsequently characterized the image of this algorithm in real quadratic fields as precisely the $1$-reduced ideals. We give a quantitative answer to Schoof's question in the real-quadratic setting by determining the asymptotic proportion of reduced ideals missed by the algorithm. A key arithmetic input is the criterion that a reduced ideal with associated reduced form $(A,b,-r)$ fails to be $1$-reduced if and only if $A-r<b<2(A-r)$. A lattice-point count gives the average asymptotic over fundamental discriminants $\Delta\le X$, with error $O(X^{-1/4+\varepsilon})$. This distribution is supported on $[0,1/\sqrt3-1/2]$, and its lower tail is asymptotic to $\frac{2}{\log(4/3)}t\log(1/t)$ as $t\to0^+$. An exact relation between the integer defect and an endpoint coordinate of the natural extension allows us to transfer equidistribution to the defect law.

The construction suggests a conjectural theory of Iwahori-types for BD covers beyond the tame case. 1) The pair $(\bar{I},\sigma)$ is a Bushnell-Kutzko type for the Bernstein block analogous to the block of unramified principal series in a linear reductive group. This is a non-tame analogue of the Borel-Casselman theorem.

3) The representation $\sigma$ extends to a hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup $\bar{K}$. Among the genuine $\bar{K}$-representations, $\sigma$ has minimal depth with respect to the principal congruence filtration. It also yields a finite Shimura correspondence, generalizing a result of Savin.

Title: On a classical zero-sum invariant II: Disproof of a long-standing conjecture
Alfred Geroldinger, Guoqing Wang, Wenkai Yang
Subjects: Combinatorics (math.CO); Number Theory (math.NT)

For a nontrivial finite abelian group $G$, let $\nu(G)$ be the smallest {nonnegative} integer $\ell$ such that every zero-sum free sequence $T$ over $G$ of length at least $\ell$ has the following property: all nonzero elements of $G$ that do not occur as a subsequence sum of $T$ lie in a proper coset of some subgroup of $G$. This conjecture has since been confirmed for many families of groups (including all p-groups and groups of rank at most two). In this paper, we disprove the conjecture.

Title: Seven ordered points and the nonexistence of arithmetic ball quotient compactifications

Comments: 44 pages
Subjects: Algebraic Geometry (math.AG); Complex Variables (math.CV); Number Theory (math.NT)

We prove that the ordered GIT quotient of seven points on $\mathbb P^1$ with equal weights is not isomorphic to the Baily--Borel compactification of any arithmetic complex ball quotient. Together with our companion paper and the known positive examples, this determines the equal weight case for every $n\ge6$: such a realization exists precisely for $n=6,8$. The lattice and the algebraic isomorphism are unrestricted, and torsion and divisorial ramification are allowed. Title: Weighted configurations and the nonexistence of arithmetic ball quotient compactifications
Ryunosuke Nakano (Hokkaido University), Hisato Matsukawa (Hokkaido University)
Comments: 57 pages
Subjects: Algebraic Geometry (math.AG); Complex Variables (math.CV); Number Theory (math.NT)

We prove that, for every $n\ge9$, no ordered GIT quotient of $n$ points on $\mathbb P^1$ with positive rational weights smaller than one and of total weight two is isomorphic to the Baily--Borel compactification of an arithmetic complex ball quotient. The weights may lie on stability walls, and the lattice may have torsion and ramify along divisors. No compatibility with a prescribed period map is assumed. The proof compares homomorphisms from the tangent bundle to line bundles of negative degree with normal bundles of arithmetic fixed subvarieties. We describe these homomorphisms through coincident marked points and prove vanishing statements for their kernels. The comparison, together with integrality of Chern classes and the invariant theory of finite reflection groups, gives the nonexistence theorem without a Fano assumption on the GIT quotient.
