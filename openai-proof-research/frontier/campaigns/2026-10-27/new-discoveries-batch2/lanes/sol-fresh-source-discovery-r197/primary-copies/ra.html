[1] arXiv:2610.36174 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Finite Non-Commutative Centrally Essential Rings of Minimal Order
Adel Abyzov, Oleg Lyubimtsev, Askar Tuganbaev
Comments: 11 pages
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

A ring $R$ with non-zero identity element is said to be centrally essential if for any non-zero element $a$ of $R$, there exist non-zero central elements $x$ and $y$ such that $ax = y$. In the paper, we prove that the minimal order of a finite non-commutative centrally essential ring is equal to $64$. A partial classification of such rings is presented, and the existence of non-isomorphic non-commutative centrally essential rings of minimal order is proved.

[2] arXiv:2610.36447 [pdf, html, other]
Title: On module endomorphisms annihilated by residually square-free polynomials over Artinian local PIRs
Abdelghaffar Chibloun, Mustapha Najmeddine
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

We consider finitely generated free modules and their endomorphisms over an Artinian local PIR, also known as an SPIR. We characterize submodules of such modules by constructing minimal generating sets from given bases. We prove that any endomorphism possessing a square-free annihilating polynomial modulo the maximal ideal admits a principal annihilating ideal, and establish that every free invariant submodule under such an endomorphism has an invariant complement. Moreover, for such endomorphisms, we detail the construction of invariant submodules and compute the number of free invariant submodules when the ring is finite. Furthermore, we introduce canonical matrices associated with these endomorphisms, yielding a complete characterization of matrix similarity via invariant polynomials in this context. Finally, we address the non-derogatory case, obtaining a sharper characterization of submodule invariance.

[3] arXiv:2610.36903 [pdf, html, other]
Title: The Kothe Radical in Novikov Algebras
Alexander S. Panasenko
Comments: 13 pages
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA); Quantum Algebra (math.QA)

We study the Kothe radical (the upper nil radical) in the variety of Novikov algebras. We prove that the class of weakly right nilpotent Novikov algebras coincides with the class of r-nil algebras and forms a radical class. We establish that the Kothe radical is a hereditary radical in the variety of Novikov algebras. Furthermore, we obtain a structural description of the Kothe radical as the intersection of ideals whose corresponding quotient algebras are prime K-semisimple algebras. Finally, we construct an example of a prime r-nil Novikov algebra over a field of characteristic p>0, which implies that the Baer radical is strictly contained in the Kothe radical in this case.

[4] arXiv:2610.37027 [pdf, html, other]
Title: On δ-derivations of \mathfrak{saff}_{n+1}
Kazbek Nuratdinov, Rustamjon G'aybullaev
Comments: 7 pages
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

We study $\delta$-derivations of the finite-dimensional perfect Lie algebra $\mathfrak{saff}_{n+1}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$, where $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ is the natural $\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})$-module. For $n>1$ and $\delta\ne1$, we prove that every nonzero $\delta$-derivation occurs at $\delta=\frac12$ and is a scalar multiple of the identity. For $n=1$, we explicitly describe the four-dimensional space of $(-2)$-derivations.

[5] arXiv:2610.37150 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Finite Matrix Rings of Even Size Are Determined by Their Commuting Graphs
Hamid Shahverdi, Vahid Shahverdi
Comments: 4 pages
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

The commuting graph of a noncommutative ring has its noncentral elements as vertices, with two distinct vertices adjacent when they commute. Akbari, Ghandehari, Hadian and Mohammadian conjectured that a full matrix ring over a finite field is determined, among rings with identity, by its commuting graph. In this paper, we prove the conjecture for every even matrix size.

[6] arXiv:2610.37155 [pdf, html, other]
Title: The existence of the compatible lattice order on the complex field $\mathbb{C}$
Zhengmao He
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

In this paper, we give an affirmative answer to the open question posed by this http URL and this http URL in 1956 concerning the existence of a compatible lattice order on the complex field $\mathbb{C}$. Using valuation rings theory, basis extension in infinite-dimensional vector spaces and extension of field embeddings to algebraic extensions, we obtain that for every real $t>1$ transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$, the compatible lattice order can be chosen such that $0\prec1$, $0\vee i=t$, and $1\wedge t=0$. Consequently, $\mathbb{C}$ admits at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct compatible lattice orders, each extending the usual order on $\mathbb{Q}$.

[7] arXiv:2610.22000 (cross-list from math.NT) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Classification of vanishing fusion rings of rank four
Jingcheng Dong, Sebastien Palcoux, Arnaud Plessis
Comments: 17 pages, Theorem 1.8 has been strengthened (we now classify all nonpointed Burnside fusion rings of rank four), and streamlined Section 3. Comments are welcome!
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Mathematical Physics (math-ph); Quantum Algebra (math.QA); Rings and Algebras (math.RA); Representation Theory (math.RT)

A fusion ring is said to be vanishing if one of its multiplication matrices has determinant zero. We classify all vanishing fusion rings of rank four. We then provide a more explicit classification in the integral case, which leads to new consequences: there are infinitely many nonisomorphic simple integral vanishing fusion rings of rank four and no simple integral fusion ring of rank four is 1-Frobenius. We also classify all Burnside fusion rings of rank four.

[8] arXiv:2610.35940 (cross-list from math.DG) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Rigidity and Affine Deformations of Foliations on Nilpotent and Solvable Contact Lie Algebras
Cheikh Khoule, Rafik Khalfi
Comments: 5 pages, amsart
Subjects: Differential Geometry (math.DG); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

Let g be a Lie algebra of dimension 2n+1, let beta be a contact form, and let alpha be a nonzero integrable 1-form. We study when the affine path alpha_t = (1-t)alpha + t beta consists of contact forms with the orientation determined by beta \wedge (d beta)^n. We first establish an algebraic criterion: affine deformability is equivalent to the non-negativity of a scalar obtained from the interaction form Theta(alpha, beta) = alpha \wedge (d beta)^n + n beta \wedge d alpha \wedge (d beta)^{n-1}. For unimodular Lie algebras this scalar is (n+1)alpha(Z_beta), where Z_beta is the Reeb vector. We also derive a formula involving the modular character tau(X) = tr(ad_X) in the non-unimodular case. On nilpotent contact Lie algebras, every integrable 1-form is closed; using the standard structural properties of nilpotent contact Lie algebras, this implies that every left-invariant foliation is an affine origin for every fixed contact form. An explicit three-dimensional example illustrates that the modular term can yield affine deformability even when alpha(Z_beta) = 0. We discuss the implications for left-invariant forms on Lie groups and compact quotients. We do not claim a general obstruction example in the non-unimodular case; establishing such an example requires a separate verified construction.

[9] arXiv:2610.35968 (cross-list from math.HO) [pdf, html, other]
Title: On the frieze patterns by Conway and Coxeter
Anna Felikson, Pavel Tumarkin
Comments: This survey was written in November 2025 for the prospected book with a tentative title 'The Conway Book', in preparation
Subjects: History and Overview (math.HO); Combinatorics (math.CO); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

The story of frieze patterns started in 1970s with a paper by Coxeter and two short papers by Conway and Coxeter, one listing questions and the other providing brief answers. During the first three decades it was followed by only a handful of papers observing some further properties of the same objects. Then Propp (2005) and Caldero and Chapoton (2006) established connections between frieze patterns and the recent theory of cluster algebras introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky in early 2000s. Since then the theory of frieze patterns exploded with new results, and frieze patterns found a wide spectrum of connections in various domains of mathematics, including combinatorics, geometry, representation theory and integrable systems. The literature on frieze patterns grew vastly and continues to expand. In this note we give a brief overview of some of the directions emerging from the papers by Conway and Coxeter and single out core ideas which were later replayed in different contexts.

[10] arXiv:2610.36401 (cross-list from math.GR) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Degree-one cohomology of metacyclic $2$-groups: a low-bit dichotomy
Marina Palaisti
Subjects: Group Theory (math.GR); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

We study the subalgebra generated by degree-one mod-$2$ cohomology for metacyclic $2$-groups $G=\langle a,b\mid a^{2^\alpha}=1,\ b^{2^m}=a^c,\ b^{-1}ab=a^r\rangle$ with unique normal forms, where $\alpha,m\ge2$ and $r\equiv-1\pmod4$. Presentation compatibility forces $c=0$ or $2^{\alpha-1}$ and $G^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_2\times C_{2^m}$. If $c\equiv0\pmod4$, the degree-one-generated algebra is $\mathbb{F}_2 [x,y]/(y^2,x^2+xy)$, a four-dimensional algebra shared by the four nonmodular types of Aaboun--Zekhnini. The unique exceptional case $\alpha=2$, $c=2$ is modular and has infinite-dimensional degree-one algebra $\mathbb{F}_2[x,y]/(y^2,x^2y)$. The regular algebra is PBW, Koszul, universally Koszul, and strongly Koszul, whereas the modular algebra is not Koszul. We realize the regular algebra by inflation from the common quotient $C_4\rtimes_{-1}C_4$ and identify its quadratic dual with the characteristic-two Jordan plane. For the inversion tower $C_4\rtimes_{-1}C_{2^m}$, we give uniform presentations of the full mod-$2$ cohomology rings and determine the kernels and images of inflation in every degree. Their direct limit yields the continuous cohomology ring of $C_4\rtimes_{-1}\mathbb Z_2$. We also record an arithmetic consequence for Galois groups arising over cyclotomic $\mathbb Z_2$-extensions of real quadratic fields.

[11] arXiv:2610.36638 (cross-list from math.RT) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Young's lattice, invariant ideals, and weak identities
Vladimir Dotsenko, Artem Lopatin
Comments: 21 pages, comments are welcome
Subjects: Representation Theory (math.RT); Combinatorics (math.CO); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

For a $d$-dimensional vector space $V$ over a field of characteristic zero, we show that $GL(V)$-stable ideals of the universal enveloping algebra of the free two-step nilpotent Lie algebra $\mathfrak n_2(V)$ are in natural correspondence with filters in Young's lattice of integer partitions with at most $d$ rows. The same classification holds for $GL(V)$-stable Poisson ideals of $S(V\oplus\Lambda^2(V))$ with its natural Poisson bracket. As applications, we determine all weak polynomial identities of the $n$-th Weyl algebra relative to its standard generating subspace and of the universal enveloping algebra of the $(2n+1)$-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra relative to the Lie algebra itself.

[12] arXiv:2610.37061 (cross-list from cs.CR) [pdf, html, other]
Title: GKS Fully Homomorphic Encryption: Security Analysis
John Graham, Soundes Marzougui, Miloš Pavlović, Vladimir Shpilrain
Comments: 41 pages
Subjects: Cryptography and Security (cs.CR); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

All traditional encryption schemes share a fundamental operational vulnerability: encrypted data must be decrypted before it can be used. Fully homomorphic encryption eliminates this vulnerability by enabling computation directly on encrypted data, but existing FHE schemes impose substantial practical constraints that limit deployment. This paper presents a formal security analysis of the Gribov-Kahrobaei-Shpilrain (GKS) private-key FHE scheme. GKS requires no bootstrapping or de-linearization, produces bit-exact decryption, supports native encrypted search, and scales to unbounded depth.

[13] arXiv:2610.37490 (cross-list from math.NT) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Rank functions, $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extensions and class numbers
Iq Souza
Comments: 21 pages, comments are welcome!
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Group Theory (math.GR); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

If $F$ is a number field and $F = F_0 \subset F_1 \subset F_2\subset \cdots$ is a tower of Galois extensions with $\mathrm{Gal}(F_n/F)$ cyclic of order $p^n$, K. Iwasawa gave a closed formula for the growth of the $p$-part of the class number of $F_n$ whenever $n$ is large enough. We present a new proof of this formula, obtained by reinterpreting its summands in terms of Sylvester matrix rank functions on the Iwasawa algebra $\Lambda$ without resorting to the classification of finitely-generated $\Lambda$-modules up to pseudo-isomorphism.

[14] arXiv:2501.01955 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Positive semidefiniteness of $h$-Shuhan matrices (analogs of Borcherds Cartan matrices) and associated determinant sequences
Weicai Wu, Mingxuan Yang
Comments: 29 pages
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

We introduce an $h$-Shuhan matrix as a real square matrix $H=(h_{ij})$ with a common diagonal entry $h\ge0$, nonpositive integral off-diagonal entries, and the following Cartan-type condition on each unordered pair $\{i,j\}$: either $h_{ij}=h_{ji}$, or $\max\{h_{ij},h_{ji}\}=-1$ and $\min\{h_{ij},h_{ji}\}\le -2$. These conditions imply that the scalar shift $H+(2-h)I_n$ is a generalized Cartan matrix; not every generalized Cartan matrix arises from an $h$-Shuhan matrix in this way. We classify, for $0\le h<2$, all indecomposable $h$-Shuhan matrices that are positive semidefinite, generalized positive semidefinite, or virtual positive semidefinite (the last notion means that all principal minors are nonnegative). For $h<2$, virtual positive semidefiniteness forces $H+(2-h)I_n$ to be of finite Cartan type, the positive-type case of Vinberg's trichotomy (1971). At $h=2$, we describe the boundary case in terms of finite and affine Cartan matrices. The associated determinant recurrences hold for general $h$. We study congruences for integral $h$ and asymptotic and Fibonacci-type identities for $h>2$.

[15] arXiv:2608.31087 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Properties of the $\mathcal V$-Monoid of Weighted Leavitt Path Algebras
Rishabh Goswami, Alfilgen Sebandal
Comments: 23 pages. v2: corrected typos and some errors in Sections 3 and 4. Comments are welcome
Subjects: Rings and Algebras (math.RA); Combinatorics (math.CO)

For a row-finite weighted graph $(E,w)$, Preusser showed that the monoid $\mathcal{V}(L_k(E,w))$ of finitely generated projective modules over the weighted Leavitt path algebra $L_k(E,w)$ is isomorphic to a combinatorially defined weighted graph monoid $\mathcal{M}(E,w)$. We study two structural properties of $\mathcal{M}(E,w)$: confluence and cancellativity. We introduce a reduction system on the free commutative monoid presenting $\mathcal{M}(E,w)$, obtain sufficient conditions for non-confluence by constructing explicit non-confluent triples, and provide a complete confluence characterization for certain classes of weighted graphs. Turning to cancellativity, we work within Preusser's class of weighted graphs satisfying Condition (LPA), for which $L_k(E,w)$ is isomorphic to an unweighted Leavitt path algebra $L_k(F)$ via a two-step construction. We introduce an auxiliary graph associated to the intermediate step of this construction and use it to give a graph-theoretic characterization of when $\mathcal{M}(E,w)$ is cancellative. Finally, under Condition (LPA), we show that Preusser's construction upgrades to a graded isomorphism $L_k(E,w) \cong_{\operatorname{gr}} L_k(F)$ with respect to the standard $\mathbb{Z}^{\lambda(E,w)}$-grading of weighted Leavitt path algebras, yielding $\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_k(E,w)) \cong \mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_k(F))$ as $\mathbb{Z}^{\lambda(E,w)}$-monoids.

[16] arXiv:2409.02333 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Admissible groups over number fields
Deependra Singh
Comments: 23 pages; exposition improved based on referee comments
Journal-ref: J. Algebra 690 (2026), 832-859
Subjects: Number Theory (math.NT); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

Given a field $K$, one may ask which finite groups are Galois groups of field extensions $L/K$ such that $L$ is a maximal subfield of a division algebra with center $K$. Such a group is called admissible over $K$. This connection between inverse Galois theory and division algebras was first explored by Schacher in the 1960s. In this manuscript we consider this problem when $K$ is a number field. For the case when $L/K$ is tamely ramified, we give a complete classification of number fields for which every solvable Sylow-metacyclic group is admissible, extending J. Sonn's result for $K = \mathbb{Q}$. For the case when $L/K$ is allowed to be wildly ramified, we give a characterization of admissible groups over several classes of number fields, and partial results in other cases.

[17] arXiv:2501.02466 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Comparing classical tilting modules and $τ$-tilting modules
Xiao-Wu Chen, Zhi-Wei Li, Xiaojin Zhang, Zhibing Zhao
Comments: 11 Pages,Typos and errors have been corrected
Subjects: Representation Theory (math.RT); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

We characterize support $\tau$-tilting modules as classical tilting modules over quotient algebras satisfying a tensor-vanishing condition, and characterize classical tilting modules as $\tau$-tilting modules satisfying a ${\rm Tor}^1$-vanishing condition. We use delooping levels to study \emph{Self-orthogonal $\tau$-tilting Conjecture}: any self-orthogonal $\tau$-tilting module is classical tilting. We confirm the conjecture when the endomorphism algebra of the module has finite global delooping level.

[18] arXiv:2504.14844 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Geometric construction of Kashiwara crystals on multiparameter persistence
Yasuaki Hiraoka, Kohei Yahiro
Comments: Published version
Subjects: Representation Theory (math.RT); Algebraic Geometry (math.AG); Rings and Algebras (math.RA)

We establish a geometric construction of Kashiwara crystals on the irreducible components of the varieties of multiparameter persistence modules. Our approach differs from the seminal work of Kashiwara and Saito, as well as subsequent related works, by emphasizing commutative relations rather than preprojective relations for a given quiver. Furthermore, we provide explicit descriptions of the Kashiwara operators in the fundamental cases of 1- and 2-parameter persistence modules, offering concrete insights into the crystal structure in these settings.
