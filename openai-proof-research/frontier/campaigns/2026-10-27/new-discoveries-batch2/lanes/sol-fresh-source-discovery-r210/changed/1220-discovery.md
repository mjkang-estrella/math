Conjecture 41, printed p20: for EVERY pair of integers m,F with 2 ≤ m ≤ F+1 and m not dividing F,

|L(m,F)| ≤ |I(m,F)|.

A numerical semigroup S is a cofinite additive submonoid of the NONNEGATIVE INTEGERS containing 0. Its multiplicity is min(S minus {0}); its Frobenius number is max(Z minus S). Its unique minimal generating set is msg(S), and μ(S) counts generators strictly GREATER than F(S). I(m,F) comprises all S with multiplicity m, Frobenius F and μ(S)>0; L(m,F) has the same invariants and μ(S)=0. These refer to internal/leaf status in the FULL numerical-semigroup tree, not the restricted enumeration tree (Remark 40). All admissible F are positive. Counts are finite since S is determined by S intersect [1,F]. There is no characteristic or field parameter. No fixed genus, embedding dimension, symmetric/pseudosymmetric restriction or asymptotic qualification is allowed. The endpoint m=F+1 is included. Conjectures 25, 35 and the genus/Fibonacci questions are not additional fresh slots.

Here is the full applicable classical enumeration bridge. D=<m> union [F+1,infinity) belongs to R(m,F), the family of all semigroups with the two invariants, and is its minimum. For S≠D there is a smallest nonmultiple r(S)<F. It is a primitive generator: a decomposition into positive summands would include a smaller nonmultiple in S. Deleting it preserves additive closure (only a primitive can be removed), multiplicity and F. Repetition reaches D and deletes one integer at each step. Conversely adding a gap x preserves the semigroup exactly when 2x is in S and x+s is in S for every positive s in S. Imposing m<x<r(S), x≠F gives the unique reverse tree edges, since the added x becomes the smallest nonmultiple. This proves exact finite enumeration and is implemented directly by checks.py. It provides no comparison of leaf/internal cardinalities.

For 2m>F+1, every positive sum is at least 2m>F+1, so F+1 is primitive and every S in R(m,F) is internal. For m<F<2m the interval model is S={0,m} union A union [F+1,infinity), A⊆{m+1,...,F−1}, because sums of positive small elements exceed F. Thus there are 2^(F−m−1) semigroups, and when additionally 2m>F+1 all are internal. For m=F+1 the ordinary semigroup is unique and internal. This is the complete applicable high-m bridge behind source Proposition 42, not a new outcome. Merely m>F/2 is insufficient at the odd boundary F=2m−1: <3,4> has m=3,F=5 and is a leaf. The initial checker mistakenly used the weaker threshold, failed, and is preserved.

For fixed m,F the internal class is downward closed under inclusion within R(m,F): if T⊆S and S has a primitive x>F, then x belongs to T (all integers above F do), and a positive decomposition in T would be a decomposition in S. This proves downward closure. Downward closure and a common minimum alone do NOT imply a majority: an arbitrarily long abstract rooted chain may have just its minimum marked downward closed. The actual numerical-semigroup structure must supply more. No shortness-based exclusion or claimed full classical subsumption is made.

## Sustained universal route and exact obstruction

A sufficient, stronger route is an injection assigning to every leaf S an INTERNAL T⊂S with the SAME m,F. Define N(A)={T∈I(m,F): T⊂S for some S∈A} for any set A of leaves. The exact missing lemma is |N(A)|≥|A| for EVERY admissible m,F and EVERY A⊆L(m,F). Finite Hall matching would then give the desired injection and hence Conjecture 41. This Hall property is sufficient, not known equivalent to the original count inequality; failure of this route would not refute the source.

The simplest deterministic map repeatedly deletes the current ratio and stops at the first internal semigroup. It reaches an internal ancestor because D is internal. It is NOT injective. At m=4,F=11, the two leaves

This supplies an exact ordinary-math obstruction to the deterministic injection, not a source counterexample. An augmenting-path selection among alternative internal subsemigroups repairs every tested pair, but no uniform Hall argument is proved.
