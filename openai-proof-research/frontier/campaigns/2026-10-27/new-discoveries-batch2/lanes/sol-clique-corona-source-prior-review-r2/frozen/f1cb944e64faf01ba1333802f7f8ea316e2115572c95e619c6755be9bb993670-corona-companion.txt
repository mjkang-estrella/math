Unimodality of the Independence Polynomial of G ◦ (Kp ∪ Kq )

Do Trong Hoang1 · Vadim E. Levit2 · Eugen Mandrescu3 · My Hanh Pham4

Received: 11 April 2024 / Accepted: 22 August 2024 / Published online: 19 November 2024

Abstract
In this paper, our research primarily focuses on f -symmetric and unimodal properties of
independence polynomials I (G ◦ (K p ∪ K q ); x). More precisely, we prove that the indepen-
dence polynomial I (G ◦ (K p ∪ K q ); x) is unimodal for every graph G, whenever the positive
integers p and q are large enough. In addition, we establish several inequalities involving the
coefficients of I (G ◦ (K p ∪ K q ); x) for arbitrary p and q.

Keywords Independence polynomial · Unimodality · f -symmetric polynomial

The independence polynomial, first introduced by Gutman and Harary in 1983 [12], has
been extensively discussed in a substantial number of publications. Recall that a polynomial
P(x) = a0 + a1 x + a2 x 2 + · · · + an x n is:

 – unimodal if there exists an index 0 ≤ k ≤ n, called the mode of the P(x), such that
                             a0 ≤ a1 ≤ · · · ≤ ak ≥ ak+1 ≥ · · · ≥ an ;
 – log-concave if ai2 ≥ ai−1 · ai+1 holds for all 1 ≤ i ≤ n − 1;
 – symmetric if an−i = ai for all 0 ≤ i ≤  n2 ;
 – f -symmetric if an−i = f (i) · ai for all 0 ≤ i ≤  n2 [20].

   It is known that I (G; x) is unimodal whenever it is log-concave. It is worth reminding
that 1-symmetric polynomials are also known as self-reciprocal or palindromic polynomials.
   The problem of unimodality has been thoroughly investigated across multiple areas,
including algebra, combinatorics, and geometry (see [1, 3, 4, 6–8, 13, 18, 19, 21, 22, 24,
25, 30] and their references). The independence polynomial of an arbitrary graph G is
not necessarily unimodal or symmetric. For instance, the polynomial I (K 127 + 3K 7 ; x) =
1 + 148x + 147x 2 + 343x 3 is neither unimodal nor symmetric.
   In [1], Alavi, Malde, Schwenk and Erdös proved that for any permutation π of {1, 2, . . . , α}
there is a graph G with α(G) = α such that sπ(1) < sπ(2) < . . . < sπ(α) . Nevertheless, for
trees, they proposed the following conjecture, which is still open.

Conjecture 1.1 [1] The independence polynomial of a tree is unimodal.

   Recall that a graph G is well-covered if all its maximal independent sets are of equal
size [29]. It has been shown that the independence polynomial of well-covered graphs is not
unimodal in general [23]. Some more findings related to well-covered trees may be found
in [16] and [17]. Despite these results, Conjecture 1.1 remains unresolved. Actually, the
independence polynomial of well-covered graphs may sufficiently vary from unimodality
[23, 28]. In this context, it is worth mentioning the following conjecture regarding the partial
unimodality of the coefficients of the independence polynomial.

Conjecture 1.2 (Roller–Coaster Conjecture) [28] For every permutation σ of the set { α2 ,
. . . , α}, there is a well-covered graph G with α(G) = α such that the coefficients of I (G; x)
satisfy sσ ( α2 ) < sσ ( α2 )+1 < · · · < sσ (α) .

that if H is λ-quasi-regularizable, then H has no isolated vertices and moreover it is also λ -
quasi-regularizable. In the case λ = 1, H is called a quasi-regularizable graph. It is known
that well-covered graphs without isolated vertices are quasi-regularizable [5]. The vertex
expansion ratio of an independent set S in the graph H is defined as λ H (S) = |N H|S|(S)| , where
in the case S is an empty set we consider λ H (S) to be infinite. [2]. The vertex expansion

6 Conclusions

This paper explores the f -symmetric and unimodal properties inherent in the independence
polynomials of graphs. More specifically, our results demonstrate that I (G ◦ (K p ∪ K q ); x)
exhibits f -symmetry and unimodality for any graph G when p and q are sufficiently large.
   A very well-covered graph G is defined as a well-covered graph without isolated vertices
for which n(G) = 2α(G). For example, G ◦ K 1 is always very well-covered for every graph
G. In [6], it is demonstrated that every graph G can be viewed as an induced subgraph of a
very well-covered graph whose independence polynomial is unimodal, using the k-th iterated
leafy extension of G. However, the order of the graph resulting from the k-th leafy extension
may be quite large. According to Theorem 1 and Corollary 6.2, it follows that each graph G
can also be embedded as an induced subgraph of a non-well-covered graph G ◦ (K p ∪ K q )
with sufficiently large p and q, or even p = q = 1, such that its independence polynomial is
unimodal. Moreover, since α(K p ∪ K q ) = 2, it is worth mentioning that if I (G; x) is real-
rooted, then by [21, Theorem 5.4], I (G ◦ (K p ∪ K q ); x) is also real-rooted and, consequently,
unimodal. Thus, Theorems 1 and 2, along with the results from [8] and [21], lead us to the
following.

Conjecture 6.1 For any positive integers p and q, I (G ◦ (K p ∪ K q ); x) is unimodal for all
graphs G.

   By substituting p = 1 into Corollary 5.6(1) and applying the fact that the independence
polynomial of G ◦ 2K 1 is palindromic, as established in [31], we obtain the following.

Corollary 6.2 ([26, Theorem 2.2]) I (G ◦ 2K 1 ; x) is unimodal with the mode at n.

   In the case where G is a tree of order n, G ◦ 2K 1 is a tree of order 3n. Hence, G can
be considered as a sub-tree of a larger tree whose independence polynomial is unimodal,
regardless of whether I (G; x) is unimodal. It motivates the following.

Conjecture 6.3 Let T  be a sub-tree of a tree T . If I (T ; x) is unimodal, then I (T  ; x) is
unimodal as well.

   Clearly, Conjecture 1.1 is equivalent to Conjecture 6.3.
