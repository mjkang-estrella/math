Title:The ultraspherical background of the Jacobi phase expansion, in closed form
    
Authors:Iván Area            
View a PDF of the paper titled The ultraspherical background of the Jacobi phase expansion, in closed form, by Iv\'an Area
    View PDF
    HTML (experimental)



    
            Abstract:The large-degree phase of the Jacobi polynomial $P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\cos\theta)$ contains, at each order in $\Nn^{-1}$, $\Nn=n+(\alpha+\beta+1)/2$, an additive constant $\kappa_r$ that the phase equation does not determine. These constants are antisymmetric in $(\alpha^2,\beta^2)$ and vanish for $\alpha=\beta$. We obtain three results. First, on the diagonal $\alpha=\beta$ the logarithm of the midpoint amplitude is computed in closed form: if $L_n$ denotes the coefficient of $(2\Nn)^{-n}$ in that logarithm, then $\Upsilon_A(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}L_nt^n/(n-1)!$ equals \[
\Upsilon_A(t)=\frac{e^{t}\bigl(\cosh2\alpha t-\cosh t\bigr)}{\sinh 2t}. \] The proof is algebraic: the identity is established for half-odd-integer $\alpha$, where the expansion terminates, by means of two classical recurrences for Bessel polynomials, and is extended to all $\alpha$ by a degree argument. Second, the derivative of the logarithm of the amplitude with respect to $\alpha^2$ at the Legendre point $\alpha=\beta=0$ is computed in closed form to all orders. An exact reduction of an Appell $F_3$ function to a Gauss function shows that it is a parameter derivative of a zero-balanced ${}_2F_1$ at argument $2$; the real part of its expansion is a trigamma series, and the imaginary part is the unique formal solution of an explicit central-difference equation. Third, we deduce that the coefficients $w_m$ of an explicit even power series attached to the imaginary part satisfy $\nu_2(w_m)=-2\nu_2((2m)!)$ for every $m\ge0$ ($\nu_2$ the $2$-adic valuation). This determines, for every $r$, the exact power of $2$ in the denominator of the coefficient of $\alpha^2$ in the polynomial that represents $\kappa_r$. We also give a factorisation of the amplitude into products of pairs of Gauss functions, further valuation formulas, and a numerical illustration for the zeros.
