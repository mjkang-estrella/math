For n × n n\times n matrices A A and B B , we consider the inequality Re ⟨ [ B , A ] , [ B , A ] q ⟩ ≤ c ⁡ ( q ) ​ ‖ A ‖ 2 ​ ‖ B ‖ 2 , \mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\langle[B,A],[B,A]_{q}\rangle\leq c(q)\|A\|^{2}\|B\|^{2}, where ⟨ A , B ⟩ = tr ⁡ ( A ∗ ​ B ) \langle A,B\rangle=\tr(A^*B) is the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, ‖ A ‖ \|A\| is the Frobenius norm, [ A , B ] = A ​ B − B ​ A [A,B]=AB-BA is the commutator, and [ A , B ] q = A ​ B − q ​ B ​ A [A,B]_{q}=AB-qBA is the q q -deformed commutator. We prove that when n = 2 n=2 , or when A A is normal with any size n n , the optimal bound is given by c ⁡ ( q ) = ( 1 + q ) + 2 ​ ( 1 + q 2 ) 2 . c(q)=\frac{(1+q)+\sqrt{2(1+q^{2})}}{2}. When q = 0 q=0 , this inequality leads the sharp bound for the r r -function which is recently derived for the application to universal constraints of relaxation rates in open quantum dynamics. Keywords: Hilbert-Schmidt Inner Product, Frobenius Norm, Commutator, BW inequality, 1 Introduction For any complex matrices A , B ∈ M n ​ ( ℂ ) A,B\in M_{n}(\mathop{\mathbb{C}}\nolimits) , the sharp bound for the norm of commutator is known: ‖ [ A , B ] ‖ 2 ≤ 2 ​ ‖ A ‖ 2 ​ ‖ B ‖ 2 , \|[A,B]\|^{2}\leq 2\|A\|^{2}\|B\|^{2}, (1) where [ A , B ] := A ​ B − B ​ A [A,B]:=AB-BA denotes the commutator and the matrix norm is the Frobenius norm ‖ A ‖ = tr ⁡ ( A ∗ ​ A ) \|A\|=\sqrt{\tr(A^*A)} . The inequality is often called the Böttcher-Wenzel (BW) inequality because of the following history: The inequality was first conjectured by Böttcher and Wenzel in [ 2 ] giving a proof for real 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrices and also for normal matrices. Later, Lásló proved BW inequality for 3 × 3 3\times 3 real matrices [ 12 ] , Lu [ 13 ] and, independently, Wong and Jin [ 15 ] proved it for n × n n\times n real matrices, after which the complex case was proved by Böttcher and Wenzel in [ 3 ] . On the other hand, motivated by a problem in the field of open quantum systems [ 1 ] , we have introduced the following real-valued functional in [ 4 ] : r ⁡ ( A , B ) = 1 2 ​ ( ⟨ [ B , A ] , B ​ A ⟩ + ⟨ [ B , A ∗ ] , B ​ A ∗ ⟩ ) r(A,B)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle[B,A],BA\rangle+\langle[B,A^{*}],BA^{*}\rangle\right) (2) for any A , B ∈ M n ​ ( ℂ ) A,B\in M_{n}(\mathop{\mathbb{C}}\nolimits) where A ∗ A^{*} is the Hermitian conjugation of A A and ⟨ A , B ⟩ = tr ⁡ ( A ∗ ​ B ) \langle A,B\rangle=\tr(A^*B) is the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product. The sharp bound for the r r -function was shown in [ 4 ] : r ⁡ ( A , B ) ≤ 1 + 2 2 ​ ‖ A ‖ 2 ​ ‖ B ‖ 2 . r(A,B)\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2}\|A\|^{2}\|B\|^{2}. (3) This has been used to derive a universal constraint [ 6 , 10 , 11 ] for relaxation rates which is satisfied by any quantum Markovian dynamics described by completely positive dynamical semigroup, or GKLS master equation [ 7 ] . For this reason, we called the functional ( 2 ) the r r -function, where “ r r ” stands for relaxation. In [ 4 ] , we also observed a close relationship between BW inequality and inequality ( 3 ). If B B is normal, r r -function reduces to the commutator: r ⁡ ( A , B ) = 1 2 ​ ‖ [ A , B ] ‖ 2 . r(A,B)=\frac{1}{2}\|[A,B]\|^{2}. Hence, the direct application of BW inequality leads the bound r ⁡ ( A , B ) ≤ ‖ A ‖ 2 ​ ‖ B ‖ 2 . r(A,B)\leq\|A\|^{2}\|B\|^{2}. However, this is only true for a normal matrix B B and does not hold for general matrices. On the other hand, applying the triangle inequality, Schwarz inequality, the norm inequality ‖ A ​ B ‖ ≤ ‖ A ‖ ​ ‖ B ‖ \|AB\|\leq\|A\|\|B\| and BW inequality for ( 2 ) implies the following general bound: r ⁡ ( A , B ) ≤ 2 ​ ‖ A ‖ 2 ​ ‖ B ‖ 2 . r(A,B)\leq\sqrt{2}\|A\|^{2}\|B\|^{2}. However, this in turn is a loose inequality. In the end, the sharp bound for general matrices is given by ( 3 ) and, therefore the relation between BW inequality and ( 3 ) is not yet fully understood.
