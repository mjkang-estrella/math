Introduction Let X X be a random vector taking values in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Suppose that it has density f f with respect to the Lebesgue measure. The classical differential entropy (also called Boltzmann-Shannon entropy ) of X X is defined as h ( X ) = − ∫ ℝ d f ( x ) log f ( x ) d x . h(X)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\log f(x)dx. The celebrated entropy power inequality (or EPI for short) of Shannon [ 18 ] (and Stam [ 20 ] ) states that for independent random vectors X X and Y Y in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} such that the entropies of X , Y X,Y , and X + Y X+Y exist, it holds that e 2 d ​ h ​ ( X + Y ) ≥ e 2 d ​ h ​ ( X ) + e 2 d ​ h ​ ( Y ) , e^{\frac{2}{d}h(X+Y)}\geq e^{\frac{2}{d}h(X)}+e^{\frac{2}{d}h(Y)}, (1.1) where equality holds if and only if X X and Y Y are Gaussian random vectors with proportional covariance matrices. The EPI found its original applications in the study of channel capacity in information theory. It has been known very well that EPI is closely related to geometric and functional inequalities in a variety of mathematical fields, maybe most notably the Brunn-Minkowski inequality (or BMI for short) in convex geometry. We refer the interested reader to [ 7 , 12 ] for profound connections among EPI, BMI, and many other geometric and functional inequalities. Convexity (or concavity) underpins the validity of the reversal of many geometric and functional inequalities. A function f : ℝ d → ℝ + f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+} is called log-concave if it can be written as f ⁡ ( x ) = e − V ⁡ ( x ) f(x)=e^{-V(x)} , where V : ℝ d → ℝ ∪ { ∞ } V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\} is a convex function. We say that a random vector X X taking values in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} is log-concave provided that its density is a log-concave function. (We also say that X X has log-concave distribution). The class of log-concave functions can be thought of as functional analogs of convex bodies. They are prominent objects studied in convex geometry, high dimensional probability and statistics, as well as theoretical computer science. The class of log-concave distributions include a wide range of important probability distributions, such as the Gaussian distributions, uniform distributions on convex bodies, and exponential distributions. The reversal of EPI was first addressed by Bobkov and Madiman [ 3 ] . They showed that for independent log-concave random vectors, under linear volume preserving maps, inequality ( 1.1 ) can be reversed at the cost of a constant factor on the r.h.s of the inequality. This can be thought of as a functional analog of Milman’s reverse BMI for convex bodies [ 13 ] . It is necessary to place log-concave random vectors “at right positions” via linear volume preserving maps for the reversed EPI to hold. We are interested in the sharp reverse EPI for independent and identically distributed (or i.i.d. for short) log-concave random vectors. In this case, linear volume preserving maps are not needed and the question can be phrased as follows. Conjecture 1.1 (Folklore) . Let X X and Y Y be i.i.d. log-concave random vectors taking values in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . The entropy increment h ⁡ ( X + Y ) − h ⁡ ( X ) h(X+Y)-h(X) is maximized when X X and Y Y have exponential distributions. Clearly, the entropy increment is affine invariant. An earlier result of Cover and Zhang [ 6 ] shows that log-concavity and identical distribution (without independence between X X and Y Y ) yield the upper bound d ​ log ⁡ 2 d\log 2 of the entropy increment. Huang, Slomka, Tkocz, and Vritsiou ( [ 10 ] , Section 6) observed that the improvement of Cover and Zhang’s bound for Conjecture 1.1 will lead to important progress on Hadwiger’s covering problem. Ball, Nayar, and Tkocz [ 2 ] studied the entropy increment for a two dimensional log-concave random vector ( X , Y ) (X,Y) , whose marginals have identical differential entropy (but not necessarily independent). This variant of reverse EPI and its generalizations are entropic analogs of Busemann’s convexity theorem of intersection bodies [ 4 ] and the convexity of p p -cross-section bodies conjectured by Gardner and Giannopoulos [ 8 ] (the end of Section 5, and the discussion in [ 11 ] ). Aforementioned forward and reverse EPIs have been studied for general Rényi entropies. Another related problem is the characterization of log-concave distributions that maximize the Rényi entropic increment h p ​ ( X − Y ) − h p ​ ( X ) h_{p}(X-Y)-h_{p}(X) . In this case, it is suspected that exponential distributions are always maximizers. This resembles the Rogers-Shephard inequality in convex geometry [ 16 ] . In a certain sense, the symmetry of X − Y X-Y (implicitly) makes this problem more tractable than its counterpart of the X + Y X+Y case. Melbourne and Tkocz ( [ 15 ] , Theorem VI.1) showed that the increment of Rényi entropy of order p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 is indeed maximized by exponential random vectors. A discrete analog was obtained by Melbourne and Palafox-Castillo ( [ 14 ] , Theorem 2.11) for integer-valued monotone log-concave random variables. 2.
