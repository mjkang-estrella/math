Abstract This supplementary material contains most of the proofs of the theorems and lemma
appearing in Cénac et al. (2020), together with an appendix giving an example of invariant
σ-finite measure that charges irrational infinite contexts.

Proof of Proposition 3.1. (i) =⇒ (ii). Take c ∈ C and α ∈ A. Assume that αc ∈ I.
So, if β ∈ A is any letter, then αcβ ∈ T . The item (i) implies therefore that cβ ∈ T ,
which contradicts c ∈ C.
   (ii) =⇒ (i). Take α ∈ A and w ∈ W such that αw ∈ T . If w ∈    / T then there exists a
finite context c such that w = cw0 with w0 6= ∅. It comes αcw0 ∈ T , which implies αc ∈ I
and this contradicts (ii).
   (i) ⇐⇒ (iii) is straightforward.
   (ii) =⇒ (iv) What needs to be proved is that cont (Un+1 ) only depends on Un
through cont (Un ). In other words, we shall prove that for all s ∈ R, α ∈ A, cont (αs)
only depends on s through cont(s). This is clear because

Indeed, cont(s) ∈ C and if cont(s) ∈ C f , then (ii) implies α cont(s) ∈  / I (if cont(s)
is an infinite context, this means that s = cont(s) and the above equality is straight-
forward). Therefore α cont(s) writes cw with c ∈ C and w ∈ W. On one hand, this
entails cont (α cont(s)) = c. On the other hand, this means that cw is a prefix of αs thus
cont(αs) = c.
   (iv) =⇒ (ii) We shall prove the contrapositive. Assume there exists c ∈ C f and

cont(s), but on a prefix of s strictly longer than cont(s). Thus, conditionally to Cn , the
transition to the context Cn+1 does not depend on Cn but on a strictly longer prefix of
Un . This proves that (Cn )n is not Markovian.

Proof of Lemma 3.4. 1. Assume that t is a context LIS such that c = t · · · , then,
by definition of a context LIS, there exists α ∈ A such that αt is a context alpha-LIS.
Since T is stable, Lemma 3.3 implies that αt is a context, therefore αc ∈ / C because two
different contexts cannot be prefix of one another. Thus Ac 6= ∅.
   2. Let α be in Ac so that αc is not a context. As T is stable, αc is not internal, thus
it is an external node. Let c0 = cont(αc). The context c0 is a strict prefix of αc. Since
T is stable, σ(c0 ) is non-external. But σ(c0 ) cannot be a context because it is a prefix
of c which is a context. Thus σ(c0 ) is internal. This implies that tα := σ(c0 ) is the LIS
of c0 and a prefix of c as well, and that αtα = c0 ∈ C. Besides, whenever (i) and (ii)
are satisfied, tα writes necessarily tα = σ (cont (αc)). Thus existence and uniqueness of
tα are proven. Finally, since c0 = αtα = cont(αc), tα is a prefix of c, so that for every
β ∈/ Ac , βtα is a prefix of the context βc. Consequently, βtα ∈/ C because two different
contexts cannot be prefix of one another.

Proof of Lemma 3.6. Irreducibility. Let c and c0 be finite contexts. Denote c0 = αk . . . α2 α1 ,
k ⩾ 1. In order to prove that the Markov chain (Cn ) has a non-null transition from c to c0 ,
let us add the successive letters of c0 (starting from α1 ) to the left of c and prove that at
each time, the transition is possible and non-null. Assume that C0 = c and consider the
word α1 c. As T is shift-stable α1 c ∈/ I and c1 := cont(α1 c) is a (possibly not strict) prefix
of α1 c which may be written c1 = α1 w1 with w1 a prefix (possibly empty) of c. The transi-
tion equals qc (α1 ) and is therefore non-null. Let us add the second letter and consider the
word α2 α1 w1 = α2 c1 . Again α2 α1 w1 ∈ / I and c2 := cont (α2 α1 w1 ) is a prefix of α2 α1 w1 .
Here, the point is that c2 cannot be α2 otherwise we would have σ k−2 (c0 ) = α2 α1 ∈       /T
which would contradict the shift-stability of T . Therefore c2 = α2 α1 w2 with w2 a pre-
fix of w1 . By adding successively the letters of c0 , with the same arguments, one gets
a sequence of contexts cj = cont (αj cj−1 ) = αj αj−1 . . . α1 . . . . The last step necessarily
writes ck = αk . . . α1 = c0 because c0 is a context. At each step, the transition is non zero
because all the qc (α) are non-null.

paths from c to itself equals 1. Let k be the length of c. The above proof of the irre-

similar arguments, let α be any letter and let c0 be the context c0 = cont (αc). There is
