   This paper builds directly on [3]. The fractional-index extension (3), the integer trace,
the order ⪰q , the exponent Er (s) and trace formula, the denominator-parity observation,
the coefficientwise-order properties used in Lemma 2.3, the identity underlying (29), and
the polynomial D2,k = Hk are all theirs. Our new contributions are the cross-parameter
comparison in Theorem 1.3, the canonical reduction in Theorem 1.5, the cumulative-coefficient
criterion of Lemma 2.4, and the exact verification for all parameters through k = 200. Han
and Xiong’s separate arguments for the half-integer and positive-integer families do not
provide the cross-denominator comparison needed here.
   The proof of Theorem 1.3 starts from the trace formula, which writes Ta/b,k as a signed
sum of series with nonnegative coefficients indexed by multiples of b. When the comparison
parameter u has even denominator, every surviving sign on its side is positive. The divisibility
hypothesis nests the positive summand-index set for Tr,k inside the summand-index set for
Tu,k , while u ≤ r moves every matched term to an earlier exponent. Lemma 2.3 converts
that shift into coefficientwise dominance. Taking u = 1/2 gives the half-line result, and
taking u = r♭ gives the canonical reduction.
   The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects the required preliminaries. Section 3
proves Theorems 1.3, 1.5, and 1.6, together with their corollaries, and closes by recording
the remaining open problem. Finally, in Appendix A, we describe how AxiomProver, an AI
system under development, autonomously found, formalized, and verified in Lean the proofs
of Theorem 1.3, Corollary 1.4, and Theorem 1.5, and we record precisely what was and was
not supplied to it.

The criterion is thus checked by one pass over the coefficients rather than by a search for a
pairing.

3.1. Proof of the support-dominance theorem. The proof compares the two trace
formulas supplied by Proposition 2.2 summand by summand. Recall the setting: u = c/d
and r = a/b are reduced, d is even, d | lcm(b, 2), and u ≤ r. The first hypothesis makes
every surviving summand of Tu,k positive; the second embeds the positive summand-index
set of Tr,k into the summand-index set of Tu,k ; the third places each matched summand of
Tu,k at an exponent no larger than that of its partner. Lemma 2.3 converts those exponent
inequalities into coefficientwise dominance, and the summands left unmatched contribute
with a favorable sign.
Proof of Theorem 1.3. Write u = c/d and r = a/b in lowest terms, put L = lcm(b, 2), and
