Abstract

Let Iνx be the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order ν. Motivated by a conjecture on the convexity of the ratio Wνx=xIνx/Iν+1x for ν>-2, using the monotonicity rules for a ratio of two power series and an elementary technique, we present fully the convexity of the functions Wνx, Wνx-x2/2ν+4 and Wνx1/θ for θ≥2 on 0,∞ in different value ranges of ν, which give an answer to the conjecture and extend known results. As consequences, some monotonicity results and new functional inequalities for Wνx are established. As applications, an open problem and a conjectures are settled. Finally, a conjecture on the complete monotonicity of Wνx1/θ for θ≥2 is proposed.

Keywords: Modified Bessel function, Convexity, Monotonicity, Inequality

Introduction

It is well-known that the second-order modified Bessel differential equation

x2y″x+xy′x-x2+ν2yx=0, 1.1

has linearly independent solutions, that are, modified Bessel functions of the first and second, as usual, denoted by Iν and Kν, respectively, see [1, p. 77]. The modified Bessel function of the first kind Iνx can be represented explicitly by the infinite series as

Iνx=∑n=0∞x/22n+νn!Γν+n+1,x∈R,ν∈R\{-1,-2,…}, 1.2

while the modified Bessel function of the second kind Kνx is given by

Kνx=π2I-νx-Iνxsinνπ,

where the right-hand side of the formula above is replaced by its limiting value if ν is an integer. It is known that Iνx satisfies the recurrence relations

xIν′x+νIνx=xIν-1x, 1.3
xIν′x-νIνx=xIν+1x, 1.4

which imply that

Iν-1x-Iν+1x=2νxIνx 1.5

and

yνx:=xIν′xIνx=Wν-1x-ν=x2Wνx+ν, 1.6

where

Wνx=xIνxIν+1xforν>-2andx>0. 1.7

The ratio Wνx=xIνx/Iν+1x plays an important role in finite elasticity [2, 3] and epidemiological models [4, 5], while another ratio Rνx=Iν+1x/Iνx appeared in probability and statistics [6–8], and was applied in chemical kinetics [9, 10], optics [11] and signal processing [12]. For this reason, the properties of the ratio Wνx have attracted many scholars’ interest.

In 1983 Watson [13] showed that Wνx is increasing on (0,∞) for all ν≥0, which was used in the study of a Bayesian estimation problem by Robert [7]. This result was proven again in 1984 by Simpson and Spector [2] using another way. Baricz and Neuman [14, Theorem 2.2 (d)] in 2007 proved that Wνx is increasing on (0,∞) for all ν>-2. Another proof of this monotonicity property for ν>-1 can be seen in [15, Theorem 2.2 (i)]. In this paper, Baricz [15, Theorem 2.2 (i)] proved that the function x↦xμ-νIνx/Iμx is strictly increasing on (0,∞) for all μ>ν>-1.

In [13] Watson also showed that the ratio Rνx=Iν+1x/Iνx is increasing and concave on (0,∞) for all ν≥-1/2. Using this result, Baricz [16, Eq. (2.12)] proved that the ratio yνx (=Wν-1x-ν) satisfies yν″x<1/ν+1 for ν≥-1/2, which implies that the function x↦Wν-1x-x2/2ν+2 is strictly concave on 0,∞ for ν≥-1/2. In 1984 Simpson and Spector [2] proved that the ratio Wνx is convex on (0,∞) for all ν≥0. Based on numerical experiments, Baricz [15, p. 591] made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

The function Wνx is strictly convex on 0,∞ for all ν>-1.

Remark 1

In a recent paper [17], Yang and Tian pointed out that Baricz’s conjecture may be modified as follows: Wνx is strictly convex on 0,∞ for all ν≥-1/2 and concave on 0,∞ if -3/2≤ν<-1/2.

It is worth mentioning that, as early as in 1979, Ismail and Kelker [18] showed a more deep result which states that the function x↦xν-μ/2Iμx/Iνx is strictly completely monotonic on 0,∞ for all μ>ν>-1. This, by choosing μ=ν+1, implies that the function x↦1/Wνx is strictly decreasing and convex on 0,∞ for ν>-1.

Motivated by Baricz’s conjecture and the consequence of Ismail and Kelker’s result, the aim of this paper is to further investigate the convexity of the ratios Wνx and Wνx1/θ on 0,∞ for ν>-2 instead of ν>-1 with θ≥2.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we recall some tools which will be used in this paper and establish an asymptotic expansion of Wνx. Our main results are presented in Sect. 3. In the first subsection, we present the monotonicity of the function xWν′x-θWνx for θ=1 and θ≥2; in the second subsection, we find that the ratio Wνx is strictly convex on 0,∞ if and only if ν≥-1/2, which gives answer to Baricz’s conjecture; while the functions x↦Wνx1/θ for θ≥2 and x↦Wνx-x2/2ν+4 are both strictly concave on 0,∞ for ν>-2, the former concavity improves a consequence of Ismail and Kelker; in the third subsection, we give necessary and sufficient conditions for which the functions x↦Wνx-p/x where p=ν+1/2 and 2ν+1 are respectively convex and concave on 0,∞. Some consequences of main results are given in Sect. 4, which include the monotonicity of three functions involving Wνx and several new functional inequalities for Wνx. In Sect. 5, as applications, we give answers to Gaunt’s open problem and Baricz–Neuman’s conjecture. Finally, we make a conjecture that the function x↦Wνx1/θ for θ≥2 and ν>-1 is a Bernstein function.

Three tools

To prove the monotonicity of Fθ,νx, we need three tools. The first tool is the so-called H-function Hf,g and two identities, which appeared in [20] and was called Yang’s H-function and Yang’s identities in [21] by Tian et. al. For -∞≤a<b≤∞, let f and g be differentiable on (a, b) and g′≠0 on (a, b). Then the function Hf,g is defined by

Hf,g:=f′g′g-f. 2.14

Using Yang’s H-function, we have the following simple but useful identities:

fg′=g′g2f′g′g-f=g′g2Hf,g, 2.15
Hf,g′:=f′g′′g, 2.16

where f and g are twice differentiable on the interval a,b with gg′≠0 on a,b.

The second tool is the monotonicity rules for the ratio of two power series proven first by Biernacki and Krzyz [22], which play an important role in dealing with the monotonicity of the ratio of power series. A special monotonicity rule of two power series can be seen [23].

Lemma 1

([22]) Let an and bn (n=0,1,2,…) be real numbers and let the power series At=∑n=1∞antn and Bt=∑n=1∞bntn be convergent for |t|<r. If bn>0 for n=0,1,2,…, and an/bn is strictly increasing (or decreasing) for n=0,1,2,…, then the function t↦At/Bt is strictly increasing (or decreasing) on 0,r.

The third tool is the piecewise monotonicity rule presented by Yang, Chu and Wang in [24, Theorem 1], which is a nice complement to Biernacki and Krzyz’s monotonicity rule. This new rule has been applied in the study for special functions, see for example, means and trigonometric [25–27], Bessel functions [28, 29], hypergeometric functions [30–35]. As a corollary of [24, Theorem 1], the following piecewise monotonicity rule on 0,∞, Lemma 2, first appeared in [36, Lemma 6.4] without proof and was also strictly proven in [37]. The present version of Lemma 2 was from [38] which modified something that is not rigorous in the assumptions for the sequences {ak/bk}0≤k≤m and {ak/bk}k≥m in the original version [24, Corollary 2.6] and modified version [19, Lemma 2.3].

Lemma 2

([24, Corollary 2.6], [38]) Let At=∑k=0∞aktk and Bt=∑k=0∞bktk be two real power series converging on R with bk>0 for all k. If for certain m∈N, the non-constant sequences {ak/bk}0≤k≤m and {ak/bk}k≥m are respectively increasing (decreasing) and decreasing (increasing), then there is a unique t0∈0,∞ such that the function A/B is increasing (decreasing) on 0,t0 and decreasing (increasing) on t0,∞.

The convexity of Wνx

We are now in a position to state and prove the convexity of the ratio Wν. First, we prove the following theorem, which describes fully the convex pattern of the ratio Wνx and gives an answer to Baricz’s conjecture stated in Introduction.

Theorem 3

Let ν>-2. (i) The ratio Wνx is strictly convex on 0,∞ if and only if ν≥-1/2 ; (ii) if -3/2≤ν<-1/2, then Wνx is convex then concave on 0,∞; (iii) while -2<ν<-3/2, Wνx is first convex then concave and finally convex on 0,∞.

Consequences

In this section, we give some direct consequences of the convexity of the ratios Wνx and Wνx1/θ for θ≥2, including some monotonicity and new functional inequalities for Wνx.

Three monotonicity results involving Wνx

First, we consider the difference Wνx-x, which appeared in the study of generalized Marcum Q-function, see [40, Lemma 1]. An answer to Gaunt’s open problem

The variance-gamma (VG) distribution introduced into the financial literature in [56], also known as the generalized Laplace or Bessel function distribution, is a continuous statistical distribution defined and supported on the set of real numbers. For variance-gamma approximation via Stein’s method [57] (see also [58]), it is crucial to obtain the uniform bounds for four expressions of the type listed in [59, Eq. (1.1), (1.2)]. Gaunt has obtained uniform bounds for them, where the bound for the third follows from [59, Eq. (2.30)], which in turn depends on the upper bound for the integral

∫0xeβttνIνtdtforν>-12,β∈-1,0.

For this reason, he proved Theorem 2.7 in [59] by using the inequality

Iν+1(x)<1-aνIν(x)+aνIν+2(x) 5.1

for x>0, where aν is the largest number in the interval [0, 1]. Gaunt claimed that “the inequality has not been studied in the literature” and left for readers as an open problem.

We now give an answer to the above open problem using Corollary 1.

Proposition 1

Let ν≥-1/2 and 0<aν<1. The inequality (5.1) holds for x>0 if and only if 0<aν≤ν+1/2/2ν+2.

Proof

The inequality (5.1) is equivalent to

aν<Iνx-Iν+1xIνx-Iν+2x:=qνx

for x>0. Using the recurrence formula (1.5) gives

qνx=Iνx-Iν+1x2ν+1x-1Iν+1x=Wνx-x2ν+1.

0<aν≤limx→∞qνx=ν+1/22ν+1.

An answer to Baricz and Neuman’s conjecture

Nȧsell [42] proved that the function x↦x-νe-xIνx is completely monotonic on 0,∞ for ν≥-1/2. The function x↦Iνx=2νΓν+1x-νIνx is strictly log-convex on R for all ν>-1 while the function x↦Iνx is strictly log-concave on 0,∞ for ν>0.

We now give an answer to this conjecture.

Let ν>-1. The following statements are true.

    When ν>0, the function x↦Iνx is log-concave then log-convex on 0,∞.

Let

hp,νx=xWν′x-Wνx+p.

h2ν,ν-10=0andh2ν,ν-1∞=limx→∞h2ν,ν-1x=ν+12;hν,ν-10=-νandhν,ν-1∞=limx→∞hν,ν-1x=12.

Conclusions

In this paper, using the monotonicity rules for a ratio of power series and an elementary auxiliary function we proved several convexity results:

  • For ν>-2, the functions x↦Wνx and x↦Wνx-ν+1/2/x are both strictly convex on 0,∞ if and only if ν≥-1/2.

  • For θ≥2 and ν>-2, the functions x↦Wνx1/θ and x↦Wνx-x2/2ν+4 are both strictly concave on 0,∞, while x↦Wνx-2ν+1/x is strictly concave on 0,∞ if and only if ν≥-3/2.

As direct consequences of the above main results, we describe the monotonic pattern of the difference Wνx-x on 0,∞ for ν in the intervals [-1/2,∞), [-3/2,-1/2) and -2,-3/2, and establish several new inequalities for the ratio Wνx. Finally, as applications, we give answers to an open problem and a conjecture.

Finally, let us recall that an infinitely differentiable function f:I→[0,∞) is called a Bernstein function on an interval I if f′ is completely monotonic on I (see [61, Definition 3.1]). Now, inspired the facts that Wνx,Wν′x>0 and Wνx1/θ″<0 for x>0 and ν>-1 with θ≥2, we propose a conjecture as follows.

Conjecture 3

Let τ∈(0,1/2] and ν>-1. The function x↦Wνxτ is a Bernstein function on 0,∞.
