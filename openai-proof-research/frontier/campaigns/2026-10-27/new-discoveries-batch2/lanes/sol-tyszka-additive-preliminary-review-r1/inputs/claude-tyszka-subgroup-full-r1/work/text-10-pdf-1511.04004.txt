     A bit stronger version of the Conjecture appeared in [5]. The Conjecture implies that there
is an algorithm which takes as input a Diophantine equation, returns an integer, and this integer
is greater than the heights of integer (non-negative integer, positive integer, rational) solutions,
if the solution set is finite ([5]).
    Let us pose the following two questions:

Question 1. Is there an algorithm which takes as input a Diophantine equation, returns an
integer, and this integer is greater than the heights of integer solutions, if the solution set is
finite?

Question 2. Is there an algorithm which takes as input a Diophantine equation, returns an
integer, and this integer is greater than the number of integer solutions, if the solution set is
finite?

    Obviously, a positive answer to Question 1 implies a positive answer to Question 2.

Lemma 2. Let d denote the maximal height of an integer solution of a Diophantine equation
D(x1 , . . . , xn ) = 0 whose solution set in integers is non-empty and finite. We claim that the
number of integer solutions to the equation
                                                                                                     2
        D2 (x1 , . . . , xn ) + n2 + x12 + . . . + xn2 − u21 − u22 − u23 − u24 − v21 − v22 − v23 − v24 = 0

is finite and greater than d.

Proof. There exists an integer tuple (a1 , . . . , an ) such that D(a1 , . . . , an ) = 0 and
max (n, |a1 |, . . . , |an |) = d. The equation n2 + a21 + . . . + a2n = x + y has n2 + a21 + . . . + a2n + 1
solutions in non-negative integers x and y. Since n2 + a21 + . . . + a2n + 1 ⩾ d2 + 1 > d, the claim
follows from Lagrange’s four-square theorem.                                                               

Theorem 5. A positive answer to Question 2 implies a positive answer to Question 1.

Proof. In order to compute an upper bound on the heights of integer solutions to a Diophan-
tine equation D(x1 , . . . , xn ) = 0 with a finite number of integer solutions, we compute an upper
bound on the number of integer solutions to the equation
                                                                                                     2
        D2 (x1 , . . . , xn ) + n2 + x12 + . . . + xn2 − u21 − u22 − u23 − u24 − v21 − v22 − v23 − v24 = 0

By Lemma 2, this number is greater than the heights of integer solutions to D(x1 , . . . , xn ) = 0.
