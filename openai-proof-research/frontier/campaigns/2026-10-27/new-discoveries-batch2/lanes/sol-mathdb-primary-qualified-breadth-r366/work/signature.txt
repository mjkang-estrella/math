                                                                                 1. Introduction
                                             Let p be an odd positive integer and Q(ζ) be the cyclotomic field generated by a
                                           primitive root of unity ζ of order 2p. The SU2 -TQFT constructs for any surface Sg of
                                           genus g a finite dimensional Q(ζ)-vector space Vp (Sg ) endowed with an Hermitian form
                                           hg defined over Q(ζ). These vector spaces are part of a modular functor, meaning that
                                           they support a projective unitary action of the corresponding mapping class groups and
                                           satisfy a list of compatibilities when cutting the surfaces along simple closed curves.
                                             The dimension of Vp (Sg ), given by the celebrated Verlinde formula, has received
                                           considerable attention since the nineties. On the other hand, taking 0 < q < p two
                                           coprime odd integers, one can embed Q(ζ) into C by sending ζ to eiπq/p . The Hermitian

            2. Signatures in TQFT and signed Verlinde algebras
    In this section, we recall the general formula for the signature which is used in the
proof of Formula (1) for σ2 ( pq ) (proved in Lemma 2.3 below). The reader willing to
take Formula (1) for granted may skip this section at first reading.

ζ = eiπq/p . We define a semi-simple, commutative Frobenius algebra over the rationals
V pq in the following way.

strongly depends on the choice of q. Notice that as a vector space over K, Vp (Sg,n , λ)

Remark 2.2. Just like V pq , the K-vector space Vp (S1 ) has dimension p − 1 and is in
a natural way a Frobenius algebra known as the Verlinde algebra of the TQFT. This
algebra only depends on p, not on q. The Frobenius algebra V pq (which does depend on
q) may be thought of as a signed Verlinde algebra, because the structure constants of
the multiplication in V pq coincide up to sign with those of the multiplication in Vp (S1 )

in the standard bases. For q = 1, Vp (S1 ) is isomorphic to V 1 ⊗ K as a Frobenius

algebra, hence σg,n ( p1 ; λ) = dim Vp (Sg,n , λ) for all g, n ≥ 0.

   In genus zero Vp (S0 ) is the ground field, with the canonical Hermitian form, and
in genus 1 the Hermitian form on Vp (S1 ) is positive definite for any choice of q. So
σ0 ( pq ) = 1 and σ1 ( pq ) = p − 1 don’t depend on q. Thus, for surfaces without marked
points interesting things happen only for g ≥ 2.

Remark 2.4. By definition, the signature depends on ζ = eiπq/p , that is, on the choice
of q modulo 2p, not just modulo p. But q and 2p − q give the same signature, because
the formulas only depend on the signs of the quantum integers [j], and these signs are
preserved by the transformation q 7→ 2p − q or equivalently ζ 7→ ζ −1 . Thus we may
assume 0 < q < p.
Remark 2.5. The SU2 -TQFT Vp corresponds in Conformal Field Theory to the Wess-

that is, when p is even. Their signature functions can again be computed using the
Frobenius algebra formalism of Theorem 2.1. But we won’t consider even level SU2 -
TQFTs in this paper.

# The following code computes the trace of the multiplication by P(x) on the ring QQ[x]/Q(x).
def Trace(P,Q):
    n=Q.degree()
    s=0
    for k in range(n):
        U=(x^k*P).quo_rem(Q)[1]+x^(n+1)
        s=s+(U.list())[k]
    return(s)
# This code computes the signature function
def S(p,q,g):
    M=Matrix(p-1,p-1)
    for i in range(p-2):
        M[i+1,i]=1
        M[i,i+1]=(-1)^(1+int((i+1)*q/p)+int((i+2)*q/p))
    P=M.charpoly()
    Pprime=diff(P,x)
    iota=M[0:p-2,0:p-2].charpoly()
    Om=((-iota*Pprime)^(g-1)).quo_rem(P)[1]
    return(Trace(Om,P))
