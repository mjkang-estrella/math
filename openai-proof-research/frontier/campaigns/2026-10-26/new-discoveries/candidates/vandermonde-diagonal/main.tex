\begin{abstract}
For $b>1$, the inverse of $(b^{ij})_{0\le i,j<n}$ has its largest absolute entry on the diagonal, at every positive order. Together with Sanna--Shallit--Zhang's proved index localization, this resolves their eventual diagonal-maximality conjecture. The argument uses positive definiteness. Prior-art review remains provisional; the Lean development verifies the complete theorem, including the source's index localization.
\end{abstract}

Let $b>1$ and $n\ge1$, and write
\[
 V_n=(b^{ij})_{0\le i,j<n},\qquad C_n=V_n^{-1},\qquad
 M_b(n)=\max_{0\le i,j<n}|(C_n)_{ij}|.
\]
These conventions agree with $V(a)_{ij}=a_i^j$ and the geometric nodes $a_i=b^i$ in \cite{SSZ}. In particular $V_n$ is symmetric. Set
\[
 n_0=\left\lceil\log_b(1+b^{-1})\right\rceil.
\]
Conjecture~11 of the first arXiv version of \cite{SSZ}, retained as Conjecture~12 of the accepted manuscript, asks whether $M_b(n)$ is a diagonal entry with index at most $n_0$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

\begin{theorem}
For every $b>1$ and integer $n\ge1$, there exists an integer $i$ with $0\le i<n$ and $i\le n_0$ such that
\[
 M_b(n)=(C_n)_{ii}>0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every leading principal submatrix of $V_n$ is another geometric Vandermonde matrix. Its determinant is
\[
 \det V_r=\prod_{0\le i<j<r}(b^j-b^i)>0\qquad(1\le r\le n).
\]
By Sylvester's criterion, $V_n$ is positive definite. So is $C_n$. Cauchy--Schwarz for the inner product $x^\top C_n y$ gives
\[
 |(C_n)_{jk}|^2\le (C_n)_{jj}(C_n)_{kk}.
\]
Choose $i$ with maximal diagonal entry. The diagonal is positive, and the displayed inequality implies $|(C_n)_{jk}|\le(C_n)_{ii}$ for every $j,k$. Thus a diagonal entry is maximal at every order.

If $n\le n_0+1$, the desired index condition is automatic. Otherwise, the proved Theorem~1 of \cite{SSZ} gives
\[
 |(C_n)_{jj}|\le |(C_n)_{n_0n_0}|\qquad(j\ge n_0).
\]
Consequently one can choose a maximal diagonal entry among indices $0,\ldots,n_0$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the localization used above follows directly from the source's Lagrange formulas. Put
\[
 \pi_j=\prod_{\substack{0\le h<n\\h\ne j}}|b^j-b^h|,
 \quad
 \sigma_{r,j}=\sum_{\substack{S\subset\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus\{j\}\\|S|=r}}
 b^{\sum_{h\in S}h}.
\]
Lagrange interpolation gives $|(C_n)_{ij}|=\sigma_{n-1-i,j}/\pi_j$.
Replacing the excluded node $j$ by $j+1$ replaces the available node $b^{j+1}$ by $b^j$, hence $\sigma_{r,j+1}\le\sigma_{r,j}$. Cancellation of the denominator products yields
\[
 \frac{\pi_{j+1}}{\pi_j}
 =\frac{b^{n+j-1}-b^{n-2}}{b^{n-1}-b^j}.
\]
For $j\ge n_0$, we have $b^j\ge1+1/b$, whereas
\[
 \frac{b^{n-1}+b^{n-2}}{b^{n-1}+1}<1+\frac1b.
\]
The ratio of consecutive denominators is therefore at least one. Thus $|(C_n)_{i,j+1}|\le|(C_n)_{ij}|$ for all $i$ and $j\ge n_0$. Applying this monotonicity in both coordinates, using symmetry, gives the asserted localization.

\paragraph{Formal verification.}
The accompanying Lean file uses the exact matrix $V_n$ and proves that it is invertible and positive definite, then proves existence of a diagonal absolute maximum for every $n>0$. Its positivity proof expands
\[
 b^{ij}=\exp((\log b)ij)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\log b)^k}{k!}i^k j^k
\]
as a limit of nonnegative sums of rank-one positive semidefinite matrices. Vandermonde invertibility upgrades this to positive definiteness. The Lean development also proves the Lagrange inverse coefficient formula, numerator monotonicity, exact denominator recurrences and the cutoff localization. Its main theorem states the complete result, including $i\le n_0$, for every positive order.

\paragraph{Prior work and novelty qualification.}
The matrix, conjecture and localization are due to Sanna--Shallit--Zhang. Positive-definite matrix theory is classical. Dopico--Koev already explicitly established positive definiteness of this exact geometric Vandermonde family in 2006~\cite{DK}. The all-order diagonal maximum follows immediately by classical positive-definite matrix theory; combined with the source localization it gives the stated result. The connection to the later conjecture is useful, but this packet receives zero credit toward the strict new-discovery campaign. The accepted journal manuscript still states that conjecture. A documented search has not located a resolution, but independent prior-art review remains required and no assertion of absolute priority is made.
