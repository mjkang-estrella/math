                                           Abstract
                                           In this work we investigate and characterize linear functionals L :
                                           R[x1 , . . . , xn ] → R with absolutely continuous representing measures µ, i.e.,
                                           dµ(x) = g(x) dx for some density g. We focus on the regularity of g and how it
                                           is determined and influenced by the moments sα = L(xα ).
                                           Keywords: moment sequence, moment, representing measure, absolutely
                                           continuous, signed

    In applications moments and moment problems appear frequently, e.g. in fi-
nancial mathematics [Ana06, Sto16], in (algebraic) statistics [Pea94, TSM85,
MMR05, ARS18, dD19], in optimization [Las15], in shape reconstruction
[Bal61, MN68, MR80, LR82, MVKW95, GMV99, BGL07, SMD+ 07, GLPR12,
GNPR14, GPSS18], and in pattern recognition [Hu62, DBN92, Che93, SMD+ 07,
APST19]. Especially for shape reconstruction and pattern recognition the con-
cept of derivatives of moments and moment functionals was developed [dD23],
see also [LPHT08, HK14, BJL19, MWHL20]. In these applications moments of
absolutely continuous measures are the main focus, i.e., measures µ of the form
dµ(x) = g(x) dx for some density function g. In [Amb14] L1 -densities were
treated. In the current work we extend this work and look especially at the
regularity of the density g. We are here also facing the problem of signed repre-
senting measures, see e.g. [Bor95, Pól38, Boa39, Blo53, She64, Hor77, BCJ79,
Kow84, Dur89, Hoi92, Sch24, dD24].
    The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we treat the setup of [Amb14]
in full generality, i.e., truncated moment functionals and sequences (I is finite
resp. finite dimensional vector spaces V of measurable functions) on general
measurable spaces (X , A). In Theorem 2.5 we give the main theorem which
is based on/appeared in [dD19]. Its proof is significantly easier than the more
special version in [Amb14] since we use here the concept of Dirac approximating

2.2. Density ρ can be chosen to be C ∞ and even Cc∞
    From Example 2.2 we see that in fact in Corollary 2.6 we can choose ρ to be
a Schwartz function. We can even choose ρ to have compact support when the
Dirac-approximating family F does not come from the Gaussian distribution but
from Cc∞ -functions, e.g., a molifier. We can also generate F from characteristic
functions χBε (x)∩X .
Corollary 2.7. If in Theorem 2.5 we additionally have that the Dirac-
approximating family F is absolutely continuous (with L1 -, C ∞ -, or Cc∞ -
densities) with respect to a measure µ on X then also L has an absolutely con-
tinuous representing measure (with a L1 -, C ∞ -, or Cc∞ -density, respectively)
with respect to µ on X .
    Dirac-approximation families with C ∞ - or even with Cc∞ -densities always
exists for the cases covered by Corollary 2.6. Take the molifier Sε as the densities
with suitable normalization constants.

2.3. Extension of the function space V
    We have that 0 ∈ I, i.e., 1 ∈ V, is not required in Theorem 2.5 since it
is not required in Richter’s Theorem 2.4. Additionally, when V are “only”
continuous functions instead of polynomials then Example 2.2 always gives a
Dirac-approximating family. If we have a function f ∈ V which is discontin-
uous then Example 2.3 provides an example where no absolutely continuous
representing measure exists, at least not with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

2.4. Density ρ can be chosen with respect to more than the Lebesgue measure
    While in Example 2.3 we can not get an absolutely representing measure with
respect to the Lebesgue measure λ, we get absolutely representing measures with
respect to the measure µ = λ + δ1 . To see this we have to construct a Dirac-
approximating family F which is absolutely continuous with respect to µ. If
x ∈ [0, 2] \ {1} we take for every ε > 0 a function fε,x ∈ Cc∞ ([0, 2], R) such that

2.5. Extension of X
    That X is closed in Corollary 2.6 is not necessary. Additionally, general X
can be used, especially when the Lebesgue measure λ on Rn is replaced by other
measures on X . For example let X = X1 ∪ X2 ⊆ R2 with X1 = [−1, 0] × {0}
and X2 = [0, 1) × [−1, 1]. Take on X1 the 1-dimensional Lebesgue measure
λ1 , i.e., λ1 ([a, b] × {0}) = b − a for all −1 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ 0, and take on X2 the
Lebesgue measure λ2 on R2 . Then λ = λ1 + λ2 is well-defined on X = X1 ∪ X2
since X1 ∩ X2 = {(0, 0)} and λ1 ({(0, 0)}) = λ2 ({(0, 0)}) = 0. An absolutely
continuous family F on X with respect to λ can then be constructed similarly
to the previous paragraph by using appropriate densities in C ∞ (X , R).
    We can also enforce that λ has some mass on subsets of X , e.g., by writing
X = [0, 1] × [−1, 1] = X1 ∪ X2 with X1 = {0} × [−1, 1] and X2 = (0, 1] × [−1, 1].
Then on X1 we can take the 1-dimensional Lebesgue measure and on X2 the
2-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Single points {x} must be treated with the
Dirac measure δx in a similar way as the absolutely continuous measure was
constructed in the previous paragraph with respect to µ = λ + δ1 . S
    This procedure can be extended straight forward to general X = i∈I Xi , I
some index set, which is composed of e.g. open manifolds Xi in Rn or abstract
measurable sets Xi . Important is that V are measurable functions on X and
on each Xi we take a measure λi such that we find     Pan absolutely continuous
Dirac-approximation family F with respect to λ = i∈I λi . Theorem 2.5 then
provides an absolutely continuous representing measure for all moment func-
tional in the interior of the moment cone. All that is already summarized in
Corollary 2.7.

2.6. Absolutely continuous representing measures for boundary moment func-
      tionals
    If L lies on the boundary of the moment cone then by [dDS18b, Prop. 7] we
can restrict X to some Y ( X such that V|Y spans the unique face of the moment
cone which contains L in its relative interior. Hence, L is in the (relative) interior
of a (smaller) moment cone spanned by V|Y where again Theorem 2.5 applies.
Corollary 2.8. Let n ∈ N, let (X , A) be a measurable space, and let V be a n-
dimensional real vector space of measurable functions f : X → R. If L : V → R
is a moment functional on the boundary of the moment cone then there exists a
Y ( X such that L lies in the relative interior of the moment cone generated by
V|Y with dimension dim V|Y < n. If additionally there exists a measure µ on Y
and an absolutely continuous Dirac-approximating family F for V|Y (with L1 -,
C ∞ -, or Cc∞ -densities) with respect to µ then L has an absolutely continuous
representing measure (with L1 -, C ∞ - or Cc∞ -density) with respect to µ on Y.

5. Summary

    In this work we dealt with three cases of problems finding and characteriz-
ing absolutely continuous representing measures for (signed) moment sequences
resp. functionals:
(a) truncated moment functionals (Section 2),
(b) full (signed) moment functionals on [0, 1]n (Section 3), and
(c) full (signed) moment functionals on Rn (Section 4).

Each of these problems is attacked by its own method:
(a’) Dirac approximating sequences (Definition 2.1),

(b’) the Signed Hausdorff’s Theorem 3.1, and
(c’) characteristic functions and Fourier transform [Sat99, Prop. 2.5 (xii)].

    In Section 2 we generalize the work [Amb14]. We use the concept of a Dirac-
approximating family to show that [Amb14] can easily be extended, i.e., that
as long there exists a Dirac-approximating family every moment sequence resp.
moment functional in the moment cone is representable by an absolutely con-
tinuous representing measure. In Example 2.3 we give an example of an interior
moment sequence resp. moment functional that can not be represented by an
absolutely continuous representing measure since in this example there exists
no Dirac-approximating family. We continue with discussions and corollaries to
show that the density functions can be chosen to be L1 -, C ∞ -, or even Cc∞ -
functions. We also show that representing measures absolutely continuous with
respect to more than the Lebesgue measure are also implied by Theorem 2.5.
Theorem 2.5 also includes the cases of more general measurable sets X . Also
boundary moment sequence can be represented by absolutely continuous repre-
senting measures. The proof of Theorem 2.5 is constructive, i.e., it provides a
way to gain absolutely representing measures.
    In Section 3 we considered sequences s resp. linear functionals Ls :
R[x1 , . . . , xn ] → R which can be represented by signed absolutely continuous
measures supported on [0, 1]n . We give full characterizations of such sequences
especially when additional regularity on the density is imposed. The main
technique is the Signed Hausdorff’s Theorem 3.1 and derivatives of (moment)
sequences [dD23].
    Finally, in Section 4 we consider full moment functionals on Rn . Because of
the great flexibility on Rn , especially with signed measures, we have to make
the restriction that the (signed) moment sequence s = (sα )α∈Nn0 is such that

measure µ. The function f is also known as moment generating function. As in
signal processing the image (here the measure) is recovered by the inverse Fourier
transform of f . Since the regularity of the density of an absolutely continuous
measure can be controlled on the Fourier side we find full characterizations of
such densities.
    It shall be noted that while the (inverse) Fourier approach is well-known and
widely applied we did not find references from the moment theoretic side dealing
with this tool of the moment generating function resp. characteristic function f .
In the standard literature [Ach56, AK62, Akh65, KN77, Mar08, Lau09] about
moments we did not find explicit treatments. Even in the seminal work [Sch17]
the moment generating function appears only in exercise 8 on page 91 but no
deeper treatment is provided. It is therefore interesting which properties and
characterizations of moment sequences, moment functionals, and their repre-
senting measures lie ahead, especially when the full power of Fourier analysis is
employed, see e.g. [Duo01, SS03, Gra10, Gra09, BCD11] and Remark 4.11.
    Already characterizing moment sequences, moment functionals, and their
representing measures is (NP) hard. A reconstruction or at least approximation
of representing measures goes even a step further. It is therefore surprising
that for the cases considered in Section 4 the simple and explicit formulas (8),
(10), and (11) hold. They are based on the inverse Fourier transform of the
characteristic function µ̂ = f and provide the representing measures µ, see
Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.8, and Remark 4.11.
