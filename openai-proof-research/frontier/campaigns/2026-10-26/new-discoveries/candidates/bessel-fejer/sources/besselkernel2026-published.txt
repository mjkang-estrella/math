1. Introduction

   The Riemann–Lebesgue Lemma is a classic result in Fourier analysis, which tells us that the Fourier
coeﬃcients of an integrable function, f , decay to 0 at high frequencies, i.e. fˆ︁(n) → 0 as |n| → ∞.
   By increasing the regularity of f , say f ∈ C k , we can obtain faster rates of decay of the coeﬃcients,
viz. |n| fˆ︁(n) → 0. But if we merely assume that f ∈ L1 , then the Riemann–Lebesgue Lemma is the best
        k

possible result we can expect. Indeed, we can find integrable functions whose Fourier coeﬃcients decay as
slowly as we like, as the following theorem shows—see, e.g., [2, Theorem 3.3.4].

Theorem 1.1. Let an ⩾ 0 be a sequence such that an → 0 as |n| → ∞. Then, there is a function f ∈ L1 ([0, 1])
whose Fourier coeﬃcients satisfy fˆ︁(n) ⩾ an for all n ∈ Z.
