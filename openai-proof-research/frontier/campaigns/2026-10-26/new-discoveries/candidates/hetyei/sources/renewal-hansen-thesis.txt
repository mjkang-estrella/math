1.1 Introduetion and summary

      The field of infinitely divisible random variables has grown during the last few
decades to take a permanent position in the theory of probability. Almost all standard
text books on probability now include atleast one chapter devoted to the field of
infinite divisibility. This is mainly due to its importance in solving the general central
limit problem and to its applications to stochastic processes with stationary
independent increments.

      Though practical applications do exist (cf. for example Ahmad and Abouammoh
(1977), Thorin (1977), Carasso (1987), Keilson and Servi (1987) and Hansen and
Willekens (1988)), infinite divisibility does remain a theoretica! concept. In this thesis
the emphasis is therefore on the theory. Examples and practical applications are, as a
rule, not sought.

      Our starting point is the Lévy canonkal representation of infinitely divisible
distributions (cf. Theorem 1.3.2 of this thesis), where the characteristic function of an
infinitely divisible distribution Fis related with a function M, called the Lévy speetral
function. We are interested in characterizing the distributions F which have a Lévy
speetral function M satisfying some monotonicity requirement.

      In Chapter 2 we give a review of known monotonicity results in infinite
divisibility and we present a curious conneetion with analogous results in renewal
theory. In Chapters 3 and 4 we consicter non-negative infinitely divisible random
variables whose Lévy speetral functions are either absolutely continuous or supported
by the non-negative integers. Chapter 3, which is based on the artiele On Moment
Sequences and lnfznitely Divisible Sequences, Hansen and Steutel (1988), studies
these Lévy speetral functions in the context of moment sequences and moment

functions. The main results of On Logconcave and Logconvex lnfinitely Divisible
Sequences and Densities , Hansen ( 1988), are given in Chapter 4, where log-concave
and log-convex Lévy speetral functions are considered. The set of infinitely divisible
distributions with a.-unimodal Lévy speetral functions is characterized in Chapter S.
Chapter 6 deviates from the theme of this thesis, as it studies infinitely divisible

1.5 Motivation and methodology

      In this thesis we study the relationship between an infinitely divisible distribution
and its Lévy speetral function (or canonical measure}. We prove that an infinitely
divisible distribution whose Lévy speetral function (or canonical measure) possesses
some well-known monotonicity property, for example complete monotonicity, log-
concavity, log-convexity or a-unimodality, belongs to a well-known set of
distributions, for example the set of mixtures of geometrie distributions, strongly
unimodal distributions or a-self-decomposable distributions.

       It turns out that in studying monotonicity properties in this context, it is easiest to
first consider the discrete case (cf. Theorem 1.3.4). Here the canonkal measure can be
explicitly expressed in tenns of the probabilities, which makes developing and
proving hypotheses and constructing counterexamples easier than in the case of
distributions on lR or IR;.. In many cases we can even prove the equivalent result for
distributions on IR;. from those on INo by applying a simple limiting argument (cf.
Sections 3.5, 4.3 and 5.5). Also, by studying the discrete case wedevelop insight
which can be helpful in proving the result for lR (cf. Section 5.4).

     As is seen in Chapter 2, many of the monotonicity results obtained in renewal
theory and infinite divisibility are quite similar. This observation led us to consider
log-convexity and moment sequences in the context of infinite divisibility (cf.
Theorems 2.4.2 and 2.4.3 of the next chapter).

4.1 Introduetion

      Log-concavity and log-convexity of functions and sequences in probability has
been of interest to several authors, e.g. Karlin (1968). Ibragimov (1956) calls a
distribution strongly unimodal if its convolution with any unimodal distribution is
unimodal. He proves that the set of strongly unimodal probability densities is equal to
the set of log-concave densities. An equivalent result for log-concave discrete
probability distributions has been proved by Keilson and Gerber (1971). Much work
has been done on the unimodality of infinitely divisible distributions (cf. Wolfe
(1971), Yamazato (1978) and Sato and Yamazato (1978)), but little on strong
unimodality. The study of log-concave functions and sequences is thus a relatively
unknown field in probability, with important applications in the fields of statistics and
optimizàtion. Log-convexity is of interest in the study of reliability and of infinitely
divisible random variables. Steutel (1970) proves that all log-convex discrete
probability distributions are infinitely divisible (Theorem 2.4.1 (ii)). The absolutely
continuous analogue is also proved in Steutel (1970).
     In this chapter we consicter distributions of non-negative infinitely divisible
random variables whose canonical measures are either absolutely continuous or
supported by the integers. We prove that for such distributions to be log-concave
(log-convex), it is necessary that their canonical measures be log-concave (log-
convex). Our results in the discrete case contain an analogue of Yamazato's (1982)
concavity result (it also provides an alternative proof of this result), and an analogue
to the convexity result for renewal sequences in de Bruijn and Erdös (1953) (cf.
Theorem 2.3.2 (i)).

6.1 Introduetion

      Limit distributions of sums of independent random variables has been a central
topic in probability theory and staristics for many years. The classical central limit
problem and its successive generalizations to stabie and self-decomposable random
variables are well-known examples. Also, the set of infinitely divisible random
variables can be described as the solution of the so-called general central limit
problem (cf. Loève (1977), compare Theorem 6.2.4). Recently, Jurek (1981)
introduced the set of s-self-decomposable random variables, which are defined as
limits of sums of 'shrunken' random variables. Jurek (1985) showed that this set
coincides with the set of random variables having characteristic functions in S 1(IR)
(see Notation 5.4.9). The set of 1-self-decomposable random variables satisfying (i) of
Theorem 5.4.8 is included in the set of infinitely divisible random variables and in
turn includes the sets of self-decomposable and stabie random variables.
     In contrast to the previous chapters we only study random variables on R in this
chapter. We consider random variables with chamcteristic functions in Sa(R) as
limirs of sums of independent normed or 'shrunken' random variables. In the
following section we give some preliminaries. Using the representations of S a,(R)
obrained in the previous chapter, we present in Section 6.3 four limit forms of a-self-
decomposable random variables. One of these limit forms is the one used by Jurek
(1988) to define Sa (IR). The method used is easily adapted to Sa(lNo). InSection 6.4
we introduce rwo shrinking opemtors. Both operators are closely conneered with a-

and the second operator, a stochastic shrinking operator, is a generalization of the

Samenvatting

      De bestudering van de oneindig deelbare stochastische grootheden heeft zich in
korte tijd een vaste plaats verwoxven binnen de kansrekening. In vrijwel ieder
inleidend boek over de kansrekening staat is tegenwoordig tenminste één hoofdstuk
gewijd aan dit onderwerp. Voor een belangrijk deel is dit gekomen door het gebruik
van oneindig deelbaarheid in het oplossen van problemen rondom de centrale
limietstelling en door toepassingen op stochastische processen met stationaire
onafhankelijke aangroeiingen.

     Hoewel er verscheidene praktische toepassingen bestaan (zie bijv. Ahmad and
Abouammoh (1977), Thorin (1977), Carasso (1987), Keilson en Sexvi (1987) en
Hansen en Willekens (1988)), is oneindig deelbaarheid toch voornamelijk een
theoretisch concept. Vandaar dat in dit proefschrift de nadruk zal liggen op het
theoretische gedeelte. Aan voorbeelden en praktische toepassingen zal slechts weinig
aandacht worden besteed.
      Het uitgangspunt voor deze studie is de Lévy canonieke representatie van
 oneindig deelbare verdelingen (zie Stelling 1.3.2 van het proefschrift), waarin de
relatie wordt aangegeven tussen de karakteristieke functie van een oneindig deelbare
 verdelingFen een functie M, die de Lévy spectraalfunctie wordt genoemd. We zijn
 geïnteresseerd in de karakterisering van die verdelingen F, waarvan de Lévy
'spectraalfunctie M aan bepaald eisen voldoet die betrekking hebben op de monotonie.
      In Hoofdstuk 2 wordt een overzicht gegeven van de bekende monotonie
resultaten in de theorie van de oneindig deelbaarheid en er wordt een vergelijking
gemaakt met analoge resultaten in de vernieuwingstheorie. In de Hoofdstukken 3 en 4
worden niet-negatieve oneindig deelbare stochastische grootheden beschouwd,
waarvan de Lévy spectraalfuncties ofwel absoluut continu zijn, ofwel geconcentreerd
op de niet-negatieve gehele getallen. Hoofdstuk 3 is gebaseerd op het artikel On
Moment Sequences and lnfinitely Divisible Sequences, Hansen en Steutel (1988),
waarin deze Lévy spectraalfuncties bestudeerd worden tegen de achtergrond van
momentenrijen en momentenfuncties. De belanrijkste resultaten van On Logconcave
and Logconvex Infinitely Divisible Sequences and Densities, Hansen (1988), worden
gegeven in Hoofdstuk 4, waarin speciale aandacht wordt besteed aan log-concave en
log-convexe Lévy spectraalfuncties. De verzameling van oneindig deelbare
verdelingen met a-unimodale Lévy spectraalfuncties wordt gekarakteriseerd in
Hoofdstuk 5. In Hoofdstuk 6 wordt een uitstapje gemaakt. Hierin worden oneindig
deelbare stochastische grootheden met a-unimodale Lévy spectraalfuncties
beschouwd als limieten van de som van driehoeksmatrices van stochastische
variabelen en als limieten van gereduceerde stochastische variabelen.
