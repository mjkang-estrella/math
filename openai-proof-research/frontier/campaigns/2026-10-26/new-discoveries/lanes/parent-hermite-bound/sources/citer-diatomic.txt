Abstract
In this paper, the bound state solution of the non-relativistic wave equation with a molecular potential function has
been obtained in a closed-form using the Nikiforov-Uvarov method. The solutions of the bound state are then applied
to study the information-theoretic measures such as the one-dimensional Shannon and Renyi entropic densities. The
expectation values for 〈𝑟〉, 〈𝑟 2 〉, and 〈𝑝2 〉 were obtained to verify Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle. Utilizing the
energy spectrum equation, the thermodynamic vibrational partition function is obtained via the Poisson summation.
Other thermodynamic function variations with absolute temperature have been obtained numerically for four diatomic
molecules (H2, N2, O2, and HF). The Shannon global entropic sum inequality has also been verified. The Renyi sum
for constrained index parameters satisfies the global entropic inequality. The thermodynamic properties of the four
molecules are similar and conform to works reported in the existing literature. The obtained vibrational energies are
in fair agreement with the ones obtained using other forms of potential energy. The result further indicates that the
lowest bounds for the Shannon, Renyi, and Heisenberg inequalities are ground states phenomena.


1.0 Introduction
In recent times, the phase space representation of quantum mechanical systems has enjoyed renewed interest in the
measurement of global information or theoretic quantities. The Heisenberg uncertainty principle [1] forms the basic
properties of these phase-space representations in quantum mechanics. By using this principle, several researchers
have computed the theoretic quantities for many interesting properties for different potential models [2-12]. In 1925
the concept of fisher information was introduced using the Heisenberg relation [13]. The Fisher information which is
a measure of the local fluctuation of the variable proved to be a better indicative of the departure of the probability
density than the Shannon entropy [14] which measures global information. For a comprehensive analysis of the two
theoretic quantities, readers should refer to articles by Dehesa et al. [11] and Gonzalez-Ferez and Dehesa [12]. These
quantities have been applied to the complexity of the hydrogenic abstraction reaction in atomic and molecular physics
[15, 16]. In solid-state physics, the concept has been used in the calculation of statistical entropic measures in a model
of solids [17]. It has also been used in the determination of complex ecological models and ecosystem behaviour in
life sciences [18, 19]. As a result of the diverse applications of the theoretic information, it becomes necessary to
implement the theoretic information with well-known molecular potentials that the eigensolutions agree with the
Rydberg-Klein-Rees (RKR) and spectroscopic experimental data [20-30]. In a related development, the theoretic
quantities of some molecular potential have also been studied [3, 31-35]. For instance, the eigensolutions, Shannon
Entropy, and Fisher information under the Eckart-Manning-Rosen potential model have been investigated [33]. In ref.
[34], the effect of dissociation energy on the Shannon and Renyi entropies was investigated also using the generalized
Morse potential model. The eigensolutions techniques, expectation values, and Fisher information of the Wei potential
function for the non-relativistic wave equation have also been obtained [35].
On the other hand, the concept of statistical quantum mechanics over the years has assisted in describing the dynamics
of particles at the microscopic (ensemble of states) levels. For instance, the improved Manning-Rosen potential was
used to predict correctly the Gibbs free energy and enthalpy for some diatomic and tri-atomic gaseous molecules [20-
28]. The simulated results obtained in their studies are in excellent agreement with the experimental data from the
national institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) database. Also, the energy spectra and partition function have
been obtained using the improved Rosen-Morse and Tietz oscillators respectively to compute the vibrational mean
energy, vibrational specific heat, vibrational free energy, and vibrational entropy for diatomic molecular systems [29,
30, 36-41]. Recently a more realistic potential model for diatomic molecules was introduced by Yanar et al. [42]. The
factorization method was used in obtaining the eigenfunctions and rotational-vibrational energy eigenvalue that agrees
perfectly well with the RKR experimental data. Also within the framework of the non-relativistic quantum mechanics,
the asymptotic iteration method has been used to obtain the eigensolutions for the general molecular potential [36].
With appropriate an choice of potential parameters, the general molecular potential reduces to Rosen–Morse and
Trigonometric Poschl–Teller potentials which have great application for the vibrations of some polyatomic molecules
[42]. Owing to the diverse applications of molecular potentials in molecular physics, in this work, we will apply the
parametric Nikiforov Uvarov (NU) to obtain the eigensolutions. Also, the position and momentum expectations as
well as the Renyi and Shannon information entropies for the H2 , HF , N2 , and 𝑂2+ diatomic molecules will be
calculated. The energy level would be used to calculate the canonical partition function and associated thermodynamic
properties. This to the best of our knowledge is lacking in literature.

4.0 Discussion of Results
The parameter used in the computation of the Shannon entropy, Uncertainty relations, and eigenvalues are presented
in Table 1, also Eq. (6) have been used for the computation of the screening parameter  for the various states of the
diatomic molecules. Table 2 shows the comparison of the vibrational energies of HF, H2, and N2 respectively, the
results obtained using the modified potential model proposed in this study are in perfect agreement with the study of
Ghanbari and Khordad [41] using the Tietz-Hua potential model. Also, the vibrational energies obtained increases
with an increase in quantum number. As we can see from the results presented in Tables 3 and 4 for the ground and
first excited state position and momentum densities space Shannon and Renyi entropies respectively for the selected
molecules, the entropies increase with an increase in the quantum number and obeys the Renyi’s sum for constrained
index parameters and satisfies the global entropic inequality uncertainty of Eq. (31). Also, in Tables 5 and 6, the
position and momentum space expectation values increases with an increase in quantum number and obeys the
Heisenberg’s uncertainty relations for the selected molecules. The vibrational partition function as a function of
temperature has been presented for the various molecules under consideration (see Figure 1a), the partition function
asymptotically increased with an increase in temperature with an overlap between hydrogen fluoride and hydrogen
molecules. Departure from the asymptotic behaviour occurs at about 300K. The internal energy increases
monotonically with temperature with hydrogen and hydrogen fluoride having the highest
value. The asymptotic decrease of internal energy with temperature is seen with an oxygen molecule (Fig. 1b).
Figure 1c shows a monotonically decrease of the free energy with temperature with H2 and HF having the highest
value of internal energy. The rate of disorderliness of the molecules with temperature is seen in Figure 1d.
Asymptotic behaviour was observed from absolute
temperatures up to 300K for all the molecules with overlap occurring between H2 and HF respectively. The
molecules increase with an increase in the entropy. The specific heat capacity increases monotonically with an
increase in temperature for all the molecules under consideration.

5.0 Conclusion
The non-relativistic wave equation has been solved with a proposed molecular potential using the Nikiforov-Uvarov
method. The eigenvalue and eigenfunction have been obtained in a closed form and applied to obtain the global
theoretic and thermodynamic quantities for some diatomic molecules of interest. The expectation values for both the
position and momentum space obtained obeys Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle. Also, Shannon and Renyi’s sum
for constrained index parameters satisfies the global entropic inequalities and has been verified using the various
diatomic molecules under consideration. Furthermore, the explicit classical partition function has been derived and
other thermodynamic functions such as entropy (S), mean thermal energy (U), mean free energy (F), and specific heat
capacity (C) were all plotted as a function of temperature for the various molecules considered. The dependence of
these functions on temperature has been discussed in detail.
