                                              A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO MATKOWSKI’S CONJECTURE FOR
                                                      QUASI GRAPH-ADDITIVE FUNCTIONS

                                                                                        TIBOR KISS

                                                  Abstract. In this paper we investigate a conjecture of Janusz Matkowski concerning
                                                  the continuous solutions of the functional equation
                                                                                                
                                                                      f f (−x) + x = f − f (x) + f (x),     x ∈ R.




arXiv:2602.15548v1 [math.CA] 17 Feb 2026
                                                     Matkowski conjectured that all continuous solutions must necessarily be linear on both
                                                  the negative and the positive half-line. We show, however, that the family of continuous
                                                  solutions to the equation in question is far richer than anticipated: there exist continuous
                                                  solutions that admit an arbitrary part.
                                                     In addition, we provide a sufficient condition which, in the continuous setting, enforces
                                                  the conclusion predicted by Matkowski’s Conjecture.




                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                              On 18 September 2025, on the fourth day of the 21st International Conference on
                                           Functional Equations and Inequalities in Będlewo, Dorota Głazowska delivered an excel-
                                           lent and engaging plenary lecture on weakly associative functions and means. In her talk,
                                           she mentioned a result, which, in the family of translative binary operations, character-
                                           izes weak associativity in terms of a functional equation. She also presented a conjecture
                                           concerning the continuous solutions of this functional equation. The result in question
                                           and the conjecture originally appeared in the paper [9] of Janusz Matkowski. In order to
                                           formulate them, first we introduce some basic concepts.
                                              As it is well known, a binary operation F : R × R → R is called associative if
                                                                                                      
                                                                          F F (x, y), z = F x, F (y, z)

                                           holds for any choice of x, y, z ∈ R. This property is frequently studied in the theory of
                                           means as well. It is easy to see that any quasi-sum mean is associative. In contrast,
                                           strictly weighted quasi-arithmetic means never satisfy the above equality. In her talk,
                                           Głazowska addressed the question of how one might weaken the associativity equation
                                           so that strictly weighted quasi-arithmetic means would also satisfy it. This leads to the
                                           concept of weak associativity, namely to the identity
                                                                                              
                                                                  F F (x, y), x = F x, F (y, x) ,    x, y ∈ R.

It turned out that every strict weighted quasi-arithmetic mean is weakly associative. The
interested reader can find further information in this direction in [4].
   Now we turn to the direction initiated by Matkowski in his paper [9]. A binary operation
F : R × R → R is called translative if
                               F (x + z, y + z) = F (x, y) + z

holds for all x, y, z ∈ R. Then the characterization theorem can be stated as follows.

Theorem (J. Matkowski, [9]). Let F : R × R → R be translative and f : R → R be
defined as f (x) := F (x, 0). The binary operation F is weakly associative if and only if
                                                      
(1)                          f f (−x) + x = f − f (x) + f (x)

is satisfied for all x ∈ R.

  Equations of this kind have been extensively investigated by numerous authors since
the 1970s. Related results can be found, for instance, in [11], [3], and [10]. A widely
studied variant is
                                          
(2)                   f f (x) + x = f f (x) + f (x),      x ∈ R,

which essentially expresses that f is additive on its own graph. G. L. Forti and J.
Matkowski obtained the same results concerning the solution of (2) under various regu-
larity assumptions in [2], [7] and [8]. The most general result was obtained by Witold Jar-
czyk, who solved the equation assuming only continuity. The details of his far-from-trivial
approach can be found in [5] and [6].

Theorem (W. Jarczyk, [5]). If f : R → R is a continuous solution of equation (2) then
either there exist non-negative numbers c− and c+ such that
                                         
                                         c x if x ≤ 0,
                                           −
                                 f (x) =
                                         c+ x if x > 0,

or there exists a negative number c such that
                                    f (x) = cx,    x ∈ R.

  Motivated by this result, Matkowski, in his paper [9], formulated the following conjec-
ture.

Conjecture (J. Matkowski, Remark 8. and Conjecture 2. of [9]). Let f : R → R be
continuous. The function f solves equation (1) if and only if there exist a, b ∈ R with
a ̸= b such that                       
                                       ax if x ≤ 0,
                               f (x) =
                                       bx if x > 0,

where a, b ∈ [0, 1] or a + b = 1.

  Although Matkowski’s conjecture was well-founded, it turned out that the family of
solutions of (1) is much richer. Equation (1), even in the continuous case, admits solutions
that possess a nonlinear component.

                 2. Solutions of (1) with non-linear components
  In all that follows we shall assume that f is known on the non-positive half-line, and
indeed that it is linear there. More precisely, we assume that there exists a ∈ R such that
(3)                                 f (x) = ax,      x ≤ 0.

   This is not a serious restriction in the following sense. One can easily deduce that if
f is a solution of (1), then x 7→ −f (−x) is a solution as well. As a consequence, each of
our results has a corresponding counterpart, its dual, namely the case when the function
is linear on the non-negative half-line. As the required modifications are self-evident, we
do not describe these consequences separately.
   We also take into account the origin of our functional equation, namely that it arose
as a restriction of a two-variable function. In order to obtain further conditions on f , we
assume that F : R × R → R is a mean, that is
(4)                           min(x, y) ≤ F (x, y) ≤ max(x, y)

holds for all x, y ∈ R. Because of the translativity of F , for any x, y ∈ R, we can write
                         F (x, y) = F (x − y, 0) + y = f (x − y) + y,

thus, in the light of (4), it follows that
(5)                      min(x, 0) ≤ f (x) ≤ max(x, 0),         x ∈ R.

We will refer to condition (5), from now on, as the bow-tie condition. We also note that
this forces, in (3), that 0 ≤ a ≤ 1. Furthermore, by the squeeze theorem, for any function
satisfying (5), we must have f (0−) = f (0+) = 0.
  We are now in a position to formulate our first statement.

Proposition 1. Assume that f : R → R satisfies condition (3) and that 0 ≤ f (x) ≤ x
for all x ≥ 0. Then f solves the functional equation (1) if and only if it is λ-homogeneous
on the non-negative half-line with λ ∈ {a, 1 − a}.

Proof. Assume first that f solves equation (1). If u ≤ 0, then f (−u) + u ≤ 0, hence the
left and right hand side of (1) reduce to af (−u) + au and f (−au) + au, respectively. By
equating them, simplifying and putting x = −u, we obtain that
                                  f (ax) = af (x),     x ≥ 0.

  If x ≥ 0, then −x ≤ 0 and f (x) ≥ 0. Thus, after a computation similar to the preceding
one, we obtain that

                         f (1 − a)x = (1 − a)f (x),      x ≥ 0.
