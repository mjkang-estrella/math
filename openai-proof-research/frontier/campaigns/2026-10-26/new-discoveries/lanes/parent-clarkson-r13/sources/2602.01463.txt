1.1. Thompson’s inequality and its development. In 1976, Thompson [16, 17] obtained
his famous triangle inequality for the operator right modulus.
Theorem 1.1 (Thompson). Let A, B ∈ Mn . Then there exist two unitaries U, V such that
                                 |A + B| ≤ U |A|U ∗ + V |B|V ∗ .
    Thompson [18] also characterized the equality case in Theorem 1.1.
Theorem 1.2 (Thompson). Let A, B ∈ Mn . The following are equivalent:
   (i) there exist unitaries U, V ∈ Mn such that
                                 |A + B| = U |A|U ∗ + V |B|V ∗ ;
    (ii) there exists a unitary W ∈ Mn such that
                                  A = W |A|,       B = W |B|.
   Passing from matrices to operators on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, the triangle
inequality persists with unitaries replaced by isometries thanks to Akemann, Anderson and
Pedersen [1]:
Theorem 1.3 (Akemann–Anderson–Pedersen). Let A, B ∈ B(H). Then there exist two isome-
tries U, V such that
                         |A + B| ≤ U |A|U ∗ + V |B|V ∗ .
  Naturally, we also ask when equality can occur in Theorem 1.3. However, the equality
characterization in Theorem 1.2 is genuinely finite-dimensional. Indeed, Ando and Hayashi [2]
remarked that Theorem 1.2 fails in the infinite-dimensional setting: one can find A, B ∈ B(H)
and unitaries U, V such that
                                 |A + B| = U |A|U ∗ + V |B|V ∗ ,
while there is no partial isometry W satisfying simultaneously
                                 A = W |A| and B = W |B|.
For instance, let P be an orthogonal projection and let Q be a subprojection of P . Assume
that P , Q, and P − Q all have infinite rank. Then there exist (at least countably many)
unitaries U, V for which
                             |P + (−Q)| = U |P |U ∗ + V |−Q|V ∗
holds, whereas there is no partial isometry W such that
                              P = W |P | and (−Q) = W |−Q|.
  Even though Theorem 1.2 breaks down in infinite dimension, Ando and Hayashi [2] showed
that the intrinsic triangle equality for the usual modulus still admits a clean description.
Theorem 1.4 (Ando–Hayashi). Let A, B ∈ B(H). If
                                      |A + B| = |A| + |B| ,
then there exists a partial isometry U ∈ B(H) such that
                                  A = U |A| ,      B = U |B| .
    Aujla and Bourin [3] established a matrix subadditivity inequality for concave functions.

1.3. Clarkson–McCarthy type inequalities for the quadratic symmetric modulus.
Let ∥ · ∥p denote the Schatten p-norm for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞, and the quasi-Schatten p-norm for
0 < p < 1. The classical Clarkson–McCarthy inequalities [15] state that for A, B ∈ Mn and
p ≥ 2,
                       ∥A + B∥pp + ∥A − B∥pp ≤ 2p−1 ∥A∥pp + ∥B∥pp .
                                                                 
                                                                                     (1.7)

refinement of (1.7): for p ≥ 2, there exist unitaries U, V such that
                      U |A + B|p U ∗ + V |A − B|p V ∗ ≤ 2p−1 |A|p + |B|p .

For 0 < p ≤ 2, the inequality is reversed. The Clarkson–McCarthy inequalities have since
been extended in various directions [4], including to multivariable inequalities involving several
