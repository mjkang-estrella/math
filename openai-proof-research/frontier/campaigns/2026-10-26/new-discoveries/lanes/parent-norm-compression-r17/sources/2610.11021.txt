   The endpoint r = 1 is the Gram matrix [ν(Pi Pj )]3i,j=1 in L2 (N , ν). For matrix density
operators, the endpoint r = 1/2 is the three-state root-fidelity matrix. Its positivity was
asserted in [13, Proposition 1]; Lin and van den Driessche subsequently pointed out a serious
gap in the published proof [24, p. 835]. The endpoint follows independently from Drury’s
trace-norm theorem [10, Corollary 1.3], and is also recovered by our argument. To the best
of our knowledge, the intermediate range 1/2 < r < 1 is new even for matrices.
   Theorem 1.1 is genuinely a finite-order statement, not an assertion that Krν is a positive
definite kernel on arbitrary finite sets. The explicit four-pure-state construction in Remark 2.4
gives a negative eigenvalue for every 1/2 ≤ r < 1; hence order three is sharp throughout this
nontrivial range. At r = 1, by contrast, K1ν is a Gram kernel at arbitrary order. Our proof
of (4) is not an interpolation between the two endpoints; it is based on a one-versus-many
trace inequality that controls all sign patterns in a three-dimensional quadratic form.

Remark 7.5. A faithful density in L1 (M, τ )+ exists if and only if M is σ-finite. Indeed,
such a density determines a faithful normal positive functional, whereas every σ-finite von
Neumann algebra admits a faithful normal state. In this case, faithful densities are dense in
L1 (M, τ )+ : if h is one fixed faithful density, then
                                     A + εh −→ A          in L1
as ε ↓ 0, and hence also in dt . The cone of faithful densities is not complete, since its
L1 -closure contains nonfaithful elements. If M is not σ-finite, then no faithful normal state,
and consequently no faithful state density, exists.
