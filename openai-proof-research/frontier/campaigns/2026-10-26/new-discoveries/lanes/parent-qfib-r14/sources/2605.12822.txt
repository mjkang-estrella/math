2.1. Fibonacci Facts. The Fibonacci numbers Fn are defined by the recurrence Fn =
Fn−1 + Fn−2 for n ≥ 2 with initial conditions F0 = 0 and F1 = 1.
We collect a few well-known Fibonacci identities (see, for example, [BQ03]) that will be

    (1) F1 + F2 + F3 + · · · + Fn = Fn+2 − 1.
    (2) F1 + F3 + · · · + F2n−1 = F2n .
    (3) F2 + F4 + · · · + F2n = F2n+1 − 1.
    (4) If m divides n, then Fm divides Fn .

We begin with the following observation about how q-degrees behave within a block.

Lemma 3.3. Let T ∈ Tm,2 with π(T ) ̸= T . Then w(T ) = q · w(π(T )).

Proof. We consider each type of move separately.
In move (M1), we obtain π(T ) by deleting a tile of weight q F1 F2 = q, so w(T ) = qw(T ′ ).
In move (M2), suppose the leftmost bottom row domino of T occupies (1, k). Then in order
to obtain π(T ), we are replacing this tile of weight q Fk with the tiles of weight q Fk−1 , q Fk−3 ,
and so on up until q F3 or q F2 , depending on the parity of k. If k is odd, the product of
the new terms is q Fk−1 +Fk−3 +...+F3 = q Fk −1 by Proposition 2.1(2). Similarly, if k is even, the
product of the new terms is q Fk−1 +Fk−3 +...+F2 = q Fk −1 by Proposition 2.1(3). In both cases,
the total degree of the weight goes down by 1.
Finally, similarly to move (M2), move (M3) replaces a tile of weight q Fk with the tiles of
total weight q Fk−1 +Fk−3 +... = q Fk −1 .

Lemma 3.5. The minimal and maximal tilings admit the following description.
    (1) The tiling S is minimal if and only if S has no vertical dominoes and no horizontal

Lemma 3.6. S ∼ T if and only if the top horizontal tilings of S and T coincide.
