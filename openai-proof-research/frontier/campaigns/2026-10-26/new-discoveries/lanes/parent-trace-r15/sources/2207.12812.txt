subspaces, for any p ≥ 1.

not linearly isometric to any subspace of Lp . In fact one may consider the space of real symmetric

                                                  3. Discussion
   It would be extremely interesting to know whether conjecture 1.0.2 is true in general. In addition
to the case p = 3, it is also clearly true for p = 2 (S2 is Hilbert space) and p = ∞ (L∞ is universal).
The case p = 1 is also well known; every 2-dimensional normed space is isometric to a subspace of
L1 , as observed by Lindenstrauss [11, Corollary 2].
   In the following discussion we shall assume that A and B are Hermitian n × n matrices.
3.1. p is not an integer. To prove conjecture 1.0.2, one might try to modify the proof of Theorem
1.0.1. Lemma 2.1.1 has generalization as follows. Define
