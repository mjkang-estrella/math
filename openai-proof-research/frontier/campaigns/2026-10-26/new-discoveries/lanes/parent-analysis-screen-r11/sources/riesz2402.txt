    The problem of constructing suitable examples appears to be much harder when
d ≥ 3. Indeed, it may well be that the cases d = 1 and d = 2 are exceptional and
that new phenomena occur for d ≥ 3, in a similar way as was seen in a somewhat
related context in [4, Theorem 1.2]. This state of affairs means that our conjecture
in higher dimensions is not as well founded as in the low dimensions d = 1 and
d = 2.
    However, we do have examples exhibiting the right asymptotic behavior when
d → ∞. Konyagin, Queffélec, Saksman, and Seip have recently established that
pd (q) → 2 as d → ∞ for every fixed 2 ≤ q ≤ ∞ (see [11, Corollary 2]), but their
argument does not provide an effective upper bound for pd (q) to compare with the
critical exponent conjecture. In analogy with this result, it is natural to expect that
q(d) → 2 as d → ∞ (see Section 4 for an elaboration).

Organization. In Section 2, we summarize the state-of-the-art on the critical ex-
ponent conjecture and discuss its relationship with dual extremal problem on Hardy
spaces. Section 3 contains new evidence in favour of the critical exponent conjecture,
including the proof of Theorem 1 and a discussion about its associated extremal
functions. Section 4 concerns the critical exponent conjecture in higher dimensions
and presents in particular the proof of Theorem 3.
