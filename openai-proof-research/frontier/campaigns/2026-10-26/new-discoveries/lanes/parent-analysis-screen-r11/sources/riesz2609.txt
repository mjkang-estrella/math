Recently, Brevig, Llinares, and Seip [5] confirmed that the endpoint inequality
(3) indeed holds for all f ∈ L1 . They also formulated a conjecture for the
critical exponents of Riesz projections in several variables and proved that,
if the one-dimensional critical exponent conjecture (2) is true, then the
corresponding conjecture in two dimensions is also true [5, Corollary 4].
However, this implication does not necessarily extend to higher dimensions.
We mention that these contractive inequalities have important applications
to Hardy spaces on higher-dimensional tori and, via the Bohr lift, to Hardy
spaces of Dirichlet series; see, for instance, [2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 13, 14, 15].
   The main result of this note is to prove that (2) is valid for all the remaining
values 1 < q < ∞.
Theorem 1.1. For every 1 < q < ∞, the Riesz projection
                               P : Lq → L4(1−1/q)
is contractive.
   Our approach goes back to Cole’s work on sharp inequalities for conjugate
functions, where best constant inequalities were related to the existence of
suitable subharmonic minorants; see [8, 10]. This method was later developed
in a effective plurisubharmonic form by Hollenbeck and Verbitsky [11], and
has been widely used to determine exact norms and sharp constants for Riesz
projections and Hilbert transforms [1, 10, 16].
