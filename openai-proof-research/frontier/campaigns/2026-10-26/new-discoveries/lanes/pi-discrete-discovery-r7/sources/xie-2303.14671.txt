                                           1       Introduction
                                           In this paper all graphs we consider are undirected, finite and simple. A hypercube of dimension
                                           n (or n-cube for short), denoted by Qn , is a graph whose vertex set is corresponding to the
                                           set of 0-1 sequences x1 x2 · · · xn with xi ∈ {0, 1}, i = 1, 2, · · · , n. Two vertices are adjacent
                                           if the corresponding 0-1 sequences differ in exactly one digit. G is called partial cube if it is
                                           isomorphic to an isometric subgraph of Qn for some n.

                                           [9, 28]), plays an important role in studying the partial cubes. This relation was used by
                                           Winkler [28] to characterize the partial cubes as those bipartite graphs for which Θ is an
                                           equivalence relation on edges. Its equivalence classes are called Θ-classes. Let G be a partial

   Combined with the fact that the independence polynomial of a graph is equal to the clique
polynomial of its complement, Theorem 1.1 indicates that the investigation of the cube poly-
nomials of median graphs can be transformed to the study on the clique polynomials and the
independence polynomials of general graphs.
   A sequence (s1 , s2 , · · · , sn ) of nonnegative real numbers is unimodal if
                              s1 ⩽ s2 ⩽ · · · ⩽ sm ⩾ · · · ⩾ sn−1 ⩾ sn
for some integer 1 ⩽ m ⩽ n and log-concave if
                              si−1 si+1 ⩽ s2i ,   for 2 ⩽ i ⩽ n − 1.
The sequence is said to have no internal zeros if there are not three indices i < j < k such that
si , sk > 0 and sj = 0. In particular, the positive sequences have no internal zeros. It is well-
known that a log-concave sequence with no internal zeros is unimodal [6]. A polynomial is called
unimodal (resp. log-concave) if the sequence of its coefficients is unimodal (resp. log-concave).
By the definitions, the coefficient sequences of the cube polynomials, the clique polynomials
and the independence polynomials of graphs are positive. Thus, for these graph polynomials,
the log-concavity is stronger than the unimodality.
      When studying graph polynomials, the unimodality and the log-concavity are always con-
sidered. For instance, it has been proved that the matching polynomials of graphs [12], the
independence polynomials of claw-free graphs [10] and the signless chromatic polynomials of
graphs [15] are log-concave, but the conjecture about the unimodality of independence polyno-
mials of trees is still open [1].
      Zhang et al. conjectured:
Conjecture 1.3. [31] The cube polynomials of median graphs are unimodal.
    We disprove this conjecture by providing counterexamples, which are obtained from Qn
(n ⩾ 9) by attaching sufficiently many pendant vertices.
    The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we introduce some terminology and
properties of partial cubes and median graphs. Then, we prove the main theorem (i.e., Theorem
1.1) in Section 3 and disprove Conjecture 1.3 in Section 4. Finally, we conclude the paper and
propose some future problems in Section 5.


2     Preliminaries
Let G be a graph with vertex set V (G) and edge set E(G). For S ⊆ V (G), the subgraph
induced by S is denoted by G[S]. For v ∈ V (G), NG (v) is the neighbourhood of v in G, i.e.,
NG (v) := {u ∈ V (G)|uv ∈ E(G)}. The subgraph G[NG (v)] is written as Gv simply. For
u, v ∈ V (G), the distance dG (u, v) (we will drop the subscript G if no confusion can occur) is
the length of the shortest path between u and v in G. We call a shortest path from u to v a
u, v-geodesic. A subgraph H of G is called isometric if for any u, v ∈ V (H), dH (u, v) = dG (u, v),
and further, if for any u, v ∈ V (H), all u, v-geodesics are contained in H, we call H a convex
subgraph of G. Obviously, the convex subgraphs are isometric and the isometric ones are
induced and connected. A graph G is called a partial cube if it is isomorphic to an isometric
subgraph of Qn for some n.
    The Djoković-Winkler relation (see [9, 28]) ΘG is a binary relation on E(G) defined as
follows: Let e = uv and f = xy be two edges in G, e ΘG f ⇐⇒ dG (u, x) + dG (v, y) ̸=
dG (u, y) + dG (v, x). If G is bipartite, there is another equivalent definition of the Djoković-
Winkler relation: e ΘG f ⇐⇒ dG (u, x) = dG (v, y) and dG (u, y) = dG (v, x). Winkler [28]
proved that a graph G is a partial cube if and only if G is bipartite and ΘG is an equivalence
relation on E(G). The following property can be obtained easily by definition.

    G is called a median graph if for every three different vertices u, v, w ∈ V (G), there exists
exactly one vertex x ∈ V (G) (maybe x ∈ {u, v, w}), called the median of u, v, w, satisfying
that d(u, x) + d(x, v) = d(u, v), d(u, x) + d(x, w) = d(u, w) and d(v, x) + d(x, w) = d(v, w),
that is, there exist the geodesics between each pair of u, v, w where x lies on all of them. If H
is a convex subgraph of a median graph G, then H is also a median graph. There are many
equivalent characterizations of median graphs (see [20]). The most famous one is the following
proposition:
Proposition 2.6. [17] A graph G is a median graph if and only if G is a partial cube and
G[Uuv ] is convex in G for every uv ∈ E(G).
    Let G be a graph and G1 , G2 two isometric subgraphs with G = G1 ∪ G2 and G0 = G1 ∩ G2
not empty, where there are no edges between G1 − G2 and G2 − G1 . Let G∗i be isomorphic copy
of Gi for i = 1, 2. For every u ∈ V (G0 ), let ui be the corresponding vertex in G∗i (i = 1, 2).
The expansion G∗ of G with respect to {G1 , G2 } is the graph obtained from the disjoint union
G∗1 and G∗2 by adding an edge between the corresponding vertices u1 and u2 for each vertex
u ∈ G0 . It is known that partial cubes are characterized as graphs that can be obtained from
K1 by a sequence of expansions [8]. If G is a partial cube, all edges u1 u2 for u ∈ G0 compose
a new Θ-class by the definition of the Djoković-Winkler relation. So idim(G∗ ) = idim(G) + 1.
The expansion is convex if G0 is convex, and peripheral if G0 = G1 or G0 = G2 . The second
equivalent characterization of median graphs we will use is:

                                                 4
Proposition 2.7. [26] Let G be a connected graph. G is a median graph if and only if it can
be obtained from K1 by a sequence of peripheral convex expansions.

    Let G be a partial cube and not K1 , Fab , Fuv two Θ-classes of G. We say Fab , Fuv cross if
Wab ∩ Wuv ̸= ∅, Wba ∩ Wuv ̸= ∅, Wab ∩ Wvu ̸= ∅ and Wba ∩ Wvu ̸= ∅. For a subgraph H of
G, we say Fuv occurs in H if there is an edge of Fuv in E(H). Another equivalent definition
of crossing is: Fab and Fuv cross if Fab occurs in both G[Wuv ] and G[Wvu ]. The crossing graph
of G (see [21]), denoted by G# , is the graph whose vertices are corresponding to the Θ-classes
of G, and θ1 = Fab , θ2 = Fuv are adjacent in G# if and only if they cross in G. The following
proposition is an equivalent expression of crossing relation.

Proposition 2.8. [21] Let G be a partial cube, θ1 , θ2 two Θ-classes. Then θ1 and θ2 cross if
and only if they occur on an isometric cycle C, i.e., E(C) ∩ θ1 ̸= ∅, E(C) ∩ θ2 ̸= ∅.


3     Proof of Theorem 1.1
The proof of Theorem 1.1 is organized as follows. First, we prove that the ‘=’ in (1) holds if G

3.1     C(G, x) = Cl(G# , x + 1) if G is a median graph
Klavžar and Mulder obtained the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1. [21] Let G be a median graph and uv, uw ∈ E(G). If Fuv and Fuw cross, then
v, u, w are in a 4-cycle.

    Let G be a partial cube, θ1 , θ2 two Θ-classes. A 4-cycle uvwxu is called to be θ1 , θ2 -
alternating if uv, xw ∈ θ1 and ux, vw ∈ θ2 . We have the following lemma, which is stronger
than Lemma 3.1.

Lemma 3.2. Let G be a median graph, θ1 , θ2 two Θ-classes. Then θ1 and θ2 cross if and only
if there exists a θ1 , θ2 -alternating 4-cycle.

Proof. Sufficiency. Assume C = uvwxu is a 4-cycle where uv, xw ∈ θ1 and ux, vw ∈ θ2 . Since
4-cycles must be isometric in a bipartite graph, by Proposition 2.8, θ1 and θ2 cross.
    Necessity. Denote the two components of G − θ1 as G1 , G2 . By the definition of crossing,
there exist a1 b1 , a2 b2 ∈ θ2 satisfying that a1 b1 ∈ E(G1 ), a2 b2 ∈ E(G2 ) and d(a1 , a2 ) = d(b1 , b2 ) =

is an edge cutset, there exists an edge in E(P ) ∩ θ1 , denoted by c1 c2 . Since c1 , c2 is on P , which
is an a1 , a2 -geodesic, by Proposition 2.6, c1 , c2 ∈ Ua1 b1 . Thus, there exist edges c1 d1 , c2 d2 ∈ θ2 .
By Observation 2.2, d1 , d2 are adjacent and d1 d2 ∈ θ1 , that is, the 4-cycle c1 c2 d2 d1 c1 is θ1 , θ2 -
alternating.

there is a θ1 , θ2 -alternating 4-cycle in H, we say that θ1 and θ2 cross in H.

5     Conclusion and future work regarding unimodality
In the present paper, we obtain an inequality relation between the cube polynomials of par-
tial cubes G and clique polynomials of their crossing graphs, i.e., C(G, x) ⩽ Cl(G# , x + 1).
Moreover, the equality holds if and only if G is a median graph.
    A hexagonal system (or benzenoid system) is a 2-connected finite plane graph such that every
interior face is a regular hexagon of side length one. Let H be a hexagonal system. The Clar
covering polynomial (or Zhang-Zhang polynomial) ζ(H, x) is an important graph polynomial
in mathematical chemistry, which was introduced by Zhang and Zhang [32]. Zhang et al. [31]
proved that ζ(H, x) = C(R(H), x), where R(H) is the resonance graph of H (further a median
graph [30]).
    Although Conjecture 1.3 associated with median graphs is false, the conjecture on the
resonance graphs of hexagonal systems (a subclass of median graphs) is still open.

                                                   11
Conjecture 5.1. [33] For a hexagonal system H, C(R(H), x) (i.e., ζ(H, x)) is unimodal.

    Further, since all coefficients of ζ(H, x) are positive, a stronger conjecture was proposed by
Li et al. [22] after checking large amounts of numerical results as follows.

Conjecture 5.2. [22] For a hexagonal system H, the Clar covering polynomial ζ(H, x) is log-
concave.

    By Theorems 3.8 and 1.2, the cube polynomials of median graphs, the clique polynomi-
als and the independence polynomials of general graphs can be transformed into each other.
Considering the close relationship among these polynomials, here we mentioned the following
well-known conjecture proposed by Alavi, Malde, Schwenk and Erdős in 1987.

Conjecture 5.3. [1] The independence polynomial of every tree is unimodal.

    In particular, Conjecture 5.3 cannot be strengthened up to its log-concave version, which is
disproved by Kadrawi et al. [18, 19] by providing counterexamples—several infinite families of
trees of order at least 26.
    For some special unimodal polynomials P (x), e.g. log-concave polynomials [14], nondecreas-
ing polynomials [5] or unimodal polynomials with degree n and mode at least n−4 [29], P (x+1)
is also unimodal. Combined with Theorem 3.8, the following problem related to Conjecture 5.3
can be studied in the future. Note that a co-tree is the complement of a tree.

Problem 5.4. Is the cube polynomial C(G, x) unimodal if G# is a co-tree?
