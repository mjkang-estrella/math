                                                  Abstract. For Stirling expansions truncated after at least two Bernoulli terms, we disprove
                                                  the three real-degree conjectures recorded by Mahmoud and Qi for the signed remainders
                                                  of the logarithm of the gamma function and their derivatives. We give a unified proof
                                                  of the corresponding integer-degree formula, recovering known cases and resolving Zhu’s
                                                  conjectures for higher derivatives in this range. Every real completely monotonic degree




arXiv:2610.33699v1 [math.CO] 24 Oct 2026
                                                  lies strictly between the predicted integer and the next integer. We obtain a universal
                                                  fractional lower bound through an explicit inverse function and a simpler rational bound
                                                  valid for every derivative order. A local estimate for the negative part of the Laplace kernel’s
                                                  derivative with respect to its logarithmic variable makes the lower improvement proportional
                                                  to the reciprocal of the combined truncation and derivative order. Together with a uniform
                                                  upper bound, this determines the exact order of decay of the fractional excess for each fixed
                                                  derivative order and for its supremum over all derivative orders. Sharper kernel estimates give
                                                  stronger bounds for the first two degrees. The proof combines fractional positivity criteria,
                                                  the classical Laplace representation, a sign characterization of the associated densities, and
                                                  a convexity obstruction at the upper integer endpoint. All estimates used in the proofs are
                                                  analytic. The exact real degrees and the optimal asymptotic constants remain open.




                                                                         1. Introduction and main results
                                              The distinction between real and integer exponents is decisive for the completely monotonic
                                           degrees of Stirling remainders. We show that the conjectured integer values are their exact
                                           integer parts, while every real degree in the truncation range considered here is strictly
                                           larger. We also determine the order of decay of this fractional excess as the truncation order
                                           increases.

1.2. Conjectures and prior results. Complete monotonicity of the signed Stirling remain-
ders was established by Alzer [1, Theorem 8]. Koumandos [7] used Euler–Maclaurin bounds
for the Bernoulli generating function to give simpler proofs of complete monotonicity results
for gamma and polygamma expressions. Diamond and Straub [4] extended this analysis to
shifted asymptotic expansions with Bernoulli-polynomial coefficients. Earlier classifications
of real power weights preserving complete monotonicity for differences of shifted polygamma
functions appear in Alzer and Berg [2, Theorem 4.3].
   Koumandos and Pedersen [8] studied complete monotonicity of positive order and the
Laplace kernels of Stirling remainders. Their Remark 1.6 also gives a fractional-order
formulation. They later related the Laplace kernels of Euler and Barnes gamma remainders
to absolutely monotonic functions [9]. These structural results provide the setting for the
degree questions considered below.
   Qi and Liu [15] proposed conjectures for the completely monotonic degrees of gamma
and polygamma remainders. We recall separately the three real-degree predictions in the
formulation of Mahmoud and Qi [12], whose Definition 1.1 explicitly permits real degrees.
The labels below are local to this paper, and the statements are restricted to the truncation
range n ≥ 2. Conjectures A, B, and C correspond, respectively, to [12, (2.5), (2.7), (2.9)].

1.3. Main results and resolution of the conjectures. Mahmoud and Qi [12, Theorem 2.1]
proved 2n − 1 ≤ dCM (−Rn′ ) < 2n for n ≥ 2. Bouali [3, Proposition 1.1] states the companion
bound 2n − 2 ≤ dCM (Rn ) < 2n − 1 in a preprint. The Mahmoud–Qi interval already
gives ⌊dCM (−Rn′ )⌋ = 2n − 1 for every n ≥ 2, and the interval stated by Bouali gives
⌊dCM (Rn )⌋ = 2n−2. Zhu [18, Theorems 3.1–3.2 and 4.2–4.3] had established the corresponding
integer-degree cases with n = 2, 3 and m = 0, 1. Thus the integer formula in Conjecture D
includes cases already available in the literature.
   For the trigamma remainder, Qi [14] proved 4 ≤ dCM (R2′′ ) ≤ 5. None of these intervals
alone determines whether a positive fractional improvement of the lower integer endpoint is
admissible. The theorem below gives a common proof of the integer-degree formula for every
derivative order and establishes the strict fractional improvement throughout the family,
including the cases whose integer degree was already known.

1.4. Contribution and proof strategy. The rational Laplace representation, Bernstein’s
theorem, and the fractional-order interpretation of complete monotonicity are established
tools; see [8]. The signed-density characterization below is an application of classical unique-
ness of Laplace transforms to this particular family. The broader connection between
derivative inequalities and positive integral representations includes the generalized Stieltjes
characterizations of Sokal [16] and Koumandos and Pedersen [11]. For generalized Stieltjes
functions, monotonicity of asymptotic remainders can also characterize moment properties of
the representing measures [10]. Karp and Prilepkina [6] developed criteria for exact Stieltjes
order and fractional integral transformations of representing measures. That order concerns
generalized Stieltjes representations and is distinct from the weighted completely monotonic
degree in (1.1).
   The quantitative step developed here is a fractional positivity criterion that uses the
negative part of the kernel’s derivative with respect to its logarithmic variable, only up to the
point being tested. For the common kernel, this local estimate yields an admissible fractional
order comparable to p−1 . It gives the simultaneous disproof of Conjectures A–C, extends
the integer-degree formula to arbitrary derivative order, and supplies the lower half of the
exact decay scale. A separate scaled-density argument supplies the upper half, uniformly in
all derivative orders.
   Section 2 develops the positivity criteria and the elementary facts about admissible
exponents. Section 3 derives the common kernel and proves Theorem 1.1. Section 4 sharpens
the first two cases, and Section 5 establishes the asymptotic bounds, including derivative orders
growing proportionally to the truncation order. Section 6 states the remaining questions.

                     6. Scope of the results and open questions
   Theorems 1.1–1.3 resolve the distinction between the real and integer versions of the
conjectures for every n ≥ 2 and m ≥ 0. The real degrees are nonintegers, their integer parts
are exactly 2n + m − 2, and the excess has order n−1 for fixed derivative order. The same
order holds uniformly when the derivative order grows at most proportionally to n, as stated
in Corollary 5.1.
   The positivity criteria give sufficient conditions for each individual scaled kernel to have a
nonnegative fractional derivative. The density characterization in Proposition 3.2 instead
tests the full sum Qn,m,α . Cancellation between summands may preserve nonnegativity
beyond the range certified term by term. Thus the lower bounds, including those with exact
auxiliary kernel constants in Section 4, are not asserted to be the exact degrees. In particular,
optimality of MH in the inequality sH ′ (s) ≥ −MH does not imply dCM (R2 ) = 2 + βH .
                          NONINTEGER DEGREES OF STIRLING REMAINDERS                                          13

   The first remaining problem is to determine the exact values of dCM (R2 ) and dCM (−R2′ ).
A second is to decide whether (2n − 1)δn,m converges for fixed m, and to identify its limit if it
exists. The present estimates place its lower and upper limits between 3/π and 2π 2 /J. The
limit pβp → 3/π describes the constructed lower bound, and does not identify the limiting
coefficient of the actual degree. Determining the optimal constants in the uniform estimates
is a separate question. Finally, the behavior as m → ∞ with n fixed is not settled by the
asymptotics in this paper.
