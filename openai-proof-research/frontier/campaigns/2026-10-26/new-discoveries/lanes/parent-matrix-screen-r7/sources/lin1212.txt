                                          1     Introduction
                                          As customary, we reserve M, m for scalars and I for the identity operator. Other capital
                                          letters denote general elements of the C ∗ algebra B(H) (with unit) of all bounded linear
                                          operator acting on a Hilbert space (H, h·, ·i). Also, we identify a scalar with the unit
                                          multiplied by this scalar. The operator norm is denoted by k · k. We write T ≥ 0 to
                                          mean that the operator T is positive and identify T ≥ S (the same as S ≤ T ) with
                                          T − S ≥ 0. A positive invertible operator T is naturally denoted by T > 0. Finally, the
                                          absolute value of T is denoted by |T | = (T ∗ T )1/2 , where T ∗ stands for the adjoint of T .

Remark 2.11. As |X| ≥ X for any self-adjoint X, we find that (2.9) is stronger than
(2.10). The reason to present (2.9) and (2.10) separately is that they are different. As
Φ(A−1 ) and Φ(A) do not commute generally, Φ(A−1 )Φ(A) + Φ(A)Φ(A−1 ) need not be
positive in general.
Remark 2.12. Both (2.9) and (2.10) may be regarded as operator versions of Kantorovich
inequality. We note that a similar version to (2.10) had been established earlier by Sun
[15], in the process of extending Bauer-Fike inequality on condition numbers. However,
his line of proof is quite different from ours. Sun’s operator version of Kantorovich
inequality seems to be overlooked by the community, though it was proved almost at
the same time as the appearance of (1.2). Interestingly, Sun’s extension of Bauer-Fike
inequality on condition numbers can also be proved using (1.2); see [15] for more details.
