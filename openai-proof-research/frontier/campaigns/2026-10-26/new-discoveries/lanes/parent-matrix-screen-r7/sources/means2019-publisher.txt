1. Introduction

     Throughout, let B(H) be the C∗ -algebra of bounded linear operator on a complex Hilbert space H and
the identity operator is denoted by IH . For two self-adjoint operators A, B ∈ B(H), A ≤ (<)B means B − A
is a positive (invertible) operator. A linear map Φ : B(H) → B(K ) is called positive (strictly positive, resp.)
if it maps positive (invertible, resp.) operators into positive (invertible, resp.) operators and is said to be
unital if it maps identity operator to identity operator.

   The axiomatic theory for operator means for pairs of positive invertible operators have been developed
by Kubo and Ando [12]. A binary operation σ defined on the set of positive invertible operators is called
an operator mean provided that

  1. IH σIH = IH ;
  2. C∗ (AσB)C ≤ (C∗ AC)σ(C∗ BC);
  3. An ↓ A and Bn ↓ B imply An σBn ↓ AσB, where An ↓ A means A1 ≥ A2 ≥ · · · and An → A in the strong
     operator topology;
  4. A ≤ B and C ≤ D imply that AσC ≤ BσD.
