# MathDB 369283: excluded because already solved

Screened 2026-10-26 before proof construction or numerical search. New discovery credit: **0**.

The original target is Conjecture 5.1 of Nathaniel Johnston, Sarah Plosker, Charles Torrance, and Luis M. B. Varona, *Generalizing the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality: Hadamard powers and tensor products*, [arXiv:2507.10327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10327). I read its actual PDF: printed p. 13 states the inequality for every real p≥2 and strictly positive vectors. Entrywise powers, Euclidean norms, and the dot product have the same meanings as in the MathDB statement.

The exact conjecture has already been proved by **Nathaniel Johnston and Mohammad Sababheh**, *Hadamard powers of Gram matrices and Generalized Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities*, [arXiv:2610.12063v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12063v1), submitted **8 October 2026**. The actual PDF's **Corollary 3.2, printed p. 6**, proves it for every real p≥2 and all entrywise nonnegative vectors, a stronger domain than the target. I read the corollary and its proof and visually checked the PDF page. The proof uses their Hadamard-power Gram-matrix inequality (Theorem 3.1), unit normalization, and AM–GM. This is an explicit primary resolution, not an inference from an abstract or an unrelated integer-exponent theorem.

The author's [current publication page](https://njohnston.ca/publications/hadamard-powers-of-gram-matrices-and-generalized-cauchy-schwarz-inequalities/) also identifies the earlier paper's two conjectures as solved. The preprint additionally resolves the maximum-gap conjecture and equality cases; those are excluded from novelty credit too. The author's webpage spells the coauthor's given name differently; the PDF/arXiv primary byline says **Mohammad**, used here.

The freshly retrieved `sources/369283-live.json` still says open and has no posted solutions. It is stale relative to the 8 October primary resolution. No proof or formalization was started after finding the resolution, and no external message was posted.
