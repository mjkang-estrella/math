  C*-algebraic Gauss-Lucas Theorem and C*-algebraic Sendov’s
                         Conjecture

                                                  K. M. Krishna ∗

School of Mathematics and Natural Sciences, Chanakya University Global Campus, NH-648,
Haraluru Village, Devanahalli Taluk, Bengaluru North District, Karnataka State, 562 110, India

   Abstract. Building on a foundational result established by Robertson [Proc. Edinburgh Math. Soc., 1976 ], we
   develop a framework for differentiation of maps defined on specific classes of unital commutative C*-algebras.
   This construction allows us to extend classical analytic ideas into the C*-algebraic setting, thereby enriching the
   interplay between operator theory and complex analysis. Within this framework, we derive a C*-algebraic analogue
   of the Gauss–Lucas theorem, which traditionally describes the geometric location of polynomial zeros in relation to
   their derivatives. Furthermore, we propose a C*-algebraic version of Sendov’s conjecture, a long-standing problem
   in complex analysis concerning the proximity of critical points to polynomial zeros. Our formulation adapts this
   conjecture to the algebraic context and provides new insights into its structure. As a concrete verification, we establish
   the validity of the C*-algebraic Sendov’s conjecture for all polynomials of degree two, thereby offering evidence for
   its broader applicability.
       Key words: Sendov’s conjecture, C*-algebra, Gauss-Lucas theorem.

Introduction

    Let a be a complex number, r > 0 be a real number and D(a, r) be the closed disc
in C centered at a of radius r. Let C[z] be the set of all polynomials over C. In 1958,
Sendov made the following conjecture which became known as Sendov’s conjecture.
    Conjecture 1. [1–3] (Sendov’s conjecture) Let n ∈ N \ {1} and p(z) = (z −
a1 )(z − a2 ) · · · (z − an ) ∈ C[z] be such that a1 , a2 , . . . , an ∈ D(0, 1). Then for each aj ,
1 ≤ j ≤ n, there exists a zero b of p ′ such that b ∈ D(aj , 1).
    A direct calculation says that Conjecture 1 holds for degree two polynomials. For
n = 3, Conjecture 1 is proved in [4]. For n = 3, 4, Conjecture 1 is proved in [5]. For
n = 5, Conjecture 1 is proved in [6]. For n = 6, Conjecture 1 is proved in [7,8]. For n = 7,
Conjecture 1 is proved in [9]. For n = 8, Conjecture 1 is proved in [10]. Conjecture 1 is
proved for all polynomials after certain (unknown) degree in [11]. On the other hand,
Conjecture 1 is proved for various special cases of polynomials and position of roots
(inside the closed unit disc) in [5, 12–39]. Non-Archimedean version of Conjecture 1 has
been solved in [40].
    A subsection of noncommutative geometry strives to obtain complex analysis results
by replacing C with a C*-algebra. A few prominent articles in this way are [41–44]. As
C*-algebras offer greater challenge and Sendov conjecture is still open for vast set of
polynomials, we believe that by generalizing the problem itself, we may get some light
on six decades old conjecture itself.
    In this paper, we derive Gauss-Lucas theorem for polynomials over certain C*-
algebras. We then formulate Sendov’s conjecture for polynomials over C*-algebras.


C*-algebraic Gauss-Lucas Theorem and C*-algebraic Sendov’s
Conjecture
    Let A be a unital commutative C*-algebra and let G(A) be the set of all invertible
elements of A. Let A[z] be the set of all polynomials over A. We assume G(A) is dense
in A. Such C*-algebras exist and a characterization of such C*-algebras is given by
Robertson [45]. We now introduce the following definition.
    Definition 1. (C*-algebraic differentiation) Let A be a unital commutative
C*-algebra and G(A) be dense in A. Let f : A → A be a function and ω ∈ A. We
say that f is C*-algebraic differentiable at ω if there exists an L ∈ A satisfying the
following: for each ε > 0, there exists a δ > 0 such that if z ∈ A satisfies ∥z−ω∥ < δ
and z − ω ∈ G(A), then

                              ∥(z − ω)−1 (f(z) − f(ω)) − L∥ < ε.

In this case, we write f ′ (ω) = L.
   Note that Definition 1 is nontrivial for any commutative C*-algebras which is not
C. The reason is unlike C, nonzero elements need not be invertible. We note that,
because G(A) is dense in A, Definition 1 is well-defined. Now we derive C*-algebraic
Gauss-Lucas theorem.

invertible elements. We verified the C*-algebraic Sendov conjecture only for degree 2
polynomials. Like classical case, for degree 3-8, we believe that it can be solved by
computations, at least for special polynomials.

finitely many elements using functional calculus.

Conclusion
   The paper establishes a C*-algebraic framework for classical results in complex
analysis by defining differentiation on unital commutative C*-algebras where invertibles
are dense, leveraging Robertson’s result. It proves the C*-algebraic Gauss-Lucas
theorem, showing that critical points of polynomials over such algebras lie in positive
convex combinations of the roots. Additionally, it formulates two versions of the C*-
algebraic Sendov’s conjecture—one requiring differentiability and one without—and
verifies the latter for degree-2 polynomials.
