                                              COMONOTONE APPROXIMATION AND INTERPOLATION BY ENTIRE
                                                                 FUNCTIONS II

                                                                                                MAXIM R. BURKE


                                                   Abstract. A theorem of Hoischen states that given a positive continuous function ε : R → R, an
                                                   integer n ≥ 0, and a closed discrete set E ⊆ R, any C n function f : R → R can be approximated by
                                                   an entire function g so that for k = 0, . . . , n, and x ∈ R, |Dk g(x)−Dk f (x)| < ε(x), and if x ∈ E then




arXiv:2512.23949v1 [math.CA] 30 Dec 2025
                                                   Dk g(x) = Dk f (x). The approximating function g is entire and hence piecewise monotone. Building
                                                   on earlier work, for n ≤ 3, we determine conditions under which when f is piecewise monotone
                                                   we can choose g to be comonotone with f (increasing and decreasing on the same intervals), and
                                                   under which the derivatives of g can be taken to be comonotone with the corresponding derivatives
                                                   of f if the latter are piecewise monotone. The proof for n ≤ 3 establishes the theorem for all n,
                                                   assuming a conjecture (shown in previous work with Haris and Madhavendra to hold for n ≤ 3)
                                                   regarding the set of 2(n+1)-tuples (f (0), Df (0), . . . , Dn f (0), f (1), Df (1), . . . , Dn f (1)) of the values
                                                   at the endpoints of the derivatives of a C n function f on [0, 1] for which Dn f is increasing and not
                                                   constant.




                                                                                               1. Introduction
                                              In this paper we continue the investigation begun in [2] into comonotone approximation and
                                           interpolation of C n functions and their derivatives by entire functions when the derivatives are
                                           piecewise monotone. Comonotone approximation by polynomials of functions on a compact interval
                                           has been treated extensively. See [5], Chapter 1. See also [4] where characterizations are provided
                                           for the best comonotone polynomial approximation to a piecewise monotone continuous function on
                                           a compact interval, and the references therein. See the introduction to [2] for historical information
                                           on the approximation of monotone functions by monotone entire functions on the real line.
                                              Our work is motivated by the following result of Hoischen.
                                           Theorem 1.1. [6] Let n be a nonnegative integer. Let E ⊆ R be a closed discrete set. Suppose
                                           f : R → R is a C n function and ε : R → R is a positive continuous function. Then there exists an
                                           entire function g such that g(R) ⊆ R and for all k = 0, . . . , n and all x ∈ R, |Dk g(x) − Dk f (x)| <
                                           ε(x) and moreover, if x ∈ E then Dk f (x) = Dk g(x).
                                              The approximating function g and its derivatives Dj g are entire and hence piecewise monotone.

   We use the words increasing, decreasing and monotone in the non-strict sense, i.e., f is increasing
on I if f is ≤-increasing (x ≤ y ⇒ f (x) ≤ f (y)), f is decreasing on I if f is ≤-decreasing
(x ≤ y ⇒ f (y) ≤ f (x)), and f is monotone if f is increasing or decreasing. Two functions on
I have the same monotonicity if they are both increasing or both decreasing. We say that f is
piecewise monotone if there is a set K ⊆ I which is a closed discrete subset of I and is such that
f is monotone on each component of I \ K. K will be called a witnessing set to the piecewise
monotonicity of f .
   For continuous piecewise monotone functions, the failure of monotonicity is witnessed by the
existence of a turning platform.
Proposition 1.2 ([2], Proposition 2.1). Let I be a nontrivial interval of R and let f : I → R
be continuous and piecewise monotone. Then f has a turning platform if and only if f is not
monotone.
   Real-analytic functions are piecewise monotone on the real line. Proposition 1.4 records the
(standard) fact that more generally C 1 functions whose derivative has a closed discrete set of zeros
are piecewise monotone and the fact that the nonexistence of flat points for a C n function is a
sufficient condition to ensure that the zero set is closed discrete.
Definition 1.3 ([2], Definition 2.4). With n a nonnegative integer or ∞, we say that p ∈ I is an
n-flat point, or just a flat point, for a C n function f : I → R if Dk f (p) = 0, 0 ≤ k ≤ n, k ∈ Z.
   Notice that a flat point for f is also a flat point for each Dk f , 0 ≤ k ≤ n, k ∈ Z.1 Thus, if
for some nonnegative integer k ≤ n, Dk f has no flat points, then none of the derivatives Di f ,
i = 0, . . . , k, have flat points either.
Proposition 1.4 ([2], Propositions 2.5, 2.6, 2.7). Let n be a nonnegative integer or ∞, k a non-
negative integer with k ≤ n. Let I be a nontrivial interval of R and let f : I → R. Write Zi for the
zero set of Di f (when Di f exists), and write Ei for the set of turning points for Di f .
  (1) If f is a C n function and has no flat points, then Z0 is closed discrete in I.
  (2) If f is a C 1 function and Z1 is closed discrete, then E0 is closed discrete in I and f is strictly
      monotone on the components of I \ E0 .
  (3) If f is a C n function and Dk f has no flat points, then ki=0 Zi is closed discrete in I and,

   Two functions f, g : I → R are comonotone with witnessing set K if they are both piecewise
monotone with K as a witnessing set, and f and g have the same monotonicity on each component
of I \ K.
Remark 1.5. (a) Because constant functions are monotone, comonotone functions need not be
monotone on the same intervals. For example, the functions f (x) = 0 and g(x) = x2 on R are
comonotone with witnessing set {0}, but f is monotone on R whereas g is not.
   (b) Even when comonotone functions are monotone on the same intervals, they can be comono-
tone for some witnessing sets and not for others. For example, the functions f, g : R → R given by
f (x) = max(x, 0), g(x) = max(−x, 0) are both monotone and hence every closed discrete set K is a
common witness to their piecewise monotonicity. However, f and g are comonotone using a closed
discrete set K as a witnessing set if and only if 0 ∈ K.

Proposition 1.7 ([2], Proposition 2.9). Let I be a nontrivial interval of R and let f, g : I → R be
C 1 functions. If the zero set of Df is a closed discrete set in I, and Df and Dg have the same

Proposition 1.8. Let K be a witnessing set for a piecewise monotone continuous function f on a

  (a) K is ⊆-minimal if and only if the points of K are all turning points and K has exactly one
      point on each turning platform.
 (b) K contains a ⊆-minimal witnessing set.

Claim. K ′ = {xP : P is a turning platform} is also a witnessing set.

  (a) and (b) follow easily from the observation before the claim and the claim itself.              □
Proposition 1.9. Let I be a nontrivial interval of R, and let f : I → R be a C 1 function. If P is
a platform of positive length for f , then P is a also a platform for Df .
Proof. On P we have Df = 0, so P is at least contained in a platform Q for Df . If a = min P
exists in I and I ∩ (−∞, a) ̸= ∅, then arbitrarily close to a we have points x < a where f (x) ̸= f (a)
and hence there is a point cx ∈ (x, a) where Df (cx ) = (f (x) − f (a))/(x − a) ̸= 0 and hence cx is
not on the same platform of Df as a. Thus, Q has no points in (−∞, a). Similarly, it has no points
above max P if it exists. Thus, P = Q is a platform of Df .                                          □
Corollary 1.10. Let n be a nonnegative integer. Let I be a nontrivial interval of R, and let
f : I → R be a C n function. Let p ∈ I. Write Pk for the platform of Dk f containing p. Then
P0 ⊆ . . . ⊆ Pn . More specifically, for some initial segment A ⊆ {0, . . . , n} and for some closed
interval P of I of positive length, we have for k = 0, . . . , n that Pk = {p} for k ∈ A and Pk = P
for k ̸∈ A.
   Here A can take the extreme values A = ∅, A = {0, . . . , n}. In the latter case P is irrelevant
since the corollary says nothing about it.

Theorem 1.14 ([2], Theorem A). Let n be a nonnegative integer, m an integer or ∞, with n ≤ m.

Proposition 1.17. Let n be a nonnegative integer, and let f be a polynomial whose derivatives

  (3)g Dj g(x) has the same sign as Dk w(x), x ∈ R, k = 0, 1, 2, 3.
From the properties (1)h – (5)h of w and (1)g – (3)g of g, we see that g is as desired.             □
