## Closest older work, read at the statement/formula level

1. **Toda1936, SectionsII–III, pp313–317.** Kiyoshi Toda, *A Method of Approximation of Convex Functions*, Tohoku Mathematical Journal, First Series42(1936),311–317. Author's original7-page paper is freely served by the journal: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tmj1911/42/0/42_0_311/_pdf/-char/en . It studies the binomial finite difference of test-function means on roots of successive derivatives. On pp315–316 it uses binomial-normalized polynomial coefficients and Waring's formula to compute root moment differences. This is close prior art for the degree-in-the-derivative-index phenomenon in our proof; it must be credited. The paper's TheoremD concerns the leading sign of those differences after local rescaling; TheoremE characterizes a constant-sign property for convex functions. Neither states the2017same-degree odd self-evaluation identity or the zero-degree/apolar boundary in our argument. The article's displayed formulas were inspected in rendered originalPDFpp315–316, rather than relying on incomplete PDF text extraction.

2. **Popoviciu1940, Section11, pp85–86.** Tiberiu Popoviciu, *Notes sur les fonctions convexes d'ordre supérieur (III)*, Mathematica16(1940),74–86. The institute-hosted French original is linked at https://ictp.acad.ro/notes-on-higher-order-convex-functions-iii/ . Define M_h(P) as the average of h over the real root multiset of P. Section11 forms

       F_n h(P)=sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i binom(n,i)M_h(P^(i)).

   It explicitly credits Toda with F_n h(P)=0 for a test polynomial h of degree≤n, in the regime n<degreeP where every mean in the expression is defined. It then refutes a proposed higher-order convexity sign rule. This is a genuinely close, already-published finite-difference/moment result. It is not the same-degree boundary n=degreeP with the zero-degree root mean omitted. The latter is precisely where the proposed proof identifies the polynomial continuation with a self-apolar pairing. The source does not state that endpoint.

3. **Toda1934, currently unread full body.** Kiyoshi Toda, *On Certain Functional Inequalities*, Journal of Science of the Hiroshima University, SeriesA4(1934),27–40. DOI https://doi.org/10.32917/hmj/1558749761 . Both1936Toda and1940Popoviciu cite this work, especially pp39–40 for root-mean finite differences. Its publisher page/PDF endpoint returned an anti-bot HTML page in this session. No bypass was attempted. The fact that the later two sources describe neighboring results does not rule out a relevant additional formula in the1934fullbody. Parent is independently searching legitimate repository/metadata alternatives. This is the most material unresolved novelty risk.

4. **Classical apolarity, explicitly already known.** Giorgio Ottaviani, *Five Lectures on Projective Invariants*, Section4.3, arXiv:1305.2749v1(2013), PDFpp49–50, Proposition18: every odd-degree binary form is apolar to itself. The preceding formula is exactly sum_i(-1)^i binom(D,i)a_i a_(D-i)=0. Primary author manuscript https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.2749 ; accepted version in the author's institutional repository https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c37a-64e6-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/ottaviani.pdf . This terminal coefficient cancellation is wholly classical. No novelty is claimed for it. The proposed contribution is the intervening source-mean/zero-parameter identification and resulting full source-conjecture resolution, if not already published elsewhere.

5. **Source paper's own ingredients.** Wojnar–Wojnar–Brin explicitly use Girard–Waring/Newton formulas, normalized symmetric coefficients and the Appell derivative truncation rule in Section1. They do not claim those ingredients new; neither do we. They compute finite-degree mean relations and conjecture the displayed all-odd-degree relation. Our proof formalizes the Appell rule, the arbitrary-degree normalized moment continuation, the apolar zero endpoint, and the resulting finite difference. It does not establish their other conjectures, the claimed dimension of all relationships, or an exhaustive classification of mean relations.
