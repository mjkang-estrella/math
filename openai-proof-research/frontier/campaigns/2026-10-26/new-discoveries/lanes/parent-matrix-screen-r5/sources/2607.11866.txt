                                            Counterexamples to a multivariable matrix Young conjecture
                                                                                          Zhekai Pang∗



                                                                                            Abstract
                                                        We disprove Conjecture 5.1 of Lin concerning a multivariable extension of Ando’s
                                                     matrix Young inequality for positive semidefinite matrices. For every integer m ≥ 4, the
                                                     conjectured eigenvalue inequality fails at the largest eigenvalue for 2 × 2 real rank-one
                                                     orthogonal projections. We also give a 3 × 3 real positive semidefinite counterexample for
                                                     m = 3, where the failure occurs at the second eigenvalue.

                                           Keywords: Hua determinant inequality; Young type inequality; Rank-one projection.

                                           1        Introduction
                                           Young’s inequality is a fundamental scalar estimate that has prompted a substantial literature
                                           on matrix, norm, and trace analogues. Ando established a two-factor version in the Loewner
                                           order (Ando, 1995). Manjegani studied Hölder and Young inequalities for traces of operators
                                           (Manjegani, 2007), Bhatia and Kittaneh revisited a related matrix arithmetic–geometric mean
                                           problem (Bhatia and Kittaneh, 2008), Košem developed further matrix versions (Košem,
                                           2009), and Drury settled the singular-value question posed by Bhatia and Kittaneh (Drury,
                                           2012).
                                              Let Mn denote the set of n by n complex matrices. For X ∈ Mn , write

                                                                                        |X| = (X ∗ X)1/2 .

                                           For Hermitian matrices X and Y , the notation X ⪯ Y means that Y − X is positive
                                           semidefinite. If A, B ∈ Mn and p, q > 1 satisfy 1/p + 1/q = 1, Ando’s matrix Young inequality
                                           asserts that there is a unitary matrix U such that
                                                                                                   1 p 1 q
                                                                                  U |AB ∗ |U ∗ ⪯     |A| + |B| .                                  (1)
                                                                                                   p      q
                                           Equivalently, each singular value of AB ∗ is bounded by the corresponding eigenvalue of the
                                           matrix on the right.
                                               Multivariable inequalities of this type are more delicate because the order of the factors
                                           matters. Liu, Poon and Wang (2017) obtained a generalized Hölder eigenvalue inequality
                                           for products of contractive matrices. More recently, Lin (2024) proved a version of Young’s
                                           inequality in which the summands on the right may be independently unitarily conjugated.
                                           In the same paper, Lin proposed a stronger conjecture for positive semidefinite matrices, with
                                           no unitary conjugations on the right. A determinant-level consequence was already known
                                           from Manjegani (2007), but the coordinatewise eigenvalue inequalities were left open.
                                               This paper shows that Lin’s conjecture fails for every m ≥ 3. For m ≥ 4, we give a uniform
                                           family of 2 × 2 counterexamples consisting of rank-one orthogonal projections. The case
