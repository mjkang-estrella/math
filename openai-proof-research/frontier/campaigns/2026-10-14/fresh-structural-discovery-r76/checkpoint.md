## Concrete operation tested

Source 288 gives a universal scalar-to-matrix commutator estimate. Could it yield a new simultaneous correction that changes a bounded representation into a *-representation while retaining an independently prescribed commuting *-representation? The unrestricted answer is already negative by a precise primary antecedent, even with completely bounded input maps. There is no full new theorem here.

The stipulated source is the pinned *Kadison’s similarity theorem through uniform derivation estimates*, revision fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb. Its introduction, Theorem `thm:universal`, states that a numerical C bounds every finite amplification of ad(Y) on every represented unital von Neumann algebra by C times its scalar norm. There are no dimension, separability, trace, nuclearity or faithfulness restrictions. Proposition `prop:all-derivations` bounds the cb norm of every bounded derivation in a *-representation by C times its norm. The similarity theorem supplies an ambient invertible implementer; it does not promise an implementer in the von Neumann algebra generated by the image.

## The paid transfer

Arveson’s commutant-distance identity, explicitly used in the source’s final subsection, gives dist(T,P') <= (C/2) sup_{a in P_1} ||[T,a]||. Kaplansky density permits the same scalar test for a represented C*-algebra and its weak closure.

For unital C*-algebras A,B on the same H with unit-ball Hausdorff distance delta, and T in A'_1, approximation of b in B_1 by a in A_1 gives ||[T,b]|| <= 2 delta (with an arbitrarily small slack when the infimum is not attained). Thus dist(T,B') <= C delta. If Z in B' approximates T, replacing Z by Z/max(1,||Z||) costs at most the first approximation error. Consequently d(A',B') <= 2 C d(A,B), after also reversing A,B. This is a direct transfer, not a new mechanism.

Cameron, Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White and Wiggins, *A remark on the similarity and perturbation problems*, arXiv:1206.5405v1, already give exactly this universal Lipschitz implication as equation (2), with 4K and property D_K. Their Remark 2 treats reversed near inclusions of commutants. Remark 3 explicitly identifies equivalence of ordinary and completely bounded Kadison–Kastler metrics with the similarity problem and discusses K-theoretic/Cuntz-semigroup consequences. These operations are unavailable as substantial nominations here merely because the pinned source pays their antecedent.

## Why simultaneous correction is not an available upgrade

The attempted full statement would require that, for arbitrary unital C*-algebras A,B and commuting unital bounded homomorphisms u:A->B(H), v:B->B(H), there be one invertible S making both conjugated maps *-homomorphisms. Even the case where v is already a *-homomorphism is stronger than individual similarity.

Pisier, *Simultaneous similarity, bounded generation and amenability*, arXiv:math/0508223v2, Theorem 1, equates nuclearity of A with this simultaneous property for all B and all completely bounded u,v. Its condition (iv) already assumes v is a *-homomorphism. Thus for each nonnuclear A that universal property fails with completely bounded u and a *-representation v. Those individual maps already satisfy the ordinary similarity premise without source 288. Applying the new source to each map separately cannot establish the false simultaneous conclusion.

The precise obstruction is an internal-implementer constraint. After polar decomposition a positive simultaneous implementer must commute with every unitary v(b): if S>0 and SUS^{-1} is unitary, then U*S^2 U=S^2, hence SU=US. Taking B to be the commutant of the von Neumann algebra generated by u(A) forces S into that generated algebra. Pisier’s Theorem 1(ii) says that availability of this stronger implementation for all completely bounded u characterizes nuclearity. This is the same operation, not a merely adjacent title match. His proof gives the reduction (iv)->(ii) explicitly.

No nuclear special case is proposed as a rescue: Theorem 1(i)->(iii) already proves it using the delta tensor norm. No claim is made that the source’s scalar estimate is false, or that its ambient implementers fail to exist. The stipulated source theorem is not audited.
