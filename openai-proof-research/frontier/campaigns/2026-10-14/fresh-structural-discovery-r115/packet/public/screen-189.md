# 189: extremal-order cycle/clique structure

This is a prefreeze investigation whose provisional full question is excluded by a known small graph; it is not a theorem claim. The provisional full question was whether, for all integers m≥n≥3 other than (3,3), every C_m-free graph on (m−1)(n−1) vertices with independence number at most n−1 has a spanning collection of n−1 disjoint K_(m−1)'s. Cross-edges between those cliques are not forbidden by this formulation. Neither this statement nor a star-critical consequence was frozen. No dependence on a large-parameter cutoff is proposed as a replacement.

The source189 proof is stipulated. Write k=m−1 and a=n−1. Its reduction `red:smaller`/`red:expansion` works at ka+1 vertices: the smaller-independence Ramsey bound supplies |N[I]|≥k|I|+1, and in particular minimum degree at least k. At ka vertices, the corresponding deletion calculation supplies only |N[I]|≥k|I| and minimum degree at least k−1 (in the smaller-parameter range where that Ramsey invocation applies). This is a change in input to a true source operation, not an objection to the source.

The initial clique-layer growth (`clq:bound`, `clq:triangle`, `clq:layer-interface`) uses the strict reserve. I therefore tested the more compatible operation in `06-paths.tex`, rather than stopping at this first mismatch. Its `path:interval` is independent of that reserve: for a clique Q of size t and h=k+1−t, a linear forest of paths with disjoint interiors outside Q, q interior vertices and v incident clique vertices cannot have h≤q≤k+1−v, because completion within Q would give a forbidden cycle. The optimal forest has total interior length L<h and is chosen with fewest paths subject to maximizing L. With S its union with Q, |S|=t+L≤k. `path:optimality` controls changes to that forest.

The next operation, `path:exterior`, uses minimum degree k to guarantee at least k+1−|S|≥1 exterior neighbors for a relevant vertex. Under the extremal-order degree bound this becomes k−|S|. At |S|=k it may be zero. `terminal:increment` can absorb supplied representative connections, but it does not create an exterior vertex in this exhausted case. I did not prove that the exhausted set S is a removable clique, nor that different exhausted sets partition all vertices. Those are exact missing steps; their absence is not a counterexample to the proposed factor statement.

## Final primary-figure check: the provisional full question is false

After the comparisons above, and still before any freeze, the later exceptional cases in the same Jayawardene–Narváez–Radziszowski paper were inspected. Lemma5/Lemma6 and Figure6, page8, include R_(9,5). Its apex belongs to no triangle. The provisional (m,n)=(4,4) case therefore fails. The earlier descriptions of missing boundary arguments remain records of attempted routes, not the final disposition of the full provisional question. No exception-deleted target is frozen or nominated.

Here is a direct verification independent of trusting the picture's classification. Name the apex a; name the next left/right vertices b,c; the next left/right vertices d,e; and the four bottom vertices, from left to right, f,g,h,i. The edges read from Figure6 are

    ab, ac, bd, bi, ce, cf, df, dg, fg, eh, ei, hi, gh.

The graph consists, after deleting a, of triangles {d,f,g} and {e,h,i}, joined by the three internally disjoint links d-b-i, f-c-e, and g-h. A four-cycle avoiding a would have to use two of these links and at least one edge within each triangle. Even the shortest pair of links has total length three, so such a cycle has length at least five. A four-cycle through a would require a common neighbor of b and c other than a; their other neighborhoods {d,i} and {e,f} are disjoint. Thus there is no C4.

An independent set containing a omits b,c and takes at most one vertex of each triangle, so has size at most three. An independent four-set omitting a would have to contain b,c and one vertex of each triangle. Avoiding b,c forces those two triangle vertices to be g,h, which are adjacent. Thus α≤3; {a,d,e} shows equality. Finally N(a)={b,c} and bc is not an edge, so no triangle contains a and no triangle factor spans all nine vertices. This is exactly the order and independence bound of the provisional (4,4) case.

This is a classical exclusion and a correction to the initial unresolved assessment, not a new research finding. The source189 exact Ramsey theorem remains consistent: it concerns the next vertex count. The figure and its reading extent are private primary evidence. No method obstruction was promoted into a refutation; the explicit graph refutes the actual provisional full statement.
