# Operation-level contribution comparison

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

The substantial unpaid operation remains unchanged: a finite-field all-degree
self-orthogonal nonprojective module over a finite symmetric algebra, or a
universal refutation. None of the following supporting operations is nominated
as a substantial theorem or as new to world literature.

The pinned main construction uses actual invertible twist bimodules fixing X,
a derived-induction bimodule with a proved two-degree mixed-Hom profile, and
a trace-duality proof that ordinary twist-to-fiber maps evaluate nontrivially.
The triangular full End comparison is then invertible in the needed positive
and negative ranges. The ordinary trivial extension transfers BOTH ranges.
This round does not merely replace its scalar rows: it changes the starting
algebra and objects and independently recalculates the entire bridge.

The new test operation uses restriction bimodules between explicit small
symmetric F_2-algebras. Their left and right freeness is exhibited; their
cohomology projections and negative zero action are proved from resolutions
and stable maps. The triangular algebra, finite module, total acyclicity,
cone comparison, symmetric extension, module dimension and self-Ext dimensions
are all supplied. Its failure is the unbounded comparison kernel, including
the negative range. Those exact dimensions are a supporting checkpoint on
this choice of data, not evidence that arbitrary restriction-based or other
side-projective constructions must fail. Sparse fibers cannot fix these
specific branches; non-sparse fibers and other objects remain open.

EHSST Definition 2.1/Proposition 2.2/Theorem 2.3 already provide actual
side-projective Hochschild pushout bimodules and characterize their splitting
after evaluation. Their operator cuts alter the object and do not prescribe
an arbitrary endomorphism of its original self-Hom. Their Corollary 1.3
already gives the relevant finite-generation obstruction. Our short direct
proof records its exact hypotheses and shows why it does not cover all FT2.
No finite-field finite-generation theorem is inferred.

Bergh--Sandoy--Solberg Theorem 2.2's actual support-moving construction uses
invertible automorphism twists. Its general failure of tensor-support
intersection does not supply a noninvertible action, a fixed evaluated
object or the source's lift/fiber. It also prevents using unsupported
support-intersection rules to prove an impossibility for all bimodules.

Linckelmann Section 2 already supplies the perfect composition duality used
in the socle-detection checkpoint. The deduction that a functor fixing a
stable brick and nonzero on H^{-1} is injective in every degree is elementary
from that pairing. It is a necessary constraint on a replacement, not a
classification of exact functors or a new duality theorem. The explicit
restriction factorization also shows why a trace-preservation assumption
for arbitrary exact functors would be false.

Bohmler--Marczinzik's finite-field Theorem 3.1 has two self-Ext vanishings
and explicitly nonzero third self-Ext. The earlier exact-zero-divisor part
has all-degree Tor vanishing for two distinct modules. Neither meets FT2.
Their numerical program was not rerun or promoted to an all-degree argument.

R14's finite windows, trace restriction, finite counting, orbit product and
failed named H=0 bimodule remain supporting results with their original
limits. No new uniform F_2-dimension bound is established. No restricted
classification, fixed coefficient reduction or growing syzygy argument
has been recast as a full answer. Full FT2 is unresolved after this round.
