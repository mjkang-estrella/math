## 3. The exact summability mechanism under mixing

Fix the phases and fractional suspension time. There are only finitely many offset patterns. The three old output groups give real functions u_l, v_l, w_l on independent probability spaces Omega_1, Omega_2, Omega_3, each bounded by one. Every group in the remaining case has a centered occurrence. A selector event indexed by (k,c) has probability p=p_{k,c}. Its unnormalized output density c_{k,c}=p rho_{k,c} satisfies

  0 <= c_{k,c} <= 1,  integral c_{k,c}=p,
  E[c_{k,c} | any two groups] = p.

The full family also satisfies sum_{k,c} c_{k,c}=1 almost everywhere, for fixed selector a and phases. These are actual additional constraints, not merely separate L2 bounds. They hold because the initial spatial coordinate is independent of every proper set of groups.

Put m=floor(c 2^k/h), z_l=u_l tensor v_l, C_m=sum_{l<m} z_l z_l^*, and let P_m retain d_m leading covariance eigenvectors. The projected part of the selected trilinear average is at most p alpha_m, where

  alpha_m = m^-1 sum_{l<m} ||P_m z_l||.

This uses precisely the unchanged TWO-group and ONE-group marginals in Cauchy–Schwarz. For the residual, tr(C_m)<=m gives a remaining spectral ceiling m/d_m. The Gram matrix of w has trace at most m. Consequently its product-law average has L2 norm at most d_m^-1/2; pairing with c_{k,c}, whose L2 norm is at most sqrt(p), gives sqrt(p/d_m).

Higher-order mixing supplies bounded-degree exceptional correlation graphs at each FIXED eta, after finitely many initial l. The source packing theorem bounds alpha_m by

  epsilon + C(1+L)/(m epsilon^2)
      exp(C epsilon^-2 sqrt(d_m) log(2+d_m)) + o(1).

Take d_m=ceil((1+log m)^(3/2)). The exponential is m^o(1), whereas sum_{k,c} 1/d_{m(k,c)} converges. Summing the projected terms uses sum p=1, and summing the residuals gives

  sum_{k>=a,c} sqrt(p_{k,c}/d_{m(k,c)})
    <= (sum_{k>=a,c} 1/d_{m(k,c)})^(1/2) -> 0.

All thresholds may depend on the fixed tuple, epsilon, offsets and speeds. Epsilon and eta are fixed before m increases. This is why the proof needs no mixing rate. It already pays arbitrary selector probabilities; treating its residual one event at a time would miss the principal operation.

## 4. The weak-mixing reduction is stronger than merely sparse bad pairs

Several source steps survive replacing mixing by weak mixing, without a new estimate. Finite products of nonzero powers are weakly mixing. The source's relative-norm lemma over the trivial factor gives the product limit in L2 for every finite set of distinct nonzero linear slopes; its proof uses the Cesaro conditional-correlation criterion, not pointwise decay. The selected-law, lower-slot, relative-disjointness and saturation steps do not require mixing of the auxiliary systems.

The proper-group part of closure also survives. A coordinate expectation is

  r_l = integral product_i f_i(T^(e i l+a_i)x) dmu,

with a centered occurrence. On X x X, write F_i(x,y)=f_i(T^a_i x) f_i(T^a_i y). A centered occurrence gives integral F_i=0. The same relative-norm lemma for T x T gives m^-1 sum r_l^2 ->0, hence m^-1 sum |r_l| ->0. This bound is uniform over the tail of all averaging lengths by the definition of convergence, and there are finitely many offsets. Conditioning on the initial coordinate therefore causes no loss in this proper-group case.

There is also a useful full tensor-square control. Absorb the integer output speeds into the weakly mixing product transformation S=product_gamma T^(e_gamma) on one group's independent coordinates. Then

  u_l = product_i G_i(S^(i l) y),

with offsets absorbed into G_i and at least one mean-zero G_i. Apply the norm lemma on the doubled product system to G_i(y)G_i(y'). It gives

  rho_u(m) := m^-2 sum_{l,t<m} <u_l,u_t>^2
            = ||m^-1 sum_{l<m} u_l tensor u_l||^2 ->0.

The same holds for v and w. This is an actual proved composition of the relative-norm lemma, not an assumed multivariate mixing theorem. For z_l=u_l tensor v_l, let

  r_m = m^-2 sum_{l,t<m} <z_l,z_t>^2.

Since all inner products have modulus at most one, r_m<=min(rho_u(m),rho_v(m)), so r_m->0. These statements concern fixed data and provide no rate.

## 5. Two integrated spectral repairs, and their remaining scale cost

First use the source packing estimate after pruning high-degree vertices. At a fixed eta, the union of the two bad-pair graphs has at most

  m^2 (rho_u(m)+rho_v(m))/eta^2

ordered edges. Delete vertices of degree exceeding theta_m m. The deleted fraction is at most (rho_u+rho_v)/(eta^2 theta_m), and the remaining maximum degree is at most theta_m m. Therefore, with E_epsilon(d)=exp(C epsilon^-2 sqrt(d) log(2+d)), one obtains

  alpha_m <= epsilon + (rho_u+rho_v)/(eta^2 theta_m)
               + C epsilon^-2 (m^-1+theta_m) E_epsilon(d_m).

The average is normalized by the original m, so the deleted vectors cost exactly their fraction. Balancing theta_m gives the schematic requirement (rho_u+rho_v) E_epsilon(d_m)->0 (with epsilon,eta fixed), in addition to E_epsilon(d_m)/m->0. An arbitrarily slow diverging d_m can meet this pointwise-in-m condition. It need not satisfy the selector residual sum.

Second, pay the stronger covariance Hilbert–Schmidt estimate directly, without any exceptional graph. Since ||C_m||_HS=m sqrt(r_m), for a rank-d projection P,

  m^-1 sum ||P z_l||^2 = tr(P C_m)/m
       <= sqrt(d) sqrt(r_m),

and hence alpha_m <= (d r_m)^(1/4). For leading spectral P the same residual calculation as in section 3 is compatible. Thus the complete selected estimate available from this repair is

  sum_{k,c} p_{k,c} (d_{m(k,c)} r_{m(k,c)})^(1/4)
    + (sum_{k,c} 1/d_{m(k,c)})^(1/2).

This is stronger than declaring that the bounded-degree graph is missing. It gives a sufficient scale tradeoff. It still does not close from r_m->0 alone. For example, the abstract allowance r_{2^k}=1/log(k+2) would require d_{2^k}=o(log k) in this bound, which is incompatible with sum_k 1/d_{2^k}<infinity. This scalar profile is only a diagnostic for the ESTIMATE; no dynamical realization, impossibility theorem, or counterexample to weak-mixing convergence is claimed.

Using exact eigenvalues instead of the trace ceiling can help particular systems. But a sequence of nearly flat covariance spectra with slowly increasing effective rank would retain the same summation issue. No theorem here rules such spectra in or out in the required dynamics. A uniform spectral improvement cannot be assumed from qualitative norm convergence.

## 6. Probability normalization, scale blocking, and the actual joint-density constraint

One cannot claim closure merely by choosing d_m after inspecting p. For any nonnegative residual ceilings beta_k, the exact supremum of sum sqrt(p_k) beta_k over finite probability vectors is the l2 norm of beta. Even adding the trivial event bound p_k does not solve this when sum beta_k^2 diverges: choose a finite tail block with S=sum beta_k^2>=1 and p_k=beta_k^2/S. Then sum min(p_k,sqrt(p_k) beta_k)=1. This shows only that those upper bounds do not force vanishing; it does not construct actual events attaining them. It correctly distinguishes a failure of bounds from a theorem counterexample.

There is no selector-entropy bound in the failure construction. A measurable first-witness selection may spread its law over arbitrarily many late scales. Truncating each selector distribution to finite support pays its tail probability for that selector, but the retained number and maximum scale can grow with a. Finite-support approximation by itself gives no bound uniform in a. Renormalizing each event destroys its small p factor; reweighting cannot eliminate the l2 issue.

Thinning to a sparse good subsequence of scales is also insufficient: the length-change estimate needs a grid with relative gaps tending to zero. A qualitative norm-convergence subsequence may have unbounded ratios. Grouping long blocks of dyadic scales must control the maximum inside each block; endpoint convergence alone does not do this.

The joint density constraint sum c_k=1 is stronger than separate L2 bounds and must not be discarded as if no repair were possible. It yields the crude maximal domination

  sum_k |integral c_k A_{m_k}| <= integral sup_{k>=a} |A_{m_k}|,
  A_m = m^-1 sum_{l<m} u_l v_l w_l.

An L1 vanishing tail maximum would close, but is not furnished by the norm lemma. It would already be a pointwise nonconventional assertion on a product weakly mixing system. Invoking the desired general result there would be circular.

There is a second possible joint-density repair: c_k>=0 and sum c_k=1 imply pointwise total variation sum |c_{k+1}-c_k|<=2. Thus a rejection based only on 'arbitrarily varying inputs' would be too weak. The triple companion's actual rows.tex, lem:row-maximal and lem:row-depth, was read with its proof. It DOES handle bounded-variation schedules with constants independent of the number of depths, provided one has a common Bessel family, persistent columns with private-coordinate lower bounds, and support/observation partitions that assign each entire row before the next stop.

Those hypotheses are not supplied for the covariance projections P_m above. Leading eigenspaces at different m need not be nested, and separate spectral trace bounds do not give a joint Bessel bound on the same arbitrary input. Simply replacing P_m by the span of earlier leading spaces can increase rank to sum_{j<=k}d_{2^j}; with the source choice d_{2^j} about j^(3/2), this is about k^(5/2), whose packing exponential no longer fits m=2^k. This is a calculation for that specific nested-union construction, not a proof that every persistent reorganization fails. Arbitrarily fine finite measurable partitions also do not provide uniform column budgets or a joint operator bound. No analogue satisfying these requirements has been built here.

  n^-1 sum_{m<n} |integral F(T^(pm)x) g(x) dmu| -> 0
