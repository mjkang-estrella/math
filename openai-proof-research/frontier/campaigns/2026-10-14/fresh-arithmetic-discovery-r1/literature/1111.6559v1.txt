                                                       SÁRKÖZY’S THEOREM FOR P-INTERSECTIVE POLYNOMIALS

                                                                                                   ALEX RICE


                                                  Abstract. We obtain an estimate on the size of the largest subset of {1, 2, ..., N } with no nonzero differences
                                                  of the form h(p) for any prime p, where h is in the largest possible class of polynomials.

   Just as there are intersective polynomials without integer roots, it seems natural to think that a result
analogous to Theorem D should hold for a larger class of polynomials. A moments consideration indicates
that the correct analogue to the intersective condition on a polynomial h when looking for differences of the
form h(p) is to insist that h not only has a root modulo q for every q ∈ N, but has a root at a congruence
class that admits infinitely many primes, leading to the following definition.
Definition 1. A polynomial h ∈ Z[x] is called P-intersective if for every q ∈ N, there exists r ∈ Z such that
(r, q) = 1 and h(r) ≡ 0 mod q. Equivalently, for every p ∈ P, there exists zp ∈ Zp , where Zp denotes the
p-adic integers, such that h(zp ) = 0 and zp 6≡ 0 mod p.

   Examples of P-intersective polynomials include any polynomial with a root at 1 or −1, any polynomial
with two rational roots a/b and c/d such that (ab, cd) = 1, and presumably there are lots more. The necessity
of this condition is almost as clear as that of the original intersective condition. To exhibit this, suppose we
have h ∈ Z[x] and q ∈ N such that the only roots of h modulo q share common factors with q. In particular,
there is at most one prime r such that h(r) is divisble by q. Letting m = h(r)/q if such a prime exists
and m = 0 otherwise, we see that the set q(m + 1)N is a set of positive upper density which contains no
differences of the form h(p). Here we show that this condition is also sufficient, borrowing primarily from [8]
and [6] to obtain the following quantitative result.

2. Preliminaries: Auxiliary Polynomials, the Fourier Transform, and the Circle Method

   To begin our effort to prove Theorem 1, we fix a P-intersective polynomial h with deg(h) = k and an
arbitrary ǫ > 0. By the symmetry of difference sets we can assume without loss of generality that h has
positive leading term. We will use the letters C and c to denote appropriately large or small constants, which
we will allow to depend on h and ǫ.

2.1. Auxiliary Polynomials. As previously mentioned, we will apply the modified density increment strat-
egy described in [8], which allows for the polynomial to change at each stage of the iteration. The following
definitions describe all of the polynomials that we could potentially encounter.
Definition 2. For each p ∈ P, we fix zp ∈ Zp with h(zp ) = 0 and zp 6≡ 0 mod p. By reducing and applying
the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the choices of zp determine, for each natural number d > 1, a unique
integer rd ∈ (−d, 0], which consequently satisfies h(rd ) ≡ 0 mod d and (rd , d) = 1. We also take r1 = 0.
We define the function λ on N by letting λ(p) = pm , where m is the multiplicity of zp as a root of h in Zp ,
and then extending it to be completely multiplicative.
For each d ∈ N, we define the auxiliary polynomial hd ∈ Z[x] by hd (x) = h(rd + dx)/λ(d). Further, for
N ∈ N, we define
