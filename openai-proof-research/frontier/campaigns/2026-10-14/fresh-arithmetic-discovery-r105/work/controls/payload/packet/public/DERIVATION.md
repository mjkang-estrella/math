## 2. The commuting root subgroup is available in the original presentation

We first justify a compatibility detail rather than assume the source group is literally a familiar matrix group. Distinct U-types commute by a defining relation. The same-type commutation also follows from those relations.

For i≠j, conjugation by W_ji(s) fixes U_i(t), while

    W_ji(s) U_j(r) W_ji(s)^(-1) = U_j(r) U_i(r+s).

Indeed [U_j(r),W_ji(s)]=U_i(r+s), and these U elements commute and square to1. Conjugating the relation [U_j(r),U_i(t)]=1 therefore gives [U_i(r+s),U_i(t)]=1. As r,s,t vary, all elements U_i(a) commute, even at the same index. Thus the subgroup generated by all U_i(a) is elementary abelian. This deduction uses no unstated relation among W's and no identification of G with its finite matrix image.

The analogous argument using [W_ij(s),V_j(r)]=V_i(r+s) and that W_ij fixes V_i(t) shows that all V_i(a) commute as well. We do **not** assert that these V elements have order2 in G. Only their invertibility is needed below.

Let L=F_2[t,t^(-1)]. For f=sum f_a t^a define U_i(f)=product U_i(a)^(f_a). This is a homomorphism from the additive group of L. For g=sum g_b t^b, choose V_i(g)=product V_i(b)^(g_b); no additive mod2 identity for V is needed. Since the cross commutators are central, on a representation with z_n=(-1)^(c_n) we have

    [U_i(f),V_j(g)] = (-1)^(delta_ij ell(fg)) I,
    ell(t^n)=c_n.                                             (2)

Here ell:L→F_2 is the linear functional determined by the arbitrary bilateral binary sequence c.

## 3. Finite Hankel rank and exact dimension divisibility

Define

    I_c = { f in L : ell(fg)=0 for every g in L },
    h(c) = dim_F2(L/I_c),

allowing infinite dimension. I_c is an ideal: ell((af)g)=ell(f(ag))=0 for every a,g. The matrix of the pairing in the Laurent monomial bases is (c_(a+b))_(a,b in Z). Consequently h(c) equals the supremum of the ranks of all its finite submatrices. To see the infinite-dimensional statement directly, any finite independent collection of functionals ell(f_a -) can be separated by finitely many test vectors g_b; conversely every finite submatrix is an image of that pairing.

Suppose V is a nonzero finite-dimensional complex G-representation with those scalar central signs. For every integer h0≤h(c), choose f_1,...,f_h0 and g_1,...,g_h0 in L so that ell(f_a g_b)=delta_ab. Such choices follow by finite-dimensional linear algebra on a nonsingular pairing minor and row/column operations.

The 3h0 commuting involutions U_i(f_a) are simultaneously diagonalizable over C. Decompose V into their simultaneous sign eigenspaces. Equation(2) says that V_i(g_b) flips precisely the sign indexed by (i,b) and preserves the other signs. Each V_i(g_b) is invertible. Starting from any nonzero weight space, all 2^(3h0) weight spaces therefore occur and have equal dimension. In particular,

    2^(3h0) divides dim_C V.                                  (3)

If h(c)=infinity this is impossible for a fixed positive finite dimension. If h(c)=h<infinity, it proves divisibility by 2^(3h), and thus the required lower bound. This proof covers reducible and nonsemisimple G-representations. Only the finite elementary abelian U-subgroup is diagonalized, not G.

This is precisely the finite weight-space operation in the Stone–von Neumann–Mackey proof: the actual proof in Prasad, arXiv:0912.0574v1, §4.2, decomposes into abelian weights and uses translations/modulations to permute them transitively with equal dimensions. Our finite split-involution proof makes its applicability independent of any assumption that the whole G-representation is unitary. The three source root pairs contribute the exponent3. This is a compatible classical reduction, not an unpaid obstruction.

## 4. Matching representations and the bilateral classification

Assume 0<h(c)=h<infinity, and put S=L/I_c. This is a finite commutative F_2-algebra of cardinality 2^h; the image of t is a unit. The functional ell descends to S, and the pairing ell(ab) on S is nondegenerate by the definition of I_c. No reducedness, field, odd-order or squarefree-polynomial assumption is used.

On the complex vector space with basis {v_x:x in S^3}, use the following operators:

    tau_a(v_x) = v_(x+a),
    mu_b(v_x) = (-1)^ell(b dot x) v_x,
    L_A(v_x) = v_(Ax),                  A in GL_3(S).

All are signed permutation matrices. The identities needed are

    [tau_a,mu_b] = (-1)^ell(b dot a) I,
    L_A tau_a L_A^(-1) = tau_(Aa),
    L_A mu_b L_A^(-1) = mu_(A^(-T)b).                         (4)

Assign the original generators as follows, with standard column vectors e_i:

    U_i(r) -> tau_(t^r e_i),
    V_i(r) -> mu_(t^r e_i),
    W_ij(r) -> L_(I+t^r E_ji),
    T_i -> L_(diag(1,...,t^(-1),...,1)).                      (5)

The reversed matrix indices E_ji in W are intentional. Verify every defining relation from source section02:

* The T's commute. Their conjugation gives weights -e_i for U_i, +e_i for V_i and e_i-e_j for W_ij, exactly the original weights.
* All U_i(r) square to1. Different U-types and V-types commute; U_i and V_j commute for i≠j by (4).
* W_ij fixes U_h for h≠i and V_h for h≠j.
* Since A=I+t^s E_ji has A^(-1)=A in characteristic2, (4) gives [U_i(r),W_ij(s)]=U_j(r+s) and [W_ij(s),V_j(r)]=V_i(r+s).

There are no additional W–W defining relations to check. Thus (5) gives an actual representation of the original presented group, not only a projective representation of a quotient. Its z_n is (-1)^ell(t^n)I, as required, and its dimension is 2^(3h). Together with (3), this is the least possible dimension. It is irreducible: any nonzero invariant subspace has the same central signs and hence already has dimension at least 2^(3h). All matrices have rational entries0,1,-1. For c=0, take the one-dimensional trivial representation; h=0 and all conclusions remain valid.

There is also an elementary equivalent description of finite h. If h<infinity, multiplication by t is an invertible linear operator on the finite F_2-space S. This operator has finite order q≥1, so t^q=1 on S and c_(n+q)=c_n for every integer n. Conversely, if c is bilateral periodic of period q, then t^q-1 belongs to I_c and dim L/I_c≤q. Thus

    finite-dimensional scalar-sign representation exists
      iff h(c)<infinity iff c is periodic on all of Z.         (6)

This is not a classification of all finite-dimensional representations of G. It classifies the scalar central sign sequences and their minimum dimensions. It also does not assert that the period equals h; for example a recurrence can have period much larger than its dimension.

The possible even-order Weil concern does not leave an unpaid step. Dutta–Prasad 1010.3528v2 §1.3 uses an odd-order Weyl normalization and a general projective symplectic action; that normalization cannot simply be imported here. But our T,W all preserve the translation polarization and have the explicit genuine permutation operators L_A. Equation(4) verifies the entire needed action, including nonreduced S, without dividing by2 or extending to the full symplectic group. Failure of the odd-order shortcut therefore supplies no contribution by itself.

## 5. Exact extinction cost for all finite R-modules

For a nonzero finite R-module Y, the commuting central involutions z_n admit a simultaneous decomposition

    Y = direct sum_c Y_c

with finitely many nonzero sectors. To justify simultaneous decomposition of an infinite family, diagonalize its finite-dimensional commutative image algebra: commuting diagonalizable involutions can successively refine eigenspaces only finitely many times. Equivalently, the algebra they span lies in a finite-dimensional diagonal algebra. Every sector is G-invariant because the z_n are central.

By (6), each occurring sequence c is periodic. For c≠0, let m(c)≥1 be the first index with c_(m(c)-1)=1; it exists because the sequence is nonzero and bilateral periodic. Formula(1) preserves this sector until step m(c), then kills it. For c=0 it never kills the sector.

A sharp dimension estimate uses only its initial signs. If c_0=...=c_(m-2)=0 and c_(m-1)=1, the m by m Hankel submatrix (c_(a+b)) for 0≤a,b<m has zeros below the first anti-diagonal and ones on it. Reversing columns makes it triangular with diagonal1, so its determinant over F_2 is1. Thus h(c)≥m. Equation(3) yields

    dim Y_c >= 2^(3h(c)) >= 2^(3m(c)).                        (7)

Let d=dim Y and L_d=floor(log_2 d/3). Every nontrivial sector dies by step L_d. The remaining underlying subspace is exactly

    Y_triv = { y : z_n y=y for every n in Z },
    H^j(Y) = alpha^(j)*Y_triv for every j>=L_d.                (8)

This includes L_d=0: when d<8 no nontrivial central sign sequence occurs. The R-action in (8) is twisted, so it does not claim eventual periodicity of all module isomorphism classes.

If Y has exact finite extinction m, one surviving sector at stage m-1 must have first1 at m-1. Hence dim Y≥2^(3m). The bound is attained: take the periodic impulse sequence c_n=1 exactly when n≡m-1 (mod m). It has rank at most m by periodicity and at least m by the anti-diagonal minor. The construction(5), equivalently with S=F_2[t]/(t^m-1) and ell taking the coefficient of t^(m-1), has dimension2^(3m) and exact extinction m. This works also for m=1 and for even m, when S can be nonreduced. Therefore

    min{dim Y : H^(m-1)Y !=0, H^mY=0} = 2^(3m),  m>=1.       (9)

In particular, (8) provides a finite central test: Y_triv is the image of e_0...e_(L_d-1) on Y. Computing at most floor(log_2 d/3) central matrices suffices once the representation is given. No cost model for evaluating the group words is suppressed: the formula describes the number of central tests, not a proved bit-complexity bound for arbitrary complex inputs.

The old arithmetic r13 proof gave a connected-component bound for the much more general finitely presented-algebra/endomorphism interface. Its scope stays unchanged. Equations(6)–(9) give an exact source-specific calculation, not a replacement for that general statement or an undecidability claim.

## 6. Strongest compatible homological transfer

Fix the source realization choices once. Retain its three-vertex B, bounded right-projective bimodule complex P, interval [a_*,b_*] with b_*=b≥0, radical-square-zero chain algebra C_chain, E=B tensor C_chain, D=B×E, off-diagonal bimodules O,T, X=O⊕T, and A=D⋉X. Let g=max(2,gldim E), which is finite. All these choices precede Y and m. M(Y) has Y at each of three vertices, so dim M(Y)=3 dim Y.

Write F=X tensor_D^L -. The source's actual objectwise splitting and its ordinary simulation give

    F^(2j) M(Y)
      = direct sum_(q=0)^j M(H^jY)[jb+3q]^(binom(j,q)).        (10)

The ordinary right-B-projective O makes the odd iterates equally explicit:

    F^(2j+1) M(Y)
      = direct sum_(q=0)^j Q_j[jb+3q]^(binom(j,q)),
    Q_j=O tensor_B M(H^jY), supported on E.                   (11)

There is no derived-versus-ordinary ambiguity in Q_j. It is an ordinary E-module, possibly zero. Neither (10) nor (11) needs a splitting natural in Y.

The strongest already available formula is Minamoto–Yamaura 1710.01469v1 Corollary4.11, with Proposition2.5/Lemma3.6 and passage to opposite algebras for the source's left convention:

    pd_A N = sup_(r>=0) (pd_D(F^rN)+r)                       (12)

for inflated D-modules N. Its proof chain through Theorem4.10, Lemmas4.13–4.14 and the graded-projective decomposition was read. It also follows directly here from the source's bar decomposition. Indeed, with S0=D/radD=A/radA,

    S0 tensor_A^L N
      = direct sum_(r>=0) (S0 tensor_D^L F^rN)[r].            (13)

Minimal projective resolutions over the finite-dimensional algebras identify projective dimension with the largest nonvanishing Tor degree against S0, also for each bounded finite complex. Direct sums do not cancel cohomology. Formula(12) follows with pd_D(0)=-infinity and pd_D(Z[s])=pd_D Z+s. This credits rather than improves the primary result.

If Y_triv≠0, (8) and (10) make every even iterate nonzero, hence pd_A M(Y)=infinity by the source lower bound. If Y_triv=0 and m is its exact extinction time, every iterate r≥2m vanishes and (12) becomes finite. In (10)–(11), for each j<m the largest shift is jb+3j. Thus

    pd_A M(Y)
     = max_(0<=j<m) { (b+5)j
          + max(pd_B M(H^jY), 1+pd_E Q_j) }.                 (14)

The first term is finite and between0 and2. The second term is absent if Q_j=0; otherwise it is at most g+1. Consequently

    (b+5)(m-1) <= pd_A M(Y)
                    <= (b+5)(m-1)+g+1.                      (15)

Together with (9), this gives encoded witnesses of dimension 3*2^(3m), and an upper logarithmic scale for **every encoded module** of finite projective dimension:

    pd_A M(Y) <= (b+5)(floor(log_2(dim Y)/3)-1)+g+1.          (16)

Here the nonzero finite-pd assumption implies dim Y≥8, so the parenthesis is nonnegative. The constants depend on the fixed realization and chosen support interval. No canonical numerical choice of b or a minimal final algebra is claimed. These are source/primary compositions, not new homological transfers.

## 7. Why this full supporting calculation is not nominated

The exact lower bound and matching representation were genuinely derived in this round, rather than inferred from a finite experiment or a catalogue. But project novelty is not the contribution gate. After the strongest compatible reductions, the construction consists of:

1. elementary conjugation in the actual source presentation to expose commuting U roots;
2. the classical Heisenberg equal-weight-space operation for three paired root directions;
3. the elementary annihilator quotient L/I_c and a polarization-preserving permutation action;
4. a triangular Hankel minor and the periodic impulse character;
5. the already proved tensor simulation and Minamoto–Yamaura projective-dimension formula.

No incompatible hypothesis remains in these steps: the quotient may be nonreduced, even finite additive groups are covered without the odd-order Weyl normalization, whole G-representations need not be semisimple/unitary, and the ordinary bimodule is right-projective exactly where that property is used. Thus the attractive quantitative result does not leave an identified nonroutine operation. A lengthy proof or a failed naive odd-order shortcut cannot change that assessment. This is an author decision not to nominate, not a self-awarded independent TECHNICAL verdict.

The source199 companion was also read at its actual introduction and consequences proof. It already produces modules with every prescribed finite projective dimension by successive cokernels in a projective-injective resolution, over its stated characteristic-two transcendental field extensions. No such consequence is renamed here, and its stronger companion scope is not replaced by the present complex encoded calculation.

## 8. Exact remaining whole-algebra question and strongest reduction

The broader question considered in this research is the following, with the full source construction fixed:

    Does there exist C_A<infinity such that
    pd_A N <= C_A log_2(dim_C N+1)
    for every nonzero finite-dimensional A-module N
    with pd_A N<infinity?                                   (U105)

No proof-feasible full statement of (U105) emerged; it is not asserted, frozen as a nominee, weakened, or claimed open in the literature. Formula(16) is not substituted for it.

The missing operation is control of arbitrary module actions and their cancellation under repeated derived tensor. This is not merely the task of extending an ordinary extinction formula. The stronger actual primary Theorem4.10 already treats two-degree **graded** modules via the action morphism xi:F(N_0)→N_1 and its iterates: finite projective dimension is characterized by eventual isomorphism, and quantified by the projective dimensions of the corresponding cones. Passing to opposites gives the same left-module mechanism. That operation is paid and must be retained in any extension.

What remains unpaid is a dimension-controlled bound on the finite tail of those cones, and a compatible reduction for arbitrary **ungraded** finite A-modules. The central-sign calculation controls M(Y), not arbitrary complexes over D nor arbitrary action morphisms. There is no established bound here on the sizes, lengths or persistence of such cones in terms of dim N. Restricting N to D and treating it as inflated loses its X-action; replacing N by an associated graded object does not automatically preserve its finite projective dimension. No conclusion that such a bound is impossible follows. We do not promote this remaining question to a result.

r104's original all-p,r,k,Y global tensor-growth equality remains unproved with only its supplied lower bound, and is mathematically distinct from (U105). Its fixed-module subexponential obligation, its Carlson-body access limit, late reads and finite-GF4 limitations are preserved. r103, T009P102, I28, all old involution/matrix-algebraicity scopes, and accepted R041 remain immutable. No second count or independent gate is claimed.
