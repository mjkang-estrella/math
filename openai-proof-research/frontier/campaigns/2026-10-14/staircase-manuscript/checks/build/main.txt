                      An integral regular summand in every
                          staircase Specht tensor cube
                                          Myeongjin Kang

                                          October 15, 2026


                                                 Abstract
         For every staircase partition ρ = (m, m − 1, . . . , 1) of N = m(m + 1)/2, we prove that
      the integral regular module Z[SN ] is a direct summand of (SZρ )⊗3 . Ordered row and column
      polytabloids give a tensor whose translates form a basis of that summand. A single integral
      coordinate functional gives an explicit equivariant retraction. The splitting survives base
      change to every commutative unital ring, including nonﬂat base change, with no restriction on
      the characteristic. The proof uses the separating-product mechanism of Bryant and Kovács,
      Ikenmeyer’s triangular palette uniqueness, and a square-deﬁcit calculation from the pinned
      cyclic-polytabloid construction.

    Harman and Ryba proved complex constituent coverage for the staircase cube [3, Theorem 1.1].
Their modular argument already gives every projective indecomposable as a summand in char-
acteristic 2 [3, Section 3, Remark 3.3]; that occurrence is not a new consequence here. Regular
multiplicities are stronger. The conclusion also applies at odd primes p ≤ 2m − 1, where the
staircase Specht module is not projective: its hook lengths are 2(m + 1 − i − j) + 1, and the
occurrence of a hook of length p makes its dimension fail the divisibility by the order of a Sylow
p-subgroup required of a projective module. The construction below does not use projectivity of
the Specht factor.
    The proof combines inherited mechanisms. Bryant–Kovács [1, Lemma (ii)–(iii)] give the
regular-summand criterion over a ﬁeld and the product of separating witnesses. Their proof
of the ﬁeld criterion uses injectivity of the regular group algebra. We instead write and verify an
integral retraction directly. Ikenmeyer [2, Sections 4–5] supplies the triangular labeling and its
row/column uniqueness. The ordered block construction and the square-deﬁcit step appear in the
pinned OpenAI manuscript [6, Sections 2–3]. All needed integral arguments are included here.

4    The unique surviving term
Lemma 2 (Triangular palette uniqueness, after Ikenmeyer). A word a : B → {1, . . . , m} whose
letters on row i are exactly 1, . . . , m+1−i and whose letters on column j are exactly 1, . . . , m+1−j
must equal β.

Proof. In the top row, letter m can occur only in column 1, as every later column has smaller
maximum. Once its ﬁrst k −1 positions have been ﬁlled by m, m−1, . . . , m−k +2, letter m−k +1
must occupy column k: earlier positions are occupied and later columns have smaller maxima.
Thus the top row is m, m − 1, . . . , 1. Removing it removes the largest allowed letter from each
column. Decrease the remaining row indices by one; the palettes are those of the triangle with
parameter m − 1. Induction, starting at m = 1, proves the claim. Conversely β has all the
required palettes. This is the row/column uniqueness used in [2, Section 5, equation (5.2)].

The complex regular conclusion already follows from the earlier project note [8, Section 2.1] and
Harman–Ryba [3, Theorem 2.4]. The former identiﬁes C[G](vR ⊗ vR ) with M ρ , using the pair-

The latter puts M C(ρ) = M (1 ) = C[G] inside M ρ ⊗ S ρ . Semisimplicity gives complex regular
containment in the cube. That comparison does not give an integral splitting, since the scalar need
not be a unit. The present third factor and reﬁned letters give the unit identity of Proposition 3.
    For context, we use OpenAI revision fd4aeeb2ee4f (the full pin is recorded in the refer-
ences). The pinned source stipulates the stronger complex Saxl square theorem [6, Theorem 3.1,
thm:cyclic-saxl]; its companion [7] treats universal complex squares. Those results are ac-
cepted as stipulated source context here. The integral proof above does not use their complex
coverage conclusions. The characteristic-2 projective occurrence in [3] follows the modular ap-
proach of Bessenrodt–Bowman–Sutton [4, Section 5]; their strengthened conjecture concerns the
square. The square-splitting identity in [5, Theorem 3.7] concerns a diﬀerent multiplicity ques-
tion. We claim neither those results nor the classical separating criterion, triangular uniqueness, or
square-deﬁcit mechanism as new. The advance asserted in this note is the integral and every-ring
regular-summand package. No worldwide priority claim is made.
    The accompanying code copies two previously independent ﬁnite controls with their prove-
nance preserved. One constructs polytabloids and checks all 1 + 6 + 720 = 727 orbit elements
for m ≤ 3, including equivariance and sign mutations. The other tests the product-coordinate
condition for m ≤ 5 by exhaustive backtracking with literal block-support pruning, and includes
an unpruned m = 3 check and a characteristic-2 obstruction to splitting the intermediate per-
mutation module from the square at m = 2. These calculations are reproducibility support, not
premises of the all-m proof. No full Lean formalization is supplied or claimed. Exact source
interfaces, hashes, build instructions, and the formal-coverage status accompany the source.
