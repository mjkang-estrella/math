## Full working question and credited reduction

The effective direction asks for a computable characteristic-zero division ring D, algebraic over its actual center F, with D=F[x,y] and infinite F-dimension. Computable means a presentation on a decidable subset of the natural numbers with computable ring operations and equality, not merely a coefficient-generation procedure or a sequence of finite approximations. A successful source construction over a computable central subfield k with D=k[x,y] would also make F decidable inside D: an element is central exactly when it commutes with x and y. The two commutator tests suffice because k is central. This observation is elementary; it does not construct D.

The source's lifting theorem preserves whole formal polynomial identities, increases the finite division degree, and restores generation and TWO transverse directions. It does not preserve every equation of the constant tuple. Its division realization uses symbol and cyclic factors, a central Laurent field, finite linear descent, then a second Laurent translation and a countable subfield generated by all needed coefficients. These are paid source operations, not new bridges.

Actual r87 already supplies the formal-identity direct limit, algebraic inversion, finite word-span argument, actual-center descent, eventual constant zero/nonzero status for every polynomial, and independence of the final ultrafilter. It already records both the equality problem for coefficientwise computable series and the conditional simple-algebra word-problem procedure. Those deductions are not reoffered. Actual r83 credits Goodearl–Letzter's matrix-algebraic rational-extension operation; actual I28 records the first-kind involution constraints. Neither neighboring full scope is weakened here.

The present sustained calculation tries to improve the coefficient interface itself: replace arbitrary independent iterated Laurent behavior by a uniform denominator bound compatible with the actual correction graph, then use the strongest compatible commutative approximation theorem. The conclusion is a finite-system approximation route, with the full effective construction still unpaid.

## 1. A right inverse for the actual correction, with uniform valuation loss

Fix ONE spectral lifting step, after all source choices of stable sizes and common response spaces have been made. This does not assume those choices are computable. Work in a finite splitting extension E of the complete central Laurent field F=F0((epsilon)), with the epsilon valuation extended to E. Fix bases once for the finitely many response spaces, local arrow spaces and coefficient matrices. Coordinate valuation is the minimum coordinate valuation. All nonzero rational integers have valuation zero here; this is not a p-adic estimate.

The exact input is correction.tex, prop:eleven-block-correction, using the source's common G and p_i from global-defect.tex. At a vertex i, loop responses have image L_i in E^a and quotient H_i. Identify H_i with G* by the dual of p_i. For each of the four prescribed forward arrows out of each of the eleven vertices, its variable reverse arrow maps onto

    {(h,-h): h in G*} subset H_i + H_j.

Indeed the annihilator is exactly {(p_i(c),p_j(c)): c in G}; finite-dimensional duality gives this entire antidiagonal image, not just containment. Choose a linear right inverse for this map. Also choose right inverses for every loop map onto L_i and sections for E^a -> H_i. There are finitely many such maps, independent of word length. Their matrix entries have a common finite valuation lower bound.

For length ell, form the graph whose vertices are (i,omega), where i is modulo11 and omega is a binary word of length ell. An edge joins

    (i,s_c B) to (i-a,B s_c),

where a is1 or2 for c=1, and3 or4 for c=2. Its orientation encodes the antidiagonal transfer above. Consecutive offsets connect all eleven block indices at a fixed word, and an edge then effects one cyclic rotation. Consequently the connected components are exactly one component consisting of all eleven block indices times each orbit of DISTINCT cyclic rotations of words. Periodic words introduce no multiplicity.

The source's cyclic-sum identity says that the quotient error has sum zero on each component. Choose a spanning tree in that component. Root it, and assign to each edge the sum of the errors in the subtree below it, with the orientation sign required by divergence. This solves the quotient correction. In a nonarchimedean valuation, every subtree sum has valuation at least the least input valuation, regardless of the number of vertices. Lift the flow through the fixed local arrow right inverses, and remove the remaining L_i errors by the fixed loop right inverses. Sums of arbitrarily many incident contributions still incur no additional valuation loss.

It follows that there is a constant c>=0, depending on this fixed spectral step but NOT on ell, such that corrections can be chosen with

    v(new reverse and loop coefficients) >= v(selected error) - c.       (1)

This uses the common response-space identification, rather than unrelated rank bounds for individual arrows. When G=0 the quotient step is empty and the loop inverses suffice.

For descent, take a finite normal splitting extension containing the finitely many needed constants and linear maps. The actual polynomial correction system, allowed offset subspaces and old partial solution are defined over F. A split correction solves that finite affine system. Average its Galois conjugates to obtain an F-solution. The valuation on a finite extension of a complete discretely valued field is unique, so conjugation is isometric. Division by the finite group order has epsilon valuation zero. Coordinate changes to a fixed F-basis contribute one additional fixed loss, absorbed in c. The allowed reverse/diagonal spaces are the source's F-defined V_r, so averaging preserves them. This is an explicit compatible version of the source's linear descent, not descent of nonlinear solutions.

## 2. Linear denominator growth for the free corrections

At the start of this step, every old coefficient is in k, which has trivial epsilon valuation. The constant input R is epsilon-small. Expand the finite collection of fixed-root Schur functions at R as formal functions of the positive-order input H. There are constants a,b>=0 such that every multilinear coefficient of H-degree r has valuation at least

    -a*r-b.                                                        (2)

Here is why the bound is uniform in r. Evaluation of an original free series at R+H has coefficients given by sums with r insertions and small constant factors between them; the constants have positive valuation and the old coefficients valuation zero. These sums converge, with a common lower bound. Fixed matrices, projections and eigenvalues add bounded losses. Each complementary inverse is expanded around its invertible constant matrix: a term of H-degree r has at most r positive-degree factors and at most r+1 fixed inverses. Its loss is linear in r. A finite number of products and such inverses preserves a bound of form(2). This is a coefficient estimate after admissible evaluation, not an assertion that every inverse expansion converges at an arbitrary later non-small input.

Let a0>=0 bound the loss of the prescribed forward coefficients. For every high coefficient of degree n>=2, seek the inductive bound

    v(Z_n) >= -M*(n-2)-B.                                        (3)

Choose nonnegative integers B,M large enough that

    B >= 3*(a+a0)+b+c,
    M >= B+3*a+2*a0+b+c+1.

Ceilings may be used when the splitting valuation is fractional. At step ell, the already assigned reverse coefficients have degrees at most ell-2; diagonal coefficients have degrees at most ell-1. A term in the uncorrected degree-ell Schur error contains h high coefficients and f prescribed forward coefficients, with r=h+f. Its valuation, using(2) and(3), is at least

    -M*ell + M*(2*h+f) - h*B - f*a0 - a*(h+f) - b.              (4)

The following exclusions are essential. A term with one high coefficient and no forward coefficient would need a degree-ell coefficient, not yet present. A term with one high and one forward coefficient would need the high coefficient of degree ell-1. At this stage that coefficient is diagonal; one forward off-diagonal arrow cannot return to a fixed Schur block, so its contribution is zero. The only remaining cases are h=0,f=ell>=3; h=1,f>=2; or h>=2,f>=0. This uses the source's exact support pattern and two-stage coefficient stabilization; an arbitrary formal implicit equation would not have these exclusions.

Subtract c from(4), and compare with the desired bound for the new reverse coefficient of degree ell-1. The difference is

    (2*h+f-3)*M - (h-1)*B - a*(h+f) - f*a0 - b - c.             (5)

For h=0 the minimum is at f=3 and is nonnegative by the choice of B; increasing f adds M-a-a0>=0. For h=1,f>=2 the minimum is at f=2 and is nonnegative by the choice of M. For h>=2 the minimum is h=2,f=0, where it is M-B-2*a-b-c>=0; increasing h adds 2*M-B-a>=0 and increasing f adds M-a-a0>=0. Thus(1) gives the new reverse bound. The loop coefficient has degree ell and a weaker required bound, so it satisfies(3) as well. The induction begins at ell=3 with only prescribed forward terms. Each coefficient changes at most twice; taking the resulting stable coefficients preserves(3).

This calculation establishes a supporting uniform-denominator choice for the fixed source step, subject to its stipulated spectral/response/compatibility contracts. It is not an effective selection of those contracts, nor a replacement main theorem.

## 3. Composition and rescaled translation

Enlarge M if necessary. The bound above implies v(Z_n)>=-M*n for n>=1, with a fixed additive allowance harmless for coordinate changes. Composing the original finite tuple X with Z then gives

    v(Y_omega) >= -M*|omega|-C

for one C, because positive-degree insertions have total degree |omega| and all remaining constant insertions are small. Fix a new word of length r after translating s1 by gamma. Its coefficient is a sum of old coefficients of lengths n>=r, multiplied by gamma^(n-r), with finitely many terms for each n. Put gamma=epsilon^(M+1)*tau. After multiplying that coefficient by epsilon^(M*r+C), the term of tau-degree n-r has epsilon valuation at least n-r. In particular the scaled coefficient belongs to

    F0[[epsilon,tau]].

The substitution is compatible with the source translation: over F0((epsilon)), scaling the new central Laurent variable by a nonzero element gives an isomorphism of Laurent-series fields. Thus generation and the TWO tangent ranks are retained exactly as in generation.tex. No evaluation tau=0 or epsilon=0 is used to infer generation.

All finitely many coordinates of any finite subset of the output data, including q, consequently lie in Frac(F0[[epsilon,tau]]). The countable coefficient field is a subfield of this ordinary two-variable fraction field. Arbitrary iterated Laurent series do not have this property: coefficients can have epsilon poles growing faster than linearly. The support-aware estimate is the operation enabling this particular comparison with commutative approximation.

## 4. What the strongest Artin approximation actually pays

Artin1969 Theorem1.10 applies to the henselization R of F0[epsilon,tau] at(epsilon,tau), with completion Rhat=F0[[epsilon,tau]]. His Theorem6.1 is stronger for a FIXED finite polynomial system: sufficiently accurate polynomial solutions can be replaced by exact henselian solutions with a prescribed finite jet. The full Section6 argument, and the operative Section5 reduction/implicit-function argument, are read; this is not an appeal only to the abstract or a generic slogan about approximation.

Take a finite list of scalar coordinates from the preceding construction, a finite list of polynomial equalities over F0(epsilon,tau) that they satisfy, and finitely many nonzero expressions in them. Express the coordinates as fractions of Rhat-elements. Introduce numerator/denominator variables and clear all denominators. The equations become a finite polynomial system over R; record finite jets at which each of the finitely many required nonzero numerators and denominators has a nonzero coefficient. Artin1.10 gives an exact solution in R with those jets, so all equalities and nonzero conditions survive in Frac(R).

This also preserves division of the finite coefficient algebra: the approximating field embeds in Frac(Rhat), which embeds in F0((epsilon))((tau)); the source division algebra D0 remains division after the latter central Laurent extension. Therefore its base change to any such subfield has no zero divisors and, being finite-dimensional, is division. This is stronger than merely approximating split matrices or preserving a determinant at a single point.

For a fixed free-word cutoff N, finitely many relations on a tuple truncated modulo words of degree>N become finitely many scalar polynomial equations in its finitely many coordinates. Artin consequently pays this finite truncation, together with prescribed finite generation/tangent minors. It does NOT simultaneously pay the countably many coefficient equations of whole free-series identities. Treating the free letters as commuting variables changes those equations. Artin6.1 fixes the system and its number of unknowns; our number of coefficient variables grows with N. No uniform finite system whose solutions encode the entire corrected noncommutative tuple has been produced. Nor has a compatible choice of the approximants at ALL N been made with decidable equality in the union.

This is the precise remaining compatibility problem after the stronger source-plus-classical composition, not a conclusion that approximation is inherently unusable.

## 5. Further effective choices not supplied by countability

Two source selection steps require attention even if all finite linear algebra were made effective. In formal-generic.tex, after finding n0, the construction chooses p for which t_p=d*p/e_p is a GLOBAL minimum over n>=n0. In global-defect.tex, the bounded sequence g_N=N*a_r-rho_(rN) has a tail minimum g attained arbitrarily late; a corresponding N0 is chosen so every later g_N>=g. This is not a claim that g_N is eventually constant.

A useful partial improvement for the first choice follows from the source disjoint-spectrum specialization. For every r>=n0 and every positive integer N, independent r-blocks give e_(Nr)>=N*e_r, hence t_(Nr)<=t_r. Taking B_j=lcm(n0,n0+1,...,j), the positive integer t_(B_j) is nonincreasing and eventually equals the global minimum. This is a valid stabilization statement, but it supplies no stopping certificate. A bounded nonincreasing computable integer sequence can conceal a later drop; mere boundedness is not an algorithm for its limiting value. The tail-minimum choice for g_N has an analogous certification issue. Neither observation proves that these PARTICULAR source sequences have noncomputable minima; additional structure may permit effective bounds.

Several apparent algorithmic difficulties are already paid and should not be inflated. In a finite-dimensional algebra over a computable field, minimal polynomials are obtained by finite linear dependence among1,a,...,a^dim(A); a general polynomial-factorization oracle is unnecessary for that operation. The source also descends linear equations to the center, so an algorithm need not explicitly construct every eigenvalue merely because the proof passes to a splitting field. Harrison–Trainor–Melnikov–Miller's Theorem3.2 supplies compatible algebraic-closure embeddings GIVEN a computable base with a splitting algorithm; it does not make a newly generated Laurent coefficient field computable. Passing the coefficient division algebra to its algebraic closure would split it and change the construction.

The first polynomial-input lifting step has only finitely many rational operations for each corrected coefficient once its spectral data and local right inverses are fixed. The final translation nevertheless collects infinitely many such coefficients into individual central series. An assertion that they have a finite algebraic or otherwise decidable description still needs proof. Analytic convergence and the linear denominator estimate do not imply algebraicity over F0(epsilon,tau).

## Final feasibility status

No full effective division ring, matrix-algebraic division ring, involutive division ring or other full-scope nominee was obtained. No statement was frozen. The retained derivation supplies a fixed-step graph-flow correction, a support-aware linear denominator estimate, and a compatible finite-system Artin approximation route. These are supporting operations only. What remains unpaid is an exact effectively presented coefficient/identity construction through infinitely many source steps, including effective stable spectral choices and compatible whole free-series relations. A finite jet, a field generated by computable coefficients, eventual stabilization without a stopping test, or an ultrafilter-free abstract limit does not fill that obligation. No impossibility, priority, exhaustion, independent grade or acceptance claim is made.
