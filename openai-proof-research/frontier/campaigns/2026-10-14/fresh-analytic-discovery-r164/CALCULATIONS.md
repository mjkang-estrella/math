## A bounded-defect double ray is too cheaply reducible

For a positive proper weighted locally finite graph let c(P) be path cost and T its distance. A simple double ray p has additive defect at most C if c(p[i,j])-T(p_i,p_j)<=C for all i<j. This full simultaneous, direction-free scope was considered; it has not been frozen.

For iid nonatomic finite weights let a be the essential infimum. The shifted weights t-a are positive almost surely. Choose theta with 4 E exp(-theta(t-a))<1. Counting simple paths and Borel–Cantelli gives, simultaneously for every root and integer K, only finitely many simple paths starting at that root with shifted cost <=K. Thus the sum of t-a along either arm of any simple double ray diverges. No moment is used in this counting step.

Given a C-defect double ray, choose a finite central segment F with sum_F(t-a)>C+2. Choose epsilon>0 with |F| epsilon<1 and independently replace the weights on F by samples from G conditioned on [a,a+epsilon]. The saving exceeds C+1. For endpoints p_i,p_j outside F, the modified cost of their segment is less than T_t(p_i,p_j)-1. Every path avoiding F keeps its cost and costs at least T_t(p_i,p_j), so every modified minimizer must use F. The modified metric remains proper (all finite changes have a finite total cost discrepancy). A diagonal subsequence, with a fixed vertex of F selected along infinitely many minimizers, produces a bigeodesic.

The event/path may depend on the entire original configuration. To justify absolute continuity, first reduce by countability to a deterministic integer C, finite simple segment F, and rational epsilon for which a witness event has positive probability. In the enlarged coupling the conditional replacements always save enough on this witness event. The unconditional modified marginal has density product_e in F [1_[a,a+epsilon](t_e)/G([a,a+epsilon])] relative to the iid law, and hence gives probability zero to source-forbidden bigeodesics. Contradiction. One does not condition the marginal on the witness event and call it iid. This yields the bounded-defect exclusion at author-calculation level under the source law hypotheses. The accepted moment-free history is not reproved or used as a new result.

This finite lowering plus compactness is stronger than attempting to extend the source label budget to approximate paths. It pays the operation before any approximate-ray labels or exceptional-direction theory are needed. Thus it is not a substantial candidate merely because the conclusion looks stronger.

## A metric detour gap, with the overlap issue retained

For finite K and radius R put
 D_K(R)=inf_{x,y outside B_R, z in K}[T(x,z)+T(z,y)-T(x,y)].
A bounded sequence of such excesses yields two rooted exact geodesic arms after compactness. If the arms share an initial portion of cost L, each finite connecting walk incurs a removable loop of cost 2L, so L<=C/2. Properness confines every common vertex to a finite passage ball. Finite uniqueness says that two rooted minimizing paths split at most once; the limit arms consequently have one last common vertex w and thereafter are disjoint. Their concatenation is a simple C-defect double ray, contradicting the preceding construction. Hence D_K(R) tends to infinity. This will be written carefully with moving endpoints and all segment inequalities, rather than treating the original concatenation as simple.

## Finite and summable changes

For s>=0 with A=sum_e |s_e-t_e|<infinity, simple paths change cost by at most A and |T_s-T_t|<=A. Properness of s follows from T_s>=T_t-A and bounded-length compactness from the analogous simple-path bound. A simple s-bigeodesic would be a 2A-defect t-double ray; none exists. Any simple s-minimizer intersecting K satisfies
 T_t(x,z)+T_t(z,y)-T_t(x,y)<=2A,
so D_K(R)>2A excludes every such minimizer for remote x,y. The same holds for t-minimizers.

If s-t has finite support F, take K to contain its incident vertices. Both minimizers eventually avoid F. Comparing each in the other metric proves exact equality of distances and equality of the minimizing-path sets for all pairs of remote endpoints. This is simultaneous over all finite alterations, including environment-dependent choices and values outside G's support, because the deterministic argument takes place on the single event D_K(R)->infinity for every finite K.

For general summable changes choose finite F with sum_outside F |s-t|<epsilon. Remote minimizers avoid its vertices. Cross-comparison gives |T_s(x,y)-T_t(x,y)|<=epsilon uniformly when both endpoints are remote. This is a genuine completed feasibility chain, but still elementary composition after the source and finite lowering.
