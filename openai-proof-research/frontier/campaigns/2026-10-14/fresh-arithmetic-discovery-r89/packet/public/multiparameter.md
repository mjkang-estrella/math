## Question tested, without a new target freeze

Let m be any positive finite integer. Let B be a neighborhood of 0 in C^m and let F be holomorphic near {0}×B in C^3×B. Assume F(0,t)=0, that each f_t=F(·,t) has an isolated critical point at 0, and that its finite Milnor number is the same μ for every t. The old question asks for one ambient homeomorphism germ Φ(x,t)=(φ_t(x),t), with φ_t(0)=0, φ_0=id and F(φ_t(x),t)=f_0(x), jointly continuous with its inverse after shrinking. There is no supplied simultaneous resolution, common Milnor radius, Whitney condition, direction restriction or prescribed finite bound on m. No effective neighborhood size or regularity of Φ beyond homeomorphism is sought.

The initial feasibility concern was the familiar one in r14: separately chosen trivializations on lines need not depend continuously on their directions. Extending the source's resolved vector fields in 2m directions would be compatible *if* a common resolution were supplied, but it does not supply that resolution. This round tested a different, already existing topological interface. The primary reading below occurred during preselection, before any freeze; none ever occurred.

The present reduction uses the completed disk theorem, not a claim that its resolution works over a higher-dimensional base.

Henry C. King, *Topological Type in Families of Germs*, Inventiones mathematicae 62 (1980), 1–13, DOI [10.1007/BF01391660](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01391660), Theorem2 and its proof in §4, is the stronger compatible input. For a conelike family over a real parameter ball, with no coalescing critical points, it constructs link homology cobordisms W_t, well-defined up to composition with invertible cobordisms. Theorem2(3) makes invertibility of **every** W_t equivalent to a joint ambient right trivialization over that ball. There is no dimension exclusion in this theorem. King's complex Corollary3 does exclude three spatial complex variables; that corollary is NOT the input used here.

King's proof constructs one compact family C_t and an outer link collar q, then defines W_t by removing a smaller conical neighborhood of the origin from Z_t=C_t∩f_t^{-1}(0). For t=0, Z_0 is the central closed cone. Changing the inner cone adds an invertible cobordism; this is the cone-neighborhood operation explicitly used on printed pp12–13. His final construction uses all W_t at once. The end of §3, printed p10, gives an actual open embedding on a product neighborhood satisfying Fh(z,x)=F(x). Thus the output is not merely an assertion of individually equivalent germs with uncoordinated equality neighborhoods. ## Trying the existing reduction in full

**1. Obtain no coalescing on one representative.** The local critical scheme is cut out by the three spatial partial derivatives of F. Its central fiber is isolated of length μ. The finite mapping theorem supplies a finite representative over a smaller base. The three partials form a complete intersection there: in the regular analytic ring of dimension m+3 their quotient has dimension m. The quotient is Cohen–Macaulay and finite over the regular m-dimensional base, hence flat; equivalently the base parameters form a regular sequence and the finite module is locally free. Its degree is μ. Since the origin in every fiber already has length μ, there are no other spatial critical points in this representative. This is the source's finite-Jacobian conservation argument with a regular base of dimension m, not a uniform Milnor-radius assertion. Conical structure of each individual analytic zero germ is the usual input also explicitly used by King.

**2. Fix King's outer tube once.** Apply the construction of §4 to the real 2m-dimensional parameter ball U, shrunk and convex. It yields C_t, the fixed outer link collar, and the compact zero fibers Z_t. The boundary of Z_t is the outer link transported by q; away from the origin the spatial derivative is nonzero. The restriction of Z to any compact parameter segment is proper. These facts concern the fixed compact outer family, not the existence of a common radius for all inner conical neighborhoods.

**3. Test the compact zero family along each segment.** Fix t in U and restrict parameters to s↦st, 0≤s≤1. Around *each* s_0 this is the restriction of a holomorphic family on a complex disk in the line through t. Source `thm:main` supplies a joint origin-preserving homeomorphism in a neighborhood of that segment point. Its restriction to the zero set is a local product chart at the singular section. At every other interior point of Z_s, the implicit function theorem supplies a smooth product chart. At the outer boundary, King's q supplies product charts that preserve the boundary. Thus projection of this compact zero family to the segment is a topological submersion in Siebenmann's Definition6.8, including at the origin and at the boundary.

Every fiber is locally conelike with strata the origin, the outer boundary, and the punctured interior (with connected components separated if necessary). Near the origin this follows from the individual analytic conical structure; away from it the fiber is a smooth manifold, and near its outer boundary it has a collar. In particular it lies in LOC and satisfies the deformation property of Theorem2.3. The vertex and boundary are closed substratified sets. The local charts just described preserve them, so Complement4.3, the respectful union lemma6.10 and Corollary6.14/Remark6.16 give local bundle charts for the **whole compact fiber**, preserving vertex and boundary.

Here is the operative gluing, rather than an appeal to bare pointwise topological constancy. At a fixed s_0 take a small closed conical neighborhood inside the source product chart, and finitely many product charts covering its compact exterior. Lemma6.12 extends a transition near a compact overlap to one supported in a slightly larger overlap. Union lemma6.9 then joins charts, fixing the first chart on a smaller compact subset; its respectful form preserves vertex and boundary. Finite induction covers the fiber. Properness excludes additional points outside that chart after the base is shrunk, exactly as in the proof of6.14. The entire operation already appears in Siebenmann's proof. It needs neither commuting vector fields nor a resolution over B.

Local bundle identifications along finitely many subintervals of [0,1] now identify (Z_t,0,∂Z_t) with (Z_0,0,∂Z_0). One can compose the successive fiber homeomorphisms; no compatible choices as t varies are needed at this stage. Disk neighborhoods and the finite subdivision may depend on t. Compactness of a **single** segment is all that was used.

**4. Verify the actual cobordism hypothesis, not only equality of links.** Since Z_0 is a closed cone, the previous step makes Z_t a closed cone with its vertex and outer boundary specified. Choose its inner cone c_t as in King's construction. The region between this inner cone and the outer cone boundary is invertible by the same cone-neighborhood comparison used in King's §4. If one starts with another inner cone, King's change-of-cone statement composes with an invertible cobordism and preserves invertibility. Therefore the very W_t appearing in Theorem2 is invertible. This holds for every t∈U, with the same fixed outer family. Merely having homeomorphic abstract links would not have supplied this step.

**5. Invoke King's joint conclusion.** Theorem2(3) now gives a continuous family of ambient right equivalences over U. The end of §3 supplies a joint representative, and the open embedding supplies its continuous inverse. If its central map is h_0 rather than id, compose all maps on the right with h_0^{-1}; because h_0 preserves f_0 this normalizes the central map without changing the equation. Further shrinking gives the required germ neighborhoods. There is no dependence on a uniform linewise radius, no selection of linewise maps continuous in direction, and no finite-dimensional restriction beyond the original arbitrary finite m.

## Operation comparison and outcome

The disk theorem pays the singular-point charts. No-coalescing is the existing finite-Jacobian argument. The compact collar and canonical W_t are King's construction. Joining the singular chart to the compact exterior is Siebenmann's actual relative union/proper-submersion proof. Cone comparison pays invertibility, and King supplies the simultaneous ambient choice. These inputs fit the full original multiparameter question together; the stronger conclusion does not itself exhibit an additional unpaid compatibility operation.

The already present r15 reduction, supplemented here by the explicit relative product-chart check, resolves the **identified interface in this preselection attempt**, subject to its ordinary classical inputs and the stipulated disk theorem.
