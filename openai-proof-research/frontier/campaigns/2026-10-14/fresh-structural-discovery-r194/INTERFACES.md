## G: generic primes, full frame, and the actual finite filtration

G assumes n≥1 and p>n+1. The cofiber indexed by a nonempty subset I of {1,…,n} is the appropriate shift by minus the sum of (2i−1), with chromatic localization L_{n−max(I)}; the empty subset gives L_{n−1}S. The source defines an order of 2^n stages and supplies the first localization unit. It does not assert uniqueness of the order-realization data or that the filtration splits. Its last rational block is an additive construction; one must not presume all its classes are products of a single additional integral sphere primitive.

The operative coefficient argument prepares finite kernels before localization in the stratum parameter. Full sections of the finite kernels, including nilpotents, retain the normalized determinant framing. Root rings and distribution parameters are different objects. The translation dual has the dimension of the full marking torsor, not merely that of a visible central subgroup. The support calculation treats independent extensions with incidence/Steinberg–Bruhat data. The compact-to-poles-to-full-frame induction is ordered by height and coheight, and a finite forgetful Ext kernel controls restriction to a uniform open subgroup.

The full-frame Koszul comparison sends a basis exterior generator indexed by I to a product of missing coordinate powers times the corresponding target generator. Lower rows become pro-zero after a bounded filtration argument. This is not a formality theorem for all intermediate complexes. Full norm characters, rather than a guessed central power, remove the unwanted rows. The simultaneous sphere primitives use the integral regulator/image-of-J construction with its actual unit factors; integral exterior generators and rational trace generators cannot be interchanged without that calculation.

Finally, finite cubical Adams/Amitsur descent gives quick convergence after any exact test used in the proof. The spectral comparison evaluates ordinary cooperations at a common finite stage for each finite collection of coefficients, preserves cofaces and degeneracies, and passes through completion only with the stated uniform control. It is stronger than an equality of second pages, but does not turn an arbitrary exact test into a symmetric monoidal functor.

These are paid inputs to the source theorem, not r194 operations. Their main use here is to prevent a stage-count argument from being mistaken for an exponent bound in a different tensor category.

## P: ordinary thick assembly, all jets, and the full generic action

P is exclusively the case p=3, height three. Finite assembly means the ordinary thick closure of L_0S,L_1S,L_2S in E(2)-local modules over the derived complete sphere: finite sums, shifts, cofibers, and retracts. It is not closure under tensoring with arbitrary objects.

The proof does not identify the action by setting x=0. It constructs nonlocal parameter maps k[[a,t]]→R[x]/x^m through all jets. At the analytic/decompletion stage the norm-one group has rows 0 and 3. The order-two norm quotient kills the twisted distribution target but retains the untwisted upper row. ## W: actual beta kernel and actual descent, with different heights

W distinguishes the sphere from its derived p-completion. Its weak-splitting counterexamples concern the latter at height p for p≥5 and at height p+1 for p≥7. The detecting lower height is h=p−1, not three. The power is β_1^{h^2}.

At the upper height the actual Adams filtration is multiplicative and has the proved strong horizontal termination. Finite quotient/Galois arguments use semilinear trace-one elements, rather than division by an arbitrary finite group order.

W separately proves finite descent with actual maps. The Brown–Comenetz/Gross–Hopkins interface eliminates the relevant sphere phantom before asserting an Adams map is null.
