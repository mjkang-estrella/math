## Actual primary readings

Liu–Raz–Zhan, Memory-Sample Lower Bounds for Learning with Classical-Quantum Hybrid Memory, arXiv2303.00209v1 (1 March2023), private/primary/liu-raz-zhan2023v1.txt lines1–675 read (including the exact hybrid-channel model and main Theorem2; the subsequent truncation proof was not read). The main theorem treats finite binary-label extractor learning, requiring either quadratic classical memory, linear quantum memory, or exponentially many samples in the parity specialization. It permits arbitrary sample-controlled channels. Sections2.3–2.4 explicitly explain why the attempted pure quadratic-quantum-memory extension runs into truncation and badness-level obstacles, and how small quantum dimension enables their hybrid proof. This does not imply Q140 at D=2^(A d²), nor does it refute it. The technical proof after the overview has not been read; no claim to have excluded every consequence of that paper.

Zhao–Zlokapa–Neven–Babbush–Preskill–McClean–Huang, Exponential quantum advantage in processing massive classical data, arXiv2604.07639v2 (1 October2026), actual text220–290 and449–542 read. The main model processes classical samples once; the linear-system application samples entries of a sparse well-conditioned finite matrix and returns an efficiently measurable normalized quadratic form. The oracle sketch accumulates small sample-controlled rotations, with orthogonal supports giving O(N/epsilon) samples per approximate phase query and O(N Q²/epsilon) for Q queries. This is neither the fresh dense Gaussian equation distribution nor a full classical angular reconstruction theorem. Its large appendices, formal F.3 and D.12 proofs, have NOT been read. No stronger exclusion of quantum regression algorithms is claimed.
