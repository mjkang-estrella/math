For a strongly Rayleigh binary law nu, let c(i,j)=-Cov(X_i,X_j)>=0 for distinct sites. The sum over j is at most Var(X_i)<=1/4. Threshold graphs c(i,j)>=1/n have finite degree. Negative association should make different such components independent, by the elementary four-sign covariance inequality described below. Apply the stipulated source Brownian sampler separately on these components. This would yield a canonical sampler depending measurably on nu, equivariant under arbitrary coordinate relabelings; it would not require regular actions, finite generation, transitivity, amenability, or an invariant locally finite graph supplied in advance.

Four-sign covariance calculation: for disjoint finite A,B, let f,g be arbitrary real cylinder functions. Choose F=sum_i L_i X_i and G=sum_j M_j X_j, where L_i,M_j dominate coordinate oscillations of f,g. Each F+/-f and G+/-g is increasing. NA applied to the four sign pairs and summing opposite signs gives |Cov(f,g)|<=-Cov(F,G)=sum_ij L_i M_j c(i,j). Hence zero cross covariances imply block independence. This is a standard negative-association consequence, not itself the proposed new theorem. The new operation under examination is using the law's own canonical covariance components to construct the sampler with full permutation naturality and joint law-parameter measurability.
