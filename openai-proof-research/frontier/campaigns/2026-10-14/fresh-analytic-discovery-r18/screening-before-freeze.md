# Primary-operation and eligibility screen

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

## Selected family and the unpaid operation

The selected family is 231, not family 098. The actual pinned family231
introduction and strongly-Rayleigh section restrict their general-law FIID
theorem to the regular action on a countable group. They explicitly say
that arbitrary actions on another countable set lack equivariant finite
exhaustions and are not asserted. The decoder proposition requires finite
sets exhausting its entire index set. Its deterministic inverse preserves
equivariance only when the chosen drift does.

Target CS replaces the supplied geometry by geometry extracted from the
law. Covariance thresholds give locally finite graphs; their connected
components are independent factors of the law. The proposed construction
uses these components to obtain canonical finite observations and a single
jointly Borel sampler natural under every relabeling, even when the full
action has infinite stabilizer orbits. Establishing independence of the
discarded coordinates, not just finite threshold degree, is essential:
otherwise the sampler would target the wrong conditional law.

This is a concrete operation with a complete prospective route, not an
attempt to drop an assumption from a famous conjecture without a bridge.
Its significance must still be judged independently. In particular, the
covariance inequality, summable rows, and stochastic decoder are credited
antecedents, not advertised as new lemmas.

## Actual primary bodies compared before scope freeze

* Alishahi–Barzegar–Zamani, *On Tail Triviality of Negatively Dependent
  Stochastic Processes*, arXiv:2203.03935, sections 2.1–2.2: Proposition
  2.2 proves the exact four-sign covariance inequality for cylinder
  indicators. Its zero-covariance specialization already gives independence
  across a partition with zero cross covariances. Lemma 2.5 and equation
  (1) prove summable covariance rows for strongly Rayleigh processes.
  Their proof then uses an external enumeration to obtain tail
  decorrelation. It does not construct covariance-threshold observation
  neighborhoods or a law-natural IID sampler. Thus independence and
  summability are expressly paid by this paper; the proposed new operation
  is their use to remove the sampler's symmetry/exhaustion obstruction.
* Borcea–Brändén–Liggett, *Negative dependence and the geometry of
  polynomials*, arXiv:0707.2340v2: Definition 2.13, Proposition 3.5 and its
  determinant proof, Theorem 4.9 and its homogeneous-extension proof.
  These establish the countable-marginal convention, determinantal
  strong Rayleighness, and negative association, respectively. They do
  not assert CS. Their external-field/projection closure and symmetric
  homogenization are the source of the response bound used upstream.
* Lyons–Thom, *Invariant Coupling of Determinantal Measures on Sofic
  Groups*, arXiv:1402.0969, section 7 through Question 7.7 and the proof
  interface of Theorem 7.3: the operation is approximation by finite-range
  kernels in d-bar via invariant monotone joinings. Corollary 7.4 uses
  amenability and Bernoulli-isomorphism closure. Question 7.7 asks about
  equivariant determinantal factors. These operations do not supply the
  pointwise, law-parametrized sampler CS. CS is not being called a new
  solution of all differently formulated Bernoulli-shift questions:
  its independent labels live on the same index set I.
* Pinned family231, entire `decoder.tex`, `strongly-rayleigh.tex`, and
  `implementation.tex`, plus introduction and posterior lemma: the
  all-input Picard inverse, Brownian innovations, asymptotic readout and
  uniform 1/2 response are antecedents. Applying its theorem separately
  on canonical independent components will be explicitly justified.
* The cited pinned family236 companion's actual introduction and main
  theorem concern free Ising laws on regular trees and the sharp
  theta<=1/sqrt(d-1) range. Its supplied tree geometry and positive
  correlations differ from CS. No nonreconstruction-to-factor transfer
  or Ising extension is used here.

The literature search is bounded. No worldwide priority conclusion is
drawn from missing hits. Independent contribution review must decide
whether covariance-component canonicalization is substantial or merely
a technical assembly of the credited results.
