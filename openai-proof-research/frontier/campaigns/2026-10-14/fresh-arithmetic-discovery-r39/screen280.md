# Family 280: pre-freeze fixed-point descent screen

No target was frozen. The intended interface was arbitrary finite unitary automorphism groups G of a simple strongly rational unitary complex VOA V. The source gives strong locality and complete rationality of A_V. CKLW Theorem 7.1 and Proposition 7.6 already give strong locality of U=V^G and A_U=A_V^G; Theorems 7.4–7.7 also pay subnet reconstruction and compact Galois correspondence. These operations are not new.

The exact algebraic obstruction is visible before any categorical construction. Since averaging over G is exact in characteristic zero and C2(V) is G-invariant, there is a surjection

    U/C2(U) -> (V/C2(V))^G

whose kernel is (U intersect C2(V))/C2(U). The target is finite dimensional. Thus the missing assertion is finite dimensionality of this kernel; no bound follows from the displayed surjection. Averaging a generator a_(-2)b of C2(V) gives |G|^-1 sum_g (ga)_(-2)(gb), but its factors need not lie in U. Replacing this by (average a)_(-2)(average b) discards the nontrivial-isotypic pairings and is not an identity. This is a precise unpaid descendant-control operation, not a counterexample to orbifold rationality.

Source08 Proposition exhaustion invokes regularity of its input VOA to extract an ordinary simple submodule from a reconstructed weak module. Substitution of U here would assume the desired algebraic finiteness. Henriques–Tener Theorem C/Remark9.8 reconstruct unitary modules from net representations, but do not prove that every weak U-module is unitarizable or exhausted this way. The source extension correspondence is upward from an already strongly rational base; it does not supply descent.

Carnahan–Miyamoto introduction and stated Corollary5.25 concern finite solvable G. Miyamoto arXiv1812.00570v3 is explicitly withdrawn for a serious gap; its old abstract is not a usable theorem. The general finite-G claim was therefore not selected merely because it is broad. No credible source-based control of the displayed kernel has emerged. No nomination and no impossibility claim.
