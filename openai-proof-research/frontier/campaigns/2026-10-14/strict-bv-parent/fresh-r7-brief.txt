## Stage 3: one-valley rigidity and a varying-baseline estimate (proved auxiliary deduction)

This is NOT B, not a result nomination, and not a globally integrable planar input. It strengthens the exact model calculation to clarify, rather than assume away, a remaining obstruction.

Let q be any bounded nondecreasing real function with limits -A and B, where A,B>=0. Set h=|q| and

    k_r(s)=2/(pi*r)*sqrt(1-s^2/r^2)_+,
    u_t(s)=sup_{0<r<=t} (k_r*h)(s),       t>0.

No absolute continuity of q is assumed. Its jump and Cantor parts are included. Then u_t is quasiconvex: every sublevel set is an interval. Consequently it decreases and then increases, in the a.e./BV sense, with endpoints A,B. If m_t=ess inf u_t, then

    Var(max(c,u_t)) = max(c,A)+max(c,B)-2 max(c,m_t)       (3.1)

for every real constant c. In particular, if B>=A and c>=A, max(c,u_t) is nondecreasing and

    Var(max(c,u_t))=(B-c)_+.                             (3.2)

Thus the midpoint baseline used in r2 is stronger than needed for this exact model. This assertion uses log-concavity of the disk marginal; it does not assert this stronger threshold for every merely unimodal kernel.

Proof. Write Dh=P-N, where P=D(q_+) and N=-D((-q)_+). These are finite nonnegative measures of masses B,A, with supp N<=z<=supp P for a zero-crossing z. An atom in both P,N at z is permitted. For each r, F_r=k_r*h is C1 and

    F_r'=p_r-n_r,      p_r=k_r*P, n_r=k_r*N.

Continuity of k_r and finiteness of P,N imply continuity of its distributional derivative, hence C1. The kernel is log-concave on (-r,r):

    (log k_r)''(s)=-(r^2+s^2)/(r^2-s^2)^2 <0.

Extended by zero, it obeys the order-two kernel inequality, for s1<s2 and a<=b,

    k_r(s2-b) k_r(s1-a) >= k_r(s1-b) k_r(s2-a).         (3.3)

If the right side is positive, all points on the left lie in the support interval, and concavity of log k_r gives (3.3) by comparing increments over s2-s1. If the right side is zero the inequality is immediate. Integrating (3.3) against N(da)P(db) gives

    p_r(s2)n_r(s1) >= p_r(s1)n_r(s2).                    (3.4)

It is therefore impossible that F_r'(s1)>0 and F_r'(s2)<0: then p1>n1 and n2>p2, contradicting (3.4). Thus its continuous derivative has all strictly negative values before all strictly positive values, allowing zero intervals, and F_r is quasiconvex. This also covers a zero mass P or N. A supremum of quasiconvex functions is quasiconvex because its sublevel sets are intersections of intervals. Since radii are bounded by t, the endpoint limits of u_t are A,B uniformly over the radius family. A bounded quasiconvex function on the line is monotone on each side of a minimum interval; its distributional variation is the endpoint sum minus twice its essential minimum. Apply this to max(c,u_t) to obtain (3.1). If c>=A and B>=A its left endpoint is also its lower bound c, forcing nondecrease, which proves (3.2).

Here is an exact varying-baseline consequence. Assume B>=A. Let b be any bounded real BV function on R (finite distributional variation, finite limits b_-,b_+; global integrability is NOT required for this model). Let H=max(u_t,b), and ell=ess inf b. Then

    (DH)^-(R) <= (A-ell)_+ + (Db)^-(R),                  (3.5)
    Var H <= max(B,b_+)-max(A,b_-)
                +2(A-ell)_+ +2(Db)^-(R).                (3.6)

If b>=A a.e., the first term in (3.5) vanishes. If b>=A-epsilon, the model's unfavorable variation costs at most epsilon plus the unfavorable variation of the baseline. Reflection gives the A>=B version.

To prove (3.5) without an invalid BV equality-set chain rule at jumps, put c=min(A,ell) and v=max(c,u_t). The negative mass of Du_t carried ABOVE c on its decreasing branch is at most A-c. Precisely, Var^-(v)<=A-c, because u_t is quasiconvex with left endpoint A, and clamping below c truncates that single descending branch. Since b>=c, H=max(v,b). For arbitrary 1D BV functions v,b,

    Var^-(max(v,b)) <= Var^-(v)+Var^-(b).                 (3.7)

Indeed for any x<y, (max(v(x),b(x))-max(v(y),b(y)))_+ is at most (v(x)-v(y))_+ +(b(x)-b(y))_+. Sum along ordered partitions and take the supremum using one-sided representatives; this gives (3.7), including jumps and Cantor parts. Hence (DH)^-<=A-c+(Db)^-, which is (3.5). Finally Var H=H(+infinity)-H(-infinity)+2(DH)^-(R) gives (3.6). No derivative locality assumption is needed.

The relevance is exact: the old signed-valley model is robust to an arbitrary BV baseline bounded just below the lower absolute trace. The remaining planar problem is NOT solved by (3.5): strict planar sequences need not be one common monotone profile; controlling tangential geometry or reducing to such profiles through the maximal operation is still unpaid. In particular the estimate is not applied to a spatial slice of Mf_j without establishing that its disk averages have this one-dimensional convolution representation.

## Stage 5: an exact planar failure of the slice-transfer attempt, NOT a counterexample to B

It is false that monotonicity of f in the normal variable alone permits applying Stage 3 to a planar slice of its disk maximal envelope, even with a midpoint baseline. The following exact example records the unpaid geometry rather than hiding it in a model assumption.

Let A=1, B=2, t=1, a=1/20, L=32. Define the monotone profile

    q(s)=-1 (s<=-8),  q(s)=s/8 (-8<=s<=0),
         =s/4 (0<=s<=8),  q(s)=2 (s>=8).

Define the planar local model F(s,y)=q(s) when |y|<a and F(s,y)=q(s+L) when |y|>=a. Each normal slice is nondecreasing with signed endpoints -1,2. Put

    U(s)=sup_{0<r<=1} A_r |F|(s,0),
    H(s)=max(3/2,U(s)),
    p=(2/pi)(arcsin(a)+a sqrt(1-a^2)).                    (5.1)

Here p is the disk area fraction in |y|<a at radius 1. At general r it is p(r)=1 for r<=a and otherwise the same formula with a/r. It is nonincreasing in r, so p(r)>=p.

At s=-9 every tested disk sees |F|=1 in the strip and |F|=2 outside, up to null boundary points. Thus

    U(-9)=2-p.                                          (5.2)

At s=0 the outside-strip value is 2 on every tested disk, while within the strip |q(s')|<=1/4 for |s'|<=1. Hence for ALL 0<r<=1,

    A_r|F|(0,0) <= 2-(7/4)p(r) <= 2-(7/4)p,
    U(0)<=2-(7/4)p.                                     (5.3)

The radius-1 average also gives U(0)>=2(1-p). Since p<=4a/pi<1/4, U(0)>3/2, so the midpoint baseline does not remove the dip. At s=9 all tested values are 2, so U(9)=2. We have

    H(-9)-H(0) >= (3/4)p>0,  H(9)=2,                   (5.4)

whereas H has far-left endpoint 3/2 and far-right endpoint 2. Each normal slice of F is globally 1/4-Lipschitz in s, uniformly in y. Its averages, their supremum U, and H are therefore 1/4-Lipschitz in s. They are constant outside a finite interval, so H has finite variation. Its total variation is at least

    1/2 + (3/2)p > 1/2;                                (5.5)

as follows by the partition through -9, 0 and 9 and the two endpoint limits. This disproves the attempted universal planar analogue of (3.2) under the sole hypothesis that each normal slice is monotone. # Two prescribed orders, round 2: the scalar core of T2 is an open weighted-discrepancy problem

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. 2026-10-16. Original prose CC BY 4.0;
original code MIT. Third-party material keeps its own rights.

Nothing here is nominated as a substantial result, and nothing is counted.
The computations are the author's own finite checks. They are not
independent reviews.

The main new finding is a status result. Already in dimension one, T2 is
exactly equivalent (up to a factor 2 in the constant) to the following
question: does every weighted *one-block* 0-1 matrix have bounded
discrepancy? Cherniavskii, Kupavskii, Moiseev and Shekhovtsov posed this
question as open in arXiv:2610.16079v1, §6 (12 October 2026). It is also
exactly the boundedness of the "weighted two permutation discrepancy" of
Bansal, Rohwedder and Svensson (STOC 2022, Definition 19). Reis's 2023
weighted-discrepancy conjecture (in either of its stated forms) implies it.
Bérczi, Liu, Reis and Tarnawski's 2026 prefix-constrained basis conjecture
also implies it. So a proof of T2 would settle a recently posed open problem,
and a counterexample to T2, even at d=1, would refute two published
conjectures. The pinned one-order theorem gives nothing beyond greedy in d=1.
The missing step is therefore the coupling of the two orders, not the
dimension.

No restriction is placed on orders, coordinate signs, dimension, palettes,

## 2. Notation

For d ≥ 1 let B^d be the closed Euclidean unit ball. All suprema below are
taken in [0,∞].

* **Two-order constant.**
  D_2(d) := sup_{N, pi, rho, v ∈ (B^d)^N} min_epsilon max_{sigma,k} ||Σ_{j≤k} epsilon_{sigma(j)} v_{sigma(j)}||.
  T2 says exactly that D_2(d) ≤ C sqrt(d) for all d.
* **Interval-coverage constant.** For finitely many closed bounded intervals
  I_1,…,I_N ⊂ R with vectors v_j ∈ B^d, put
  cov_epsilon(x) := Σ_{j : x ∈ I_j} epsilon_j v_j. Then
  D_I(d) := sup min_epsilon sup_{x∈R} ||cov_epsilon(x)||.
* **One-block constant.** Fix a row order of a 0-1 matrix M. M is *one-block*
  if the ones in every column form one consecutive block of rows. Put
  D_OB(d) := sup over one-block M (m×N) and v ∈ (B^d)^N of
  min_epsilon max_r ||Σ_j M_rj epsilon_j v_j||.

When d=1, put d_j := |v_j| ∈ [0,1] and absorb sign(v_j) into epsilon_j.
Then D_OB(1) is exactly the supremum, over 0-1 one-block matrices A, of
wdisc(A) := sup_{W diagonal in [0,1]} disc(AW). Reis, Chapter 8, uses this
notion. Likewise, D_2(1) is exactly the supremum over n of the "weighted two
permutation discrepancy" C_n in BRS Definition 19, where the first order is
the identity. Relabeling the indices makes this no loss.

## 3. Exact interval reformulation (new to this project; elementary)

**Theorem 1.** For every d ≥ 1,

    D_2(d) ≤ D_OB(d) ≤ 2 D_2(d)    and    D_2(d) ≤ D_I(d) ≤ 2 D_2(d).

So T2 holds iff D_I(d) = O(sqrt d) iff D_OB(d) = O(sqrt d). In particular,
the d=1 case of T2 holds iff weighted one-block 0-1 matrices have uniformly
bounded weighted discrepancy.

*Proof.* (a) *Two orders embed as intervals.* Fix pi, rho and v. Let
a_i := pi^{-1}(i) and b_i := rho^{-1}(i), both in [N]. Put
I_i := [a_i, 2N+1−b_i]. This interval is nonempty because a_i ≤ N < N+1 ≤ 2N+1−b_i.

* For an integer x=k with 1 ≤ k ≤ N, x ∈ I_i iff a_i ≤ k. The upper
  condition k ≤ 2N+1−b_i always holds. So cov_epsilon(k) is the k-th pi-prefix.
* For x = 2N+1−k with 1 ≤ k ≤ N, the condition a_i ≤ x always holds, and
  x ≤ 2N+1−b_i iff b_i ≤ k. So cov_epsilon(x) is the k-th rho-prefix.
* The empty prefixes are zero.

Hence, for every epsilon, the two-order maximum is at most
sup_x ||cov_epsilon(x)||, and the minimum over epsilon gives D_2 ≤ D_I.
Restricting to the integer points 1,…,2N gives the 2N×N matrix whose rows,
in order, are P_1,…,P_N, Q_N,…,Q_1. Here P_k and Q_k are the k-th pi- and
rho-prefix sets. Column i has ones exactly in rows a_i,…,2N+1−b_i, which is
one block. The same signs therefore give D_2 ≤ D_OB.

(b) *Intervals reduce to two orders.* Take closed intervals I_j = [l_j, r_j]
with l_j ≤ r_j. Let sigma_L order the indices by (l_j, j) and sigma_R by (r_j, j).
For each x define L(x) := {j : l_j ≤ x} and R(x) := {j : r_j < x}.

* L(x) is an initial segment of sigma_L, because any index with smaller
  l also lies in L(x).
* R(x) is an initial segment of sigma_R, for the same reason.
* R(x) ⊆ L(x), because l_j ≤ r_j < x.
* {j : x ∈ I_j} = L(x) \ R(x).

Therefore cov_epsilon(x) = S_L(x) − S_R(x), where S_L(x) and S_R(x) are
prefix sums of epsilon_j v_j in the orders sigma_L and sigma_R. Signing this
two-order instance within D_2(d) + eta, for any eta > 0, makes every coverage
value at most 2(D_2(d) + eta). Letting eta → 0 gives D_I ≤ 2 D_2.

For a one-block matrix, the same argument applies to the integer intervals
{s_j,…,t_j} of row indices. An all-zero column is placed last in both orders.
It never lies in L(r) or R(r), so it does not change the coverage. This gives
D_OB ≤ 2 D_2. ∎

Finite exact checks of both directions are in `checks.py` (A1–A3, with a
deliberate off-by-one control in C).

**Proposition 3 (prefix-constrained bases imply the scalar core).** Suppose
Bérczi–Liu–Reis–Tarnawski's Conjecture 5 holds with constant C. That is, for
every matroid M on [n], every w ≥ 0 and every x in the base polytope, some
basis B satisfies |Σ_{i≤t} w_i(1_B(i) − x_i)| ≤ C max w for all t. Then
D_2(1) ≤ 4C.

*Proof.* Take scalars v_1,…,v_N ∈ [−1,1] and orders pi, rho.

* Let the ground set be {i^+, i^- : i ∈ [N]}, ordered as
  pi(1)^+,…,pi(N)^+, rho(1)^-,…,rho(N)^-.
* Let M be the partition matroid that chooses exactly one element of each
  pair {i^+, i^-}. The point x ≡ 1/2 lies in its base polytope.
* Give both copies of i the weight w(i^±) := |v_i| ≤ 1.
* Take the basis B from Conjecture 5 and set delta_i := +1 if i^+ ∈ B and
  −1 otherwise.

Then 1_B(i^+) − 1/2 = delta_i/2 and 1_B(i^-) − 1/2 = −delta_i/2. The t=k
prefix (k ≤ N) equals (1/2) Σ_{j≤k} |v_{pi(j)}| delta_{pi(j)}, so every
pi-prefix has absolute value at most 2C. The t=N+k prefix equals

    (1/2) Σ_all |v_i| delta_i − (1/2) Σ_{j≤k} |v_{rho(j)}| delta_{rho(j)}.

The first term is the full pi-prefix, so every rho-prefix has absolute value
at most 4C. Finally, epsilon_i := delta_i sign(v_i) with sign(0) := 1. ∎

Check M replays both identities and the derived 2E/4E bounds exactly. A

  and with it BRS's weighted two-permutation question.
* A counterexample to T2 already at d=1 would refute the O(1) form of Reis's

## 4. Where the difficulty lives

**Remark 4 (d=1 one-order is greedy).** For one order and scalars in [−1,1],
choose each sign opposite to the current prefix sum, using +1 at zero. Every
prefix then stays in [−1,1]. The induction step is |s − |v|| ≤ 1 when
s ∈ [0,1] and |v| ≤ 1, and symmetrically. So at d=1 the pinned one-order
theorem `thm:signing` adds nothing beyond this greedy bound. Yet the d=1
two-order problem is exactly the open question of Corollary 2.

This shows where the gap lies. The source's analytic machinery (filtered
Dirichlet energies, adaptive shifts, Gaussian path correlation) is what turns
O(d) into O(sqrt d) for one order. The missing step in T2 is the coupling of
two orders, and that step is already unresolved when d=1. Any proof of T2
must therefore contain a new argument for weighted one-block matrices. This
is a statement about content, not a theorem that excludes particular proof
methods.

**Remark 5 (norm classes are without loss).** Suppose T2 holds with constant
C' for families whose norms all lie in (1/2,1] ∪ {0}. Then T2 holds with 2C'.

*Proof.* Split the indices into U_b := {i : 2^{-b-1} < ||v_i|| ≤ 2^{-b}} for
b ≥ 0. Only finitely many classes are nonempty, and zero vectors get any sign.
Sign the rescaled family 2^b v_i, i ∈ U_b, in the two orders restricted to
U_b. Each prefix of pi or rho meets U_b in a prefix of the restricted order.
So each class contributes at most 2^{-b} C' sqrt d, and the classes sum to at
most 2C' sqrt d. ∎

So making all norms comparable is no restriction. In particular, the scalar
core is equivalent to its case with weights in (1/2,1]. The hard regime is
many distinct but comparable weights. With finitely many distinct weights
a_1 > … > a_K, signing each weight class by the unweighted two-permutation
bound (1, BRS §5 and the Bansal–Nikolov survey §2.3) gives at most Σ a_k.
That bound is not uniform in K.

## 5. Unconditional baseline over the full scope

This is a standard linear-algebraic bound. It adapts Bansal–Nikolov,
*Discrepancy Theory: An Algorithmic and Geometric Perspective*, Theorem 2.8
(k permutations, scalar), to vectors. The proof is self-contained and is
included so that the round has an honest unconditional bound over all
quantifiers. It is not a resolution, because it grows with N.

**Lemma 6.** Let x ∈ [−1,1]^N, let A := {i : |x_i| < 1}, and let
Lambda : R^A → R^q be linear. Put

    Q := {y ∈ [−1,1]^N : y_i = x_i for i ∉ A, Lambda(y_A − x_A) = 0}.

Then Q is a nonempty polytope, and each vertex y of Q has at least
|A| − rank(Lambda) indices i ∈ A with |y_i| = 1.

*Proof.* Q contains x and is bounded. Let K := {z ∈ R^N : z_i = 0 off A,
Lambda z_A = 0}, so dim K = |A| − rank(Lambda) =: k. Let T ⊆ A be the set of
coordinates of A that are tight at y. If some nonzero z ∈ K has z_T = 0, then
y ± tz ∈ Q for small t > 0, since the coordinates of A outside T have slack.
That would contradict y being a vertex. Hence K ∩ {z_T = 0} = {0}, and this
subspace has dimension at least k − |T|. So |T| ≥ k. ∎

**Proposition 7 (baseline).** For all d,N, every v ∈ (B^d)^N and every pi,rho,
some epsilon has every prefix in both orders of norm at most
(8d − 2)(⌊log2 N⌋ + 1).

*Proof.* Start with x^(0) = 0 and put g := 4d. Phase t runs while
A_t := {i : |x^(t−1)_i| < 1} is nonempty. It does the following.

* For each sigma ∈ {pi,rho}, list A_t in sigma-order and cut the list into
  consecutive groups of exactly g indices. Any shorter tail is left unconstrained.
* For each complete group G, impose the d scalar equations
  Σ_{i∈G}(y_i − x_i) v_i = 0.
* There are at most 2⌊|A_t|/g⌋ d ≤ |A_t|/2 equations. Let x^(t) be a vertex
  of the resulting Q.

By Lemma 6, at least |A_t|/2 coordinates of A_t become tight, so
|A_{t+1}| ≤ |A_t|/2. Hence |A_t| ≤ N/2^{t−1}, and the process stops after
T ≤ ⌊log2 N⌋+1 phases. The final x^(T) is a sign vector epsilon.

Now fix a sigma-prefix S. In phase t, only coordinates in A_t move.
S ∩ A_t is an initial segment of the active sigma-list. It is therefore a union
of complete groups, which preserve their weighted sum, together with fewer
than g further indices. Each coordinate moves by at most 2 and each
||v_i|| ≤ 1, so the prefix sum changes by at most 2(g−1) = 8d − 2 per phase.
Summing over the phases, starting from 0, gives the bound. ∎

Check B runs this exact algorithm in rational arithmetic, with d ≤ 3 and
N ≤ 40. It checks the equation count, the halving, the per-phase prefix
change and the final bound. This bound is O(d log N). T2 asks for O(sqrt d)
uniformly in N. CKMS Theorem 4 (statement read, proof not used) gives an
O(log)-type bound for weighted one-block matrices at d=1.

## 6. Mechanisms tried in this round, and where each fails

These are honest attempts at the scalar core. None of them closes the gap.
Each item records exactly where the attempt fails. Proved statements are
marked as proved; anything else is labeled heuristic or numerical.

1. **Bit-level decomposition (failed).** Write |v_i| = Σ_b beta_ib 2^{-b} and
   U_b := {i : beta_ib = 1}. If the signs satisfy |Σ_{i ∈ P ∩ U_b} epsilon_i| ≤ D_b
   for every pi- and rho-prefix P, with Σ_b 2^{-b} D_b ≤ C', then the scalar
   core holds with C'. This step is proved by the triangle inequality. The
   conditions form a system of 2(B+1) restricted prefix chains with one
   common signing. For three or more unrelated chains, unweighted discrepancy
   can be Ω(log n) (Newman–Neiman–Nikolov, as cited by CKMS Theorem 5). The
   level-b chains are not unrelated, since they restrict the same two
   permutations, but no structure was found that removes the coupling across
   levels. The geometric allowances D_b do not help at levels 0 and 1, which
   need constants. The LSV-type bit rounding cannot be used, because it needs
   independent signs at each bit level, while T2 needs one sign per index.
2. **Weighted pairing and merging (failed).** BRS Lemma 17 and survey §2.3
   use consecutive pairs in both orders. Reis's Theorem 141 merges
   weight-sorted pairs. With weights, opposite signs inside a pair leave the
   residual w_a − w_b, and the union of the two matchings ties all residual
   orientations on a cycle to one bit. The residual problem is a signing of
   cycles whose contributions are spread over both orders. It is not a
   smaller instance of the same kind, and no recursion was found in which the
   residuals shrink.
3. **Robust online game (closed in the literature).** BRS Theorem 20 shows
   that a robust constant-bound maker strategy for the one-dimensional game
   would bound D_2(1). BRS Lemma 21, whose proof was read in full, gives
   instances with values in [1/2,1] where the breaker forces
   Ω(log n / log log n). So this sufficient route cannot give a constant.
4. **Iterated rounding (logarithmic loss).** Proposition 7 loses a constant
   in each of log N phases. The Beck–Fiala alternative also fails. That
   alternative would drop a prefix row once at most t of its active
   elements are still fractional. Within one chain, the rows that still
   contain more than t fractional elements, restricted to the fractional set
   F, are |F|−t distinct nested nonempty sets. Such sets are linearly
   independent (proved). With two chains, the kept rows can therefore have
   rank |F| once |F| ≥ 2t, for example whenever the second chain adds a
   single independent row. Then no free direction remains. Some other
   accounting is needed to avoid paying in every phase.
5. **Relaxations (numerical only).** The semidefinite (vector-coloring)
   relaxation was solved with CLARABEL in exploratory scripts. On random and
   bit-reversal two-order instances with N ≤ 128, and in weight-climbing
   searches with N = 12 and 24, its value never exceeded 1. So no SDP-dual
   certificate of a large lower bound appeared at these sizes. This is
   numerical evidence, not a theorem; boundedness of the relaxation was not
   proved. For one order, BRS §6 proves SDP prefix discrepancy at most 1 via
   Banaszczyk. Their argument needs a one-order prefix theorem, and no
   two-order analogue is available.
6. **Small exact values (numerical search; one exact check).** Weight
   hill-climbing and an exact cutting-plane minimax with MILP inner solves
   (HiGHS) never found a two-order scalar instance with value ≥ 2 for N ≤ 21.
   The cutting-plane search certified the maximum over weights for Reis's
   k=3 orders as 5/3, at weights (1,5/6,1,5/6,1,2/3,1). Check E verifies this
   value exactly. That is above Reis's 8/5 for the same N but below his
   limiting lower bound 2. These searches say nothing about large N.
7. **Lifting two orders into one order (not achieved).** A route through the
   pinned theorem would need each rho-prefix to be a bounded combination of
   one-order prefix functionals of lifted vectors in O(d) dimensions with
   O(1) norms. The interval form of Theorem 1 shows what is needed:
   cov(x) = S_L(x) − S_R(x) is one *arrival* prefix minus one *departure*
   prefix, and the departures follow a second, unrelated order. No bounded
   encoding was found. Arbitrary encodings were not proved impossible.
8. **Source filter route (not retried).** The r4 obstructions are retained.
   Remark 4 explains why that route alone cannot reach the d=1 coupling.

## 7. Exact unpaid step

* **(U1) Scalar coupling.** D_I(1) < ∞. That is, finitely many closed

T2 implies (U1). This round does not prove (U1), (U2), any intersection of

## 3. U-tail is paid for the full signed strict sequence

We prove, with an absolute constant C_1, that EVERY real f in BV(R^2)

### 3.1 Sobolev tail estimate

Let g>=0 be in W11. Fix s>0 and let theta vanish on B_s, equal one outside
B_{2s}, satisfy 0<=theta<=1 and |grad theta|<=C/s. Put h=theta g. Then

    ||grad h||_1 <= integral_{|x|>s}|grad g| + C||g||_1/s.     (3.3)

For |x|>4s, every ball centered at x of radius r<=|x|/2 lies where h=g.
If such a radius attains Mg(x), then Mh(x)=Mg(x), since h<=g. The same
equality holds at Lebesgue contact points Mg(x)=g(x), because h(x)=g(x)
and Mh(x)>=h(x). The equality-set rule for W11_loc functions therefore
charges all such points to ||grad Mh||_1, at most C||grad h||_1 by the
pinned bound. The equality-set rule for Sobolev functions follows by ACL
and the one-dimensional zero-level-set derivative rule, or by the density
argument in the source appendix.

At every remaining Lebesgue point with nonzero g, some attaining radius
r>|x|/2 exists: disk averages are continuous in r, tend to zero at infinity,
and tend to g(x) at zero. This radius is an interior radial maximizer.
For a fixed x define V(r)=integral_{B(x,r)}g and let J(r) be the integral of
the nonnegative radial trace of g on the circle. On each compact annulus,
the map r -> g(x+r omega) is W11 in r with values in L1(S^1), hence has a
continuous version there. This follows by the polar change of variables,
Fubini and the radial weak derivative grad g(x+r omega) dot omega.
Consequently J is continuous, V'=J, and integration by parts gives

    grad A_r g(x) = (pi r^2)^(-1) integral_{B(x,r)} grad g,
    |integral_B grad g| <= J(r),
    partial_r A_r g(x) = J(r)/(pi r^2)-2V(r)/(pi r^3).

The formulas first hold at almost every r and then at every positive r
using these continuous versions. Joint continuity of the ball integrals
of the L1 function grad g also gives the displayed spatial derivative at
each x,r. At the maximizing radius partial_r A_r g=0, so

    |grad Mg(x)| = |grad A_r g(x)|
       <= (2/r) A_r g(x)
       <= 16 ||g||_1/(pi |x|^3).                            (3.4)

Touching supplies the equality at differentiability points of Mg; no
derivative of a selected radius is taken. Integrating (3.4) outside B_{4s}
costs 8||g||_1/s, since integral_{|x|>4s}|x|^(-3)dx=pi/(2s).
Together with (3.3) this proves

    integral_{|x|>4s}|grad Mg|
       <= C integral_{|x|>s}|grad g| + C||g||_1/s.             (3.5)

Measurability of the partition is obtained by writing the suprema over
the continuous radius families (or rational radii and compact intervals).
One can use the entire measurable equality set {Mh=Mg} as the first set;
its complement has only large-radius optimizers, up to null sets.

For T_t, the signed-band bound and passage through the increasing dyadic
bands [t,2^N t] give

    integral |grad T_t h| <= C||grad h||_1.                  (3.6)

It is Lipschitz, so there is no singular derivative. All its optimizers
exist when h is nonzero; zero input is harmless. The same equality-set
argument works. If s>=t, any remaining optimizer r>|x|/2>2s is strictly
greater than t, so (3.4) still applies. Thus (3.5) holds for T_t g as well.

## 4. Fixed positive lower radii: a BV compactness argument

For every t>0, the ORIGINAL signed strict sequence satisfies

    grad T_t |f_j| -> grad T_t |f| in L1(R^2;R^2).            (4.1)

There is no assertion that |f_j| converges strictly. The mechanism uses
domination by |Df_j| and a special feature of circles, instead of assuming
strong convergence of D|f_j|.

### 4.1 Atomless measures and the circle interface

The vector measure Du of any planar BV function has no atoms. To prove
this directly, suppose a=Du({x_0}). Its distributional curl is zero.
Test that identity with epsilon phi((x-x_0)/epsilon). Its first derivatives
are bounded uniformly and supported in a shrinking ball. The non-atomic
remainder contributes a quantity tending to zero; the atom contributes

    a_2 partial_1 phi(0)-a_1 partial_2 phi(0)=0.

Arbitrary gradients of phi at zero force a=0. Since |Du|({x_0})=|a|,
the variation is atomless too.

For ANY finite atomless positive measure lambda in the plane, at most
countably many distinct circles have positive lambda mass. Distinct
circles intersect in at most two points, of zero lambda mass. Thus any
finite family of such circles is essentially disjoint, and the sum of
their masses is bounded by lambda(R^2). For each positive integer N,
only finitely many can have mass >=1/N; their union over N is countable.
In particular, the centers of all positive-mass circles form a countable
set. Outside it, lambda(partial B(x,r))=0 for EVERY r>0.

For g in BV, A_r g is globally Lipschitz with constant at most
|Dg|(R^2)/(pi r^2), by the translation bound. Its weak gradient is

    grad A_r g(y) = Dg(B(y,r))/(pi r^2) a.e. y.              (4.2)

This is the distributional convolution identity; Fubini proves it with
the indicator disk kernel. If |Dg|(partial B(x,r))=0, that right-hand
side tends to Dg(B(x,r))/(pi r^2) as y->x: the symmetric difference of
the balls lies in an annulus of thickness |y-x| whose mass tends to zero.
The bounded weak gradient then implies differentiability of A_r g at x
with this value. In detail, on a sufficiently small ball the essential
supremum of the difference of its weak gradient from that constant is
arbitrarily small; subtract the corresponding affine function and use
the Lipschitz/weak-gradient bound on that convex ball. This bounds the
first-order remainder and proves differentiability. Thus (4.2) holds
at every such x, not merely for a fixed r almost everywhere.

Write g_j=|f_j|, g=|f|, eta_j=Dg_j and eta=Dg. We have eta_j -> eta
against C_0 tests by L1 convergence, and |eta_j|<=mu_j. If r_j->r>0
and mu(partial B(x,r))=0, then

    eta_j(B(x,r_j)) -> eta(B(x,r)).                          (4.3)

To see this, approximate the fixed disk indicator from inside and outside
by continuous cutoffs whose discrepancy is supported in a thin annulus.
The error for eta_j is bounded by mu_j of that annulus. Narrow convergence
of mu_j and then the zero boundary mass make the limsup error arbitrarily
small. The moving disks lie between disks of radii r-delta and r+delta
eventually, so the same argument gives the moving-radius assertion.

### 4.2 Optimizers and global gradient convergence

Exclude the countable union of the circle-center exceptional sets for
mu and all |Dg_j|, and the null sets where the Lipschitz functions
T_t g,T_t g_j are not differentiable. At any remaining x, all disk
averages in question are differentiable at x for ALL positive radii.

If f is nonzero, T_t g(x)>0. Convergence (3.7) holds with g_j in place
of g_epsilon. Optimizing radii r_j>=t exist, and the bound
A_r g_j(x)<=||g_j||_1/(pi r^2), together with the positive limiting value,
bounds r_j above at this fixed x. Any convergent subsequence r_j->r
therefore has r>=t and attains T_t g(x), by uniform convergence of the
averages and continuity in r of A_r g(x). Touching and (4.3) give

    grad T_t g_j(x)
       = eta_j(B(x,r_j))/(pi r_j^2)
       -> eta(B(x,r))/(pi r^2)
       = grad T_t g(x).

All possible limiting optimizers give the same last gradient. The
subsequence principle proves a.e. convergence of the full sequence.
Their gradients are bounded by sup_j mu_j(R^2)/(pi t^2), so dominated
convergence proves local L1 convergence. If f=0, strictness gives
mu_j(R^2)->0, and the global signed-band bound proves (4.1) immediately.
In the nonzero case, the T_t tail bound of Section 3 and (2.2) make
these gradients uniformly tight. Combining local convergence with that
tail bound proves the global assertion (4.1).

This extends the positive-radius proof interface to the actual topology
of B. It does NOT permit sending t to zero uniformly in j.

## 5. Fine scalar cancellation budgets really do vanish under strictness

This section concerns Df_j itself, not D|f_j|. It isolates a useful source
operation that survives staircase concentration, while explaining why it
has not solved B.

For a finite vector measure h, let phi_{k,q} be the planar product tents
on mesh 2^(-k), exactly as in `eq:hat-refinement`. Write

    h_{k,q}=integral phi_{k,q} dh,
    A_k(h)=sum_q |h_{k,q}|,
    A_k^c(h)=sum_q |c(h_{k,q})|,

where c:R^2->R is a fixed linear functional. Positivity, partition of unity
and positive refinement show that A_k(h) increases to |h|(R^2), and
A_k^c(h) increases to |c(h)|(R^2). For completeness, the upper bounds
are the triangle inequality. For the reverse limit choose a continuous
compact vector test n with |n|<=1. Then

    A_k(h) >= sum_q n(q) dot h_{k,q}
            = integral (sum_q phi_{k,q}(x)n(q)) dot dh(x).

The interpolant converges uniformly to n, and the test-field variation
formula proves the claim. Scalar tests give the c version.

Strict vector-measure convergence h_j->h also implies

    |c(h_j)|(R^2) -> |c(h)|(R^2).                            (5.1)

Here is a direct proof of the specific Reshetnyak-type fact needed.
Write h_j=sigma_j |h_j| and choose n in C_c with |n|<=1 approximating
the polar sigma of h in L1(|h|). Put

    delta_j = |h_j|(R^2)-integral n dot dh_j.

These nonnegative deficits tend to the arbitrarily small corresponding
deficit for h. Since
|sigma_j-n|^2<=2(1-n dot sigma_j), Cauchy--Schwarz bounds

    integral |sigma_j-n| d|h_j|
       <= [2 |h_j|(R^2) delta_j]^(1/2).

Replacing |c(sigma_j)| by |c(n)| costs at most ||c|| times that bound.
The integral of the continuous compact function |c(n)| against |h_j|
converges by narrow convergence of the variation measures, proved as
in (2.2). Do the same for h and then improve the polar approximation.
This proves (5.1). Existence of n follows from regularity: approximate
sigma by finitely many constant unit vectors on disjoint compact sets
and give these disjoint continuous cutoff neighborhoods.

At each FIXED k, A_k(h_j)->A_k(h) and A_k^c(h_j)->A_k^c(h). The individual
tent integrals converge; tightness bounds the contribution of all tents
outside a finite set by an arbitrarily small tail. Consequently

    lim_{k->infinity} limsup_j
       ( |h_j|(R^2)-A_k(h_j) ) = 0,                        (5.2)
    lim_{k->infinity} limsup_j
       ( |c(h_j)|(R^2)-A_k^c(h_j) ) = 0.                    (5.3)

Define scalar defects at level l by one half of the increase in the
scalar absolute tent mass, exactly as in the source. Their total from
all levels l>=k is

    sum_{l>=k,q} d^c_{l,q}
       = ( |c(h_j)|(R^2)-A_k^c(h_j) )/2.                    (5.4)

This follows by finite telescoping and the limit just proved. For smooth
inputs stopped at level m, the source's terminal density defect yields
the same right side, independent of m>=k. Thus (5.3) really makes the
entire fine scalar-pairing budget small. It is not a false claim that
||Df_j-Df|| becomes small. In the monotone staircase example, the
derivative measures are mutually singular but their relevant scalar
pairing budget is zero in the interior.

This also makes the ACTUAL discounted source prices vanish at fine
observation scales. Indeed, starting its price formula at level k and
stopping the density at any level m>=k, column normalization and the
41^2 neighboring-root multiplicity give

    sum_{l=k}^m sum_Q D_Q
       <= [41^2/(1-2^(-alpha))] sum_c sum_{l=k}^m sum_q d^c_{l,q}
       = [41^2/(2(1-2^(-alpha)))]
           sum_c ( |c(h_j)|(R^2)-A_k^c(h_j) ).                (5.5)

The terminal defects are included in the equality, just as in the
source. The fixed palette is finite. Thus (5.3) sends the limsup in j
of (5.5) to zero as k->infinity, UNIFORMLY in m>=k. No endpoint price
or spatial-weight price has been erased in this algebraic assertion;
(5.5) concerns precisely the D_Q prices defined in that source.
For a BV measure, the same numerical formula uses its terminal variation
measure in place of the terminal density; the telescoping proof is
unchanged. This observation does not extend any local analytic source
estimate to nonsmooth inputs without proof.

There is a parallel precise improvement for the neighboring-direction
budget. The source proof gives at each level l, with L=12,

    sum_q a_{l,q} E_l(q) <= C(A_l(h_j)-A_{l-L}(h_j)).

This follows there by charging the squared polar disagreements to
H_{p,l}=sum_q lambda_{pq} a_{l,q}-a_{l-L,p}, using the lower bounds on
the two relevant positive refinement coefficients. Summing from l=k
to m, every positive increment A_v-A_{v-1} occurs at most L times,
and none occurs before k-L+1. Consequently

    sum_{l=k}^m sum_q a_{l,q} E_l(q)
       <= CL( |h_j|(R^2)-A_{k-L}(h_j) ).                    (5.6)

This tends to zero in the same iterated limit by (5.2). These two
refinements identify a real vanishing-defect mechanism in the strongest
source operations. They do not complete a sharp coefficient-one estimate
for the output. The source also has initial and terminal run endpoints,
spatial-weight and winning-radius operations that must be compared with
the limiting output's calibrated flux, rather than simply bounded by
another C times the input mass.

There is a precise signed obstruction to applying (5.3) to the actual
averaged input. Across a monotone transition from -1 to +1, Df_j is
positive normal-directed with mass 2, so strictness is possible; D|f_j|
has negative and positive normal masses of size 1 each, while the
limiting |f| is locally constant. At any fixed coarse mesh before
j->infinity those opposite tent masses cancel, whereas
|D|f_j||(local patch) stays near 2. Therefore the fine scalar defect
budget for h_j=D|f_j| need not vanish. This does not contradict (5.3),
whose hypothesis is strict convergence of h_j, not domination by mu_j.

Even for nonnegative sequences, (5.2)--(5.4) alone are not presented as
a proof of U-sharp: one must compare the output's signed calibrated flux,
not replace its variation by C times an input budget. This continuation
has not completed that assembly with coefficient one.

## 6. Arbitrary monotone signed flat layers: an exact model calculation

This is a one-normal-variable, translation-invariant model, not a
globally integrable planar BV input. It does not impose a profile
assumption on B. It tests the sign-crossing obstruction in Section 5
without the old symmetric-ramp restriction.

Let q:R->R be ANY bounded nondecreasing function with limits

    q(-infinity)=-A, q(+infinity)=B,    A,B>=0.

Jumps and singular continuous parts are allowed. Put h=|q| and

    k_r(s)=2/(pi r) sqrt(1-s^2/r^2)_+,
    S_t h(s)=sup_{0<r<=t}(k_r*h)(s),
    c >= (A+B)/2,      H_{t,c}=max(c,S_t h).

If B>=A, then H_{t,c} is nondecreasing and

    Var_R H_{t,c} = (B-c)_+                 for EVERY t>0. (6.1)

If A>=B, reflection makes it nonincreasing and its variation is
(A-c)_+. Hence in all cases the answer is (max(A,B)-c)_+.
For c=(A+B)/2 this is |A-B|/2, including zero when A=B.

Proof. If c>=B>=A, the conclusion is immediate from h<=B. Suppose
B>A and c<B. The case A=0 is also immediate because h=q is
nondecreasing. For A>0 choose a crossing position z separating the
nonpositive and nonnegative parts of q; if q has a zero interval,
choose any point of that interval. In the BV chain rule in one variable,

    Dh = P-N,
    P,N >=0,    P(R)=B, N(R)=A,
    supp N subset (-infinity,z], supp P subset [z,infinity).

One can obtain this explicitly by P=D(q_+) and N=-D((-q)_+).
If q jumps across zero, P and N may both have an atom at z; this is
permitted and the displayed identity remains exact. Thus no
absolute-value strictness is assumed.

Let s be a continuity point of q with q(s)>A. Then s>=z and
P([z,s])=q(s)>A. The kernel k_r is continuous, nonnegative, even and
nonincreasing in |s|. For y in [z,s], k_r(s-y)>=k_r(s-z), whereas for
y<=z the reverse inequality holds. Therefore

    (k_r*Dh)(s)
       >= k_r(s-z)(P([z,s])-N(R)) >=0                       (6.2)

for EVERY r>0; the P mass to the right of s contributes nonnegatively.
Since k_r is continuous with compact support and Dh is finite,
k_r*Dh is continuous and is the distributional derivative of k_r*h.
Each average is C1. Inequality (6.2) at the continuity points, whose
complement is countable, makes every average nondecreasing on the
right-hand region {q>A}, and its supremum retains monotonicity there.

On the left-hand region {q<=A}, for almost every y<=s we have h(y)<=A,
and for y>=s we have h(y)<=B. Evenness of k_r gives

    (k_r*h)(s) <= (A+B)/2 <= c.                             (6.3)

Thus H_{t,c}=c on that region. There is no downward mismatch at the
boundary: the averages are continuous, their values from the left
are <=c at the boundary, and their supremum on the right is
nondecreasing and at least c after taking the maximum. The limits
of H_{t,c} are c at -infinity and B at +infinity, since q(s)->B
and the zero-radius limit is h(s) at Lebesgue points. It follows
that its variation is B-c. Reflection proves the A>B case; when
A=B all h<=A<=c. This proves (6.1).

The same conclusion holds with a nonnegative even continuous unimodal
probability kernel in place of each k_r, by the same proof. No such
generality is nominated as a theorem for the campaign.

Why this matters and why it is insufficient: a genuine flat jump's
nonlocal maximal baseline is at least (A+B)/2. A constant baseline of
that size removes the valley's extra variation for EVERY monotone
signed profile, at EVERY cap size, without symmetry or absolute
continuity. But an arbitrary planar strict sequence need not have
exactly one-dimensional monotone layers on a common patch. Turning
near-alignment of derivative polars into stability of (6.1), with
varying tangential geometry and a varying positive-radius baseline,
is NOT proved here. Nor does (6.1) settle diffuse/Cantor contact
concentration or staircase approximants in smooth contact regions.

## 7. Exact unresolved step, full scope preserved

Let nu_j=|D(Mf_j)|. These measures are bounded by the pinned C estimate;
Section 3 makes them tight for every original signed strict sequence.
The entire remaining condition is

    for EVERY sequence in (B) and EVERY weak-* cluster nu of nu_j,
                      nu <= |D(Mf)|.                       (U-sharp)

Equivalently, for every nonnegative phi in C_c(R^2), prove

    limsup_j integral phi d|D(Mf_j)|
           <= integral phi d|D(Mf)|.                        (7.1)

This condition is still unpaid for arbitrary real inputs, with all
absolutely continuous, jump and Cantor concentrations included.
The fixed-positive-radius theorem (4.1) pays compactness wherever
one can restrict optimizing radii uniformly below by t>0; it does
not provide a uniform t->0 passage.

For clarity, once U-sharp is proved, B follows with no additional
condition: from each subsequence tightness gives a further subsequence
with narrow positive-measure limit nu and convergence of total masses.
Local L1 convergence and the test-field variation formula give
|D(Mf)|<=nu. U-sharp forces equality, hence convergence of total masses.
Conversely B and local L1 convergence force equality of every cluster
by the same argument. Thus, with U-tail now paid, U-sharp is genuinely
equivalent to the full remaining assertion, not a narrowed target.

One can reduce attempts at the upper passage to smooth compactly
supported approximants without assuming their derivatives converge
strongly: if an original sequence violated the upper bound, choose
u_j in C_c^infinity with ||u_j-f_j||_1<1/j,
||grad u_j||_1 within 1/j of |Df_j|, and

    |D(Mu_j)|(R^2) >= |D(Mf_j)|(R^2)-1/j.                    (7.2)

To justify simultaneous choices, first cut off each fixed BV f_j far
away (the product-rule error is <=C||f_j||_1/R and the derivative
tail tends to zero), then mollify. These are strict smooth compact
approximations of that fixed f_j. Local L1 output convergence and
global variation lower passage ensure (7.2) eventually along the
approximation. Choose the diagonal term after j is fixed. Then u_j
is itself a smooth strict sequence tending to the ORIGINAL f, and
the violation survives. This is a reduction of counterexample
search or proof obligations, not a positive answer to B with a
regularity restriction substituted.

The attempted new mechanism is therefore concrete: fine signed
polarization defects vanish by (5.2)--(5.4), positive-radius gradients
converge by (4.1), and exact monotone sign layers lose the spurious
absolute-value budget by (6.1). What is still missing is a sharp
calibrated estimate that combines these facts for arbitrary planar
vanishing-radius winners. The pinned local estimate with a constant
C, or the individual half-jump bound, cannot be cited as that estimate.
No counterexample to B has been found.

## 8. Bounded updated primary comparison

Gonzalez-Riquelme's centered one-dimensional continuity result,
[arXiv:2109.09691v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.09691v1), Theorem 1,
assumes strong W11 convergence. Proposition 12 subtracts affine
functions and controls the derivative residual; this is the already
credited technical route for A. The derivative residual is not small
for strict staircase approximants. The present budget (5.4) instead
tracks the exact signed scalar cancellations in the pinned source.
Its vanishing does not itself assemble a sharp bound for Mf_j.

Gonzalez-Riquelme--Kosz,
[arXiv:2009.05729v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.05729v1), Theorem 1,
treats the ONE-DIMENSIONAL UNCENTERED operator. Its input norm is
|f(-infinity)|+Var(f), applied to the DIFFERENCE in continuity; this
is not the strict topology. Lemma 3 uses this norm for uniform input
and maximal convergence; Proposition 8 uses ordered partitions to
recover interval variation; Lemmas 9--12 use uncentered optimizing
intervals and flatness on the contact set. None is the planar centered
strict-sequence upper passage. This paper was newly downloaded and
its exact norm/interface and selected proof operations were read.

Lahti--Weigt,
[arXiv:2510.01936v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01936v1), Theorem 1.2
and Proposition 4.15, give individual half-jump control, with the
BV_loc regularity premise supplied here by the pinned source. Their
Theorems 1.1 and 4.14 retain the Cantor blow-up/contact hypotheses;
Remark 1.7 records singular measures with only linear blow-ups.
Their jump proof excludes small radii above upper traces and uses a
continuous positive-radius envelope. It does not bound continuous
variation accumulating at a jump along a strict sequence. Formula
(6.1) treats full model variation, but its exact flatness remains
an explicit limitation, so it does not remove this full-scope gap.
The arXiv version page checked on 2026-10-16 lists only v1.

The older fractional and optimizing-radius references and their read
limits remain in the frozen r5 contracts. No alpha=0 transfer from a
positive-order estimate is made. Search strings and primary leads are
in `search-log.md`; this bounded check is not a worldwide novelty
