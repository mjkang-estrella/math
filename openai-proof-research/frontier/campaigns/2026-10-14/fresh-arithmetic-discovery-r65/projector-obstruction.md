# Finite projector extraction: exact obstruction and limits

This is a pre-freeze construction attempt, not a candidate theorem. Source156 is stipulated. Its compact witness consists of all rank-one real orthogonal projectors in Sym_4 with trace1, at Frobenius diameter sqrt2. We tested whether a finite exact-diameter obstruction can be extracted from that witness. Structural-r16/B8C is different: its full actual README concerns complex CP2 in R8 and explicitly uses no source theorem. No r16 review was read.

## Route1: exact orthogonality extraction is impossible

Let F be any finite set of rank-one orthogonal projectors P_u in dimension k, and suppose diam F=sqrt2. Then F has a partition into at most 2^(k-1) sets of diameter strictly less than sqrt2.

Choose an orthogonal matrix R such that (Ru)_i is nonzero for every unit representative u of F and every coordinate i. Such R exist: for fixed nonzero u and i, the function R->(Ru)_i has a zero set with empty interior in O(k), and its complement is open dense. The finite intersection is nonempty (or use Haar measure, since each individual zero set has measure zero). Orient each Ru to have positive first coordinate. Assign the remaining k-1 coordinate signs as its color. Two representatives of the same color have positive product in EVERY coordinate, so their dot product is strictly positive. Thus ||P_u-P_v||_F^2=2-2(u dot v)^2<2. Each color class is finite, so the maximum of these finitely many distances is strictly less than sqrt2. Empty and singleton classes are harmless. For k=4 this gives eight parts, already fewer than ten.

This refutes the proposed exact-orthogonality finite extraction, not the stipulated compact theorem. Different finite subsets can have gaps tending to zero. In particular the proof does not produce one strict-gap eight-part cover of the full compact projector set. Source geometry.tex explicitly warns that arbitrary exclusion of orthogonal pairs does not give its smooth admissible-map reduction.

Chronology/correction: an initial message said 'NO finite subset ... can be a counterexample'; that sentence was too broad. Parent immediately identified the restriction, which the author confirmed before this note. The proved conclusion assumes the finite subset ATTAINS the ambient diameter sqrt2. For a finite subset of smaller actual diameter, same-color positive inner products need not exceed the positive minimum inner-product magnitude defining that diameter. Nothing here excludes a smaller-diameter finite projector counterexample or a finite counterexample elsewhere in R9.

This is the classical sign-vector coloring operation with a generic rotation and antipodal normalization, not a new substantial result. Actual closest-primary read: Haviv, Approximating the Orthogonality Dimension of Graphs and Hypergraphs, MFCS2019, article39, p39:2 (retained PDF; extracted lines40–125). Its elementary bound colors by {-1,0,+1}^k signs. The generic-rotation finite refinement above removes zero coordinates and identifies antipodes. No claim that Haviv prints the exact 2^(k-1) bound, or that the rest of his paper was read.

## Route2: finite nets give only near-diameter edges

Let F be a finite delta-net of X in Frobenius distance. If ten color classes on F all had diameter strictly less than sqrt2-2delta, their delta-neighborhoods in X would cover X and each would have diameter strictly less than sqrt2, contradicting source156. Thus every ten-coloring has a class with a pair at distance at least sqrt2-2delta. This is a quantitative approximate-diameter obstruction, not an exact diameter graph. Its threshold graph has chromatic number at least11 when the edges include all such near-diameter pairs and delta is small. This is a direct compact-cover consequence and not nominated.

Attempted compatibility operation: move the finitely many source vectors so that every required near-orthogonality edge becomes exactly orthogonal while retaining dimension4. Any successful such operation preserving the threshold graph would produce an orthogonal representation in R4 of a graph with chromatic number at least11. Route1 proves every finite such representation is eight-colorable. Hence this entire orthogonalization strategy is impossible, regardless of numerical precision, choice of net, or number of points. The obstruction is an actual finite graph constraint, not a counting-set countermodel.

A different operation could seek a Gram matrix in R9 realizing these edges at maximal distance without requiring rank-one-projector coordinates. The source gives no such finite rank9 positive-semidefinite completion. Simply applying a high-degree polynomial kernel can enlarge ambient dimension, so a fixed eleven-color obstruction no longer beats dimension+1. No rank-preserving completion, smaller-actual-diameter construction, or finite R9 theorem was obtained. These are precise limits, not impossibility claims for the broader finite Borsuk problem.
