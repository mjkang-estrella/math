The ordinary complex regular copy is a routine inherited deduction. I21 §2.1 identifies the repeated-row orbit with M^rho, using the nonzero scalar from the pinned pair sum-letter map. This comparison removes both of those conclusions from the proposed novelty claim.

The integral issue is specific: the old pair map's scalar contains product_r r!, and cannot be canceled uniformly in arbitrary characteristic. The new local equality filter and refined triangular letters leave exactly one signed term with coefficient +1 on the displayed triple tensor. The old factorial does not survive. The self-contained proof in candidate.md never transfers a complex splitting to an integral one by assertion.

Harman–Ryba's whole §3 was read, including the second proof and Remark 3.3. It proves each projective indecomposable occurs in the characteristic-2 cube, using the projectivity of the staircase and the occurrence of 2-regular simple composition factors in its square. This is already a direct-summand statement. It neither specifies the proposed orbit basis nor states an integral retraction. Occurrence of every indecomposable projective once is also weaker than the regular multiplicity requirement dim(D) copies of P(D) over a splitting field. The regular decomposition assertion is standard module algebra, not an additional campaign result.

Bessenrodt–Bowman–Sutton's introduction, strengthened conjecture on p. 1029, and the opening of §5 put this in context: the staircase is simple projective in characteristic 2, its square is projective, and they seek all projective indecomposables in that square. The present candidate does not solve their square conjecture and does not claim to be the first use of modular projectivity. Their Theorem 5.2 supplies ordinary square positivity for height-zero characters, not a regular integral cube splitting. The saved PDF is the published version; its CC BY 3.0 notice is retained.

Bessenrodt–Bowman's arXiv v3 §§1.4 and 3 connect symmetric/exterior squares to decomposition numbers using projective characters. Its Theorem 3.7 compares staircase multiplicities in those two squares. The ordinary square refinement and that equality are not the proposed target. The journal's 2023 published record (Algebraic Combinatorics 6(4), 863–899, DOI 10.5802/alco.294) was checked for metadata; the theorem labels used here are from the archived v3.

Ikenmeyer §§4–5 already supplies the triangular beta labeling and its uniqueness from alternating row and column palettes. That entire combinatorial fact is credited, including its use to avoid cancellation in contractions. The current proof reorganizes it into a basis-level unit projection. Neither the beta labeling nor the nonnegative-square calculation is claimed as a separate new result.
