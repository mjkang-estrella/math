## Exact obstruction to a cyclic scalar-gauge shortcut

The symmetric source bound is unchanged if edge0 is multiplied by a(x0)/b(x1), edge1 by b(x1)/c(x2), and edge2 by c(x2)/a(x0), since the three factors cancel in the cyclic product. Optimizing these positive separable gauges does not by itself yield an asymmetric Holder range.

On three n-point probability spaces take F0(i,j)=1_{i=j} and F1=F2=1. For positive gauges a_i,b_i,c_i, the cube of the product of the three gauged L3 norms is

 n^-6 [sum_i(a_i/b_i)^3] [sum_i b_i^3] [sum_i a_i^-3]
       [sum_i c_i^3] [sum_i c_i^-3].

Holder gives the product of the first three sums at least n^3; Cauchy–Schwarz gives the product of the final two at least n^2. Equal constant gauges attain both bounds. Therefore the infimum of the product is exactly n^-1/3. The desired ungauged product of Lp0,Lp1,Lp2 norms is n^-1/p0. If p0<3, their ratio n^(1/p0−1/3) diverges. Permuting the three edges treats any exponent below3, which is unavoidable in a nonconstant Holder triple with sum1/pi=1.

This is a complete obstruction to that particular norm-comparison strategy. It is not a counterexample to an asymmetric triangular Hilbert estimate: the actual transform has cancellation that this gauge-norm comparison has discarded. No numerical tests or external theorem beyond finite Holder/Cauchy–Schwarz are used. No new theorem nomination follows.
