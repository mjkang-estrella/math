## 1. A path-valued inclusion-exclusion measure

Fix a finite family Q of polygonal quads in a bounded window B. Let E={0,1}^Q with its discrete metric. For each realization of the persistent base and k rare particles with histories h, every selected subset S gives a cadlag E-valued path X_S. Define the finite signed PATH measure

 M_k(base,z,h)=sum_{S subset[k]} (-1)^(k-|S|) delta_(X_S).

Its total variation is at most2^k. If it is nonzero, then for every i there is a subset of the other selected particles for which adding i changes the E-valued path. Otherwise pair the terms with and without i and they cancel as point masses on path space. A change of E-valued paths means that ONE of the fixed quads differs at SOME time. No enumeration of times is necessary.

On a life-probe disjoint from selected locations, every base color and the screened base geometry is constant for ALL times and ALL selected subsets. The presence-change witness at any time therefore implies the SAME critical four-arm (or straight-side) event for one of the FIXED quads. The union has size |Q| per life, not a factor proportional to the number of time intervals. The forest argument in generator-and-history-majorant.md consequently proves its bound for E||M_k||TV, uniformly over all allowed lifetime marks. This is the needed time-list-independent strengthening; it follows from cancellation of signed path point masses rather than an approximation by increasingly long finite time lists.

For two replicas use the common persistent base of intensity lambda exp(-2aT), carrying TWO independent fair color fields. A selected history may affect either or both labels. Nonzero path measure still forces influence at each selected location; on each life probe choose one witnessing label and one of the fixed quads. There are at most2|Q| choices per life and at most2k lives, hence only a fixed exponential factor in k. A probe uses a single chosen color field on the common base geometry. Disjoint buffered probes use independent marked Poisson variables even if their chosen labels differ. The same forest majorant therefore holds for TOTAL VARIATION of the two-replica PATH measure, not merely its finite-time scalar kernels. This coarse tail bound needs only one arm factor per selected location. The extra two-label factor is used separately for fixed-degree error removal.

Integrating M_k over locations, the base process and lifetime marks gives a signed measure mu_(e,k) on D([0,T],E). The finite-volume Poisson perturbation identity holds for every bounded measurable path functional: the actual positive path law is sum_k mu_(e,k). At each mesh its initial absolute justification follows from the exponential moments of the finite rare-point count. The geometric forest improves this to

 ||mu_(e,k)||TV <= b_k:=C_T^k k^(-theta k), theta=1-g/2>0,       (1)

uniformly for small e, with the usual k=0 convention. Thus the series converges in total variation, not merely after evaluation on fixed time cylinders. A base occupancy cutoff localizes all tests simultaneously for the whole path, because additions only improve the shielding and the base particles never disappear. Its failure probability is exponentially small. The negligible cutoff error can be added at the end.

## 2. Temporal mark domination and compactness

Each rare initial particle has one death time conditional on falling in [0,aT], whose rescaled density is a exp(-a tau)/(1-exp(-aT))<=C_T. Each birth has a uniform birth time and either no death in the window or a later death with joint (subprobability) density at most C_T a. Thus a single one-replica history has at most two interior endpoints. In the two-replica persistent-base construction it has at most two death endpoints or a birth/death pair. The same bounded-density statements hold for each component and for two endpoints belonging to the same history; conditioning on the short-lived type is unnecessary. Marks at different locations are independent.

For k selected histories, the probability that an interior endpoint is within eta of0 or T, or that two DISTINCT endpoints are within eta of one another, is at most C_T(k+k²)eta. Coincident endpoints shared by definition between observations are counted once, not as two distinct events. The bound follows by integrating the bounded one- or two-endpoint densities. In the replica construction simultaneous changes prescribed by one shared endpoint are one vector jump.

Every path in M_k changes only at these at most2k endpoints. Conditional on marks outside the bad-time event, its jumps therefore have spacing at least eta and are separated from the endpoints of the observation interval. For finite E, paths with at most2k such jumps form a compact set in the J1 topology: pass to subsequences with constant state lists and convergent ordered jump times, then use piecewise linear time changes. The positive spacing makes the time-change logarithmic slopes tend to zero. Bounded jump count without spacing would NOT suffice.

The forest estimate is uniform in the marks, so the restriction of mu_(e,k) to paths outside this compact set has variation at most b_k C_T(k+k²)eta. First truncate the summable series at K and then choose eta small. All positive actual path laws are tight on D([0,T],E). The same reasoning yields a uniform bound on the probability of any visible jump in a deterministic interval of length eta, tending to zero with eta: sum b_k O_T(k eta). It also controls near-coincident visible jumps by sum b_k O_T(k² eta). These are consequences of the signed-measure domination, not of a coupling that removes short-lived particles.

In particular histories with a birth and death both inside the window have O_T(a) mass in the normalized rare mark law. Equation(1), uniform in marks, bounds the sum of coefficients containing any such mark by O_T(a) sum_k k b_k. This gives a whole-path LAW approximation in total variation after their removal. It does not claim that the original and reduced processes agree pathwise under their natural coupling. The same argument applies to double-death initial marks in the replica construction.

## 4. Identification and quenched convergence

Section2 also precludes fixed deterministic jump times in subsequential limits: approximate H by a finite core as in (2), and use the uniform small-interval visible-jump bound before letting the approximation error tend to zero. At such times evaluation of a cadlag path is continuous almost surely under the limiting path law. The fixed-time state marginals are the critical law by stationary snapshots and the stipulated source critical convergence. GPS Theorem2.7, actually read at extracted lines820-828, states that the boundary of every fixed quad-crossing event has zero critical-law probability. Thus subsequential path limits inherit the crossing finite-dimensional distributions established in fixed-coefficient-replacement.md.

Hence those finite-dimensional laws identify every subsequential limit with the triangular GPS dynamical path law. This proves annealed path convergence within the author assembly.

For the quenched claim, form two processes independent conditional on the initial uncolored geometry. Each marginal is tight as above, so their joint laws are tight on the PRODUCT of the two J1 path spaces. Their two-replica finite-dimensional laws converge to the independent square by fixed-coefficient-replacement.md. The same identification argument gives convergence of the pair of paths to two independent target paths. For every bounded continuous path functional Phi, if Z_e=E[Phi(omega_e)|initial uncolored geometry], then

 E Z_e -> mu(Phi),   E Z_e² -> mu(Phi)².

Thus Z_e converges in L². A countable convergence-determining bounded-Lipschitz family plus annealed tightness upgrades this to weak convergence in probability of the random conditional path laws. More explicitly, conditional mass outside an annealed compact K has expectation P(omega_e outside K), so Markov gives tightness of conditional laws in probability; on compact sets finitely many bounded-Lipschitz tests approximate the metric. This proves the exact requested conditional formulation, not merely convergence after averaging geometry.
