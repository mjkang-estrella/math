# Family333: finite-order exterior control and the exhaustion gap

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY4.0. PRE_FREEZE_SCREEN_UNPAID; no target frozen, nominee, or count. Pinned upstream truth is stipulated at fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb.

The source constructs smooth isometric immersions of all closed smooth surfaces into R4. Its finite primitive-addition theorem preserves a global preferred normal, a nowhere-zero second-form tensor and prescribed later-curve conditions. It allows arbitrarily small exterior C2 changes of the map, while keeping the exterior induced metric exactly unchanged. It does not fix the exterior map. Its exact-correction proposition explicitly does not assert regularity in the oscillation parameter.

The screened question was whether the construction can give smooth isometric immersions of arbitrary noncompact surfaces, by a compatible compact exhaustion. The source headline alone gives compact pieces after extending their metrics to closed doubles; it does not make the resulting maps compatible. No complete exhaustion construction was obtained.

## A technical finite-order refinement

The exact-correction proof has more finite-order control available than the stated C2 conclusion. For the oscillatory approximate families occurring in primitive-realization, whose every fixed derivative norm is O(z^(-j)), fix an integer m>=2. Start its smoothing schedule at an integer k0>=max(10,2m), instead of 10. The source's exact-step lemma is stated for every k>=10, with all observation orders and the two options delta'=tau^k or tau^(k+1). Its exponent table remains strictly positive.

Require throughout ||U||_(C^floor(k/2)) <= rho_z t, with rho_z=z^4, as in exact-summable-increment. The bound is available because the exponent k+(6/5)(1-floor(k/2)) exceeds one. At the initial block only finitely many inequalities are required, with constants polynomial in z^(-1). Choose mu>1 large enough, t0=z^mu, R=r(k0)=40(k0+1), and N>2k0 mu+1. As in the original initialization, the normalized metric defect tends to h_* in C^R, and its weighted norm is below B/2. The initial rescaling displacement in C^m is O(z^(2k0 mu-m)); enlarge mu if needed so this is at most rho_z/4. The later all-order recurrence and finite waiting argument are unchanged.

Since every block now has floor(k/2)>=m, summing the same increments yields ||f_tilde_z-f_z||_(C^m) <= rho_z/2 ->0. Higher derivatives still converge by increasing k; no parameter regularity or support preservation is inferred. This is a change of the starting index and finite accuracy in the existing proof, not a new analytic mechanism.

For the slow maps in primitive-realization, the displayed bound |j^2 U|_(k,s_(l+1)) <= C_k z^(d_l-sigma_l) implies ||U||_(C^m) <= C_m z^(d_l-(m-1)sigma_l) for m>=2. Choose sigma additionally so that (m-1)sigma<d1/2, with d1=1/4. The other finitely many strict scale inequalities allow this smaller sigma. Thus the slow map converges to F in C^m. All fast periodic correctors vanish on the exterior, so the preceding exact correction gives exterior C^m convergence for this one primitive operation, while its original second-form asymptotics and boundary conditions persist. The required finite R,N,L and thresholds may depend on m and the current fixed data. This is not a single family with unasserted uniform estimates over all m or arbitrary surfaces.

## Why this does not solve the global question

The finite-order improvement would control tails of a compatible exhaustion, but it does not construct the compatible extensions. Each primitive theorem and exact correction is proved on a closed surface. Its slow-map initialization rescales the entire map, and the final correction need not vanish outside the support. Cutting it off on an arbitrary collar generally changes the induced metric. There is no exact prescribed-collar extension supplied by these statements.

One must also avoid asserting that an arbitrary already-isometric compact piece can be retained approximately inside a global solution. There is an elementary metric obstruction. Let a compact flat disk carry its standard planar inclusion F into R4, and let p,q be opposite boundary points with |F(p)-F(q)|=2. A smooth surface containing this disk can be given an exterior thin handle joining small neighborhoods of p,q with a path of length less than 1, while retaining the disk metric and smooth collar compatibility. Every isometric immersion of the whole surface is length preserving on paths, hence has endpoint distance less than 1. It therefore cannot agree with F on the disk, or be uniformly within epsilon<1/2 of F there. This obstructs arbitrary-local-map extension; it does not obstruct choosing a different compatible immersion from the outset, and does not contradict the source's closed-surface theorem.

The missing operation is a global construction retaining the necessary compatibility while exhausting a noncompact surface (or a proved localized correction in an appropriate fine topology). Arbitrary compact choices followed by diagonal subsequences have neither the needed derivative bounds nor compatibility. A finite-order adjustment of the existing smoothing schedule is insufficient. No noncompact theorem, parametric theorem, embedding theorem, or relative theorem is asserted.
