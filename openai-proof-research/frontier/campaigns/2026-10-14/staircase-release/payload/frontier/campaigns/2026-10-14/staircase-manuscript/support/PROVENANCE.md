# Executable provenance

The two mathematical checkers were already copied into this interrupted task directory. They are preserved byte-for-byte, including their original headers. Their independence was established by their original review authors; running copies here is reproduction, not a fresh independent review.

- `proof_controls.py`: copied from `staircase-proof-r1/independent_controls.py`, copyright 2026 Myeongjin Kang, MIT. SHA-256 `d7a0095b305c63f5166312fe277ae134972ac9f547cafa90496315dd5742c2cb`. The original review says it was written without reading or importing candidate finite-check programs. It verifies polytabloid identification, transpose scalar +1, all 727 position-orbit cases at m=1,2,3, 3,072 arbitrary-basis composite-map equivariance cases, 1,296 content-projection cases, and four mutations.
- `coordinate_controls.py`: copied from `staircase-contribution-factory-r1/coordinate_controls.py`, copyright 2026 Myeongjin Kang, MIT. SHA-256 `ddeecf2833a382048a106d3d8a9c125e867383ed5c64b05e8cc7156517c1f200`. The original review says it was written without importing candidate or proof-review code. Its backtracking checks m=1,...,5; a separate full 720-permutation m=3 loop tests the product coordinate and mutations. Its m=2 characteristic-2 linear algebra distinguishes splitting of the trivial module in the permutation module from the Specht square. Its legacy relative license-path comment points to its source task; this package supplies the same MIT notice in LICENSE-CODE.

`checks/expected-proof_controls.json` and `checks/expected-coordinate_controls.json` preserve prior outputs; their hashes are frozen in input-manifest.json. The new `reproduce.py` runs temporary copies with assertions enabled and compares the entire output, not just a reported status. It imports neither original script as a module. It also performs a small direct basis check of the newly written equivalence explanation; that part is new implementation code, not independent review evidence.

`build.py`, `reproduce.py`, `verify_package.py`, and `freeze.py` are new packaging and verification utilities, copyright 2026 Myeongjin Kang, MIT. No code was executed at its original source location. Finite results prove no all-m assertion and establish no novelty. No Lean source or formal proof is supplied.
