# The strongest compatible reduction: Alessandrini-type quantification of the stipulated source argument

This file records why the complete frozen theorem T372L127, conditional on its correctness, is reproduced by a compatible generic route built from (a) the stipulated source372 operations, (b) the I112/I115 variational residual, (c) classical boundary-determination stability (oscillating data), and (d) the standard Alessandrini quantification pattern "small weak norm + a priori bounds + interpolation + h ~ 1/log(1/delta)". The author's proposed new integrated step (proof.md (9)-(10), transport-calculation.md) is one valid way to do one of these steps; it is not the only way, and the alternative uses only classical operations. This is a contribution assessment, not a proof gate: the reviewer's alternative is sketched at the level of operations and has NOT been written line by line or independently audited.

## 0. What the source proves and where its qualitative steps are

Source372 (stipulated) proves uniqueness by: exact jets -> `lem:common-extension`; `prop:physical-transfer` (exact gluing); `prop:carleman` (6)-type bound; `prop:physical-amplitudes` (frames, O(h) L2 convergence, weighted first-derivative bound); `prop:transport-matching` (weak limits of transferred columns along a subsequence; supported comparison on a.e. plane; `lem:supported-transport` (iii) to extend to every plane; distributional dbar_v differentiation of the EXACT equation and `lem:supported-transport` (i) to get holomorphy); `lem:conic-bundle` + `prop:comparison-rigidity` (algebra of holomorphic sections on P^1); `lem:finite-type` (ODE uniqueness).

Qualitative steps that must be quantified: (Q1) exact jets; (Q2) exact transfer; (Q3) weak limits as tau -> infinity; (Q4) a.e.-plane to every-plane via uniqueness; (Q5) dbar_v uniqueness; (Q6) vanishing of holomorphic sections / rank argument; (Q7) ODE uniqueness. Also (Q8) constants depending on the coefficients must become class-uniform.

## 1. Generic quantification of each step

(Q1) Boundary jets. For the conductivity equation, Hölder stability of boundary values and all normal derivatives from (local) DN data via oscillating boundary data is classical: Alessandrini (1988) for the values and first derivative, Sylvester-Uhlmann CPAM 41 (1988) continuous dependence at the boundary, Kang-Yun SIAM J. Math. Anal. 34 (2002) Hölder stability of the boundary reconstruction of values and derivatives (abstract). For Lame, Akamatsu-Nakamura-Steinberg (1991) already give stability of boundary values from the DN map (abstract), and Nakamura-Uhlmann (1995)/Lin-Nakamura (2017)/Tan-Liu (2022) give the exact higher jets qualitatively. The author's boundary-calculation.md is exactly the classical scheme: localized oscillating data phi_N = eta(y'/t) e^{i y_1/t^2} a with t = N^{-1/2}, frozen boundary-layer profile with Jordan term, finite-order quasimode recurrence, energy identity, triangular two-polarization system, recursion t_m <= C[(delta+t_{m-1})N^m + N^{-1/2}], exponents b_m = b_{m-1}/(2m+1), then interpolation between the C^0 jet bound and the uniform tangential C^101 bound. Every ingredient is a standard boundary-layer/quasimode operation (the author says so: "classical boundary-layer operations of the type used by Lin-Nakamura"). New application to Lame jets of order up to 119, not a new mechanism.

Collar replacement with C delta^(a/10) error in C^100 and the Lipschitz bound ||Lambda_2' - Lambda_2|| <= C(||lambda_2'-lambda_2||_inf + ||mu_2'-mu_2||_inf) (energy identity) are routine.

(Q2) Noisy transfer. Replace exact gluing by the pasted field plus the coercive correction z with ||z||_{H^1} <= C delta e^{C/h}; this is the I112/I115 variational residual, credited by the author. Routine.

(Q3) Finite tau instead of weak limits. With h ~ 1/log(1/d) chosen so that d e^{C/h} <= d^{1/2}, the source's own identities give (6) and the O(h) residual of the finite-tau second-medium normalized columns in a weak norm (the source's passage-to-the-limit paragraph in `prop:transport-matching` is precisely the statement that the tau^{-1} R_2 terms are O(h) when tested against smooth functions). The author's (9) is this computation. Routine given the source.

(Q4)-(Q5) Every plane and holomorphy, in weak norms. Here the author inserts the supported least-squares projection with its adjoint normal equation (transport-calculation.md) to convert the H^{-2} residual to a C^12 residual before running the holomorphic argument. A compatible alternative stays in weak norms:

  - `lem:supported-transport` (i) is stated for DISTRIBUTIONS with compact support; the same contradiction argument (ellipticity pre-estimate + Rellich + (i)) gives the supported estimate ||W||_{H^{s+1}} <= C_s ||P W||_{H^s} for supported W at every real s, in particular s = -3, -4, with constants locally uniform in parameters, and (by the smooth operator-valued multiplier property of the left inverse T_{rho,s}) jointly in mixed norms with negative regularity in the parameters (t, direction).
  - The approximate comparison G_h(x, v) = I + chi (V_2 - V_1) Y_1^{-1}, patched across direction charts and transverse plateaus with partitions of unity in (v, t) (annihilated by D_theta, as the author notes), is L2 in (x, v), equals I outside a fixed x-ball for every v, and satisfies P_{theta(v)}(G_h - I) - f_{theta(v)} = R_h with ||R_h||_{H^{-2}} <= C epsilon uniformly in v, epsilon = h + delta e^{C/h}.
  - In a holomorphic chart with a holomorphic frame of E (x-independent), P_{theta(v)} and f_{theta(v)} are holomorphic in v. Hence, distributionally, P_theta (dbar_v G_h) = dbar_v R_h, and ||dbar_v R_h||_{H^{-2}_x H^{-1}_v} <= C epsilon. The supported negative-index estimate gives ||dbar_v G_h||_{H^{-1}_x H^{-1}_v} (or any convenient weak mixed norm) <= C epsilon. No parameter derivatives of frames, CGOs or least-squares inverses are needed; the source's own device (differentiate the equation in dbar_v at fixed x, use supported uniqueness) is applied to the approximate equation in the distributional sense.
  - Hodge decomposition on the compact conic: G_h = Pi G_h + dbar^* N dbar G_h with Pi the projection onto the finite-dimensional holomorphic sections of End(E); ||G_h - Pi G_h||_{weak} <= C ||dbar_v G_h||_{weak}. So Gamma_h = Pi G_h = [F, 0; A, f] (by `lem:conic-bundle`: no holomorphic sections of H, f constant in v) with x-coefficients in a negative Sobolev space, and Gamma_h - G_h = O(epsilon) weakly.
  - The transport equation for Gamma_h has right side O(epsilon) in a weak norm; it is holomorphic in v with finite-dimensional v-structure, so each x-coefficient is O(epsilon) in H^{-k}(x). The block algebra of `prop:comparison-rigidity` (F theta - theta f, (D_theta f) I_H - theta A) consists of x-independent finite-dimensional linear maps plus the operator D_theta; their injectivity (quotient-bundle argument; rank-one versus rank-two) gives ||F - f I||, ||A||, ||grad f|| = O(epsilon) in H^{-k}; f = 1 outside a fixed ball gives ||f - 1||_{H^{-k+1}} = O(epsilon); hence G_h - I = O(epsilon) in H^{-k}.
  - Then f_theta = M_{2,theta} - M_{1,theta} = P_theta (G_h - I) - R_h = O(epsilon) in H^{-k-1}(x) for a spanning set of directions. Since M_j are built from <= 2 derivatives of coefficients with C^100 bounds, Sobolev interpolation gives ||M_1 - M_2||_{C^4} <= C epsilon^b with an explicit b > 0. This is the standard Alessandrini step (smallness in a negative norm + a priori regularity => smallness in a positive norm with a power loss).

(Q6) Already contained above: the rigidity algebra is finite-dimensional and linear, so its quantitative form is norm equivalence. Routine.

(Q7) `lem:finite-type` with forcing: Gronwall along straight segments from the exterior, exactly as the author's Section 6 and as the source's ODE uniqueness. Routine.

(Q8) Uniform constants. The source's constants depend on smooth coefficients. Uniformity over the C^1000-bounded class is obtained by the finite-regularity compactness argument of proof.md Section 2 (Cauchy-transform frames exist and perturb at C^{s,alpha}; finite cover of the C^{70,1/2}-compact closure by contraction neighborhoods (4); Carleman constants use finitely many derivatives; supported coercivity by Rellich contradiction with the holomorphic identity theorem valid for C^1 frames). This is the standard way to make an argument uniform over a bounded finite-regularity class (compactness in a weaker topology plus persistence of the construction at finite regularity). It is needed by both the author's route and the alternative; it is careful bookkeeping, not a new mechanism.

Final assembly: h = C'/log(1/d) with d = C(delta + delta^{a/10}), giving [log(1/delta)]^{-b}; large delta absorbed by the L^inf bound 2M; delta = 0 by the stipulated theorem. This is the author's Section 7 and is the standard closing.

## 2. Assessment of the author's "proposed new integrated step"

The least-squares/adjoint-pairing step (transport-calculation.md (2)-(6)) is correct in structure as far as read: the normal-equation residual Q_j e solves P^dagger Q_j e = 0 in the interior, hence is smooth there, and pairing the weak residual against it with a strict support margin yields ||e||_{H^j} <= C epsilon^{1/2}. This is an Aubin-Nitsche-type duality argument (regularity of the adjoint problem converts a weak residual into a strong bound for a best approximation). It is elementary, about one page, and uses only elliptic interior regularity and Hilbert-space projection. It is an acceptable way to pass (Q4)-(Q5), but the weak-norm alternative above shows it is not forced by the problem: the holomorphic structure lives in the direction variable, where the exact operator is holomorphic, so distributional differentiation is clean and no strong residual is needed before the rigidity step. In either form the step is a standard functional-analytic conversion, not a new analytic mechanism.

The finite-regularity frame cover (proof.md Section 2) is the expected uniformization of the source's frame lemma, whose only genuinely global input (Oka/Grauert trivialization over the disk) is reduced by the author to perturbation of a fixed frame within a finite cover. Standard compactness bookkeeping.

## 3. Why this is not a mere "naive route fails" verdict

The brief warns that failure of a naive route does not establish substantiality, and conversely that the absence of an identical statement does not either. The reduction above does not fail: each qualitative step of the stipulated proof has a direct, classical quantitative counterpart, and the author's own packet performs most of them (noisy transfer, O(h) weak residual, uniform constants, Gronwall, boundary induction, final log choice) with the single non-generic-looking device replaced here by a weak-norm argument of the same classical type. The theorem is therefore a routine (though long) Alessandrini-type quantification of the stipulated uniqueness theorem.

## 4. Limits of this assessment

The alternative in Section 1 (Q4)-(Q5) is a reviewer sketch. Points that a full write-up must check: the joint mixed-norm form of the negative-index supported estimate; measurability/distributional regularity of the patched G_h in v for fixed h; the exact Sobolev indices through the Hodge projection and interpolation. None of these is expected to need a new idea; if any failed, the author's least-squares route remains available and is itself classical in type, so the verdict would not change. The verdict is conditional on the author's proof being correct; a correctness failure is a separate matter for the proof review.
