## 3. Literature answer to the parent's questions

(a) Is log stability for both Lamé coefficients from the full 3D elastic DN map, without boundary agreement, already in the literature? No. Eight targeted searches (public/search-receipts.json) and the bodies/abstracts listed in public/literature-comparison.md find only: finite-dimensional or piecewise-constant Lipschitz stability (Beretta–Francini–Vessella IPI 2014; Beretta–Francini–Morassi–Rosset–Vessella IP 2014; Eberle–Harrach 2019/2020; arXiv:2505.05116; Cârstea arXiv:2605.06354, Hölder-from-uniqueness for finite-dimensional analytic classes only), boundary-value stability (ANS 1991), and uniqueness results (NU 1994/2003 under small grad mu, Eskin–Ralston 2002/2004 perturbative, IY 2015 in 2D, IUY partial data with mu constant, CJLT 2609.20134 structured, the pinned source, and the independent Diao–Durdiev arXiv:2610.14741v1 of 10 Oct 2026, whose full body contains no stability statement). Since unconditional uniqueness for the full smooth class was itself open until September/October 2026 (stated in both the pinned source introduction and Diao–Durdiev Section 1.2), no unconditional full-class stability theorem can predate it. REDISCOVERED is excluded.

(b) Does source372 plus standard Alessandrini-style quantification already give the result routinely? Yes, in the sense relevant to this gate. Each qualitative step of the stipulated proof has a classical quantitative counterpart, and the author's packet itself performs most of them (noisy transfer = I115 residual; O(h) weak transport residual = the source's own limit computation at finite tau; uniform constants = finite-regularity compactness; approximate conic rigidity = finite-dimensional norm equivalence; finite type = Gronwall; boundary jets = oscillating-data induction; closing h = C'/log(1/d)). The one step the author presents as new, the weak-to-strong residual conversion, is replaced in public/generic-reduction.md Section 1 (Q4)–(Q5) by running the source's own dbar_v-differentiation and supported uniqueness on the approximate comparison in negative Sobolev norms (the exact operator is holomorphic in the direction, so distributional differentiation is clean; `lem:supported-transport` (i) is already a distributional statement, so the supported estimate extends to negative index), followed by Hodge projection on P^1 and interpolation against the C^100 coefficient bounds. That alternative is a reviewer sketch and is not independently audited; even without it, the author's device (normal-equation residual is a smooth interior adjoint solution; pair the weak residual against it) is a one-page duality argument of standard type.

(c) Are the weak-to-strong supported least-squares residual and the uniform finite-regularity frame cover genuine unpaid operations or classical? Classical in type. The least-squares/adjoint step uses Hilbert projection, interior elliptic regularity of P^dagger Q_j, and a support margin; it is the Aubin–Nitsche pattern. The frame cover uses that Cauchy-transform frames exist and perturb by contraction at C^{s,alpha} and that the C^1000-bounded class is precompact in C^{70,1/2}; this is the standard way of making a smooth-coefficient argument uniform over a bounded finite-regularity class. Both are necessary bookkeeping for the quantification, not new analytic mechanisms. The boundary-layer calculation is the classical Alessandrini/Kang–Yun oscillating-data scheme carried to Lamé jets of order 119 with the full Jordan profile; ANS 1991 already covers order zero.

(d) Comparison with I112/I115/I118/I121, Rüland–Salo, Cekić. I112's Q target (metric/connection, loglog) and P112 (partial data) are different and remain open; the author does not claim them. Rüland–Salo Prop 6.1 is scalar with known support and a linear integral identity; it does not supply the constrained elastic comparison but is the generic log-stability template the present argument follows. Cekić's argument is qualitative; its holomorphic-parameter device is exactly what is quantified here.

Project calibration: I51/R013 awarded SUBSTANTIAL to a finite-error bridge where "the checked operations do not form a noncircular routine reduction"; I256 classified a quantitative extension with "meaningful uniformity" as TECHNICAL because, once the structural bridge was in place, the increment consisted of explicit estimates and generic variational/compactness steps. The quantitative increment is: variational residual (paid), finite-tau weak residual (direct), weak-to-strong or weak-norm propagation (classical functional analysis), finite-dimensional norm equivalence, Gronwall, oscillating-data boundary induction (classical), finite-regularity compactness (classical), interpolation and the log choice (classical). The author's own text anticipates this ("A compatible generic reduction may classify the complete theorem as technical").

Distinguishing new-to-project from new-to-literature: the theorem is both new-to-project and (to the limits of the recorded search) new-to-literature. Neither fact changes the technical classification under the stated standard.
