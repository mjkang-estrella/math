## 3. Sparse times do not destroy the cubic Jacobian payment

Let D be a bounded measurable subset of R3 of positive volume q_D. Suppose b:D->R3 is C1 on finitely or countably many open pieces covering D up to a null set. We can exhaust the pieces by relatively compact subsets for the area formula. Let A subset D x [0,1] be measurable, with |A_s|=lambda for almost every s in D, 0<lambda<=1. Starting with |A_s|>=lambda, fibrewise initial-time truncation gives such a measurable subevent. Put

    F(s,t)=(b(s)+ts,t),   J(s,t)=|det(Db(s)+tI_3)|.

For any monic cubic P, and any measurable A_s of length lambda,

    |{t in A_s: |P(t)| < (lambda/12)^3}| <= lambda/2.

Indeed factor P over C. If the product of the three distances is smaller than (lambda/12)^3, one distance is smaller than lambda/12. Each such real-time set is contained in an interval about the real part of a root of length lambda/6. The union of three has length at most lambda/2. No assumptions on reality, separation or location of the roots are required.

It follows that

    integral_{A_s} J(s,t) dt >= lambda^4/3456,
    integral_{A_s} min(J(s,t),lambda^3) dt >= lambda^4/3456.                (1)

The second integral is also at most lambda^4. These inequalities hold even if A_s has many components and depends on s. There is no appeal to independent choices of a good time and a good line. The same proof in n spatial slope coordinates gives power n+1.

This repairs the shortcut that insists a shading must contain a time interval of length comparable to lambda. That requirement is unnecessary for the determinant integral. The repair is an elementary extension of the source/primary root-exclusion calculation, not a substantial nominee.

If F_t has at most B regular preimages almost everywhere, the area formula applied to A gives

    |F(A)| >= c B^(-1) lambda^4 |D|.                                    (2)

Critical parameter points contribute zero to the Jacobian integral. Countable compact exhaustion handles unbounded derivatives on C1 pieces; the argument does not need a global Lipschitz constant for b. For a fixed semialgebraic b, B depends on its format by the component bound for regular fibres. For a delta-scale cell union with complexity growing like a power of delta^(-1), that dependence is not an arbitrary-small power loss. No estimate B<=delta^(-epsilon) follows from finite complexity alone.

## 4. Why a uniform raw-preimage shortcut is too strong

Here is an actual selector example, with straight lines and no hypothetical scalar measure substituted for line geometry. Partition D=[0,1]^3, ignoring boundaries, into cubes D_i of side r=1/m, with centres c_i. Fix t0=1/2, and set

    b(s)=-t0 c_i   for s in D_i.

This is a piecewise constant semialgebraic selector. At time t,

    F_t(s)=t(s-c_i)+(t-t0)c_i,
    J(s,t)=t^3.

For |t-t0|<=c r with a sufficiently small absolute c, all the images F_t(D_i) have side comparable to r and share a cube of side comparable to r. Therefore regular multiplicity is at least c r^(-3) on a spacetime region of volume comparable to r^4. Every selected line can be shaded for a common interval of length lambda comparable to r inside a box of spatial side O(r). The entire shaded union has volume O(r^4), and the actual target lambda^4|D| has exactly that order.

Thus a uniformly bounded raw multiplicity for every witnessing selector is false even in a family consistent with the desired sharp estimate. The lower bound obtained by using B~r^(-3) in (2) wastes three powers of r. This example does NOT refute (T), (S), a better selector, or any source theorem. In fact a better centering b(s)=-t0 s for the same containing box has a single regular branch away from t0. The problem is the raw-preimage criterion applied indiscriminately to the given witnesses.

## 5. A compatible repair: cap the Jacobian before transporting mass

Define the positive measure

    dnu_lambda(s,t)=1_A(s,t) min(J(s,t),lambda^3) ds dt,
    rho_lambda=F_# nu_lambda.

By (1),

    c lambda^4 q_D <= ||rho_lambda|| <= lambda^4 q_D.                     (3)

The area formula gives its density almost everywhere,

    R_lambda(x,t)= sum_{s: b(s)+ts=x, (s,t) in A, J(s,t)>0}
                     min(1,lambda^3/J(s,t)).                            (4)

The critical set has zero nu_lambda mass. This is a genuine common-witness pushforward; all times and slopes in (4) are the same variables as in the original line family.

For the preceding example J~1 and lambda~r, so each branch contributes O(r^3). The r^(-3) coincident branches give bounded R_lambda. For the single-centre focusing selector b(s)=-t0s, there is at most one regular branch and hence R_lambda<=1, including near the time where J vanishes. Capping accommodates both examples without changing their lines or the fourth density power.

A pointwise bound for every selector is still too much. For example, on the same cubes put

    b(s)=(-t0+lambda)s-lambda c_i.

At t=t0, all image cubes have side lambda r and J=lambda^3. For |t-t0|<=c lambda r, a common inner spatial cube remains and every branch has weight bounded below by a constant. Thus R_lambda is of order r^(-3) there. This large-overlap spacetime region has volume O((lambda r)^4), so its transported mass is O(lambda^4 r): only an O(r) fraction of (3). This second example defeats an L-infinity assertion but leaves room for a substantial-mass estimate. It is again not a Kakeya counterexample.

A sufficient remaining statement, uniform in the relevant finite configurations, would be

    integral_{R_{lambda,delta} <= C_epsilon delta^(-epsilon)}
             R_{lambda,delta} >= c_epsilon lambda^4 q_D,                (5)

where R_{lambda,delta} is spatial smoothing at radius C delta at each fixed time. It has the same mass and is supported in a corresponding neighbourhood of the actual shading. For unions of delta-cells that neighbourhood has comparable volume. Equation (5) would imply (S). It is NOT proved here and is not a nominee or a claim of novelty. It is a concrete sufficient transport operation, stronger than the two diagnostic examples require but weaker than uniform raw or capped multiplicity bounds.

This model applies to actual finite tubes without assuming a smooth witness parametrization. In a fixed slope chart choose, by transverse Fubini, one parallel core in each tube with time shading at least c lambda. Thicken each separated slope to a disjoint ball of radius c delta; keep its chosen intercept and the same time set on that ball. Perturbing the slope moves the core only O(delta). This gives D of volume q_D~N delta^3 and a piecewise constant b, so J=t^3 on each piece and (1)--(4) apply. The original support is enlarged only a fixed number of cells. Constants and the reduction from Euclidean arclength to chart time are fixed. Thus no regular parametrization assumption was smuggled into (5).

Conversely, simply naming (5) is not progress on the full maximal conjecture: it is a sufficient repackaging of the unresolved overlap control. The next sections test it against stronger available operations rather than stopping at this reformulation.

## 7. A direct anisotropic K3 repair and its exact range

There is a useful geometric estimate beyond the preceding failed pointwise shortcut. Suppose all chart slopes s=(u,v) of a delta-separated finite family satisfy |v-v_*|<=theta, delta<=theta<=1. Intercepts remain arbitrary in a fixed bounded box. Then the stipulated K3 theorem gives, for every eta>0,

    |union Y(T)| >= c_eta delta^eta (delta/theta)
                               lambda^3 N delta^3.                    (9)

Here is the full reduction, including the extra normal parameter. Partition v into intervals of length c delta. For each interval centred at v_j, shear w to w-v_j t. Partition the corresponding normal intercept into intervals of length c delta. Within a group all transformed tubes lie in a fixed O(delta)-thick horizontal slab, for their whole chart time. Projected u-slopes have bounded delta-multiplicity: a projected delta-ball and one v-bin fit inside a bounded number of original delta-separated slope cells. A bounded colouring makes them separated.

For a fixed group and fixed transformed height z, let a_i(z)=|Y_i intersect {w=z}|/delta^2. Projected slice shadings lie in three-dimensional tubes of radius C delta and fixed length. Angular-cap thickening and K3 give

    delta^2 sum_i a_i(z)^3 <= C_eta delta^(-eta) |U_group,z|.

For each tube, integral a_i(z) dz>=c lambda delta, and its z-support lies in an interval of length O(delta). Jensen therefore gives integral a_i(z)^3 dz>=c lambda^3 delta. Integrating the previous display yields

    |U_group| >= c_eta delta^eta lambda^3 (# group) delta^3.

At a point, only boundedly many intercept groups of any fixed v-bin can occur. Summing their unions costs O(1). There are O(theta/delta) v-bins, each using a volume-preserving shear; summing over them costs that factor. This proves (9). Arbitrary measurable shadings are allowed. No lower bound on the length of a shaded interval is used.

Relative to (S), the extra factor in (9) is delta/(theta lambda), apart from the arbitrary-small power loss. It pays the requested density power for theta<=c delta/lambda. When lambda=delta^a with 0<a<1, this is the concrete range theta<=c delta^(1-a). It is not merely the exact-coplanar case.

For theta=1, (9) becomes a dimension-three estimate lambda^3 delta (N delta^3). The primary estimate (P) is stronger for delta<=lambda<=1: ignoring arbitrarily small losses, their ratio is (lambda/delta)^(d0-3)>=1. Thus this calculation does not replace the strongest baseline by a weaker projection argument.

A point-dependent normal is different. If the union is divided into G sets with different normal labels and (9) is applied independently, the separate supports can overlap G times. Nothing in the sum supplies G=delta^(-o(1)), or a compensating retained density. In the actual source, chart labels are recovered by specific posterior and original-law arguments. The known-atlas property cannot be inferred just from local near-planarity. This is the geometric compatibility problem that the fixed-normal estimate leaves.

## 9. Importing the K4 framework: density growth survives, but the probability budget changes

It would be too quick to say merely that K4 is qualitative. Its actual framework supplies substantial quantitative operations, so the import was tested at the density level. For clarity take q_D comparable to one, a full direction chart with bounded slope density, and suppose a putative bad configuration has shaded volume

    |U| <= delta^sigma lambda^4,   lambda=delta^a.

After the usual bounded smoothing, the original prior is a probability on lines and the unnormalized selected incidence law has total mass comparable to lambda. At terminal hidden-bin width delta, observation-space volume is O(|U|/delta). On a comparable-density refinement retaining lambda times a subpower, the terminal density therefore obeys

    n(1) >= delta^(1-sigma+o(1)) lambda^(-3),
    m(1)=delta^(-1)n(1) >= delta^(-sigma+o(1))lambda^(-3).                (11)

The projected full-line law has, by K3 duality, an L^{3/2} bound with arbitrary-small power loss. A selected comparable-density level of mass lambda has density n(0) satisfying

    lambda n(0)^(1/2) <= C_eta delta^(-3eta/2),
    n(0) <= C_eta delta^(-3eta)lambda^(-2).                              (12)

Consequently the initial hidden-density gain is at least of order

    m(1)/n(0) >= c_eta delta^(-sigma+3eta+o(1))lambda^(-1).                (13)

There is ample positive logarithmic growth. Lack of initial density growth is NOT the correct obstruction. Equations (11)--(13) are scalar necessary estimates; they do not establish a new cap-class reduction with all source contracts.

The actual cap-class exact problem requires its selected incidence mass to be inverse-subpower relative to a probability trajectory prior times independent uniform time. A law of mass lambda=delta^a, a>0 fixed, is outside that definition. Dividing the selected law by lambda fixes its total mass but destroys domination by the same product prior. Conditioning a line on success does not fix this: the time section still has length lambda, and its normalized density there is lambda^(-1). A time-dependent selected event is not a new independent-time prior.

The budget issue is concrete in the source's saturation proof. The assignment budget is sum omega_c q_c nu_c<=1. Deleting charts with q_c nu_c>H m_c costs at most H^(-1) absolute mass. To retain half of a total selected mass lambda, this argument needs H at least 2/lambda. It therefore only compares m_c/q_c to nu_c with a polynomial lambda-loss. That is not the source's subpower equivalence. Similarly, cleaning L layers at total loss <=lambda/2 requires thresholds of order lambda/L against the original assignment budgets. No density normalization cancels these retained fractions.

The candidate-conversion proof makes the same distinction explicit in another place. Its bad-pair normalization absorbs beta_n^(-1), with beta_n=N^(-o(1)), into fixed tolerances in a comparison at R=N^c. If beta_n=N^(-a), the same multiplier is R^(a/c). To fit an allowance R^epsilon_* requires a/c below that tolerance. A fixed positive a cannot be absorbed by saying the original resolution is large. One would need a new loss-balanced construction or a quantitative gain large enough relative to a; neither is established here. The already-paid original-law list recovery does not manufacture this missing mass.

Temporal compression also has a geometric obstruction. A set of marked bins can be spread almost uniformly: mark one delta-bin in each period delta/lambda. Blocks larger than that period have relative density comparable to lambda. Compressing their cumulative marked-time measure to [0,1] produces a nonlinear time substitution; b+ts ceases to be affine in the new time. Alternatively, going down to individual marked bins makes them dense but uses time length delta and consumes the available resolution. The source's full-time polynomial bounds, slope-volume test and normalization interfaces cannot be retained by this compression without further work. This is only a diagnostic of that shortcut, not a retry of the protected sparse-sampling target.
