# A full-dimensional two-matrix normal slice

This is a concrete encoding construction toward the frozen target, not an entropy transformation theorem. It avoids the atomic-marker and Gaussian-entry collapse in encoding-attempt.md.

Fix k>=2. Let D=diag(1,...,k), and let B be the real symmetric adjacency matrix of a spanning tree on{1,...,k}. The simultaneous commutant of D,B in Mk(C) is C I: commuting with D forces diagonality, and every tree edge equates the corresponding diagonal entries.

Equip pairs of Hermitian matrices with inner product sum_r tr_k(H_r K_r). The infinitesimal conjugation map is

 L(H)=(i[H,D],i[H,B]), H=H*.

Its kernel is R I, so its real rank is k²−1. Let V be its orthogonal complement in (Mk^sa)^2. Then dim_R V=2k²−(k²−1)=k²+1. Equivalently V consists of pairs(A1,A2) satisfying [D,A1]+[B,A2]=0. Choose an orthonormal real basis V_a=(V_a1,V_a2) of V. For any bounded self-adjoint n=k²+1 tuple X, define

 Z_r(epsilon)=D_r tensor1+epsilon sum_a V_ar tensor X_a,
 D_1=D, D_2=B.

This is an exactly two-variable bounded self-adjoint tuple in Mk(M), with normalized tensor trace. Source298 gives precisely n=k²+1 with k=2^32. Only common scalar multiplication of X is needed in this slice construction; no unproved anisotropic transformation rule for chi-star is assumed.

At matrix size d, tensoring the coefficient calculation with Hermitian d-by-d matrices gives the orthogonal tangent decomposition

 (M_(kd)^sa)^2 = orbit tangent direct-sum [V tensor_R M_d^sa].

The orbit is U(kd)/(I_k tensor U(d)) at the base pair. Its dimension is(k²−1)d² and the slice dimension is(k²+1)d². Thus they sum to the full ambient2k²d². There is no dimension deficit demanding a perturbing noise coordinate. The tensor-product statement can be verified by expanding both Hermitian factors in real Hermitian bases; the base commutator acts only on the first factor.

This is an infinitesimal decomposition. A local slice for the compact unitary action identifies nearby pairs up to the stabilizer, but a dimension-free tubular radius and its Jacobian in normalized matrix norms still require proof before using it in microstates volumes. The stabilizer acts by simultaneous conjugation on all X_a, exactly the original microstates symmetry. Merely counting dimensions is not an entropy estimate.

## An explicit polar chart with the same count

An alternative local chart uses first matrix diag(lambda_i I+epsilon A_i), with disjoint spectral bands. Its second matrix has k Hermitian diagonal blocks; on k−1 tree edges it has positive invertible Hermitian blocks; on all remaining unordered edges it has arbitrary complex blocks, with adjoints in reverse positions. The number of self-adjoint coefficient blocks is

 k+k+(k−1)+2[k(k−1)/2−(k−1)]=k²+1.

Spectral projections of the first matrix recover the k block subspaces. Polar parts of tree-edge compressions then synchronize their frames, beginning with one arbitrary root frame. Recursing along the tree makes every tree-edge block positive. There is no cycle-consistency condition because the graph is a tree. All resulting coefficient blocks are determined up to a common root unitary. Positivity and separation can be guaranteed by fixed scalar centers and sufficiently small perturbations of bounded input coordinates.

This gives exact finite-dimensional recovery on the open set where band ranks agree and tree-edge compressions are invertible. For microstates, ranks can differ by o(d); controlling those exceptional directions uniformly through the moment-accuracy and matrix-size limits remains part of the needed volume proof. The normal slice above makes the tangent metric explicit; the polar chart makes recoverability explicit. Neither by itself compares chi and chi-star.

## Noise covariance and the required new analytic estimate

Choose the finite coefficient bases orthonormally for normalized tr_k. A pair of scalar standard semicircular noises free from Mk and M is represented, at the entry level, by2k² freely independent standard semicircular coefficients divided by k. The same normalization holds for GUE matrices: coefficient matrices in size d have covariance1/(k²d). Orthogonal projection to the normal slice retains k²+1 such coefficients. At noise time t comparable to epsilon², the normal signal sees effective entry-noise time

 u=t/(epsilon² k²).

The orbital coefficients are independent Gaussian directions at the linearized level. This alone does not allow deleting their nonmicrostates Fisher contribution: conditioning on fixed matrix units changes the conjugate-variable problem. Any proof must account for the unitary-orbit directions in the unconditional algebra generated by Z.

A sufficient new bridge would be a matched finite-part expansion of the two entropies under epsilon->0, or a one-sided difference bound of the form

 liminf_(epsilon->0) [chi-star(Z(epsilon))-chi(Z(epsilon))]
 >=k^-2[chi-star(X)-chi(X)],

with both entropies finite at some nonzero epsilon and with the original limsup definition of chi. This is a proposed sufficient estimate, NOT an asserted theorem. The expected orbital volume terms must cancel in the difference; neither the relative compression theorem nor free entropy dimension amplification supplies finite-part cancellation.

A promising route for the nonmicrostates side is to decompose free difference quotients into normal entry variations and inner derivations of the finite matrix algebra, then control the Gaussian-time conjugate variables in the unconditional joint algebra. The zero-noise matrix-unit conditioning has infinite Fisher cost in directions which are pure gauge, so a conditional Fisher upper bound is not automatically integrable. This is the specific unproved analytic step, alongside uniform microstates chart and limsup control. The frozen full pair target remains open.

## Why linearized noise does not yet prove the finite-part estimate

At t=epsilon²k²u, analytic gauge straightening gives normal coefficients converging in norm to X+sqrt(u)S_normal as epsilon->0, with correction depending also on X and the orbital Gaussian coefficients. Norm or moment convergence does not give the required upper semicontinuity of free Fisher information. The variational formula naturally gives the opposite lower-semicontinuity direction.

Conditioning on X and the orbital coefficients supplies a Gaussian score and the crude bound n/u. But replacing Phi-star(X+sqrt(u)S) by n/u discards precisely the source entropy information that must survive. Projecting the unperturbed optimal score onto the perturbed visible algebra is not justified by closeness of generators: the conditional expectation subspaces themselves need quantitative control. The perturbation is not a function solely of the visible tuple X+sqrt(u)S.

The actual Nica–Shlyakhtenko–Speicher Proposition4.1 was read in full. It constructs matrix conjugates by transposing/scaling entry conjugates relative to fixed scalar matrix units, with its own nonselfadjoint-coordinate normalization. It proves a conditional identity and an unconditional inequality by projection; it does not remove the gauge score divergence or give upper Fisher stability for the present correlated perturbation. Its factor k³ for raw complex entries should not be confused with the k⁴ coefficient arising when all real entry coordinates are orthonormalized for normalized tr_k. No formula from it is imported without reconciling those conventions.

Thus there are now two separate established support facts: full-dimensional local encoding with finite ordinary chi (finite-entropy-chart.md), and the exact linearized normal noise covariance. Strict gap transfer remains unproved. No claim of a counterexample to the full target follows from this missing step.
