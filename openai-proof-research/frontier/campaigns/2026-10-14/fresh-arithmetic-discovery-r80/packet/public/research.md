### Exact affine-plane feasibility calculation

Here is a concrete stronger test of the constant-degree interface. Let q be an even prime power, let T be the q² points of the affine plane, and partition its q+1 directions into nonempty disjoint sets D_A,D_B of sizes r,s. On side A use all affine lines in directions D_A, equally often; on B use the lines in D_B. Every cross-intersection has exactly one point. Every point has frequency 1/q on either side. Because q² is even, T admits an inverse-letter involution. Splitting A into two identical type distributions permits the primary's bipartite random matching model.

This is more varied than the primary's example with a single B-type: both sides may have many directions and every line has q points. It passes the exact cancellation and marginal balance checks. Nevertheless it fails the operative decay requirement for every inverse-letter pairing.

For u different from bar(t), the A conditional turn weight is 1/r if the direction from bar(t) to u belongs to D_A, and zero otherwise. The B weight is similarly 1/s on D_B. The smallest common upper bound therefore has, in every row, r(q−1) entries 1/r and s(q−1) entries 1/s. If M is its entrywise square, then

    M 1 = lambda 1,
    lambda = (q−1)(1/r+1/s) >= 4(q−1)/(q+1) > 1,
    sum_W P(W)^2 = q² lambda^(h−1).

The last strict inequality holds for every q>=2. The calculation is exact, independent of the matching sample size and of the chosen inverse involution. It establishes failure of this specific source-interface test, not failure of torsion-free zero divisors over Q. Taking a larger finite plane makes no improvement. The same-side squared row sums are (q−1)/r and (q−1)/s; for q>=3 they cannot both be strictly below one. Checking only cross-side path compatibility would overlook the same-side patterns explicitly present in the source.

This is an elementary sufficient identity, not a full construction. It does not supply a type system with the source's decay, matching balance and root safeguards.
