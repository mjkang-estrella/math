# Prefreeze: carry the rank-one comparison through the screening quotient

This is a calculation inside Source069, not a frozen theorem or a contribution assertion. Arakawa–Frenkel v3 §§3.1–3.3, §4.1 and §§5.1–5.3 were read before this note. Their actual construction is stronger than an abstract isomorphism of W-modules: it identifies ambient Heisenberg algebras and compares screening kernels. Failing to use that strengthening would leave a fictitious gap.

## Fixed conventions and full label calculation

Use the primary's shifted scalar kappa, normalized by long-root square length two, with kappa irrational complex. This is the local inverse-form scalar; in the source's assembly a=-c. Thus this calculation uses kappa -> 1/kappa for sl2; it does not replace the global determinant duality c -> -1/c with a positive reciprocal.

In rank one let m,n be arbitrary nonnegative integers, the dominant weight and coweight labels allowed by the specified global form. Work first on the covering lattices; restriction to the actual labels preserves every identity below. Put

    nu = m - kappa n,
    nu_dual = n - m/kappa.

AF (3.18) identifies b(z) with -kappa b_dual(z). The OPE b(z)b(w) ~ 2 kappa/(z-w)^2 is preserved, since kappa^2*(2/kappa)=2 kappa. Pulling the dual Fock module back gives the SAME charge: -kappa nu_dual=nu.

The Virasoro field, from AF (3.22), is

    T = (1/(4 kappa)) :b^2: + ((kappa-1)/(2 kappa)) partial b.

On the highest Fock vector of b_0-charge x its weight is

    h_kappa(x) = [x^2 + 2(1-kappa)x]/(4 kappa).

Therefore

    h_kappa(nu)
      = m(m+2)/(4 kappa) + kappa n(n+2)/4
        - (mn+m+n)/2.

This exactly equals h_(1/kappa)(nu_dual), and the central charge 13-6(kappa+kappa^(-1)) is also invariant. This is the primary rank-one duality calculation, not a new operation.

## The stronger exact comparison, beyond highest weights

AF constructs the screening resolution (Theorem 5.2, proof on pp.23–24). In rank one it is a two-term complex with cohomology T_(m,n)^kappa in degree zero and zero in degree one:

    C = [F_nu --S^(m+1)--> F_A],
    A = -m-2-kappa n.

The dual resolution, transported into the same Heisenberg normalization, is

    C_dual = [F_nu --Sdual^(n+1)--> F_B],
    B = m+kappa(n+2).

Both differentials are W-linear and surjective; the latter is the rank-one instance of the vanishing in AF Theorem 5.2, not an inference just from character equality. AF's kernel comparison identifies their degree-zero simple submodules. The highest vector lies in both kernels: each screening preserves L_0, while its outgoing lowest weight is larger by the positive integer (m+1)(n+1), as computed below. After fixing that same highest vector in F_nu, irreducibility implies both kernels are the W-submodule generated by it, hence the common submodule K of F_nu. Thus there is an actual W-linear isomorphism

    theta: F_A -> F_B,
    theta(S v) = Sdual v.

It is well-defined because the two kernels are K; it is bijective because both maps are onto. The pair (identity on F_nu, theta) is a chain isomorphism of W-complexes. Scaling the primary's integration cycles changes theta by the corresponding nonzero scalar ratio; this construction is relative to those choices. This pays more than matching scalar weights or an abstract derived zigzag.

As a check, both outgoing weights equal h_kappa(nu)+(m+1)(n+1):

    A-nu = -2(m+1),
    A+nu+2(1-kappa) = -2 kappa(n+1),
    B-nu = 2 kappa(n+1),
    B+nu+2(1-kappa) = 2(m+1).

Their products divided by 4 kappa give exactly the same integer. Consequently both resolutions give the familiar character

    ch K = q^h (1-q^((m+1)(n+1))) / product_(j>=1)(1-q^j),

with the conventional overall vacuum factor omitted. This is an identity of formal graded characters, not a rational-level extension.

## Why the quotient isomorphism is not the missing parabolic map

The outgoing charges differ by

    B-A = 2[m+1+kappa(n+1)].

They cannot coincide for irrational kappa, since equality would force kappa=-(m+1)/(n+1). Every vector of a Heisenberg Fock module has the same b_0-charge; hence Hom_Heis(F_A,F_B)=0. In particular theta is NOT a Heisenberg-linear isomorphism. The screening differentials themselves also change charge and are not morphisms of ordinary Heisenberg modules. Thus C and C_dual above are W-complexes made from Fock representations, not complexes in the ordinary Heisenberg module category. It would be invalid to apply an abelian/toric equivalence term by term as though these were Heisenberg-linear maps.

This does not prove a geometric parabolic comparison impossible. It pinpoints why the completed W-complex construction does not alone give it: enhanced Jacquet functors retain their semi-infinite kernels, residual Levi action, and factorization-module maps. An appropriate enhanced category may carry screening correspondences with shifted charge. Identifying those actions and their composition, rather than merely the underlying W-complexes, is the required further operation.

For general rank, AF's BGG resolutions and root-by-root splitting supply the corresponding W-module identification and higher-cohomology vanishing. One can compare the two bounded complexes through the common H^0 by quasi-isomorphisms. This is paid. It does not assert a termwise isomorphism of every higher BGG differential, nor coherent comparison of all parabolic enhanced kernels. The primary's q-Serre/cycle construction is stronger than a collection of arbitrary root maps; arbitrary scalar choices are not already factorization coherent.
