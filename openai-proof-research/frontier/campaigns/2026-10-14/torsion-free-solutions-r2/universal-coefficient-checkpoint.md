# Checkpoint: exact universal-coefficient reduction, including ineffective groups

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

This is an application of credited primary constructions, not a substantial
claim or a replacement theorem. It does NOT assume every finitely generated
torsion-free group embeds into a finitely presented group. That assertion
would discard the ineffective groups in the original scope.

## The primary interfaces

Chiodo, arXiv:1107.1489v3, Theorem 3.14 constructs a recursively presented
universal torsion-free quotient from any recursive presentation. Theorem 2.2
gives torsion-order-preserving Higman embedding, and Theorem 4.9 gives a
fixed finitely presented torsion-free group K containing every countably
generated recursively presented torsion-free group. Read the actual proofs
of 3.14 and 4.8–4.9, their supporting Lemmas 3.12–3.13, and the precise 2.2
interface. The deeper proof of the embedding theorem referenced by Chiodo
was not audited. KMR, arXiv:2303.13240v1, Proposition 2 says that the groups
in a quasivariety admitting all nonsingular solutions within that quasivariety
form a quasivariety. Its full proof was read. Here the quasivariety is TF,
but its being a HOWIE quasivariety is not assumed.

For clarity, the effective quotient step can also be checked directly.
For finite P=<Y|R>, enumerate all finite products of conjugates of R and
their inverses. Dovetail all pairs (v,k), k≥1; when v^k is enumerated, adjoin
v as a relator in the next stage. Iterate this operation, dovetailing the
stages. Each set of relators is recursively enumerable. The union quotient
is torsion free because a power relation uses finitely many relators and
therefore occurs at a finite stage. A map to a torsion-free group kills
every adjoined root by induction. This proves precisely the recursive
universal quotient used below, not coefficient preservation for TFS.

## All torsion-free groups embed in ultrapowers of this fixed K

Let G be any torsion-free group, of ANY cardinality. Take a finite fragment
D of its group diagram: finitely many equations between words in named
elements, and finitely many inequations which hold in G. Make a finite
presentation P_D on symbols for the named elements using only the equations
in D. It has a homomorphism to G. This factors through P_D^tf, the universal
torsion-free quotient. Every inequation in D remains true in that quotient:
if its two words became equal, their images in G would be equal. P_D^tf
is recursively presented by the preceding construction, hence embeds in K
by Chiodo 4.9. Thus the entire finite fragment D can be realized in K.

Let I be the set of all finite fragments of the full diagram of G. For each
finite fragment D choose such a realization f_D into K of its named elements.
On I take an ultrafilter containing {D:D⊇D0} for every fixed finite D0;
these sets have the finite intersection property. For each g∈G assign its
realized value at indices where it is named and assign 1 elsewhere. The
resulting class in K^I/𝒰 is denoted f(g). For each g,h, the finite fragment
naming g,h,gh and asserting their multiplication belongs to the ultrafilter,
so f(gh)=f(g)f(h). For g≠h, the fragment asserting their inequation belongs
to it, so f(g)≠f(h). Therefore f is an embedding. This argument uses no
enumeration of the diagram of G. Its index set can have arbitrary size.

An ultraproduct of torsion-free groups is torsion free: if [a_i]^k=1, then
a_i^k=1 on an ultrafilter-large set, and hence a_i=1 there. Consequently
the quasivariety generated by K is exactly TF. This operation is consistent
with the fact that a single countable K cannot contain all finitely generated
torsion-free groups as ordinary subgroups.

## Equivalent remaining test over K

Full TFS is equivalent to the assertion that EVERY finite nonsingular
system over THIS fixed K has a torsion-free solution overgroup preserving K.
The forward implication is immediate. Here is a direct proof of the reverse
implication, spelling out the closure operation behind KMR Proposition 2.

Assume the assertion over K. A finite system over an ultrapower K^I/𝒰 has
finitely many coefficients; choose representatives for each. At each index
the same syntactic words form a system over K with exactly the same exponent
matrix. Choose a torsion-free solution group H_i embedding K. Restrict each
H_i to the subgroup generated by K and these finitely many solution values;
it is countable and is a quotient of the fixed K*F_n. Thus the choices range
over a set of quotients, with no global-choice assumption. Then
K^I/𝒰 embeds in (∏H_i)/𝒰 because each coordinate map is injective, and
the chosen coordinate solutions solve the given system in that torsion-free
ultraproduct. If G embeds in the ultrapower, the system over G is thereby
solved while preserving G. This proves the reverse implication for all G,
all finite m≤n, and all nonzero invariant factors, without a presentation
or residual assumption on G.

K is not itself shown to admit these solutions. The reduction, including
the quasivariety closure, is paid by the read antecedents and the elementary
argument above. It does not resolve or nominate TFS.

## A genuine finitely generated perfect test subgroup

The familiar Higman four-cycle, with conjugation x^y=y^{-1}xy, is

    J=<a,b,c,d | b^a=b^2, c^b=c^2, d^c=d^2, a^d=a^2>.

We independently verify the properties needed here. First
H=<b,c | c^b=c^2> is an HNN extension of <c>=Z, identifying <c> with
<c^2>. It embeds <c>, has b of infinite order by the exponent map to Z,
and is torsion free by cyclically reduced HNN normal form. A finite-order
element with positive cyclic stable-letter length has all powers reduced;
all other finite-order elements are conjugate into the torsion-free base.
Moreover <b>∩<c>=1, by the exponent map and infinitude of c.

A=<a,b,c | b^a=b^2, c^b=c^2> is an HNN extension of H identifying
<b> with <b^2>, so is torsion free as well. Its subgroup <a,c> is free
of rank two: a mixed reduced word in a and c has no HNN pinch, since a
nonzero power of c belongs to neither <b> nor <b^2>. Pure powers survive
by the base embedding or the stable-letter exponent map. Similarly
B=<c,d,a | d^c=d^2, a^d=a^2> is torsion free and its subgroup <c,a>
is free of rank two. Thus J=A*_{<a,c>}B is a genuine amalgam along these
free subgroups. Normal form embeds A and B; every finite-order element of
an amalgam is conjugate into a vertex group by cyclic reduction. Hence J
is torsion free and nontrivial, with a of infinite order. Its abelianization
is zero, because each of the four displayed relations kills one generator
in the abelianization. Thus J is finitely generated, finitely presented and
perfect. It is not locally free: as a nontrivial finitely generated perfect
group it cannot itself be free. This is the known Higman group, not a new
construction or a claim that the group is simple.

Chiodo's K contains J. Therefore K is outside locally indicable groups and
outside groups with a separating rational-derived series: a map from J to
a solvable group has image both perfect and solvable, hence trivial. This
explains concretely why the residual-solvable operation does not settle the
universal coefficient test. No specific system over J or K has been shown
to fail TFS, and no assertion is made that every system over J is difficult.
