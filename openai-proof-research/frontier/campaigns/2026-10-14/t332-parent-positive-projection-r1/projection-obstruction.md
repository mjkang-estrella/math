# Fixed-p nearest-point projection cannot supply the T332 retraction

Myeongjin Kang, no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0; original code MIT.
Author-side route obstruction only, not a candidate, independent review, or count.
The original T332 scope remains unchanged and unresolved. This note strengthens
the existing path/branching route checks, not the target theorem.

## Exact claim

For every fixed real p>1, nearest-point projection in the ordinary coordinate
ell-p metric onto the positive tree-flow cone has no universal Lipschitz constant
when its domain and range are measured in the tree's weighted edge ell-1 norm.
This already fails for unit-length edges and complete binary trees with one stem.
The same examples apply to projection onto probability flows. They do not exclude
an ell-1 nearest-point selection, p depending on the tree, a different metric,
a different retraction, direct cotype, or the full extension theorem.

## Construction and projection proof

Take a root o, one unit stem to v, and a complete binary tree of depth h>=1
below v, with every edge of length1. Number stem level0 and the 2^l edges in
the binary part at level l, 1<=l<=h. The cone C consists of flows whose vertex
masses are nonnegative: a flow entering a nonroot vertex is at least the sum
leaving it, with nonnegative terminal flows. Equivalently C is generated by
the root-to-vertex path indicator vectors a_w, for nonroot vertices w.

Let q0 be the flow of the probability assigning mass2^(-h) to every leaf.
It equals1 on the stem and2^(-l) on every level-l edge. Its ell-1 norm is h+1.
For 0<epsilon<1 put x=q0-epsilon e_stem. This is a nonnegative edge vector,
but at v its incoming flow is less than its outgoing flow, so x is not in C.
The ell-1 distance from x to q0 is epsilon.

For fixed p>1 define

    S = sum_{l=1}^h 2^(-l(p-1)),
    r = 1/(1+S^(1/(p-1))),
    q = (1-epsilon r) q0.

Then q is the UNIQUE minimizer over C of sum_e |q_e-x_e|^p.
To prove this, let g be the gradient of this objective at q divided by p.
On the stem, g=(epsilon(1-r))^(p-1); on a level-l edge,

    g = -(epsilon r)^(p-1) 2^(-l(p-1)).

The definition of r gives (1-r)^(p-1)=r^(p-1) S. For a vertex w at level j
(the endpoint of a level-j edge), therefore,

    <g,a_w> = (epsilon r)^(p-1)
                 sum_{l=j+1}^h 2^(-l(p-1)) >= 0.

In particular this pairing is zero at each leaf. Every z in C is a nonnegative
linear combination of the a_w, hence <g,z>>=0. Since q is a positive combination
of leaf paths, <g,q>=0. Thus <g,z-q>>=0 for all z in C. The first-order convexity
inequality proves optimality. Strict convexity for p>1 proves uniqueness. This
argument establishes the actual constrained projection, not merely the optimum
among symmetric competitors.

Probability flows form the subset P={z in C: sum of flows out of o<=1}.
Both q0 and q belong to P: their stem flows are1 and1-epsilon r. As q is already
the unique minimizer on the larger set C, it is also the unique minimizer on P.
In probability language q transfers mass epsilon r from the leaves to o.

## Unbounded transport distortion

Projection fixes q0, whereas projection sends x to q. Consequently its Lipschitz
constant measured in edge ell-1 is at least

    ||q-q0||_1 / ||x-q0||_1 = (h+1)r.

For each fixed p>1,

    S <= 1/(2^(p-1)-1),
    r >= [1+(2^(p-1)-1)^(-1/(p-1))]^(-1) > 0.

The displayed ratio therefore tends to infinity with h. In particular, at p=2,
S=1-2^(-h), r=1/(2-2^(-h)), and the exact ratio is

    (h+1)/(2-2^(-h)).

The norm between feasible probability flows is exactly W1 on this unit-edge
tree. The input x is an ambient signed flow, as permitted in the proposed
sufficient retraction route. No contradiction to a claim about feasible inputs
alone is asserted. The example does not require negative edge coordinates.
