# Explicit storage bound for the auxiliary partial-affine monoid

Myeongjin Kang, no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0. Parent author-side clarification only; not an independent gate or a revision of the frozen T250P packet.

The frozen candidate calls equality in the source monoid P directly. The source `algebra.tex`, lines1–39, defines generators u(x)=x/2, v(x)=(x+1)/2, their inverses, and the partial identities e_0,e_1 on rational tails [0,infinity),[1,infinity). Its composition formula establishes decidability. The following elementary bit bound verifies that the direct call has the required space bound, without treating computability alone as a complexity estimate.

Process a length-n word from right to left. After j generators, store a positive affine map x -> 2^m x+b restricted to a rational tail [r,infinity), allowing r=-infinity. Initially m=0,b=0,r=-infinity. When the next generator is a full affine map, compose it on the left and retain r. When it is e_c, c in {0,1}, leave m,b unchanged and replace r by max(r,(c-b)/2^m). These are exact operations on dyadic rationals, and all resulting tails are nonempty because every slope is positive.

For j<=n, |m|<=j. Writing b in reduced dyadic form, its denominator divides 2^j and |b|<=2^j-1. The latter bound follows inductively from |b_new|<=2|b_old|+1; the former follows since each full generator multiplies by 2 or1/2 and adds a dyadic constant with denominator at most2. Thus b's numerator has absolute value below2^(2j), and its numerator and denominator each require O(j) binary digits.

Each finite threshold ever introduced has the form q=(c-b)/2^m at some j<=n. Its denominator divides 2^(2j) and |q|<=2^(2j). Consequently its reduced numerator has absolute value at most2^(4j). The current r is the maximum of finitely many such thresholds, hence is one of them: storing a maximum does not require storing the list. Thus r has O(n) digits too. Dyadic comparisons use cross-multiplication of O(n)-bit integers, and temporary arithmetic also has O(n) space with ordinary elementary algorithms.

It follows that a literal P word of length n can be evaluated in deterministic polynomial time and O(n) binary workspace, with fixed generators. Two words are equal exactly when their domains, slopes and intercepts agree: each common tail has infinitely many rational points, so distinct affine parameters cannot agree there. No explicit expansion of a long canonical H code is needed, and this statement makes no assertion about compressed P words.

The evaluator only removes the clean quota already present. Its eventual mathematical derivation may insert extra workspace, but the equality algorithm need not construct that derivation. Giving existing workspace cells weight one therefore bounds stored configurations by the supplied decorated input rather than by an unbounded grade-only estimate.
