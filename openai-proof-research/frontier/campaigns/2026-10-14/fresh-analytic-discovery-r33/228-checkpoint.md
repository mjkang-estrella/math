# Family 228 finite-range feasibility checkpoint

Author: Myeongjin Kang. Original prose CC BY 4.0. Exploratory, no theorem nomination or scope freeze. Source truth is stipulated at fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb. This is a new finite-range question, not a claim that truncation preserves coexistence.

The contemplated full target is one bounded continuous stable radial compactly supported pair potential on R^3, with finite canonical thermodynamic free energy for every beta>0 and rho>0 and a strict inverse-temperature derivative jump at one common beta_c throughout a nonempty open density interval. No lattice, fixed spatial partition, extra species, multi-body term, or conditional phase-separation assumption is allowed.

## Source operation and exact unpaid interface

The algebraic-decay companion introduction and iteration sections were read in full before this checkpoint (see retained bodies; the current turn resumed from an explicit reading summary). The source uses physical q_n and ideal P_n=T_{a_n}q_{n-1}. The finite-range comparison supplies ||q_n-P_n|| <= delta_n. Its localization operation transfers three separated slope groups only to the NEXT ideal transform, at cost C delta_n/a_{n+1}+C a_{n+1}, and consumes domain C a_{n+1}. The schedule R_{n+1}=R_n^2, a_n=R_n^(-1/16), delta_n=C R_n^(-1/4) makes these losses summable. A ball in the subdifferential is established for P_n and passes to the physical pressure only after the infinite interaction-range limit.

Stopping at finite n leaves a C^0 error delta_n between the physical pressure and a pressure with a corner. There is no finite-n slope-ball conclusion. Smooth convex regularizations of the maximum of three affine functions converge uniformly to that maximum while having singleton subdifferentials everywhere. Consequently neither small pressure error nor closeness of convex conjugates supplies the required finite-range jump. This diagnoses a missing estimate, not impossibility of the target.

The honest needed input is an actual physical phase-exclusion/contour estimate at finite range, preserving at least three non-collinear pressure slope groups on a two-parameter neighborhood. Two density phases alone need not yield a canonical temperature jump on an open density interval at one beta: a segment between two slope vectors typically gives one energy at each interior density.

## Primary comparison, newly read operative bodies

He–Jauslin–Lebowitz–Peled, arXiv:2510.24825v1 (download URL and hash in private/primary/receipts.json), text lines 1–1360 read, through Lemma 4.1 and the ensuing continuity verification. This includes the exact pair interaction (1.13), main theorems, Dobrushin–Shlosman criterion and proof, and the full Section 3 proof operations including the chessboard and edge seminorm bounds. The cited general reflection-positivity proof and the cited detailed geometric Peierls implementation are not reproduced there and have NOT been separately read. Later Section 4 operations and appendices are not yet read.

The model restricts the reference pair interaction to particles in the SAME fixed cube, and makes Kac attractions constant within a cube and between nearest-neighbor cubes. Integration of positions therefore gives exactly a density-spin Hamiltonian with onsite free energy and positive nearest-neighbor squared-density difference. Its chessboard estimate controls arbitrary collections of costly edges by disseminated edge seminorms. The pressure-normalized bad-site mass and mixed-edge bound are Assumption 1, with a factor exp(-J dist(G_-,G_+)^2/2). This is a real finite-range interface mechanism, but the input Hamiltonian is not radial/translation invariant in continuum space.

Averaging the box origin and orientation restores symmetry to the pair function, but averaging Hamiltonians before exponentiation does not preserve these Gibbs/contour probabilities. The comparison required is local to contours and relative to phase partition functions, not an extensive uniform pressure error. No such estimate has been established here. The nearest-neighbor box scale also equals the attraction range, so simple averaging has no small mesh/range parameter. A separate fine-mesh physical coarse graining and three-phase estimate would be required.
