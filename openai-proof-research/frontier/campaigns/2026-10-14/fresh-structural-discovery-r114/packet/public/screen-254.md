The direction examined was an algorithm taking any finite Coxeter matrix, two standard subsets and two conjugating words, and outputting a standard subset and conjugating word describing their entire parabolic intersection. No complexity bound, rank restriction, spherical hypothesis, or partial membership output was intended. This was a pre-freeze examination; no exact theorem target was frozen and no algorithm with the full contract is claimed.

The source has stronger operations than parabolic intersection existence. In the parabolic manuscript, `cor:membership` uniformly decides membership in an explicitly supplied conjugate. Its full proof in `08_stabilizers.tex` constructs the exact cyclotomic field, planar diagram spaces and quotient bases, simple decompositions, trace-dual maps and finite algebra Z_K. In particular this is not merely finite-dimensionality over an unspecified complex field.

The same section's `stab:parabolic` is a rank induction, with `stab:conjugation` transporting stabilizers once containment is available. For a nonspherical acting type it chooses a minimum vertex of the harmonic filtration and applies `ht:containment`; the D=Y case splits off the invisible direct factor. For a spherical type it uses positive-height containment or acyclicity. `cor:arbitrary-intersections` already records the finite witness bound |S|. The K(pi,1) and no-CAT(0) companions were mapped from the complete tree, but not fully read or used to infer an additional algorithm.

A first attempted reduction enumerates candidate parabolics and uses the membership decider to check that every candidate generator belongs to both input groups. This semidecides containment of that candidate in the intersection. It does not certify that all elements of the intersection lie in the candidate. Enumerating witnesses outside it can refute equality, but absence of a witness at a finite time cannot certify equality. A descending rank bound by itself does not supply a stopping certificate for that search.

A second attempted reduction tries to implement the source's minimum-height construction. The inspected `06_heights.tex` supplies well-founded blocks, local sublevel connectivity, tied-neighborhood compatibility and minimum-set connectedness. In particular `ht:minima` starts from two *already minimum* endpoints, lowers greatest-height segments and proves connectedness. It is not itself an algorithm for recognizing that an arbitrary chosen vertex has global minimum height. The lower-neighbor descriptions quantify over representatives in spherical cosets. This screen did not finish a finite, terminating emptiness test for all those descent choices, or an alternative certified global-minimum selection. Spherical Garside reductions are a credible possible input; the screen does not assert that they cannot pay this operation. Further source cap/spherical arguments would have to be read and implemented before claiming that the operation is genuinely unpaid.
