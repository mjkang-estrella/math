## Proposed extra operation and its risks

The additional finite lemma lifts a maximal net at scale s to height s, joins each point to a scale2s parent, and attaches the last net vertically to its original base points. Heights separate coarse edges from near-base balls. For high balls only boundedly many scales and net-separated endpoints occur; low balls split into fine edges near X and finitely many intermediate scales. Bottom vertical edges are handled by a direct product cover. This establishes a uniform ambient doubling constant for the connected graph in Hilbert space before Dvoretzky transfer.

This is not an assertion that graph metric doubling passes to its straight-line image, or that a Lipschitz image preserves doubling. Neither inference is valid in general. Nor is finite total length alone used as a local doubling bound. All ambient-ball cases have a direct proof in candidate.md. The remaining finite-witness and summable-tour assembly is credited as routine/source-derived. Independent review must decide whether the height-separated lemma is a substantial additional operation or merely a known/routine thickening device. A broader final statement alone does not decide that gate.

Two preliminary routes were discarded without nomination. Filling all allowed source coordinates continuously creates arbitrarily high-dimensional finite boxes over a base point lying off the finitely many strip boundaries; this destroys a dimension-independent doubling bound. A direct star from a common root to sparse orthogonal endpoints can create many comparable points near the root even if the endpoint set itself is doubling. These are reasons to check local graph geometry, not claims that connected completion is impossible.
