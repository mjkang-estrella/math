The strongest located primary reduction is Florian Eisele, *On the geometry of lattices and finiteness of Picard groups*, published version, Proposition 5.1 and its proof. For fixed defect group and a fixed target algebra, the hypothesis is a **uniform rank bound on the images of indecomposable p-permutation modules under the chosen Morita equivalences**. The proof uses finiteness of rigid lattices and the permutation-module criterion to recover finitely many splendid Morita classes. Ordinary source order finiteness does not exhibit that uniform bound for the chosen equivalences. Thus the actual residual question is about their permutation structure, not another application of ordinary algebra rigidity. I found no construction providing the bound and did not freeze a broad Puig/Broué-type assertion. Proposition 5.1 is an existing compatible conditional reduction, not an r75 theorem. ## Integral surface motives: effective and birational categories must remain distinct

This was the extended avenue after the initial batch. Source040's full introduction and conclusion supply the Bloch statement (`thm:bloch`) and rational Tate consequences for powers and Hilbert schemes (`cor:tate-motives`). The prior r16 and r48 checkpoints were found and read before selection. They already cover the torsion-free integral upgrade using Kahn and Totaro, and localization away from the torsion exponent. Rational averaging is already part of the paid mechanism.

The preselection feasibility calculation was modest: an annihilator of universal degree-zero cycles propagates to ordered products by telescoping products of point classes. On the distinct-point locus an unordered length-n configuration splits over an extension of degree dividing n!, so restriction/corestriction introduces the factorial into the elementary bound. Removing this loss requires a further operation; this calculation does not establish an exact integral torsion order for arbitrary Hilbert schemes. No such statement was frozen. No unproved symmetric-power transfer is being used as a theorem here.

The strongest located comparisons were Kahn's author-host February 2017 version, Definition 1.5/Lemma 1.6 and Corollary 5.4; Totaro's *The integral cohomology of the Hilbert scheme of points on a surface*, Theorems 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1; Sato–Yamazaki's published 2025 *Torsion birational motives of surfaces and unramified cohomology*; and Huryn–Newman, *On the torsion in the Chow motive of an Enriques surface*, arXiv:2609.04360v1. The last paper supplies an important updated distinction, already proved by those authors, not a campaign discovery.

In Huryn–Newman §5.1–5.3, over an algebraically closed field of exponential characteristic p, take prime-power torsion summands M and M' of surface motives with the same prime ell different from p, in the stated coefficient category. The kernel of the action of Hom(M,M') on cohomology is the image of CH¹(Mᵗ) tensor CH¹(M'). This ideal has square zero under composition, but need not be zero. The proof of Lemma 5.3 uses that ideal to lift automorphisms. An isomorphism on cohomology between two different motives needs a reverse morphism inducing an isomorphism, or the self-transpose hypotheses of its part (d); it is not an automatic classification by abstract torsion groups. Proposition 5.6 supplies the self-transpose torsion summand in its specified surface situation through the integral Tate splitting.

Remark 5.8 explicitly corrects a faithfulness assertion for effective motives in Sato–Yamazaki Proposition 3.6(1), preserving the distinction from the normalized birational category. The displayed Künneth kernel and the proof of Lemma 5.3 were actually read. This is a report of a primary-source correction, not an audit of any stipulated OpenAI theorem. The underlying Proposition 4.2 and all nonvanishing examples were not independently read through, so this record does not adjudicate every correction or classify all Enriques motives. In particular, neither abstract Néron–Severi torsion nor faithfulness of a different quotient category supplies the missing integral Hilbert-scheme operation.
