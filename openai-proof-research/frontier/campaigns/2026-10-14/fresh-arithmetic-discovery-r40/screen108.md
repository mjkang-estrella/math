## Paid sum operation

The source supplies a smooth homogeneous degree-r form G in d variables as an affine projection of per_m, with r comparable to m and d comparable to m². For s independent blocks the sum of s copies of G is smooth: all partials vanish only if each block is zero. Thus the source smooth-form bound immediately gives a lower bound of order s m³ for the sum of s independent permanents. This is a direct source specialization, not a nominee.

## Product interface

The contemplated (unfrozen) target was a uniform lower bound of order s m³ for the product of s independent m-by-m permanents over C. The product of the projected forms is singular along component intersections. At a smooth point exactly one factor G_j vanishes. Its differential and all ambient first polars, modulo the equation G_j=0, are the nonzero product of the remaining factors times the corresponding first polars of G_j. Hence, on any affine slice of dimension h+1>d, their common Jacobian has rank at most d: locally the equation vector factors, up to invertible multiplication at its common zero, through the d coordinates of block j. There can be no simple zero. At an intersection, the differential of the product is zero and simplicity also fails. Therefore the exact source ambient first-polar certificate cannot use a slice dimension greater than one block's dimension. This is an obstruction to that certificate, not to the product lower bound.

## Why subtracting the constant is not a linear-size repair

For a variable traceless N-by-N matrix X, f(X)=det(I+X) has determinantal complexity at most N. But f-1 has initial homogeneous form -tr(X²)/2 on sl_N. Over C the trace pairing restricted to sl_N is nondegenerate, so this quadratic defines a smooth projective hypersurface in N²-1 variables. The source smooth-initial-form corollary yields bdc(f-1) >= (N²-2)/(4e). Thus a general constant-subtraction construction of linear determinantal size is impossible under the stipulated source theorem. This does not imply any hardness for the special product itself.
