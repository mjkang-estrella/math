# Operation and contribution comparison

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.
Third-party rights retained privately. Contribution verdict: **UNRESOLVED**.

The operation under review is a nine-center worst-case cover of CP^2:

    inf_[u] max_s |<s(epsilon),u>|^2 > 1/2.

This is equivalent to covering radius <pi/4 in the metric arccos|<u,v>|.
It is not the optimization of pairwise center separation, the classification
of equidistant centers, or minimization of average quantization error.
The complete Borsuk assertion follows after combining it with the transferred
lower bound. Its strength relative to a project ledger is not novelty evidence.

## Pinned source: the antecedent lower bound

The actual real-projector manuscript at
fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb first enlarges arbitrary
strict-gap covering sets (lem:cover-to-map), integrates a homogeneous
partition over the sphere (lem:positive-extension), and subtracts spectral
positive/negative parts (lem:signed-extension). Disjoint supports prevent
cancellation. Borsuk–Ulam gives the self-adjoint-space dimension bound
(thm:label-bound). The B8C lower proof makes the routine Hermitian
substitutions and uses only continuity. This is credited antecedent work.

The source then uses degree, real projective charts, and support cycles
(thm:equality-complex), followed by six- and ten-label combinatorics, to
exclude equality in its real dimension-nine example. It supplies no upper
cover for complex projectors. The candidate neither inherits the real
counterexample nor generalizes its equality obstruction. The nine-set
construction instead demonstrates that equality is possible for this X.

## SIC classification: different constraints

I read the entire body of [Hughston–Salamon, arXiv:1410.5862v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.5862v2),
not merely its abstract. Their Sections 4–6 construct and describe midpoint
SIC families, WH fiducials, and Hesse incidences; Sections 7–10 finish
Theorem 5.5 by equations for correctly separated points, symmetric ideals,
and elimination of additional SICs. The condition is that every pair of
the nine centers has squared overlap 1/4. Their normalization of Fubini–Study
distance is twice the candidate's. The classification does not quantify a
maximum score over every target ray for a non-SIC orbit. It therefore
does not supply, or refute, this strict covering statement.

This distinction can be checked directly, without guessing from terminology.
For the candidate's fiducial let p,q be its two nonzero squared coordinate
moduli. Then

    p=1/2+epsilon/(sqrt(2)(1+epsilon^2)),
    q=1/2-epsilon/(sqrt(2)(1+epsilon^2)),
    pq=1/4-epsilon^2/(2(1+epsilon^2)^2).

The squared overlap with a nontrivial Z translate is 1-3pq>1/4;
between different coordinate-zero groups it is pq<1/4. Thus for positive
epsilon the cover configuration is explicitly outside the SIC locus.
It sacrifices packing equidistance to improve a worst-case covering
threshold. The classification's nonexistence of additional SICs is
irrelevant to that permitted motion.

## Hesse/MUB and Weyl–Heisenberg geometry: paid antecedents

[Dang–Blanchfield–Bengtsson–Appleby, arXiv:1211.0215v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.0215v2),
Sections 1–3, gives actual displacement/phase formulas and the dimension-three
SIC family. Determinants identify the special Hesse configurations, and
the twelve line normals form four MUBs. It also discusses other WH fiducials
and linear dependencies. These bodies establish that neither a WH orbit,
moving a fiducial, nor the twelve normals is a new idea. Their inspected
operation controls dependence and SIC incidence, not a positive lower
bound for max score at all rays after a non-SIC perturbation. I did not
read its higher-dimensional sections as covering theorems or rerun searches.

In B8C the specific extra work is to orient each of the four basis triples
cyclically, preserve that rule under X and Z, and choose a tangent positive
at four assigned poles. The two-trio estimate must then control shifted
holes, including complex directions invisible to the favored trio's
spatial linear term. Positive derivatives at the twelve OLD holes alone
would not prove the new orbit covers all points. Lemma 11 pays that
additional operation. Once paid, compactness away from the holes and
conversion to smaller-diameter cells are standard.

## Complex quantization and covering: a worst-case obligation remains

The actual body of [Dai–Liu–Rider, arXiv:cs/0603039v2](https://arxiv.org/html/cs/0603039v2),
Sections II–IV and Appendices B/C, defines chordal distance and
D(C)=E_Q min_P d_c(P,Q)^2. Its volume formula specializes on CP^2 to
mu(B(rho))=rho^4 in chordal radius. The random-code proof bounds the
expectation of the minimum distance using its tail distribution for a
fixed Q, then averages over Q. This is not a uniform bound for every Q
for a fixed nine-point configuration. Packing design criteria there also
control different quantities. Neither operation proves the B8C threshold.
I did not read the rest of its communications application or the full
general volume-integral proof, or attribute an optimal nine-point cover
to it. Averages and asymptotic code-size bounds cannot fill the worst-case
gap by changing a quantifier.

## Topological covering operations: antecedent, with different spaces

[Adams–Bush–Frick, arXiv:2106.14677v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.14677v3),
Sections 1–3 and 7, has actual proofs linking real projective packings
and assumed real projective ball covers to equivariant maps of metric
thickenings. Theorem 3 starts WITH a strict ball cover; it does not produce
one from a packing. Theorem 15 has sphere-cover regularity and containment
hypotheses. These results are relevant antecedents for odd-map/covering
methods, but are not a nine-ball construction in CP^2 or a substitute for
B8C's arbitrary-set lower-bound reduction. Its real projective-space and
sphere antipodal actions cannot simply be renamed complex orthogonality.

## Decision and remaining uncertainty

These are comparisons against actual theorem/proof bodies, with the exact
reading extents in the companion log. They identify a concrete additional
operation beyond the read antecedents, not a newly named parameter.
I found neither an identical construction nor a primary theorem from
these bodies that automatically supplies the strict nine-center cover.

The bounded search still does not settle whether an older worst-case
CP^2 covering construction, a non-SIC WH codebook, or another explicit
nine-center configuration already does this. Generic complex quantization
literature is not a substitute for exhausting that closest comparison.
Two attempts to access the Mondal–Heath diversity/covering-codebook paper
(DOI 10.1155/2008/594928) failed; its search snippets were not treated as
body reading. No direct nine-center worst-case primary was located and
read. This is the reason for UNRESOLVED contribution, not evidence for
either a worldwide first or DUPLICATE. A further contribution review
should investigate that precise covering operation, with the mathematical
proof already passing independently. No manuscript or publication is
authorized by this review.
