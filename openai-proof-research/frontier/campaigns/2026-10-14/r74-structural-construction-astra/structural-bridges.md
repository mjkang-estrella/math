## 1. Primary pre-coalescence interface

DH A2 consists of translation ergodicity, square symmetries, an edge 2+epsilon moment and bounded norm shape; A2' adds unique passage times. These are supplied for s by the elementary cap construction. DH's averaged graph/cocycle construction (Section3), Proposition5.1, Proposition5.2 and Theorem5.3 therefore give a stationary subsequential law with calibrated geodesic paths, outdegree one, merging at intersections, and all paths directed in the supporting arc of the same limiting cocycle slope. These statements precede its energy step. DH Lemma6.2 and the argument immediately after it use only these facts and stationarity to extract a positive-probability triple if noncoalescence occurs. They do not require ergodicity of the enlarged graph law: their one-dimensional ergodic average has a potentially random limit of positive expectation.

Use DH's actual rounded LINE targets, not a presumed identity of line and half-plane graph laws. The target is the set of lattice cells intersecting {rho(z)=alpha}. Positive coercive metrics have unique finite paths to this set: a fixed finite competitor bounds the cost, coercivity restricts the search to finitely many simple paths, and no ties hold. Include X, s, the graph and the cocycle in the lifted averaging space. Stationarity of the lift follows from the same shift-of-alpha identity as DH3.1–3.2, because the cap map is translation-covariant.

The line-target uncapping variant is also valid: if an interior lattice vertex is a target, its half-open unit cell lies entirely inside the circuit polygon. The target line therefore meets the polygon interior and exits at a boundary edge or vertex. Rounding a point on an axis-aligned integer boundary edge gives one of that edge's endpoints. Thus there is a boundary target and the cheap circuit arc is an admissible replacement. This verifies the exact target convention; it is not an identification of two limiting measures.

## 2. Joint domination and uncapped selected-ray stability

Fix finite F and a with r=G((a,infinity))>0. Resample original t_F independently from G conditioned on t>a, keeping other originals and all U fixed. Write X' for the new field. Its marginal has density

  h(X')=r^(-|F|) 1{t'_e>a for every e in F} <= C=r^(-|F|)

relative to P. For each alpha, recompute the graph and cocycle using the SAME target line in X'. The pushforward of X' and all its derived coordinates is bounded by C times the corresponding old pushforward. The same is true after averaging alpha over [0,n].

Fix a subsequence defining the old lifted limit nu. The joint old/new laws are tight: the underlying pair law is constant in n; the graph space is compact; finite cocycle coordinates are bounded by finite cap distances, with their modified marginals dominated by C times the old ones. Take a further joint subsequential limit. Its old marginal is nu and its new marginal nu' satisfies nu'<=C nu. Indeed the inequality passes for nonnegative bounded continuous functions, then for Borel sets by regularity on the product Polish space. The underlying pair marginal remains exactly the original resampling coupling. This observation is needed later to descend a statement proved in a lifted coupling back to the prescribed original-field coupling.

The cap map is continuous at P-almost every X in the product topology. For each finite edge list, a shortest protecting circuit exists and all shorter candidates lie in a finite neighborhood. Its minimum length is locally fixed once finitely many indicators are fixed; no involved t equals M. Cap values are then continuous functions of the corresponding t,U. The modified marginal is absolutely continuous to P, so the same holds there. Thus cap coordinates retained in the limit equal the cap of the retained originals; this relation is not merely asserted from measurability.

Here is a precise stability proof. Fix a root v and a desired finite prefix. On the event A={max_F t<a}, the original change is upward. Given epsilon>0 choose deterministic R containing F and v so that, in the fixed underlying coupling, the probability of a cap change outside Q_R is at most epsilon. This is possible by finite cap influence. Next choose deterministic S>=R so that with probability at least1-epsilon every old geodesic between vertices of Q_R stays inside Q_S. There are finitely many endpoint pairs and each geodesic is finite, so such S exists; equivalently use the coercive length bound.

An old finite target geodesic from v cannot return to Q_R after leaving Q_S on this latter event. A return would produce an old geodesic between v and a return vertex in Q_R leaving Q_S.

Realize the joint convergence by Skorokhod. Suppose the limiting old ray from v is entirely uncapped and avoids F. Its prefix through its first exit from Q_S is finite. Every edge on it has strict margin t_e<K_e, since equality has probability zero for every lattice edge. Graph convergence gives exact agreement of that finite prefix in the old approximants; cap continuity and the strict margins make it uncapped there for all large indices. Also max_F t<a eventually holds in the approximants. Upward monotonicity then leaves this entire prefix unchanged in the new cap metric. On the two localization events every later edge of that finite approximating target path lies outside Q_R and is unchanged. Thus the WHOLE finite old minimizer is unchanged, and remains the unique new minimizer to the same target.

If limiting old/new desired prefixes differed, their finite discrete coordinates would differ eventually. On the asserted limiting event, at least one localization failure must then occur at every sufficiently large index. Fatou bounds its probability by 2epsilon. Let epsilon decrease to zero and intersect over roots and prefix lengths. We obtain:

  On A, every entirely uncapped limiting old ray avoiding F is
  exactly the selected new limiting ray from that root.

No almost-sure eventual localization was assumed under Skorokhod. The old/new underlying pair is fixed in law, which gives the uniform failure bounds. This is stronger than mere geometric geodesicity and is the new input needed for graph surgery.

## 3. Wall coalescence without cap finite energy

Assume nu has noncoalescing paths with positive probability. The DH pre-coalescence interface gives three disjoint rays eventually entering every translate of one of eight half-planes. Each is eventually uncapped. Cut at last contacts with a common sufficiently distant lattice level after all capped edges. Countable choices of cut level, roots and orientation, followed by stationarity, give a fixed positive-probability event of three wholly uncapped rays rooted on the level, strictly on its positive side thereafter.

For a vertical level, translate roots to (0,j1),(0,j2),(0,j3), 0<j1<j2<j3<m, and let F_m be the rungs (-1,j)--(0,j), 1<=j<=m-1. After raising these originals above H_m=4Mm³, every protecting open circuit around a rung has vertical span at least m: its crossings of x=-1/2 above and below that rung cannot occur at any of the blocked intermediate heights. Thus L'_e>=2m and K'_e>=8Mm³, and s'_e>H_m on every rung.

An open circuit C strictly enclosing the wall has length at most C0 m² with probability tending to one, by the annulus Peierls estimate. Its edges avoid F_m, remain <=M, and its total cost is <H_m for large m. Intersect this event with the fixed triple event; the intersection remains positive for some deterministic m. Then choose deterministic a>=H_m such that imposing max_{F_m}t<=a still leaves positive probability. Perform the fixed resampling of section2 above a. The stability lemma preserves the three SELECTED rays in the modified graph.

The outer rays and the finite level segment joining their roots form a simple proper separating curve. A vertex outside C and outside the right-hand corridor between the outer rays cannot belong to the middle class: its selected path to a sufficiently late middle-ray vertex outside C would either meet an outer ray, hence merge with its different class, or enter through a wall rung. The latter contradicts geodesic minimality because the two endpoints are outside C and its cheap boundary arc bypasses the expensive rung. Therefore the middle class lies in the corridor union a finite set.

This class has a least vertex in the order x first, then y. The corridor is bounded on the left and meets each fixed vertical column in a finite set, since both boundary rays eventually lie to the right of every such column. The finite exceptional region inside C does not destroy either property.

For a diagonal level h=x+y=0 use u=x-y and roots (k,-k). Raise BOTH incoming edges from h=-1 into each root for |k|<=m. Their crossing u-values on h=-1/2 are the consecutive half-integers 2k-1/2 and2k+1/2. Any protecting open circuit enclosing a raised edge must cross below and above this entire blocked interval, so its u-span exceeds4m and L'_e>=4m. The cubic cap dominates H=Mm³. The same cheap enclosing circuit and corridor argument apply, with lexicographic order (h,u). Entry through an interior level point uses a raised incoming edge; entry through an outer root meets an outer ray. Reflections/rotations cover the other six normals.

Under the stationary old nu, no coalescence class can have a least vertex in any fixed translation-invariant order: send one unit from each vertex to its class's least vertex if present. Outgoing mass is at most one, whereas a least vertex receives infinite mass from its forward ray. The mass-transport identity and countability of vertices rule this out. No ergodicity of nu is used. The new graph law is dominated by C nu, so it also has no least class a.s. The positive-probability construction contradicts this. Thus the limiting graph coalesces.

This argument does not apply DH Theorem6.1 with a false finite-energy hypothesis. It replaces that energy step using section2 and the cubic barrier. In the extremal-selector application the same surgery is valid once section6 below supplies its selector preservation.

## 4. Deterministic-slope reference extraction and conditional point mass

Choose a differentiability point of the deterministic cap norm ball. DH Corollary4.7 says that the cocycle slope for this line construction is the unique supporting functional rho there. Once the graph coalesces, its cocycle is the actual Busemann function of its rays: for any x,y choose a common future vertex z; calibration gives B(x,y)=T_s(x,z)-T_s(y,z), and all later common vertices give the same difference. Thus the graph has deterministic slope rho, not just expected slope rho.

Project the lifted limiting measure to (s,graph). AH5.3–5.6's conditional quantile argument applies to this stationary measure on coalescing families: conditional ray laws live in the planar ordered set directed in Arc(rho); quantiles are measurable and translation-covariant; for Lebesgue-a.e. fixed quantile beta they define a coalescing family G_beta with slope rho. This application uses the PROVED graph coalescence, rather than importing AH Theorem5.1's finite-energy-dependent input. Opposite/rotated line constructions supply rays outside the arc needed for its ordered-set setup.

The fixed-functional uniqueness argument of AH4.4 applies here without an energy assumption. Its operative proof uses coalescing families, uniform shape/Busemann estimates, finite uniqueness, and integrability/ergodicity of half-plane boundary increments. The latter holds for s: restricted distances between neighboring boundary sites have finite mean by fixed coordinate routes (zigzags for diagonal boundaries), and boundary translations are ergodic since s is a factor of iid. Its planar crossing identity makes the difference of two half-plane increments nonnegative with mean zero; uniqueness of finite paths then excludes two different coalescing families with the same rho.

Choose one good beta0. For almost every beta, fixed-functional uniqueness gives G_beta=G_beta0 almost surely. Fubini now gives, for P_s-almost every s, equality for Lebesgue-a.e. beta in the same environment. AH's quantile pushforward identity therefore says the conditional ray law at the origin is delta_{G_rho(s)}. Translation covariance and countability of roots upgrade this to the entire graph being a point mass at the graph of G_rho. This is an equality conditional on s, not merely on the richer underlying X, so future cap-local prediction is legitimate.

The old lifted law thus has graph=G_rho(s(X)) a.s., and domination transfers this identity to the modified law. Apply section2 at late vertices of old reference rays, beyond their capped edges and F. Corresponding reference families share tails under the prescribed original upward resampling. Because both graphs are now deterministic functions of their cap fields and the projected underlying pair law is the fixed resampling law, this is a statement on that ORIGINAL coupling, not only on an auxiliary subsequential coupling. A countable intersection treats all roots and the finite rotated set of reference functionals. Separate constructions of the four references cause no coupling problem: each resulting statement has full measure under the same underlying pair law.

## 5. A general finite-change ray-tail lemma

This elementary lemma removes a possible ambiguity in the common-sector discussion. Let d,d' be two positive coercive edge metrics equal off a finite edge set F0. Put Delta=sum_{e in F0}|d'_e-d_e| and let V contain the endpoints of F0. For a d-ray p rooted at x set C=max_{v in V}T_d(x,v). Choose a late a on p after its last visit to V and with T_d(x,a)>C+Delta/2.

For a later b on p, a simple competitor pi touching V has

 cost_{d'}(pi)>=cost_d(pi)-Delta
 >=T_d(x,a)+T_d(x,b)-2C-Delta
 =T_d(a,b)+2T_d(x,a)-2C-Delta > T_d(a,b).

The second inequality uses its first and last contacts with V and triangle inequalities. A competitor avoiding V has unchanged cost. The ray segment is itself unchanged, so it is d'-minimizing. Positivity allows competitors to be loop erased first. Coercivity supplies the required late cutoff, uniformly over rays from a fixed x. Reverse the roles for the converse. Thus ALL ray germs persist under the finite cap modification, without attaching them back to their former roots or identifying their labels. This lemma supplies the tail-label map; common reference sectors are still needed to compare labels.

## 6. Common prediction sites, symmetry, and selector preservation

Here the AH6–8 ordered-label construction is an explicit premise supplied by the main integration, including L=L_* closed a.s., extremal selectors and monotone labels. The new coupling input can be checked separately.

Reference tail stability and uniform reference-ray control give the AH9.7 unaffected cone: in some random translate of a fixed cone K of positive deterministic angle, both bounding reference rays agree from each root and the sector between them contains no changed cap edge. The geometric reason for agreement is worth stating: both old/new reference rays avoid the finite change set and share a tail; their two finite prefixes to a common tail vertex have identical costs in both metrics. Each is minimal in its own metric, hence both are minimal in the old metric; uniqueness makes them identical. Uniform cone avoidance is precisely the consequence of extended shape and extended reference Busemann estimates used in AH9.7, not a new independence claim.

Fix alpha and the finite neighboring-extremal list used in AH9.8. Under P_s, choose a stub length n so unequal neighboring-label rays have distinct n-step stubs with probability >=1-eta, then a prediction radius N for which their cap-local maximum-likelihood predictions are all correct with probability >=1-eta, adjusting eta across the finite list. Martingale convergence gives this finite-valued prediction assertion. Predictors can be chosen translation-covariantly with fixed tie-breaking. Let Q(v) denote the combined good event with P_s(Q(v)^c)<=eta.

By ergodicity, the spatial density of bad sites tends to at most eta on a P_s-full event. BOTH marginals of the original modification coupling inherit that event by absolute continuity. Their joint law need not be stationary. Consequently the union of bad sites has upper density at most2eta. A translate of K has positive density kappa; choose eta<kappa/4. Discard also the finitely many sites whose radius-N patch meets the finite cap-change region. There remain sites in the cone good in both fields with identical prediction patches. Their actual selector stubs coincide. This is a pathwise density argument and supplies the common-good-site step even for the nonstationary dominated coupling.

The deterministic planar comparison in the actual AH9.8 proof then gives new-label(G_alpha^cw)>alpha_- and new-label(G_alpha^ccw)<alpha_+ for the chosen distinct neighbors, with reference-equality cases treated separately. Quantifiers at gaps need care. If alpha is approached from below in L_*, use a countable approximating sequence. If alpha has an immediate predecessor beta, take alpha_-=beta: the STRICT inequality and membership of the new label in L_* imply new-label>=alpha. The immediate-successor case is symmetric. One cannot assume a sequence approaching an isolated label. Order then squeezes both extremals to alpha, and every intervening alpha-ray has the same new tail label.

This entire label comparison is SYMMETRIC in the two fields. Its premises are absolute continuity of both marginals, finite cap difference, corresponding reference tail coalescence, and the common-sector order. None requires the reverse pair to have been generated by an upward kernel. Apply it in both orientations. The reverse-label statement is needed in the final selector argument; an upward generation rule alone would not supply it.

Finally suppose d'=s'>=s=d and an old extremal ray G_alpha from v is wholly unchanged. It is a new ray by monotonicity and has new label alpha. If the new extremal differs on the relevant side, its tail is an old alpha-ray by the REVERSE label statement. AH9.9 implies that old finite geodesics from v to its late vertices converge to the old extremal. Choose a box containing v and all changed cap edges, and a larger no-return box containing every old geodesic between vertices of the first box. Once those old finite minimizers agree with G_alpha through its first exit from the larger box, they are wholly unchanged: the prefix is unchanged and a return to the change box is impossible. For sufficiently late targets they also differ from the new extremal prefix. Old finite uniqueness makes the old minimizer strictly cheaper in d. It remains unchanged in d', whereas the new extremal cost can only increase. This contradicts its d'-minimality. The entire selected ray is therefore preserved, exactly as required by the wall surgery.

The no-return step is necessary: convergence of finite initial segments by itself would not show that all later edges of a finite minimizer avoid the modification. The symmetric label and isolated-label steps are likewise explicit parts of the repaired interface.
