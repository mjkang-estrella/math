The surface reduction fails at the required interface for a concrete reason. Let g_epsilon(y)=epsilon^(-1)phi(y/epsilon), where phi is smooth, supported in the unit disk and positive on its half disk. Its gradient L1 norm is independent of epsilon, while its L1 mass is O(epsilon). For every x with 1<=|x|<=2 the circle centered at x through the origin has an arc of length comparable to epsilon inside that half disk. Its integral of |g_epsilon| is therefore bounded below independently of epsilon. Thus the absolute boundary supremum has no epsilon^alpha small-support discount, although grad g_epsilon has zero total vector mass and support radius epsilon. This observation even uses circles. It does not refute the disk source or the ellipse target: the tested boundary-through-bump circle need not maximize the filled signed average. It explains why surface maximal estimates cannot simply replace the source optimizing-kernel operation.

The proposed contact-moment/quadric route was chosen as a finite structural approach after these compatible reductions were checked. Its residual task is the coupled cone/error and all-shape charging estimate, including active eccentricity constraints. The failure to complete it does not establish that no compatible classical reduction exists. Bounded search and explicit reading limits remain in reading-ledger.md. Conversely, absence of a theorem with identical wording would not establish substantiality if a complete routine reduction were found. Only a full closed proof at the frozen scope could be sent for independent contribution assessment.
