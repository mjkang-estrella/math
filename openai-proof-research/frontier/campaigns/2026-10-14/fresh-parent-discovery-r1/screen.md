# Family151 finite-regularity screen: technical, no nomination

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY4. No target frozen, no count.

The pinned C1 entropy counterexample uses a fixed prediction map G independent of the amplification constant L. Its only nonsmooth expression is the series with terms L^(1-m) alpha(t) beta(T_m(t,z)) chi(z)(z/|z|)^(2^(m+1))/10. The source chooses L larger than first-derivative growth. Enlarging that same fixed L for a prescribed finite derivative order is a routine parameter transfer, not a new dynamical construction.

For each fixed integer r>=1, the chain rule gives constants C_r,A_r with all derivatives of G^m through order r bounded by C_r A_r^m on the compact periodic quotient. One proves this inductively in r: the order-r recurrence has a linear term DG times D^rG^m plus finitely many products of lower-order derivatives, already exponentially bounded; summing the resulting geometric recurrence and enlarging its exponential rate absorbs any factor m. The lifted clock derivative has the same bounds by translation invariance. On the fixed support annulus, derivatives through order r of the angular power are O_r(2^(r m)). Product and composition rules therefore bound the C^r norm of the mth input term by C D_r^m L^(1-m), for a finite D_r independent of L. Choose L>max(D_r,B,2). The series then converges in C^r. All other map expressions are smooth, with periodic seams and sphere poles covered by the source cutoffs and inverse-coordinate formula.

The source dynamical propositions are stated for the constructed map at any allowed fixed L. Its register identity cancels L^(1-m) against exactly m-1 gains; the partial-sum bound is at most1/2 for every L>1. Enlarging L preserves that interface. Thus the same source mechanism gives a separate finite-C^r example for each finite r, on the same fixed manifold. It does not give one C-infinity example: no single finite gain has been shown to dominate all derivative orders. Failure of this estimate alone also does not prove exact optimal regularity of any chosen map.
