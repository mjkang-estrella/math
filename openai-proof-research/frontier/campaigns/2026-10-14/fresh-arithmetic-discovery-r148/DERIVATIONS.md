## 1. Actual source operations and the distinct companion

The main paper has

    P=C[p,s,u,F,J], x=s²−u²+pF,
    H=x²F−(1+2sx)J−pJ²−u, A=P/(H).

Its determinant-preserving matrix change uses M=[[F,s−u],[s+u,−p]], e=(x,−J)^t, n=(J,x)^t and M'=(1−2ne^t)M. The inverse is M=(1+2ne^t)M', because e^t n=0 and x=−det M is fixed. It takes H to −J−u'. Therefore A=C[p',s',F',J] and

    x=(s')²−J²+p'F', p=p'−2x(x(s'+J)+p'J).

This is the actual degree-five source polynomial. Set

    y=s+x(x+u²), z=sx+pJ.

Then xy−z(z+1)=p(H+u), so in A it equals pu. After inverting p the inverse formulas are s=y−x(x+u²), F=(x−s²+u²)/p, J=(z−sx)/p; eliminating u gives A[p⁻¹]=C[p,p⁻¹,x,y,z].

Source one-stability is an explicit isomorphism, not a cancellation inference. In P[p⁻¹], Δ=−p∂u fixing p,x,y,z preserves P and ΔH=p. Its exponential exp(wΔ) takes H to H+pw. With x0=s²−u², set

    L=x0²F−(1+2sx0)J, N=(1−2sx0)F+4s²J,
    F=4s²L+(1+2sx0)N, J=−(1−2sx0)L+x0²N.

The determinant is one. H=L−u+pQ, where Q=2x0F²+pF³−2sFJ−J². Replacing w by w'=w+Q gives L−u+pw'=0, and hence A[w]≅C[p,s,u,N,w'] over C[p], with both directions polynomial. The names on the target denote the transported coordinates; they need not equal their original images before exp(wΔ).

At p=0 the source gives A/(p)=C[s,u,N]. At p=λ≠0 it gives C[x,y,z], by u=(xy−z(z+1))/λ. All fibers and nonrectifiability are already paid. The companion uses a DIFFERENT polynomial: x=u³+hv, y=−u²+hw, hs=x²+y³, and F_comp=h−(−2s²x+3sy²−3s³y)−1. Its Bezout lifting lemma works over an arbitrary commutative base with (h,y)=1, and gives its zero fiber C^[3]. The identity X²+Y³=s(1+F_comp) and a point with X=Y=0,s=1,F_comp=−1 give a critical point, persisting under every stabilization. ## 2. Relative Derksen algebra: an intrinsic replacement for a chosen filtration

Let D_p(A) be the algebra generated by ker D for every nonzero locally nilpotent C-derivation D of A with Dp=0. Let

    S=C[p,x,y,z,u]/(xy−z(z+1)−pu) ⊂ A.

This inclusion is injective: the presented domain and A become the same Laurent polynomial ring after inverting p. The relation is primitive linear in u after localization and not divisible by p. The relative algebra is intrinsic to the subalgebra C[p], even though it is not intrinsic to the bare A. In fact the FULL Derksen algebra of A=C^[4] is A; substituting it for D_p would be a mistake.

The source filtration has weights (−1,0,0,1,1) on p,s,u,F,J and

    gr A=B[τ,I/τ], B=C[x,y,z,u]/(xy−z(z+1)),
    s_B=y−x(x+u²), I=(x−s_B²+u²,z−s_Bx).

The nonpositive part is exactly S. Indeed any nonpositive-weight monomial can replace each F,J by pF,pJ using its available powers of p; pF=x−s²+u², pJ=z−sx, and s=s_B. Conversely all generators of S have nonpositive degree. This proves equality without declaring a presentation-dependent filtration intrinsic.

For ANY nonzero p-fixed LND D, a positive-degree invariant h would have initial form τ^(−l)b, with l>0 and nonzero b∈I^l. The source leading-derivation calculation gives a nonzero homogeneous LND D0 fixing τ; localizing and multiplying by its invariant τ-power yields a nonzero LND E on B fixing b. Source principalization, normalized bundle lift and auxiliary rigidity exclude this. The only change from source prop:coordinate-obstruction is that D is supplied directly, rather than chosen as a coordinate partial; every subsequent interface is identical. Hence ker D⊂S and D_p(A)⊂S.

For the reverse inclusion clear powers of the invariant p from ∂x,∂y,∂z on A[p⁻¹]. A sufficiently large power for each preserves all five generators of A and thus A. The restrictions remain nonzero LNDs; their kernels contain p,x,y,z between them. On S the LND

    E_x(p)=E_x(x)=E_x(u)=0, E_x(z)=x, E_x(y)=2z+1

has pE_x(s)=p(2z+1), pE_x(F)=−2s(2z+1), pE_x(J)=−2xz. It preserves A and fixes u. Thus

    D_p(A)=S.                                                (2.1)

This implements the actual r29 and Ghosh–Pal invariant/contraction strategy in its correct relative form. It is not a new abstract Derksen principle.

## 3. Recover zero, and pay the whole lifting criterion

The canonical morphism π:Spec A→Spec S is an isomorphism exactly over D(p). At p=0 its image is D=V(I)⊂Y=Spec B. There is an explicit isomorphism D≅A²_(s,u):

    x=s²−u², z=sx, y=s+x(x+u²)=s+s²x.

The inverse parameter is s=s_B. At every residue-field point of D the inverse-image fiber is the affine line given by x²F−(1+2sx)J=u; its coefficient row is unimodular. Off D in V(p) the inverse image is empty. No point of V(p) can belong to an open over which π is an isomorphism.

An automorphism of A preserving C[p] acts as p↦αp+β, α≠0. It conjugates the p-fixed LNDs among themselves and therefore preserves S by (2.1). The nonisomorphism divisor of π is intrinsic to this inclusion, so (p) is preserved in S. Its units are C*: it embeds in the polynomial A, whose units are C*. Consequently β=0.

Unlike the higher-thickness r146 issue, the ENTIRE modification here is controlled by one reduced ideal. Write in S

    f0=x−s_B²+u², g0=z−s_Bx, K=(p,f0,g0).

Then

    A=S[K/p]=S[f0/p,g0/p] ⊂ S[p⁻¹],
    pA∩S=K, S/K=C[s,u].                                    (3.1)

The first equality follows from F=f0/p,J=g0/p and s=s_B. The second is the kernel of S→A/(p)=C[s,u,N], whose image is C[s,u]; reducing modulo p computes exactly I, not only its radical. Thus K itself is radical. Every automorphism ψ of S with ψ(p)=αp and ψ(K)=K extends uniquely to A by ψ(b/p)=ψ(b)/(αp), and its inverse extends by the same rule. Conversely every normalizing automorphism of A restricts to one of these ψ. Therefore the precise remaining normalizer problem is

    Aut(A,C[p]) ≅ {ψ∈Aut(S): ψ(p)=αp for some α∈C*, ψ(K)=K}. (3.2)

No infinite list of thickened contractions remains unpaid at this thickness. This is the actual affine-modification universal-property operation from DMP §2, with the necessary intrinsic subring and exact ideal proved here. A proposed automorphism of Y alone still needs extension to S: the normal direction is part of (3.2).

As a consequence of β=0, the embedded zero fiber cannot be ambient-equivalent to a nonzero fiber. Indeed an equivalence of principal prime ideals (p−λ),(p−μ) sends their generators to constant multiples, so normalizes C[p] and must satisfy μ=αλ. This is a relative-Derksen consequence retained as a payment, not a separate nominee or a classification of the nonzero fibers.

## 4. Every extending additive action is vertical on the zero fiber

Let D be a p-fixed LND of A. Its exponential preserves S and K by (2.1),(3.1). Hence D restricts to S and induces an LND E of B preserving I. One may extract coefficients of exp(tD) by interpolation to justify D(S)⊂S from invariance for every complex t; no unstated stability under differentiation is needed.

Use the source torsor

    C=C[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac−bd−1), IC=(v),
    v=a³b+a²u²−d²,
    wt(a,d,b,c,u)=(1,1,−1,−1,0).

The normalized source lift of E to C commutes with these weights. Since E preserves I, the lift preserves (v). A locally nilpotent derivation δ on a characteristic-zero domain with δ(v)∈vC has δ(v)=0: if δv=vq≠0, its nilpotence degree would satisfy degδ(v)−1=degδ(v)+degδ(q), impossible. Source prop:rigidity then forces E=0. Thus

    D(S)⊂pS.

On A/(p)=C[s,u,N], the induced LND kills s,u. After localizing to C(s,u)[N], a locally nilpotent derivation sends N to a constant in C(s,u); intersecting back gives

    D mod p = b_D(s,u)∂N.                                  (4.1)

Accordingly the group U_p generated by all exp(tD) with Dp=0 restricts to translations (s,u,N)↦(s,u,N+b(s,u)). The restriction is stronger than the mere observation that one particular source action has small orbits. DMP Proposition3.1(4) already uses this very ideal-preserving-LND obstruction to control the entire additive subgroup of a modification. Here the source supplies the required rigidity in the nonprincipal quadric center.

## 5. Surjectivity onto ALL translations: explicit finite construction

It is not enough to exhibit one nonzero vertical action. We can pay the converse fully.

On S define the Poisson bracket by

    {x,z}=x, {x,y}=2z+1, {y,z}=−y, {p,−}={u,−}=0.

The quadratic relation is central. Write E_h={h,−}. For Z=z+1/2 and t∈C, put ℓ_t=x+2tZ+t²y; also ℓ_∞=y. These are SL2-transforms of x on the quadric: exp(−t E_y)(x)=ℓ_t. Consequently E_ℓ is locally nilpotent. For n≥1 and q∈C[u], E_(q ℓ^n) is the invariant replica n q ℓ^(n−1)E_ℓ, also locally nilpotent. It fixes p,u.

For each such h, pE_h preserves A. Namely pE_h(s)∈S, pE_h(u)=0, and

    pE_h(F)=E_h(f0), pE_h(J)=E_h(g0),

both in S. Preservation on generators suffices and local nilpotence is inherited from localization, where p is invariant. On the zero fiber its coefficient is

    (pE_h)(N) mod p = −∂s(h|D).                            (5.1)

Here is an independent check that also determines the normalization. The coefficient is

    (1−2sx) E_h(f0)|D + 4s² E_h(g0)|D.

For h=x,y,z,u respectively this equals

    −2s, −(1+4s³−2su²), −(3s²−u²), 0.

These are precisely −∂s of x|D=s²−u², y|D=s+s⁴−s²u², z|D=s³−su², u. Both sides are derivations in h, so (5.1) holds for every h∈B (and for its same-expression lift to S).

It remains to show that the allowed Hamiltonians span enough functions; declaring every Hamiltonian LND would be false. The vector space C[x,y,Z]/(xy−Z²+1/4) is spanned by 1 and powers ℓ_t^n with t∈C∪{∞}. To prove this, filter by ordinary degree. The associated graded is C[x,y,Z]/(xy−Z²), whose degree-n part identifies with the binary forms of degree 2n under x=r²,Z=rv,y=v². The leading forms of ℓ_t^n become (r+tv)^(2n). Choosing 2n+1 distinct t gives a Vandermonde basis of that space. Subtracting a corresponding linear combination lowers degree; induction proves the span assertion for the actual affine quadric. Tensor with C[u]. This is a finite constructive decomposition for each given polynomial, using only linear algebra and induction.

Given ANY b(s,u)∈C[s,u], choose a polynomial antiderivative h_D with −∂s h_D=b. Lift it to h_D(s_B,u)∈B; the quotient B→C[s,u] is surjective. Decompose that lift as a finite sum h_i=q_i(u)ℓ_(t_i)^(n_i), plus a polynomial in u. Each pE_(h_i) is an actual LND of A. The finite product

    ∏_i exp(pE_(h_i))

restricts on p=0 to N↦N+b(s,u), since the reductions commute and add their coefficients. Therefore

    image(U_p→Aut(A/(p)))={N↦N+b(s,u):b∈C[s,u]}.             (5.2)

This pays exact surjectivity, not just tangent-span generation. In particular the orbits on p=0 are exactly the N-lines indexed by (s,u), and there is no 2-transitivity even on one such line. At each λ≠0 the denominator-cleared ∂x,∂y,∂z yield all constant translations on the fiber C[x,y,z], so U_p is transitive there. No general infinite-transitivity theorem or unread replica theorem is needed for this transitivity assertion.

The compatibility in (5.2) is elementary but concrete: source rigidity gives the upper restriction; invariant replicas and finite binary-form interpolation give the whole lower restriction. These operations do not determine the quotient by U_p or the action of an arbitrary automorphism on its two-dimensional space of special orbits.

## 6. Explicit base scalings and what all finite jets fail to detect

Every η with η⁶=1 gives an actual automorphism of A:

    p↦ηp, s↦η²s, u↦η⁻¹u, F↦η⁻³F, J↦η⁻¹J.              (6.1)

Indeed x↦η⁴x and sx is fixed. Each term of H scales by η⁻¹, since η⁶=1. The diagonal inverse is obtained using η⁻¹. Thus if

    Λ={α∈C*: there is Φ∈Aut(A) with Φ(p)=αp},

then μ6⊂Λ⊂C*. The calculations do NOT prove Λ=μ6. A degree argument restricted to diagonal maps would not justify that assertion for arbitrary polynomial automorphisms.

More strongly, a finite p-adic obstruction cannot settle the remaining scaling question. In the determinant-one coordinates of §1, H=L−u+pQ(L), with coefficients in C[p,s,u,N]. There is a unique formal solution

    L=u+p l1+p² l2+… in C[s,u,N][[p]]

obtained recursively because the coefficient of L at p=0 is one. More explicitly, if L_r solves H≡0 mod p^r, correct by −H(L_r) modulo p^(r+1); its linear coefficient is 1 mod p. The inverse coordinate formulas then give an isomorphism

    lim A/(p^r) ≅ C[s,u,N][[p]].                            (6.2)

Equivalently the maps at every finite order are inverse by uniqueness of the implicit solution. For every α∈C*, substitution p↦αp with s,u,N fixed is a continuous automorphism of the right side, hence a compatible formal automorphism of the left side. This is a direct recursion, not an appeal to an unverified analytic convergence or algebraization theorem. Its coefficients may have unbounded polynomial degree as the order grows. It supplies no automorphism of A.

Thus the remaining obstruction is GLOBAL polynomial extension, not a missing finite jet or an unsupported assertion of formal failure. Formula (3.2) identifies the exact finite ideal it must preserve at the global level.

## 7. Stabilized extension is fully compatible too

The explicit source isomorphism Θ:A[w]≅C[p,S,U,M,W] from §1 transfers every polynomial base-affine map p↦αp+β, fixing the other four coordinates, to an automorphism of A[w]. Its inverse uses α⁻¹ and −β/α. In particular every pair of source fibers becomes ambient-equivalent after one trivial-line stabilization. This is not a claim that their original embeddings were rectifiable.

The same isomorphism yields the full relative Derksen algebra after stabilization: coordinate partials in the four other variables have kernels jointly generating C[p,S,U,M,W]. Hence the proper intrinsic inclusion (2.1) disappears after adding one variable. The relative additive group becomes transitive on every stabilized fiber A4, including p=0, by ordinary translations in the four source coordinates.

Dutta–Lahiri Lemma3.3 and Theorem3.4 and Blanc–Poloni Proposition2.3 already pay exponential shifts, denominator clearing and one-variable stable-coordinate constructions in their stated coefficient-ring settings. We use the actual source automorphism to avoid assuming their more general hypotheses for an arbitrary A3-fibration. These positive stable and formal constructions coexist with the unstabilized relative obstruction; neither cancels the added variable.
