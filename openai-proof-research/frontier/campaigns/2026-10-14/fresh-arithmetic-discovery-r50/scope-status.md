# T127N49: full working target, unresolved

Recorded after exploratory feasibility, actual source127 operative reading, and Blais–O’Donnell–Wimmer primary comparison, before assembling the written proof attempts. This is a fixed working question, not a theorem claim or nomination.

Does there exist an absolute finite constant C such that for every integer n>=1, every integer 1<=d<=n, every real polynomial P in n variables of total degree at most d, and every vector (p_1,...,p_n) in (0,1)^n, the following holds? Set f=sign(P) on {-1,1}^n, with sign(0)=+1, and let X have independent coordinates with Pr[X_i=+1]=p_i. Write

I_i = Pr_{X_{-i}}[f(X_{-i},+1) != f(X_{-i},-1)].

The full requested inequality is

sum_i sqrt(p_i(1-p_i)) I_i <= C d sqrt(n).

C is independent of every bias, dimension, degree and polynomial. No lower atom bound, number-of-bias-scales bound, monotonicity, regularity, or nonvanishing assumption is allowed. Multilinearization preserves the sign values and does not raise degree. A logarithmic loss or bounded-bias special case is not a replacement target. Neither proof nor counterexample to this full statement is available here. All subsequent support and method obstructions retain this scope.
