## 6. Infinite rank of the exact kernel, and what it does not obstruct

The source peak polynomials are nonzero and have the distinct orders k_j=Q^j at 0. Put Q_0=product_j q_j, holomorphic and nonzero on some neighborhood of 0 because Q_0(0)=1. Among the tensor coordinates of H are

\[
f_j=V_{\{j\}}V_{\varnothing}^{L-1}.
\]

Each f_j has vanishing order exactly k_j, with nonzero leading homogeneous term c epsilon P_j; the other factors equal 1 at 0. These infinitely many holomorphic germs are linearly independent: in any nontrivial finite linear combination the smallest remaining order cannot cancel. Thus the kernel K(z,bar z)=||H(z)||^2 has infinite Hermitian rank near 0.

Here is a direct justification of the last implication, avoiding an unproved invocation of Calabi's criterion. Polarize K as <H(z),H(xi)>. Equality of diagonal real-analytic functions forces equality of their polarized Taylor series. If K were a sum of N squared moduli of holomorphic germs, the polarized kernel would have Gram rank at most N. The isometry defined on finite linear combinations of H(z) by sending H(z) to the competing N-dimensional feature vector would then force the closed span of H(z) to have dimension at most N. All coordinate functions of H would lie in the span of N holomorphic functions, contradicting the independent f_j.

Consequently (M,ghat_1) has no local holomorphic isometric immersion into any finite-dimensional complex hyperbolic unit ball near (0,0). To see this without hiding the pluriharmonic ambiguity, compose a hypothetical local immersion f with a ball automorphism so f(0,0)=0. The diastasis at 0 of K_1 is K_1 itself: its polarized extension at a second argument (0,0) vanishes, because F(0,0)=0. The potential -log(1-||f||^2) has the same property. Their equal complex Hessians therefore imply equality of the potentials, since their difference is pluriharmonic and has no nonconstant purely holomorphic or antiholomorphic Taylor coefficients and has constant term zero. Equation (6) now implies

\[
\|f(z,w)\|^2=|z|^2+|w|^2K(z,\bar z).
\]

Expand f(z,w)=sum_{m≥0} b_m(z)w^m. The coefficient of w^m bar(w)^m for m≥2 is ||b_m(z)||^2, so b_m=0. The coefficient of |w|^2 gives K=||b_1||^2, a finite-rank representation, a contradiction.

This obstruction is **isometric**, for the specifically chosen ghat_1 and the standard fixed-curvature target normalization. It does not rule out a finite-dimensional bounded embedding, already present in the old C^5 proof, nor a proper bounded embedding in some larger dimension, nor any isometric statement after changing the source metric. In particular it is not a new obstruction to bounded holomorphic coordinates.

There is an even simpler compatibility obstruction to making the paid ghat_lambda itself a Hilbert-ball pullback when lambda>1 in this normalization. Restriction to w=0 is lambda times the standard ball metric. The normalized squared norm that any centered ball immersion would require is 1-(1-|z|^2)^lambda; its |z_1|^4 coefficient is -lambda(lambda-1)/2. A squared norm of a holomorphic Hilbert-valued germ has nonnegative diagonal Taylor coefficients. Hence no such immersion exists. The comparison in (9), rather than an asserted equality for lambda>1, is essential.
