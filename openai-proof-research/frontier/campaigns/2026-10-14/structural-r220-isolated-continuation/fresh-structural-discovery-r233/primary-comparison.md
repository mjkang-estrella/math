## The strongest supplied reduction

Source357's main conclusion already gives a dimension-only distortion L_n=4 sqrt(A(n)); a point-dependent finite constant understates it. Its coordinates, pinching and renormalization proofs supply all-center harmonic approximation, all-pole heat bounds for matrix-measure sources, monotone inverse heat matrices, cumulative normalization errors, summable directional pinching, an inverse-transpose value-coordinate iteration with all jets, and an ambient all-pairs Taylor argument. The open full-measure manifold region and countable atlas are also already supplied. None is a discovery here.

The author proof keeps those operations. The coarse leading sum I+2 sum S_i is replaced by positive localized heat increments sampled at fine centers, with a partition of unity in harmonic value coordinates. The additional assertion is its accumulated comparison to H_k with error b_L+C h+o_{L,h}(1), uniform in all scales and centers. This comparison is proved before the source iteration is reused. A scale-by-scale small relative error alone would not suffice, because the inverse matrices may grow without bound and there are infinitely many scales. The cumulative normalization estimate and Gaussian envelope are what make the replacement compatible with the pinching and differentiated-inverse estimates.

## Metric and extrinsic Reifenberg alternatives

[Gigli–Violo, arXiv:2406.13771v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13771v1), the version cited in Source357, was read at lines 269–333, 618–744, 1244–1432 and 2043–2175; lines 1244–1267 were displayed again to recover a tool truncation. The readings include the main statements, the multiplicative distortion/tail/injectivity argument, the actual inter-manifold maps, and the relative gluing construction for immersions. The construction already controls derivative errors, patch agreement and gluing margins; treating a partition of unity as automatically injective would under-credit it.

Execute its strongest applicable regime as follows. If ordinary scale errors epsilon_j have a small summable tail, its transitions distort lengths by factors 1+C epsilon_j. The product is bounded by exp(C sum epsilon_j); the tail maps converge on a fixed controlled region, while its separation argument retains injectivity. The limiting chart is locally bi-Lipschitz to the smooth initial manifold. On a smaller coordinate neighborhood, continuity of that smooth metric gives a Euclidean chart with distortion as close to one as desired; a still smaller inner region ensures ambient minimizing paths cannot escape the larger controlled region. Thus the summable-error regime of the proposed conclusion is already a classical composition. This is explicitly credited.

The source's summable E_i is a directional positive-mass budget, not an asserted summable sequence of ordinary GH errors. Substituting sqrt(E_i) in the distortion product would require sum sqrt(E_i)<infinity, which is not one of the paid estimates. No failure of that one substitution is claimed to prove novelty. The author proof supplies the different accumulated matrix estimate and checks its full compatibility. No explicit RCD example separating every metric Reifenberg hypothesis is claimed.

[David–Toro, arXiv:0910.4869v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.4869v1), the cited preprint version of Reifenberg parameterizations for sets with holes, was read at lines 724–877, 2501–2644 and 2648–2987. The selected range at 724–877 accidentally includes the beginning of the next section; that exposure is recorded and is not claimed as full coverage of its bump construction.

The actual squared-angle proof improves a first-order plane mismatch into second-order tangent-length distortion, then uses its accumulated square budget and a pair-scale argument to obtain the global bi-Lipschitz conclusion. This pays the coherent extrinsic-plane regime, including its all-pairs step. A noncollapsed RCD ball has not here been furnished with the finite-dimensional extrinsic coherent-plane data required by that theorem. The present proof instead uses fixed positive matrix coefficients and the supplied intrinsic harmonic value coordinates. No assertion that these categories cannot be related, and no novelty conclusion from a missing category bridge, is made.

[Mondino–Naber, arXiv:1405.2222v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.2222v3), was read at lines 1323–1437.
