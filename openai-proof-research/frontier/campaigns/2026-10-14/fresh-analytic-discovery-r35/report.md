## What the pinned operations already pay for

Source086 `outer.tex`, Theorem `thm:local`, bounds the absolute sum of scalar local forms, with a *single common input in each of the four slots*. Every local kernel has four vanishing one-point marginals and uniform Gevrey bounds. Its scalar coefficients may vary independently between cells. Thus signed smooth scale sums and square functions for fixed scale blocks are already available. The detailed reduction in `working-checkpoint.md` is technical support only.

The elementary binary decomposition then gives a logarithmic-in-scale-count variation bound for the smooth dyadic model. This is not the uniform target. The loss is an insufficiency of that argument, not a lower bound or an obstruction to the target.

Ordinary Hölder/maximal-function consequences in the source's admissible exponent region are likewise paid. Uniform operator bounds for fixed truncations must not be confused with a pointwise supremum over truncations. Tao's 2015 paper, pp.1–3, explicitly discusses the former equivalence; it does not furnish the latter.

## The unpaid interface

Linearizing the target gives multipliers

    m(x,t)=Σ_j β_j(x) 1_{a_j(x)<|t|<b_j(x)},
    Σ_j |β_j(x)|^{r′}≤1,

with the annuli disjoint at every x. The common fourth input f_0(x) is replaced, scale by scale, by different masked functions. Keeping those masks in the kernels destroys three anchor cancellations and their smoothness. Absorbing one common mask into f_0 is legitimate and does **not** have this problem; it does not accommodate a different mask at each scale. The explicit Fourier calculation in the checkpoint separates these facts.

This matters in an operative part of the source, not merely its theorem's wording. `counting.tex` lines145–335 uses all four Fourier zero lines to obtain the factor μ in its frequency tests and to discard near-line tuples. In the three-core case the anchor can be the side role, so retaining only the output anchor is insufficient for an unchanged proof. `completion.tex` combines this gain with the source's fixed-input base expansion to absorb C_N. No corresponding masked counting estimate or masked base expansion is proved here.

Adding one private-coordinate label per possible mask is not a uniform repair: `compression.tex` counts all available labels **along each spatial path**, including unselected labels. The literal construction with M masks at the root has D≥M and pays a positive power of log(2D). The source's independence from the total number of labels on disjoint branches does not remove this pathwise cost. This only rejects that literal encoding; it does not rule out a compressed representation using the disjoint-annulus structure.

Two comparisons indicate what a real proof would need:

* Do–Oberlin–Palsson's Walsh bilinear variation proof modifies the output wave packet by the stopping coefficient, then uses its exact tree/martingale identity (Claim2.1, (2.4)–(2.5)) and Lépingle to prove Proposition4.1. That identity, or a replacement with uniform quadratic-structure costs, has not been established for086. Bessel bounds alone are not this identity.
* The pinned triangular companion has an actual repeated-mask dissipation lemma. It bounds the energy jumps entrywise and rescales the dual matrix to obtain √n for n disjoint annuli. Its three-edge matrix trace and cubic energy are a different representation from086's four progression slots. No norm-preserving representation carrying f_0 in L∞ and the other three factors in L³ is supplied here. Merely citing the companion's theorem is not a transference proof.

Becker–Durcik's 2026 bilinear shifted-kernel result supplies a further useful distinction: its hard-averaging variation upgrade starts with an already proved **smooth variation** theorem (Theorem4.2), then estimates mean-zero endpoint errors by square functions. The procedure does not create smooth long variation from ordinary singular-integral boundedness. Their short-variation proof also distinguishes p≥2 from p<2; importing its Minkowski step at our output exponent1 would be invalid.
