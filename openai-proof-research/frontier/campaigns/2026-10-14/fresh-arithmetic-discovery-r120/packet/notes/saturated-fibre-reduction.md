## 1. What was investigated

The second selected family began with elliptic multiplication/division and was narrowed to dependence loci in elliptic powers. The natural geometric ambient is an abelian variety A over Qbar. Source016's abelian Zilber–Pink theorem provides finiteness of optimal subvarieties in every irreducible subvariety of every abelian variety over Qbar. Its section10 already treats translates by an arbitrary finite-rank subgroup, including all division denominators and torsion. These facts are credited inputs, not new r120 conclusions.

The operation tested here is whether the fixed auxiliary ambient used for point non-density also handles the entire hierarchy of positive-dimensional exceptional subvarieties. Merely observing that section10 states non-density would not demonstrate a new operation. The strongest reduction below carries every closure and defect at once. It uses the actual fixed-fibre and Poincaré operations in Pink2005Thm5.3 and Barroero–Dill2401.11193v2 §§4.1,5–7.

All varieties, points and homomorphisms below are over Qbar. Connected algebraic subgroups of an abelian variety are abelian subvarieties. A special subvariety means a torsion translate of one. A coset without this adjective may have any translating point. No positive-characteristic extension, varying-family estimate or effective bound is being asserted.

## 2. The original source construction already absorbs divisions

Let Γ⊂A(Qbar) have finite Q-rank. Choose a1,…,ar in Γ spanning Γ⊗Q. After multiplication by one positive integer, the cyclic subgroup generated by a=(a1,…,ar) has connected Zariski closure C⊂A^r. Multiplying the tuple does not change its rational span. In rank zero use C=0 and the empty tuple. For every γ∈Γ there exist n>0 and integers ni with nγ=Σ ni ai: first clear the rational coefficients, then kill the resulting torsion error.

Set B=A×C and j(x)=(x,a). Given an algebraic subgroup G⊂A and such a γ, put f(c)=Σ ni ci and ψ(c)=(f(c),nc). Then

    H=[n]_B^{-1}((G×0)+ψ(C))

contains j(G+γ). The kernel of ψ and ψ(C)∩(G×0) are finite because their second coordinate is multiplication by n on C. Consequently

    dim H=dim G+dim C,     codim_B H=codim_A G.

Every component of H is special. No uniform bound on n is needed; all H live in one fixed B. Source section10 checks that X×{a} has full special closure B if X lies in no proper arbitrary translate, and obtains its translated non-density statement. This proof is already Pink's Theorem5.3, including the connected cyclic closure and finite multiplication preimage.

The stronger no-proper-translate assumption in that corollary is used to make the auxiliary slice special-generic. It is not a defect in the source, and its absence from the closure treatment below results from working relative to the actual closure.

## 3. Identify the saturation; do not silently change Γ

Define

    Λ={z∈A(Qbar): [n]z=f(a) for some n>0 and f∈Hom(C,A)}.

Hom(C,A) is finitely generated over Z, so Λ has finite Q-rank. It contains all torsion points, is divisible in the sense that every division point of a member belongs to Λ, and is stable under End(A). It contains Γ, by the coordinate-combination relations above. It is precisely the division closure of the End(A)-module generated by Γ.

Here is the last assertion with its compatibility paid. The coordinate projections C→A show that the division closure of Σ End(A)ai is contained in Λ. Conversely, Poincaré reducibility gives a homomorphism u:A^r→C and M>0 with u|C=[M]_C. Thus M f extends to the homomorphism f∘u:A^r→A. Such a homomorphism is a sum of maps in End(A) on the coordinates, and evaluating at a gives

    M f(a)=Σ ei(ai),       ei∈End(A).

Taking division closures proves the reverse inclusion. Replacing ai by a common multiple does not change this group. Since Γ is contained in the division closure of the ai and contains them, using Γ or those generators gives the same saturated group.

This rational extension is a standard consequence of the complement isogeny C×C'→A^r and a quasi-inverse isogeny. It is the same semisimplicity operation used in Barroero–Dill Proposition6.1. No new simultaneous-denominator argument is needed.

If Γ was already divisible, contained all torsion and was End(A)-stable, then Λ=Γ. Otherwise equality is not implied by finite rank. Keeping this distinction is essential.

## 4. Every Λ-coset is a component of a special fibre

Let T0=B0+λ⊂A with B0 an abelian subvariety and λ∈Λ. Choose n>0,f:C→A with nλ=f(a). Form

    H=[n]_{A×C}^{-1}((B0×0)+(f,[n])(C)).

As above, dim H=dim B0+dim C. In the fibre over a, membership is exactly

    n x−f(a)∈B0.

Thus this fibre is a finite union of cosets of B0, one of which is T0. Select a connected component S of H containing j(λ). It is a torsion coset. Since j(λ+B0) is connected and contains j(λ), it is contained in S, and its dimension equals the fibre dimension. Hence j(T0) is an irreducible component of S∩(A×{a}). This deals with every component, not merely a chosen division point.

The projection of S onto C is all C. Indeed its projection is a special subvariety containing a. A proper torsion coset in C is contained in a proper algebraic subgroup with finitely many components, whereas the cyclic subgroup generated by a is Zariski dense in C. This proves surjectivity and the asserted fibre dimension.

## 5. Every nonempty special fibre has Λ-translates as its components

Conversely let S=D+τ⊂A×C be special and meet A×{a}. The same density argument shows that p:D→C is surjective. Let K=(ker p)^0. By Poincaré reducibility choose a homomorphism s:C→D and N>0 such that p∘s=[N]_C. To see this, complement K inside D; p restricted to that complement is an isogeny and has a quasi-inverse after multiplication by an integer.

Write τ=(τ_A,τ_C), with both coordinates torsion. Choose b∈C with N b=a−τ_C. The point

    (x0,a)=τ+s(b)

lies in S. If f=pr_A∘s, then

    N x0=f(a)+Nτ_A−f(τ_C).

The last two terms are torsion. Multiplying by their order shows x0∈Λ.

Every component of ker p is a torsion translate of K. One direct justification is that a coset in the finite component group is killed by some integer m. For a representative k, mk∈K, and surjectivity of [m] on K allows subtraction of an element of K to make k torsion. Therefore every component of the fibre S_a is a torsion translate of x0+pr_A(K). Since Λ contains all torsion, every such component is a Λ-coset.

This is exactly the abelian case of Barroero–Dill Proposition6.1: a quasi-section supplies an orbit point in one fibre component, and torsion supplies one in every component. The almost-split qualification in their general semiabelian proposition cannot be discarded; the present ambient is abelian throughout. We do not turn their non-split counterexample into a new target.

## 6. Closure and the exact defect identity

The preceding two directions show that Λ-cosets are closed under taking irreducible components of nonempty intersections. Indeed represent two cosets as fibre components of special subvarieties S1,S2⊂A×C. Components of S1∩S2 are special. The intersection of their fibres is a finite union of fibres of those components. Fibre components from a fixed Si are disjoint cosets of its connected kernel, so the components lying in the selected pair of fibre components are exactly the components of their intersection. Section5 makes each a Λ-coset.

Therefore every nonempty irreducible Z⊂A has a smallest Λ-coset M(Z). To prove existence, choose one of least dimension containing Z. Intersect it with any other such coset; the component containing irreducible Z is a Λ-coset and cannot have smaller dimension, so it equals the chosen coset. This proves minimality and uniqueness as well as monotonicity under Z⊂Z'.

Let S(Z) be the smallest special subvariety of A×C containing j(Z). Its projection onto C is all C. Let F(Z) be the fibre component containing j(Z), viewed as a coset in A. Section5 implies that F(Z) is a Λ-coset, hence M(Z)⊂F(Z). Conversely section4 supplies a special subvariety T over C whose fibre has M(Z) as a component. Minimality gives S(Z)⊂T. Since all these projections are surjective and have equidimensional fibres,

    dim C+dim M(Z) ≤ dim S(Z) ≤ dim T = dim C+dim M(Z).

Equality holds. The inclusion M(Z)⊂F(Z) and equality of dimensions force F(Z)=M(Z). Define

    δΛ(Z)=dim M(Z)−dim Z.

Then the exact identity is

    δsp(j(Z))=dim C+δΛ(Z).                       (★)

This remains valid for points, for nontransverse Z, for torsion-only Γ and for A of dimension zero. When C=0 the group Λ is A_tors, and (★) is just the usual special defect.

No proof of full Zilber–Pink is hidden here. The argument is dimension bookkeeping for a fixed fibre, using the actual saturation and the quasi-section. Barroero–Dill Lemma5.4 and Theorem7.4 already make the corresponding fibre/defect argument; Proposition7.6 treats its compatibility with weak defect. Their term “Σ-optimal” in Definition7.7 means weakly optimal with orbit-special weak closure, not the δΛ-optimal notion below. The distinction in definitions is retained instead of claiming the titles are interchangeable.

## 7. Optimality and maximal atypical subvarieties are transported

For a fixed irreducible X⊂A, call Z⊂X δΛ-optimal if every irreducible U with Z⊊U⊂X has δΛ(U)>δΛ(Z). The map j is an isomorphism X→X×{a}; every irreducible subvariety of the latter is j(U) for a unique U⊂X. Identity (★), with the SAME constant dim C for all U, gives the equivalence

    Z is δΛ-optimal in X  ⇔  j(Z) is special-optimal in j(X).

Source016 Proposition optimal-finite applies directly to j(X) in the abelian variety A×C, without a special-generic assumption. Hence it gives finiteness for the left side. This is a paid consequence, not a r120 supporting nominee.

For completeness, measure atypical intersections inside M(X). If Y is a component of X∩T for a Λ-coset T⊂M(X), and

    dim Y > dim X+dim T−dim M(X),

then δΛ(Y)<δΛ(X). If such Y is maximal for inclusion and Y⊊U⊂X had δΛ(U)≤δΛ(Y), a component V of X∩M(U) containing U would satisfy

    dim V≥dim U=dim M(U)−δΛ(U)
          >dim M(U)−δΛ(X).

Thus V is atypical and properly contains Y, a contradiction. Every maximal atypical Y is therefore δΛ-optimal. A strictly ascending chain of atypical subvarieties increases dimension and terminates. Finitely many maximal ones cover the atypical locus. Each is proper, since any Λ-coset containing all X contains M(X), and equality gives expected dimension rather than a strict inequality. This is the exact argument of source section09, with (★) providing the compatible closure.

The whole construction handles all subgroup directions, torsion components and division levels simultaneously in one fixed ambient. No omitted finite-coherence or component-selection operation remains to be supplied as a purported new advance.

## 8. Why the original group cannot be substituted silently

The obstruction can already be written in an elliptic square. Let E/Qbar have a nontorsion point P, put D=〈P〉div⊂E(Qbar), and set

    Γ=E(Qbar)_tors × D ⊂ E²(Qbar).

This is a divisible group of finite rank containing all torsion of E². The cosets

    T1={(x,y):x+y=P},       T2={(x,y):2x+y=2P}

each contain a Γ-point, namely (0,P) and (0,2P). Their intersection is the singleton {(P,0)}, which is not a Γ-point. Consequently no smallest Γ-coset contains this singleton: a smallest one would have to lie in both T1,T2 and would equal their non-Γ singleton intersection.

The endomorphism-stable saturation contains (P,0), since coordinate swap sends (0,P) to it. Thus the saturation resolves the closure issue, but changes the coset class. This example is the additive elliptic analogue of Aslanyan's explicit torus example in §5. It is not a new impossibility result for all finite-rank intersection statements.

In particular Aslanyan's actual Γ-atypical notion asks that the WEAKLY SPECIAL closure contain a Γ-point. His Theorem1.8 and full §2 proof already give finiteness for maximal such subvarieties for arbitrary finite-rank Γ. The proof uses finitely many directions, the exceptional fibre-dimension locus in a quotient, Mordell–Lang there, and induction. Sections4 and5 give uniform versions of that weaker notion and discuss the closure distinction. Barroero–Dill's Theorem7.9 similarly has a weak-optimality/orbit-special condition. One must neither sell these known results again nor infer an arbitrary-Γ δΓ theory where no closure exists.
