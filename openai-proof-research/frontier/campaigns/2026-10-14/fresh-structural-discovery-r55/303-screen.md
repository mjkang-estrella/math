# Family303: element-dependent amplification is insufficient

Pre-freeze screen. No candidate theorem or scope was frozen. Source303's
fixed amplification cannot simply be replaced by a positive-element-dependent
amplification, even under unitality, simplicity, separability and nuclearity.
This is a classical-counterexample consequence, not a new nominee.

The source introduction was read completely. It defines (F_n) with ONE n
chosen before all positive elements, including all scalar spectral cutoffs.
Theorem exact-infiniteness removes that uniform amplification for exact
algebras. The theorem individual-to-strong then yields strong pure
infiniteness without exactness; separable nuclear algebras absorb O_infinity.

Rørdam, A simple C*-algebra with a finite and an infinite projection,
Acta Math.191(2003),109–142, Theorem6.10 and Corollary7.1, gives a nuclear,
unital, separable, simple algebra A with properly infinite unit1 and a
nonzero finite projection q. The proof chooses a properly infinite corner
of the simple crossed product and moves the finite projection into it.
Actual local primary text lines1250–1346 were read, including those full
statements and proofs. Additional displayed lines180–260 concern preliminary
vector-bundle material; the full construction is not claimed read.
Primary URL: https://web.math.ku.dk/~rordam/manus/simple.pdf .

For each nonzero a in A_+, simplicity makes a full. Thus there are finitely
many x_i for which b=sum_i x_i^* a x_i is invertible: the closed ideal
generated by a is A, and the usual positive finite-sum approximate-unit
criterion for a full positive element gives such a sum. If X is the column
of x_i and D=a^{oplus m}, then Y=X b^{-1/2} satisfies Y^*DY=1. Therefore
1 preceq a^{oplus m}. Conversely a preceq1 (implemented by a^{1/2}), so
[a^{oplus m}]<=m[1]<=[1], since1 is properly infinite. Hence a^{oplus m}
is Cuntz equivalent to1 and is properly infinite. Zero has the property by
convention. This proves the element-dependent premise for every positive a.
But q is finite and nonzero, hence not properly infinite. Consequently A
is not ordinarily or strongly purely infinite and cannot absorb O_infinity.

This does not contradict the source: there is no uniform m. Indeed the
source's exact theorem implies, by contraposition, that these m cannot have
a common finite upper bound. That contraposition is technical support only.
No nonexact variant, restricted domain, or quantitative weakening is nominated.
