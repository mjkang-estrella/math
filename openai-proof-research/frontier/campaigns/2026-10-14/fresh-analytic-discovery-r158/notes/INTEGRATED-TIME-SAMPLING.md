## 3. Near-full marks are already covered by the operative proof

Here is a precise comparison model, not a nomination. Fix a bounded graph chart of lines (t,b+t v) in R4, v in R3, t in [0,1]. For a sequence N->infinity assume distinct slopes are c/N separated, the number of lines is N^(3-o(1)), and the mean fraction of marked time bins is N^-o(1). Let E_N be the union of N^-1 cells at marked centerline points. There cannot be a fixed epsilon>0 with #E_N<=N^(4-epsilon) along the sequence.

To see exactly what is being extracted, perturb each of the three intercept and three slope coordinates independently at scale c'/N and select incidences in a fixed enlargement of E_N. A marked bin supplies an interval of length comparable to N^-1; its perturbations stay inside the enlargement. Thus active mass is N^-o(1). Finite indexed copies and the union of finitely many cells are tempered, with fixed polynomial complexity. Slopes have density N^o(1), and conditional y-intercept density is O(N^2).

At terminal hidden width N^-1 the observation support has measure at most C #E_N N^-3 <= C N^(1-epsilon). Homogenization therefore gives n(1,0)>=N^(-1+epsilon-o(1)), hence g(1,0)>=epsilon. The same projected full-time plank bound as a01:projected-full-plank-cap gives g(0,0)<=0; long marks follow from the inverse-subpower duration pruning. The third profile bound remains g(r,u)<=r+2-2u. These are precisely the three inequalities used in a01:initial-density-inequalities.

The existing endpoint maximization supplies d<1 and angular parameters; cropping preserves the true-floor identity; the original semialgebraic slope-volume argument gives positive horizon. The source's exclusion of bad version-2 problems contradicts it. No new critical-path or unbounded-narrowness argument was needed.

Equivalently, for each fixed epsilon one obtains some positive tolerance eta and sufficiently large N for near-full counts and durations; failure of every eta supplies the preceding sequence. This equivalence does NOT give an explicit eta(epsilon), a sharp density power, or a maximal-function estimate. It is the familiar compactness/contradiction extraction from the actual proof. No finite-scale theorem is frozen here, and no measure or endpoint consequence is submitted as a finding.

Keleti–Mathe's actual segment-transfer proof is not itself a replacement for this reasoning: it uses lines whose intersections contain segments. Their v3 introduction explicitly distinguishes positive-length subsets of lines from full segments. Reading that limit prevents attributing the stronger comparison to the headline set theorem alone. The operative pinned proof, rather than this general equivalence, supplies the near-full marks above.

## 5. Normalization repairs tested

Renormalizing the active law by p gives a probability, but its density relative to nu dt is 1_E/p, not bounded by a subpower. Reweighting the trajectory prior by ell(l)/p changes the conditional time density to 1_E/ell(l). For homogeneous ell~p this retains the same N^kappa cost. In particular it does not restore independent uniform time.

Replicating each trajectory/index does not change the success fraction when its probability prior is normalized. Assigning the replicated worlds their correct probability weights cancels the apparent multiplication. The source's cancellation of a spatial Jacobian/prior ratio does not cancel this time-density cost.

Local time zoom can create dense marks, but consumes time length. In an s-regular diagnostic with finest scale delta and q=delta^theta, the local filled fraction behaves as (delta/q)^(1-s)=delta^[(1-s)(1-theta)]. Making this subpower by theta->1 spends essentially all depth in time; it is not a zero-cost recovery of the positive-horizon improvement. No lower regularity condition is imposed on arbitrary Frostman fibres on the strength of this diagnostic.

Even granting the source's same nested true-layer and angular-cap inputs, the two-label failure bound must now be o(p^2), which requires

 Su/2-(d+2k)s2 > 2kappa

up to the additional losses in those assumed inputs. This inequality can hold for sufficiently small kappa: it is not a categorical obstruction. It explains why the near-full case is paid. Its feasibility alone does not pay the subsequent witness, graph, matching, and original-coverage requirements.

## 6. A simultaneous cap/support obligation survives these repairs

The scalar projection theorem requires, at the same terminal width a and for the same fixed-budget law, both a density ceiling K c a^d and a support ceiling K c^-1 a^-d per projected base cell. The parameter c permits arbitrary absolute density shifts, but cancels in their product.

Suppose a proposed sparse extension only proves cap <=N^alpha c a^d and support <=N^beta c^-1 a^-d, with alpha,beta>=0 fixed. Changing c to c' and d to d' cannot make both bounds satisfy the existing theorem: at the terminal width their product allowance is N^(alpha+beta), independent of c' and d'. This is an algebraic incompatibility of those bounds, not a counterexample to an improved geometric estimate.

The actual source support count identifies a concrete way beta can arise. Replacing the Q1 witness floor q1 nu(Q1)/K by q1 nu(Q1) N^-beta changes its whole-domain count to

 L1 <= N^(beta+o(1))/n(r1),

unless a compensating upper-cap or geometric improvement is supplied. Renormalizing the sparse law may simultaneously increase the projected cap. Neither an arbitrary shift in absolute f(0), nor componentwise estimates from different restrictions, resolves this product loss. The earlier witnesses must be retained with their own law/cap pairing; an incidence posterior is not their prior.

A successful additional operation would have to recover compatible scalar cap and support estimates AND actual original-law coverage for fixed-power temporal sampling, while preserving all relevant critical-path alternatives. The K3 projection calculation pays a real part of the cost; the relative-density cancellation and greedy recovery pay other parts. This round does not close the remaining simultaneous operation. No conditional bridge is being nominated, and there is no assertion that no stronger primary method could do it.
