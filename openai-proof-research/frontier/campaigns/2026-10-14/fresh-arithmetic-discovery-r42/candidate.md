# T172R42: two projectively algebraic blocks and an exact anchored circle criterion

Status: complete proposed deduction; author disposition TECHNICAL ONLY after reading the decisive I17 review. No substantial nomination. Independent proof validation of this extension has not occurred. It is conditional on the stipulated pinned classification. No count or worldwide-priority claim is made. Full scope is frozen separately in `frozen-scope.md` (SHA256 fb60279345c90bfc1cdeab3783106c9d6fb5dbc21011e6d7945e51e6e508fbc8).

For a finite T⊂R put p(t)=((1−t²)/(1+t²),2t/(1+t²)). Say T is projectively algebraic when |T|≤3, or, on choosing distinct a,b,c∈T, all values

    C_(a,b,c)(t)=(t−a)(b−c)/((t−c)(b−a)), t∉{a,b,c},

are algebraic over Q. This is independent of the chosen triple: a change of the normalized triple is a fractional-linear map with algebraic coefficients, so all four-point cross-ratios are algebraic. We use only finite real parameters. The poles c and their normalized value ∞ are always handled by the homogeneous numerator equation, never divided by zero.

**Theorem.** (i) If T1,T2 are finite projectively algebraic sets, p(T1∪T2) is Ramsey. (ii) If finite T contains at least five algebraic parameters, p(T) is Ramsey if and only if its nonalgebraic subset is projectively algebraic.

Here Ramsey has the exact source quantifiers: for every integer r≥2 there exists integer D≥1 such that every function R^D→{1,...,r} admits a monochromatic congruent copy at the original scale. Sets, fields and dimensions are otherwise unrestricted. The empty configuration is vacuously monochromatic; one- and two-point cases follow by containment in a triple, as below.

## 1. Local tensor arithmetic without a domain assumption

Let F=Q(T) (or the field generated by all parameters in the construction), B=F⊗_Q F, m:B→F multiplication, and R=B_(ker m). Write z_L=z⊗1 and z_R=1⊗z. The map m extends to R, whose elements with nonzero m-value are units. Finite generation of F is available but is not needed for the following arguments. In particular, we do not assume B or R is a domain.

**Lemma 1.** For z∈F, z_L=z_R in R if and only if z is algebraic over Q. For transcendental z, z_L−z_R is a nonzerodivisor in R.

If z is algebraic, let f∈Q[X] be its separable minimal polynomial. The identity

    0=f(z_L)−f(z_R)=(z_L−z_R) h(z_L,z_R)

has m(h)=f′(z)≠0. Thus h is a unit in R and z_L=z_R. This explicitly removes nonidentity algebraic conjugate components at the diagonal.

If z is transcendental, put K=Q(z). Choose a K-vector-space basis of F in each tensor factor. Distributing the two direct sums gives a free module decomposition of B over K⊗_Q K, with basis the paired basis vectors. The inclusion K⊗K→F⊗F is injective (tensoring vector-space injections over Q is exact). Now K⊗Q K is the localization of Q[X,Y] obtained by inverting nonzero univariate rational polynomials in X and Y. It is a domain and z_L−z_R is its nonzero element X−Y. Hence multiplication by z_L−z_R is injective on the free module B. Localization preserves this injectivity, proving the nonzerodivisor assertion in R. Moreover R is nonzero because m extends to F and sends 1 to 1. Thus the difference cannot be zero in R. This proves both assertions, and the same argument applies to any cross-ratio z∈F.

An alternative check on the converse is the homomorphism B→F[ε]/(ε²), x⊗y↦x(y+εD(y)), for a Q-derivation D of F. All denominators inverted in R map to units. If z is transcendental, extend z to a transcendence basis and extend ∂/∂z uniquely across the separable algebraic extension; then D(z)=1. The difference cannot vanish. This alternative is not required by the proof.

**Lemma 2 (clear and annihilate before descent).** Suppose a polynomial Q∈R[x,y] of bidegree at most (2,2) satisfies Q(t_L,t_R)=0 for every t∈T and m(Q)=(y−x)². Then such a polynomial exists over B with the same multiplied polynomial.

Choose a common denominator s∈B\ker m of its nine coefficients and write Q=H/s with H∈B[x,y]. Each H(t_L,t_R) vanishes after localization, so there is u_t∈B\ker m with u_t H(t_L,t_R)=0 in B. This extra annihilator step is essential: merely clearing coefficient denominators would not justify identities in a ring with zero divisors. Set u=∏u_t and δ=us. Then E=uH has every required evaluation zero in B and m(E)=m(δ)(y−x)². Multiply E by (1/m(δ))_L∈B. This gives the claimed polynomial. The empty product convention covers no evaluation equations.

## 2. The circle interface

For |T|≥3, its circle points affinely span R². The pinned family172 `thm:classification` and `alg:equivalence` give the following exact equivalent condition:

    Q∈B[x,y], deg_x Q,deg_y Q≤2,
    Q(t_L,t_R)=0 for all t∈T, and m(Q)=(y−x)².       (1)

Here is the full interface. Put q(t)=(1,t,t²)^T,

    L=[[1,0,1],[1,0,−1],[0,2,0]],  S=diag(−1,1,1),
    J0=[[0,0,1],[0,−2,0],[1,0,0]].

Then (1,p(t))^T=Lq(t)/(1+t²), q(x)^T J0 q(y)=(y−x)², and L^T S L=−2J0. From a source matrix P, first apply flip-transpose averaging as proved in `alg:equivalence`. Its multiplied matrix H is symmetric with spatial block I2. The multiplied evaluation identities give H00+2H01 p(t)_1+2H02 p(t)_2+1=0. Three distinct circle points are noncollinear, so H=S. Set K=−L^T P L/2 and Q=q(x)^T K q(y). The separate factors 1+t_L² and 1+t_R² are units, giving all evaluations and m(Q)=J. Conversely the coefficient matrix K of Q gives P=−2L^(−T) K L^(−1), which has multiplied matrix S and the source evaluation identities. Thus it satisfies every spatial-block condition exactly. The coordinate field is F=Q(T), since t=p(t)_2/(1+p(t)_1). Lemma 2 shows that using R in place of B in (1) is equivalent, not a completion or approximate relaxation.

## 3. An exact bilinear correspondence for a projectively algebraic block

Let U⊂F be finite and projectively algebraic. There exists g_U∈R[x,y] of bidegree at most (1,1) such that

    g_U(t_L,t_R)=0 (t∈U),     m(g_U)=y−x.           (2)

For U empty use y−x. If 1≤|U|≤2, pad U with distinct elements of F to make three parameters; this is possible since F contains Q. Thus assume distinct a,b,c∈U, and form

 G=(y−a_R)(b_R−c_R)(x−c_L)(b_L−a_L)
       −(x−a_L)(b_L−c_L)(y−c_R)(b_R−a_R).

Direct expansion gives m(G)=κ(y−x), where κ=(b−c)(b−a)(a−c)≠0. Define g_U=G/κ_L. At a and c both appropriate homogeneous terms vanish; at b the two products agree. At any other t the denominators in the defining cross-ratio are nonzero in F and hence separate left/right units. Its algebraicity and Lemma 1 give C(t)_L=C(t)_R. Clearing those unit denominators gives exactly G(t_L,t_R)=0. No assumption that algebraic coefficients are rational, or that their conjugates coincide globally in B, is used.

For U of size≤3, (2) is simply three-point fractional-linear interpolation. For larger U, it is simultaneous interpolation controlled by all projective moduli, rather than a cardinality substitution into the source full-row-rank criterion.

## 4. Proof of the two-block construction

Take F=Q(T1∪T2) and the polynomials g_1,g_2 from (2). Their product Q=g_1 g_2 has bidegree≤(2,2), multiplied polynomial (y−x)², and zero evaluation at every member of T1∪T2, including overlaps. Apply Lemma 2 and the exact circle interface. If the union has at most two elements, enlarge one block of size≤2 to a triple (using additional real parameters) and use containment in the resulting Ramsey set. The empty case is vacuous. This proves (i) with no bound on either block size.

## 5. Proof of the anchored converse

Let T contain five distinct algebraic numbers a1,...,a5 and suppose p(T) is Ramsey. Obtain Q over R from (1). Lemma 1 identifies (aj)_L=(aj)_R, and hence Q((aj)_L,(aj)_L)=0. The polynomial Q(z,z)∈R[z] has degree≤4. The five evaluation points have pairwise differences that are units: each difference is the left copy of a nonzero element of F. Its Vandermonde matrix is therefore invertible over R, so Q(z,z)=0 as a polynomial.

Polynomial division by the monic y−x now gives Q=(y−x)g. The bounds on both degrees force deg_x g,deg_y g≤1: the degree in y follows by monic division, and the leading x coefficient of the product is minus the leading x coefficient of g, so no cancellation of a putative higher x degree is possible. Applying m and canceling y−x in F[x,y] gives m(g)=y−x.

For every nonalgebraic t∈T, Lemma 1 makes t_R−t_L a nonzerodivisor in R. From

    0=Q(t_L,t_R)=(t_R−t_L)g(t_L,t_R)

we obtain g(t_L,t_R)=0. Write g=Axy+Bx+Cy+D. Its multiplied coefficients are m(A)=m(D)=0, m(B)=−1, m(C)=1. Thus the matrix

    M=[[-B,−D],[A,C]]

has determinant −BC+AD with m-value 1 and is invertible over R. At every t∈T the denominator A t_L+C has m-value 1 and is a unit. The equation g(t_L,t_R)=0 says

    t_R=(-B t_L−D)/(A t_L+C).

For four distinct nonalgebraic parameters, this common fractional-linear transformation preserves their cross-ratio. This is an identity over the present ring: subtract the displayed images to get det(M)(u_L−v_L)/((Au_L+C)(Av_L+C)), and cancel the resulting unit factors in the cross-ratio. All pairwise parameter differences and denominators are units. Consequently each such cross-ratio z obeys z_L=z_R in R and is algebraic by Lemma 1. If there are at most three nonalgebraic points the condition is vacuous. Thus the nonalgebraic subset is projectively algebraic.

Conversely, the algebraic subset of T is projectively algebraic because every rational expression in algebraic numbers is algebraic. If the remaining subset is projectively algebraic, part (i) applies. This proves (ii) without weakening the frozen scope.

## 6. Scope, examples, and separation from I17

Every finite algebraic set of circle parameters can be combined with any three real parameters, regardless of their algebraic dependence. More generally, it can be combined with an arbitrarily large real fractional-linear image of an algebraic projective set. Five algebraic anchors make this latter condition necessary as well as sufficient for the entire nonalgebraic remainder.

For an explicit negative/positive boundary, fix a transcendental u>2 and five rational anchors avoiding the following parameters. The four nonalgebraic parameters u,u+1,u+2,u+3 have rational cross-ratios, so the combined set is Ramsey. Replace the last parameter by u². For the first triple a=u,b=u+1,c=u+2, its cross-ratio is −(u²−u)/(u²−u−2), a nonconstant rational function of u and hence transcendental; the anchored configuration is not Ramsey. All parameters are distinct for u>2. A fixed anchor set {−5,−4,−3,−2,−1} works in both examples.

The special case of two triples gives the already-established I17 six-point theorem by the same simpler certificate already supplied in its independent R2 review and explained in `frontier/iterations/i17-proof-integration.md` and `frontier/reviews/i17-six-point-ramsey-novelty-r3.md`.
