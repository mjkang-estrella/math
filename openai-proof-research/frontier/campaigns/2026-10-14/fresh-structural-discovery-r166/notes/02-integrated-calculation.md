# Prefreeze calculation: ideal-small holes and all mixed minor choices

These are durable feasibility/comparison calculations, not a frozen author-proof nominee. The exact source pair and its full construction remain fixed. The calculation is paid by the stipulated source operations plus the primary minor/duality proofs read in notes01. No correctness/contribution/acceptance/count is assigned.

## 1. Keep the actual source data

Use Source185 sections03–06 at the stipulated pin. D is the countable block set, J the zero block-density ideal, K the larger proper ideal generated by J and the complements of the source A-columns, U the ultrafilter disjoint from K, and rho its ordinal rank. Define on E=Z_integer times D the ideal

    H = { Z subset E : Z_n={d:(n,d) in Z} belongs to K for every integer n }.

H is an ideal under finite unions, not asserted a sigma ideal. No common finite witness for membership in K is required across the bundles. Every bundle retains a U-large set after deleting a member of H. In particular its survivor is infinite, and E minus Z is countably infinite.

Q is the actual labelled self-dual uniform matroid on L dot-union R; all bases satisfy the source admissibility rule. M0 and M1 are the two actual parity direct sums, with both central vertices viewing e_(-1) as L. No new choice of ultrafilter, prototype, or local basis family is made. For a basis B of Q the operative input is

    B_L in U  =>  y=D minus B_R in U and rho(B_L)>rho(y).

If x subset_K B_L and y subset_K x_next are U-large, the already proved source monotonicity gives

    rho(x) >= rho(B_L) > rho(y) >= rho(x_next).             (1)

The same inequalities hold for arbitrary K-errors at every vertex, not only a uniformly bounded total number of errors. This is precisely the finite-error reasoning of the original r21 checkpoint with the full stated source ideal substituted.

## 2. Pay all holes and local errors at once

Fix ANY Z in H. Suppose two sets on F=E minus Z cover F and, at every vertex, the assignment to that vertex becomes Q-independent after deleting a set whose two slices are in K. Shrink to an assignment partition of F; this preserves the local property. On the central bundle the two parts partition D minus Z_(-1). Since Z_(-1) is U-small, exactly one assigned part is U-large. Start at its endpoint and travel outwards.

Let x_k and z_k be the inward and outward assigned label sets at vertex k along that ray. Extend the corrected independent assignment to an actual Q-basis B_k. Then x_k subset_K (B_k)_L and z_k subset_K (B_k)_R. Thus largeness of x_k implies largeness of (B_k)_L. Put y_k=D minus (B_k)_R. The source gives largeness of y_k and its strict rank inequality.

On the next bundle, with hole set Z_n, the next inward part is exactly

    x_(k+1) = (D minus Z_n) minus z_k.

The difference y_k minus x_(k+1) lies in Z_n union (z_k minus (B_k)_R), a member of K. Consequently x_(k+1) is large and (1) applies. An infinite strictly decreasing sequence of ordinals follows, a contradiction.

Specializing the correction sets to empty proves: for every independent I_i of M_i, the uncovered set E minus (I0 union I1) does not belong to H. Thus some bundle has K-positive uncovered labels. This is an exact ideal statement, not a positive-density constant, uniform cardinality budget, effective bound, or finite-rank approximation.

## 3. Independent contraction/deletion splits in both colors

For the SAME arbitrary Z in H, choose ANY partitions

    Z = C_i dot-union D_i,  i=0,1,
    N_i = (M_i / C_i) delete D_i,

on their common surviving ground F. The partitions for the two colors may differ completely. There is no self-duality claim for N_i.

Contraction-independent sets are independent in the original matroid: extend by a basis of the contracted restriction and then use heredity (BDKPW Lemma3.5/Corollary3.6; BC §2). Deletion does not change independence of surviving sets. Therefore an independent covering of F by the N_i would be an independent covering of E minus Z by the M_i, which section2 excludes.

That alone does not exclude mixed packing/covering. Here is the full additional compatibility calculation.

Assume a partition F=P dot-union W with disjoint S0,S1 subset P spanning N_i restricted to P, and I_i subset W independent in N_i.W with I0 union I1=W. Set T_i=W minus I_i. Duality makes T_i spanning in N_i* restricted to W; T0,T1 are disjoint. Original self-duality, and classical commuting minor identities, give

    N_i* = (M_i / D_i) delete C_i.                         (2)

The disjoint deletion/contraction commutation in (2) follows directly from the primary basis characterization: choose a basis of the contracted set, which is unchanged when a disjoint set is deleted, and test independence of its union with a surviving set. The two orders give the identical test. The same argument identifies a restriction of M_i/C_i to P with contraction of C_i in M_i restricted to C_i union P.

Spanning in a restriction of a contraction implies

    P subset cl_(M_i)(S_i union C_i),
    W subset cl_(M_i)(T_i union D_i).                      (3)

For completeness, the first inclusion can be obtained without assuming finite character: choose a basis B_C of M_i restricted to C_i. Apply the BC spanning-gluing lemma in M_i restricted to C_i union P. Since S_i spans its contraction by C_i, B_C union S_i spans C_i union P. Restriction closure and B_C subset C_i imply the first inclusion; (2) gives the second. Classical commuting restrictions/contractions here follow from the same basis characterization, not an infinite-rank subtraction.

Now A_i=S_i union T_i union Z spans the full original M_i, by (3) and inclusion of Z. Moreover A0 intersect A1 is contained in Z: S sets are disjoint on P, T sets are disjoint on W, and P,W are disjoint. The complements

    J_i=E minus A_i

are independent in M_i*=M_i. Their union contains E minus Z, contradicting section2. This pays every mixed partition, including P or W empty, for every pair of independent deletion/contraction choices. The shared added Z is allowed to occur in both spanning sets because its complement only has to cover E minus Z.

For intersection of (N0,N1), apply the full BC Proposition3.6 equivalence: a witness would give a mixed packing/covering partition of (N0,N1*). But N1* is another allowed split of the same Z by (2). Hence the already paid mixed-partition contradiction excludes intersection too. There is no illicit substitution N1=N1*.

## 4. Structural hypotheses of these minors

Uniformity is preserved under restriction, contraction and duality (BG Lemma3). For restriction, an independent set that is not maximal inside the restricting ground is not an original basis, so the source any-element extension property permits every still-available element to be added. Thus restriction is uniform. Complementing bases proves uniformity of the dual by balanced swaps; contraction follows by duality. The source component partition remains a partition of F after minor operations; minors commute with direct sums (BC §2). Hence N_i are still countable partitional matroids.

Every K0-small subset of one Q component lies in the Lower zone: it differs from empty by K0. A Lower set contains no admissible basis, since the zone is downward closed. If it were dependent, uniformity would make it contain a basis. It is therefore independent. Adding any finite subset leaves it K0-small and independent.

Consequently C_i is independent in M_i componentwise, and C_i union A is independent for every finite A subset F. The contraction independence characterization then makes every finite A independent in N_i. Apply the same reasoning to the split (2): every finite subset is independent in N_i* too.

The whole survivor in a component is not independent in its minor. Otherwise adding the independent contracted slice would make the complement of the deleted slice Q-independent. That complement is Upper, being K0-equivalent to the whole component. An Upper set cannot lie below an admissible basis, so it is dependent. Thus neither N_i nor N_i* is finitary. No nonempty finitary summand is possible: with all finite sets independent such a summand would be free and would consist of coloops, whereas dual singletons are independent. Duality excludes cofinitary summands as well.

None of this supplies a finitary counterexample, a tame example, an uncountable theorem, a Borel presentation, or new labelled self-duality. All such restrictions remain outside these calculations.

## 5. Test the strongest natural enlargement: all U-small holes

It is tempting to replace 'Z_n in K' by merely 'Z_n not in U'. This loses the final rank inequality in (1). The loss is real, and the exact source Q supplies an explicit covering after such holes.

The set consisting of the entire L-coordinate and nothing on R is Upper: u=y=D, with equal rank. It is dependent and uniformity makes it contain a Q-basis B. Write b=B_L; B_R is empty. Admissibility forces

    b in U,  rho(b)>rho(D).

In particular D minus b is U-small but NOT in K, since K-invariance would otherwise give rho(b)=rho(D). It is even J-positive because J subset K.

Remove no central labels. On every other bundle remove exactly D minus b. Define the assignment of the remaining labels as follows:

* Split the central D into b at v0 and D minus b at v(-1).
* On every noncentral surviving bundle, assign all its labels b to the outward endpoint, i.e. to the endpoint for which that bundle is L.

Every vertex receives no R-labels. Except v(-1), each receives exactly b on L, an actual copy of the basis B. Vertex v(-1) receives D minus b on L. This latter set is Lower, since its L-slice is U-small while y=D is large; it is independent by the same uniformity argument as above. Thus each parity assignment is independent in the ORIGINAL M_i, and they partition the surviving ground. Restricting both M_i to that ground gives a covering (and the all-covering packing/covering partition).

This directly refutes the universal U-small-hole enlargement for the actual source pair. It does not claim that every K-positive hole pattern permits a covering, characterize a maximal deletion ideal, or assign a positive density threshold. The rank is reset at every surviving bundle: subtracting a U-small but K-positive set is precisely the operation for which source rank invariance was unavailable.

For comparison, removing the entire central bundle also permits the simple assignment of every other whole bundle to its inward endpoint (its R-coordinate), because R is Lower and independent. That easier cut does not test U-smallness; the construction above does.

## 6. Descriptive comparison is already paid

BG Corollary24 proves that the basis family of a countable labelled self-dual uniform matroid is not analytic, using the full flutter proof read here. In such a matroid,

    Bases(Q) = {X: X independent in Q and X^c independent in Q}.

Indeed dual independence of X^c says X spans, so independence makes X a basis. If I(Q) were analytic, continuous complementation and finite intersection would make Bases(Q) analytic. Thus I(Q) is not analytic. Fixing bases in all other components gives a continuous map reducing I(Q) to I(M_i), so the actual source independent families are not analytic either. The fixed bases may be noncanonical; continuity of the slice map needs no definable selection.

This is an elementary consequence of the actual primary theorem. It does not require recovering U from final independence and does not prove the absence of a different Borel counterexample pair. No definability conclusion about the nonsymmetric minors N_i is inferred from it. The old r114/r118 full Borel-pair obligation remains unchanged; this observation is not its solution or a weakened nominee.
