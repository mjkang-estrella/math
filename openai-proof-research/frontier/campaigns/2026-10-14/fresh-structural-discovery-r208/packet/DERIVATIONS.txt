2. Strong integrated finite-network construction (prefreeze reduction)

Let V be a finite set, B a nonempty subset (boundary), and c_uv=c_vu>=0 for distinct u,v. Put d_u=sum_v c_uv. Discard isolated interior vertices for the energy calculation, assigning them any boundary value. A connected component meeting no boundary can also be assigned one constant and contributes zero output energy. On each remaining component every interior vertex reaches B. Self-loop conductances may be omitted since they change no energy.

Given boundary data x_b in real l1, apply Source332's finite cuts to these boundary vectors. Write x_b=T(z_b), with z_b binary in Q. Let h_b=z_b and h_u=E_u z_(X_tau) on the interior, where P_uv=c_uv/d_u and tau is first hitting time of B. Finite absorption ensures tau<infinity a.s.; h_u=sum_v P_uv h_v on the interior. Run an excursion starting at boundary b, make one P step, then stop on first return to B, including immediate boundary-to-boundary steps. Write sigma>=1 for that return time and K_ba=P_b(X_sigma=a).   E_b sum_(k=0)^(sigma-1) ||Phi(h_(X_(k+1)))-Phi(h_(X_k))||_(1,w)^2
    <=108 E_b ||z_(X_sigma)-z_b||_(2,w)^4.                  (D1)
Proof: for the first jump use the flat-map inequality at u=e. The derivative and initial potential vanish, so its conditional cost is <=108 E_b F_e(h_(X_1)). Thereafter h_(X_(k wedge sigma)) is a bounded stopped martingale. Apply the source martingale estimate starting at time 1; its initial potential subtracts precisely the previous upper bound. For a random first interior state use conditional expectation. All h lie in finite-dimensional Q, so bounded convergence applies to potentials and monotone convergence to energies. If the first jump lands in B there are no subsequent terms. This proves (D1), including that case.

Start these excursions with the UNNORMALIZED boundary weights d_b. Their expected departure count at every interior u is d_u. To see this without an appeal to recurrence, put U=V\B and P_UU=Q0. Absorption implies (I-Q0)^(-1)=sum_(k>=0) Q0^k. The row vector of interior occupation counts is d_B P_BU (I-Q0)^(-1). Stationarity of d for P gives d_U=d_B P_BU+d_U Q0, hence this vector equals d_U. Boundary departure count is d_b, since a boundary visit terminates an excursion except at its start. Therefore the aggregate expected number of traversals of every directed edge (u,v) is d_u P_uv=c_uv. Multiplying (D1) by d_b and summing yields
  sum_(u,v) c_uv ||y_u-y_v||_1^2

Proof of identity: the trace Schur complement has off-diagonal entries -d_b K_ba and row sum zero; eliminating the invertible interior Laplacian gives the first equality. This is stronger than the crude three-sample telescoping constant 3.

4. Finite selection reduces to a linear operator, with exact norm

All function spaces in this section are anchored: Lip_0(S;Y) consists of f(s0)=0 and is normed by Lip(f). S and X are finite metric spaces, s0 in S subset X. For finite-dimensional Y both spaces are finite-dimensional normed vector spaces. Let R:Lip_0(X;Y)->Lip_0(S;Y) be restriction.

Suppose sigma is a C-Lipschitz right inverse of R, with no assumption of linearity or homogeneity. Identify the finite-dimensional input space E with R^m, choose a smooth compactly supported probability density rho of mean zero, and convolve sigma. The convolution sigma_rho is smooth, remains C-Lipschitz in the ORIGINAL E and output norms (average the difference inequality), and satisfies
  R sigma_rho(v)=int (v-z)rho(z)dz=v.
Differentiating at any v gives T=D sigma_rho(v), with R T=I and ||T||<=C. Conversely, a bounded linear right inverse is a Lipschitz selector with the same constant. Thus the infimum Lipschitz selection constant equals the infimum linear extension-operator norm for each finite S,X,Y. This proof avoids differentiability assumptions on sigma itself and uses no infinite-dimensional differentiation theorem. On an open input ball the same argument applies with sufficiently small support, locally; a global Lipschitz bound on the ball still yields a full linear right inverse by differentiation at an interior point.

For Y=l1^d, average T over the finite group of signed coordinate permutations, acting on both function spaces. Each conjugate has norm <=C and remains a right inverse. Sign averaging eliminates every off-diagonal coordinate block; permutation averaging makes the diagonal blocks equal. The resulting operator is
  (A f_1,...,A f_d),
where A:Lip_0(S;R)->Lip_0(X;R) is scalar linear and the amplification still has norm <=C in the vector Lipschitz norms. Since S is finite, (A g)(x)=sum_(s in S\{s0}) a_s(x)g(s). Therefore this operator preserves EVERY linear subspace Z of l1^d: its value on Z-valued data is a scalar linear combination of those data. It supplies extensions into Z with loss <=C.

Consequently the finite selection constant for l1^d is at least e(X,S;Z) for every Z subset l1^d. This is a complete obstruction, not a failure of the naive coordinatewise extension formula. Source332 supplies cheap extensions into the full ambient l1^d; it does not imply cheap extensions into arbitrary linear subspaces or a simultaneous Lipschitz choice.

6. One fixed uniformly discrete Hilbert subset: complete assembly

The previous obstruction can be assembled in one separable real Hilbert space without a bidual limit or loss of exact agreement. Take pairwise orthogonal finite-dimensional spaces H_n=R^n and orthogonal unit vectors e_n perpendicular to all H_n. Let r_n=n^(1/4) and
  H=(direct-sum_n (R e_n direct-sum_2 H_n))_2,
  S={0} union union_n {10r_n e_n+r_n a : a in A_n}.
Within a cluster the separation exceeds 1. Between distinct clusters the squared distance is 101(r_n^2+r_m^2); the distance of a cluster point from 0 is sqrt(101)r_n. Hence S is countable, uniformly discrete, closed, and unbounded.

Fix n and its anchor a0. An h in Lip_0(A_n;l1^D) defines J_n^S h on S by the value r_n h(a) on the n-th cluster, and 0 elsewhere, including 0. This assignment is linear. Since ||h(a)||<=2 Lip(h), the cross-cluster and origin inequalities have ratios <=2/10 times Lip(h); on the n-th cluster the Lipschitz ratio is exactly that of h. Thus J_n^S is an isometry of the anchored Lipschitz norm into Lip_0(S;l1), using the first D coordinates.

Suppose a C-Lipschitz right inverse sigma:Lip_0(S;l1)->Lip_0(H;l1) of restriction existed. Compose with J_n^S, evaluate sigma on {10r_n e_n+r_n x:x in X_n}, project onto the first D coordinates, and divide output by r_n. This gives a C-Lipschitz selector Lip_0(A_n;l1^D)->Lip_0(X_n;l1^D). It is anchored at a0 because sigma agrees exactly with the inserted data there. Rescaling the domain and output cancels in the Lipschitz norms. Section 5 forces C>=c' n^(1/4) for all n, a contradiction.

In fact no COARSELY LIPSCHITZ right inverse exists, meaning no selector with
  ||sigma(f)-sigma(g)||_Lip <=a||f-g||_Lip+b
for fixed finite a,b and all f,g. For each finite input/output problem above form tau_k(h)=[tau(kh)-tau(0)]/k. Restriction of tau(0) is zero, so tau_k remains an exact right inverse, and its difference bound is a||h-h'||+b/k. Its values for each fixed h lie in bounded subsets of the finite-dimensional output space. A product-compactness subnet as k->infinity gives an a-Lipschitz right inverse. Sections 4-5 contradict this for n large. No compactness of an l1 norm ball is claimed: projection to FINITE coordinates and FINITE domain precedes this limit.

A globally uniformly continuous sigma would be coarsely Lipschitz: choose delta>0 such that input distance <=delta implies output distance <=1. Subdivide each segment from f to g into ceil(||f-g||/delta) pieces. The resulting bound is ||f-g||/delta+1. Therefore no globally uniformly continuous right inverse exists either. This does NOT exclude a merely continuous choice or a uniformly continuous choice on a bounded ball; those are different assertions.

All maps on this S individually extend with the universal bound from Source332 (and section 2's compatible computation). No bounded linear extension operator exists, since such an operator is Lipschitz. This assembly is retained as a durable derivation, not a frozen nominee or a claim of worldwide novelty.
