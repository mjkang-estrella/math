## 1. Full statement and premises

**Theorem.** Let P⊂M be a unital irreducible inclusion of complex II1 factors and suppose M has separable predual. For every nonscalar u∈U(M),

    {v∈U(M): W*(P,vuv*)=M}

is a dense G_delta subset of U(M) in the operator norm. In particular, for every v0 and every ε>0 such a v exists with ||v-v0||<ε. The added element is exactly conjugate to u. There is no spectral diffuseness or index assumption. For every projection 0<p<1 the same conclusion holds with p in place of u, by applying the theorem to 2p−1.

Normalize the faithful trace by τ(1)=1. We use the pinned source *Relative generation and the generator problem for finite factors*, specifically `lem:free-haar-extension`, `lem:haar-absorption`, and the independent-word setup in `prop:perturbation`. Its extension lemma states that every norm-separable unital C*-subalgebra D of M^ω admits a Haar unitary t∈P^ω free from D. Its hypotheses are precisely the irreducible II1 inclusion above, and the subalgebras used below are generated by finitely many elements. Successive applications are therefore legitimate. We also use its proof of the classical free-group length-one bound

    ||Σ b_j λ(a_j)|| ≤ 2(Σ |b_j|²)^(1/2),                 (1)

where the a_j range over distinct free basis letters and their inverses. The source's Baire strategy is credited; we give its argument with the present parameter space in §6. No other new theorem of the corpus is required.

The source perturbs by left multiplication. Here both left and right factors are mandatory because the conjugacy orbit must be preserved. This produces an additional first-order cancellation and new words containing the arbitrary initial u. Sections 2–5 account for both changes explicitly.

## 2. A degree-majorization lemma

Let D be a unital tracial algebra free from a family of free Haar unitaries representing a free group F. Words formed from unitary elements of D and free-group letters are assigned a degree in F by replacing every D-letter by the identity and multiplying the remaining letters in their original order.

**Lemma 2.1.** Two such unitary words of distinct degrees are orthogonal in L². Consequently, for a finite sum T=Σ_i c_i w_i, if nonnegative numbers a_g satisfy

    Σ_{i:deg(w_i)=g} |c_i| ≤ a_g,

then ||T||₂≤(Σ_g a_g²)^(1/2).

**Proof.** A word whose degree is not the identity has zero trace. To verify this without assuming the D-letters are Haar, expand each D-factor into its scalar and centered parts, combine adjacent D-factors and adjacent group factors, and repeat. This finite reduction expresses the word as a sum of reduced alternating products of centered D-elements and nonidentity group words, together with possible pure group or pure D terms. Every term retains the original formal degree because scalar replacement of D-factors and multiplication within either algebra preserve degree. Reduced alternating products have zero trace by freeness. A pure group term of nonidentity degree has zero trace by the Haar group trace. A pure D term has identity degree and hence cannot occur in this reduction when the original degree is nonidentity. Thus τ(w)=0 in that case. Applying this to w_i* w_j proves orthogonality of different degrees.

Within a fixed degree, the triangle inequality gives norm at most Σ|c_i| because each original w_i is unitary and has L² norm one. Pythagoras across different degrees proves the bound. This does not say that words of the same degree are equal or orthogonal. In particular it does not replace u by 1 as an algebraic identity. ∎

Products of finite sums admit a positive scalar envelope: replace each coefficient by its absolute value and each word by its degree in F. Multiplication of the resulting nonnegative group-ring coefficients dominates the envelope of the product, since all contributions to any reduced group word are added before estimating. Lemma 2.1 then bounds the L² norm by the coefficient ℓ² norm of any such dominating envelope. This is the only deletion of D-coefficients used below.

## 3. Exact conjugation response

Fix u,z∈U(M), ξ∈M, an integer ℓ≥1 and 0<α<1/4. Set

    β = 1−|τ(u)|².

In M^ω, start with D0=C*(1,u,z,ξ) and choose successively t_j∈U(P^ω), Haar and free from D_j, where D_{j+1}=C*(D_j,t_j), for 0≤j≤ℓ. Put

    s1=t0,  s_{j+1}=u t_j  (1≤j≤ℓ),
    g_k=s1⋯s_k  (1≤k≤ℓ),
    W=t0∏_{k=1}^ℓ(u t_k),  y=zW*.

The source absorption lemma shows that s1,…,sℓ are free Haar unitaries free from D0, and that W, then y, is Haar and free from D_ℓ. The prefix family g1,…,gℓ is itself a free Haar family free from D0: the transformation s_k↦g_k is an automorphism of the free group, with inverse s1=g1 and s_k=g_{k−1}^{−1}g_k. Thus every nonidentity reduced word in the g_k has zero trace, and freeness from D0 is retained. Moreover D_ℓ=C*(D0,g1,…,gℓ). We may therefore apply Lemma 2.1 with D=D0 and free group generated by g1,…,gℓ,y.

Define

    h=(α/ℓ)Σ_{j=1}^ℓ Im(g_j* y g_j),
    w=e^{ih},  u'=w u w*,
    W'=t0∏_{k=1}^ℓ(u' t_k).

Here h=h*. Let c=α/(2ℓ), H_k=g_k h g_k*, J_k=g_k u h u* g_k*. Moving each inserted exponential through the original prefix, in the original order, gives the exact identity

    W'=[∏_{k=1}^ℓ(e^{iH_k} e^{−iJ_k})] W.              (2)

Indeed e^{iH_k}e^{−iJ_k}=g_k e^{ih}u e^{−ih}u* g_k*, and consecutive original prefixes satisfy g_k* g_{k+1}=u t_k. Cancelling these adjacent prefixes proves (2).

Write p_{kj}=g_k g_j* and q_{kj}=g_k u g_j*. The degree-one term of the product in (2) is

    C1=cΣ_{k,j}(p_{kj} y p_{kj}*−p_{kj} y* p_{kj}*
                      −q_{kj} y q_{kj}*+q_{kj} y* q_{kj}*).

For every p∈U(D_ℓ), freeness of W from D_ℓ gives

    τ(ξ* p y p* W)=τ(ξ* p z) τ(p*),                    (3)
    τ(ξ* p y* p* W)=0.                                 (4)

For (3), use y=zW* and τ(AW*BW)=τ(A)τ(B) for A,B∈D_ℓ, proved by centering B and applying freeness. For (4), the word is ξ*p W z* p* W. Center the internal factor z*p*: its centered term vanishes by free alternating words; its scalar term is a multiple of τ(ξ*p W²)=0. Also τ(ξ*W)=0.

For p_{kj}, its trace vanishes unless k=j, in which case p_{kk}=1. Its total contribution to τ(ξ*C1W) is cℓ τ(ξ*z). For q_{kj},

    τ(q_{kj}*)=0  if k≠j,
    τ(q_{kk}*)=overline{τ(u)},
    τ(ξ* q_{kk} z)=τ(u) τ(ξ*z).

The off-diagonal trace identity follows by cycling g_j* g_k next to u and using freeness of D0 from the free Haar group; g_j* g_k is a nonidentity word. The last identity follows by cycling the outside factors and using freeness of g_k from D0 in τ(zξ* g_k u g_k*). Hence the negative half contributes −cℓ|τ(u)|²τ(ξ*z). We have proved the exact first-order identity

    τ(ξ*(1+C1)W)=(αβ/2) τ(ξ*z).                        (5)

The variance β is strictly positive exactly when u is nonscalar: ||u−τ(u)1||₂²=β, and faithfulness gives the assertion. No lower bound uniform over all nonscalar u is asserted or needed.

## 4. Uniform control of every nonlinear term

Let F0 be the free group generated by g1,…,gℓ,y. Erase only D0-coefficients in the sense of Lemma 2.1. Both p_{kj}yp_{kj}* and q_{kj}yq_{kj}* then have the same degree

    g_k g_j* y g_j g_k*.

For any p in the free group generated by the g's, put Y_p=p y p^{-1}, now as a formal group element. These Y_p form a free family: expanding a reduced word in distinct successive conjugates yields nonzero y powers separated by nonidentity g-group words, and cannot reduce to the identity. In particular the finite family needed below is a free basis for the subgroup it generates. The restriction of the left regular representation of F0 to this subgroup is a direct sum of its left regular representations (decompose into right cosets), so (1) applies without changing its norm.

For fixed k define the nonnegative group-ring element

    A_k=cΣ_j(Y_{g_k g_j*}+Y_{g_k g_j*}^{−1}),
    A=Σ_k A_k.

The coefficient envelopes of both iH_k and −iJ_k are bounded by A_k. The identity label p=1 occurs ℓ times among g_k g_j*: precisely k=j. Every off-diagonal g_k g_j* is a distinct nonidentity word in the free basis g1,…,gℓ. If n_p denotes these multiplicities, then

    Σ_p n_p²=ℓ²+ℓ(ℓ−1)=2ℓ²−ℓ.

Consequently, by (1),

    ||λ(A)||≤2(2c²Σ_p n_p²)^(1/2)
            =2α(1−1/(2ℓ))^(1/2)≤2α.                 (6)

Expand the ordered product of its 2ℓ exponentials in (2) by original degree d, before any algebraic reduction, and denote the degree-d term by C_d. For fixed ℓ the expansion converges absolutely in operator norm: each H_k,J_k has norm at most α, so the sum of norm bounds is at most exp(2ℓα). Thus degree grouping is justified, C_0=1, and C_1 is exactly the term in §3.

For each degree d, replace each of the 2ℓ rows by its nonnegative envelope, in order A_1,A_1,A_2,A_2,…,A_ℓ,A_ℓ. The envelope of C_d is bounded coefficientwise by (2A)^d. To see this, each tuple of nonnegative exponential powers with total d gives one nondecreasing sequence of row positions in the distributive expansion of the dth power of their sum. Its factorial weight is at most one. The other row sequences have nonnegative coefficients and may be discarded. This argument does not commute rows and includes all free reductions and collisions.

Lemma 2.1 and (6) now give

    ||C_d||₂≤||(2A)^d||_{ℓ²(F0)}
             =||λ(2A)^d δ_1||≤(4α)^d.

The operator-norm convergence already established identifies the L² limit of the same partial sums with the product in (2). Therefore

    ||∏_k(e^{iH_k}e^{−iJ_k})−1−C1||₂
        ≤Σ_{d≥2}(4α)^d=16α²/(1−4α).                  (7)

Combining (5), (7) and Cauchy–Schwarz proves

    |τ(ξ*W')−(αβ/2)τ(ξ*z)|
        ≤||ξ||₂ 16α²/(1−4α).                         (8)

Neither orthogonality of different original degrees nor orthogonality of the arbitrary-coefficient words within a fixed group degree has been assumed.

## 5. Operator-norm-small conjugators in M itself

The elements g_j* y g_j are free Haar unitaries. Applying (1) to h gives

    ||h||≤√2 α/√ℓ=:B_ℓ.                             (9)

Here the norm in the actual tracial algebra equals the reduced free-group norm. For completeness, a self-adjoint polynomial in free Haar unitaries has exactly the group trace moments. In a finite algebra with faithful trace its operator norm is lim_{m→∞} τ(h^{2m})^(1/(2m)); the upper bound follows from ||h|| and the lower bound from the positive trace of each nonzero spectral projection 1_{(|h|>||h||−ε)}. The same formula holds in the free-group von Neumann algebra. Equality of moments thus proves the norm equality needed for (9). The coefficient squares in (1) sum to 2ℓ(α/(2ℓ))², yielding (9).

It is not legitimate to infer small norms of arbitrary coordinate representatives from (9) alone. We enforce the bound by clipping. Choose unitary representatives t_{j,n}∈P of the finitely many t_j, as justified in the source by polar decomposition in a finite factor. Form g_{k,n}, W_n, y_n and h_n by the formulas in §3. They represent g_k,W,y,h and satisfy h_n=h_n*, ||h_n||≤α. Define the continuous real clipping function

    f_ℓ(t)=max(−B_ℓ,min(t,B_ℓ)),
    k_n=f_ℓ(h_n),  w_n=exp(i k_n),  u_n'=w_n u w_n*.

Functional calculus in the tracial quotient and (9) give [k_n]=f_ℓ(h)=h. Thus [w_n]=w, [u_n']=u', and the word W_n'=t_{0,n}∏(u_n't_{k,n}) represents W'. On the other hand every coordinate satisfies the genuine operator-norm bounds

    ||k_n||≤B_ℓ,    ||w_n−1||≤B_ℓ.                   (10)

For fixed ℓ and any η>0, choose one coordinate n at which the trace pairing differs from τ(ξ*W') by less than η. This is possible by the definition of the ultralimit. Equations (8) and (10) therefore prove the following constrained perturbation statement in M:

For every ℓ≥1 and η>0, there are w∈U(M), t0,…,tℓ∈U(P), with ||w−1||≤√2α/√ℓ, such that, writing

    V=t0∏_{k=1}^ℓ(w u w* t_k),

one has

    |τ(ξ*V)−(αβ/2)τ(ξ*z)|
        ≤||ξ||₂ 16α²/(1−4α)+η.                      (11)

The coordinate may depend on ℓ, η and all prescribed data. The conclusion asserts no algorithm and no norm-small lifts of the free Haar coefficients themselves.

## 6. Norm Baire argument and full genericity

The unitary group U(M), with operator norm, is a complete metric space: a norm-Cauchy sequence has a norm limit and the identities v*v=vv*=1 pass to it. Norm separability is not needed. The Hilbert space L²(M,τ) is separable because M has separable predual.

For v∈U(M) let N_v=W*(P,vuv*) and e_v be the orthogonal projection onto L²(N_v). For ξ∈L²(M) define f_ξ(v)=||e_v ξ||₂. Finite *-polynomials in vuv* with fixed coefficients in P evaluate continuously in operator norm, hence in L². Their values are L² dense in N_v by Kaplansky density. Therefore

    d_ξ(v)=dist(ξ,L²(N_v))=inf_b ||ξ−b(vuv*)||₂

is upper semicontinuous, and f_ξ(v)²=||ξ||₂²−d_ξ(v)² is lower semicontinuous. Each f_ξ is bounded.

A bounded lower semicontinuous real function on a complete metric space has a dense G_delta set of continuity points. Explicitly, for any ε>0 the union of open sets on which its oscillation is <ε is open and dense: in any nonempty open O choose x with f(x)>sup_O f−ε/3, then use lower semicontinuity to obtain a smaller open neighborhood with f>f(x)−ε/3. Baire applied to ε=1/n proves the claim. Apply this to a countable dense family of L² vectors and intersect. Contractivity gives

    |f_ξ(v)−f_ζ(v)|≤||ξ−ζ||₂

uniformly in v, so at each point v0 of the resulting dense G_delta set every f_ξ is continuous.

Suppose N_{v0} is proper. The unitaries span M, so choose z∈U(M) outside N_{v0}. Set ξ=z−E_{N_{v0}}z and s=||ξ||₂>0. Then ξ∈M, e_{v0}ξ=0, and τ(ξ*z)=s². Put u0=v0uv0*. Its variance β=1−|τ(u0)|² is the same strictly positive number as that of u. Choose once and for all

    0<α<min(1/8, β s/128).

For each ℓ apply (11) to u0,z,ξ with η=1/ℓ. Obtain w_ℓ and put v_ℓ=w_ℓ v0. Then ||v_ℓ−v0||≤√2α/√ℓ→0, and the witness unitary V_ℓ belongs to N_{v_ℓ}. Since

    s 16α²/(1−4α)≤32sα²<αβs²/4,

(11) implies, for all sufficiently large ℓ,

    |τ(ξ*V_ℓ)|≥αβs²/4−1/ℓ≥αβs²/8>0.

But ||V_ℓ||₂=1 and V_ℓ∈L²(N_{v_ℓ}), so this is at most f_ξ(v_ℓ). Continuity at v0 would force f_ξ(v_ℓ)→f_ξ(v0)=0, a contradiction. Hence every point in the dense continuity set gives N_v=M.

Finally, for a countable dense L² family (ξ_j), the entire desired locus is

    ⋂_{j,n≥1}{v∈U(M):d_{ξ_j}(v)<1/n}.

Each set is open in operator norm. All these distances vanish exactly when L²(N_v)=L²(M), equivalently N_v=M by conditional expectation. The locus is therefore G_delta, and it is dense because it contains the dense continuity set. This proves the full frozen theorem. ∎

## 7. Contribution and validation boundary

The source already pays scalar free independence, free Haar absorption, the original coordinated perturbation, Haagerup coefficient majorization and the Baire conversion. Our proposed additional control is the conjugacy constraint: its two signs give the exact β response, and the arbitrary u-coefficients require degree-wise domination rather than the source's coefficient isometry. Clipping makes the shrinking norm of the actual free sum available in M, rather than only a shrinking trace norm. This norm estimate and clipping also apply to the source’s unchanged left perturbation, and therefore the unconstrained norm upgrade is explicitly credited as a routine paid comparator, not an independent substantive innovation. The remaining contribution question is the exact conjugacy constraint with its variance cancellation and arbitrary-coefficient remainder. These are explicitly exposed for contribution review; a proof of a different statement alone is not evidence that they are substantial.

The trace-2-norm version is not a separate claim. A small-corner graph-projection encoding already pays it, and it was rejected before the norm scope freeze. Classical prescribed-projection and prescribed-real-part generation results are compared in `comparison.md`. No worldwide priority, quantitative rate of finding generators, preservation of a prescribed conjugator, or generator-invariance of any entropy is asserted. Finite diagnostics can test word multiplicities and first-order cancellation in finite spectral models; they cannot certify this infinite-factor theorem.
