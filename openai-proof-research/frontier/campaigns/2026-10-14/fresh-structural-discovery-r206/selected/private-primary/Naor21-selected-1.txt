Proposition 6. Suppose that (M, d M ) is a metric space and fix a finite subset Ω ⊆ M. Fix also L ⩾ 1 and a
function α : Ω → [0, ∞). Then, the following statements are equivalent.
    (1) For every Banach space (Y, k · kY ) and every 1-Lipschitz mapping f : Ω → Y there is an L-Lipschitz
        mapping F : M → Y that satisfies kF (ω) − f (ω)kY ⩽ α(ω) for every ω ∈ Ω.
    (2) For every probability measure ν supported on Ω and every x ∈ M there is µx = µx (ν) ∈ W1 (Ω) such
        that kµx −µ y kW1 (Ω) ⩽ Ld M (x, y) for every x, y ∈ M, and kµω −(δω −ν)kW1 (Ω) ⩽ α(ω) for every ω ∈ Ω.

   Observe that if part (2) of Proposition 6 holds for some probability measure ν on Ω, then it also holds
for any other probability measure ν′ on Ω, as seen by considering {µx (ν′ ) = µx (ν) + ν − ν′ }x∈M . If α ≡ 0,
then Proposition 6 is the characterization of classical Lipschitz extension in [Koz05, BB07, Oht09, AP20],
while the almost extension setting of Theorem 1 and Theorem 4 corresponds to the special case of Propo-
sition 6 when α is the constant function which equals a positive multiple of ε, and Ω is the ε-net N .

Remark 7. To elucidate the geometric meaning of part (2) of Proposition 6, consider its variant in which
{µx }x∈M are probability measures on Ω instead of elements of W1 (Ω), and the analogous requirement is

     ∀x, y ∈ M,      kµx − µ y kW1 (Ω) ⩽ Ld M (x, y)   and       ∀ω ∈ Ω,    kµω − δω kW1 (Ω) ⩽ α(ω).      (10)

The ensuing proof of Proposition 6 shows mutatis mutandis that this is equivalent to the same assertion
as part (1) of Proposition 6, with the additional requirement that F takes values in the convex hull of f (Ω).
One can interpret the family of probability measures {µx }x∈M as a “randomized rounding scheme” that
associates to each point in the ambient space M a random element of the subset Ω. The first condition
in (10) is a consistency requirement for this rounding scheme, ensuring that nearby points distribute
themselves over Ω in manners that are close to each other in the sense of transportation cost. The second
condition in (10) is that each point of Ω distributes itself over Ω in a manner that is close (in the sense
of transportation cost) to the delta mass at that point; the case α ≡ 0, which corresponds to Lipschitz
extension (with the target constrained to be the convex hall of Ω), is the more stringent requirement that
each element of ω is “rounded” to itself with probability 1. In actuality, part (2) of Proposition 6 associates
to each point in the ambient space M an element of W1 (Ω), i.e., the difference of two disjointly supported
positive measures on Ω of equal total mass; this makes more use of the vector space structure of Y,
rather than only considering its convex structure, but we still think of the measures {µx }x∈M in part (2)
of Proposition 6 as a form of “stochastic retraction” of M onto Ω. We warn, however, that while in full
generality the behaviors of the extension problem into Y and the extension problem into conv( f (Ω)) ⊆ Y
are demonstrably different [Nao20], at present the relation between the two questions is for the most
part shrouded in mystery; these issues are discussed in greater detail in the forthcoming work [Nao20].
