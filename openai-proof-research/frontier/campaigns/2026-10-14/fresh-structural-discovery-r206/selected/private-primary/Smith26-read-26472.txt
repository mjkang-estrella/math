Lemma 3.8. Let η be the metric from Lemma 3.7, where (M, θ) is an arbitrary separable
metric space and ρ is as above. Then (ω <ω , η) is discrete, and in addition (T, η) is complete
whenever T ∈ WF.
Proof. Fix s ∈ ω <ω . Given t ∈ ω <ω \ {s}, by (3.3) we have
                              η(s, t) ⩾ ρ(s, t) = 2−|s∧t|−1 ⩾ 2−|s|−1 .
Hence η(s, t) ⩾ 2−|s|−1 whenever t ∈ ω <ω \ {s}, meaning that η is discrete.
   Now let T ∈ WF and let (sn ) ⊆ T be η-Cauchy. We find a strictly increasing finite
sequence r0 ≺ r1 ≺ r2 ≺ . . . of elements of ω <ω and infinite subsets L0 ⊇ L1 ⊇ L2 ⊇ . . .
of ω such that rk ≼ sn for all n ∈ Lk . Begin by setting r0 = ∅ and L0 = ω. Given rk
and Lk , either there exists m ∈ ω and an infinite subset Lk+1 ⊆ Lk such that rk⌢ m ≼ sn
for all n ∈ Lk+1 , or not. If the first case holds, set rk+1 = rk⌢ m and continue the process,

else terminate it. Because T ∈ WF, this process must terminate at some finite stage k.
By construction we know that Lk is infinite and rk ≼ sn for all n ∈ Lk but also, given
m ∈ ω, the set Hm := {n ∈ Lk : rk⌢ m ≼ sn } must be finite. If n1 ∈ Hm1 and n2 ∈ Hm2
for distinct m1 , m2 ∈ ω, then sn1 ⊥ sn2 and sn1 ∧ sn2 = rk , giving
                            η(sn1 , sn2 ) ⩾ ρ(sn1 , sn2 ) = 2−|rk |−1 .
Because (sn ) is η-Cauchy, it follows that Hm is empty for all but finitely many m ∈ ω,
and thus sn = rk for infinitely many n ∈ Lk . Again because (sn ) is η-Cauchy, we conclude
that it must converge to rk . This proves that (T, η) is complete.                      □
