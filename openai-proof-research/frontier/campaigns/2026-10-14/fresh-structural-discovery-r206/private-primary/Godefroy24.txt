Abstract
The Lipschitz-free space F(M) has an F.D.D. when M is a separable L1 -Banach space,
or when M ⊂ Rn is a somewhat regular subset. The interplay between the existence of
extension operators for Lipschitz maps and the (π )-property in Lipschitz-free spaces
is investigated. If M is an arbitrary metric space, then F(M) has the (π )-property up
to a universal logarithmic factor. It follows in particular that the (π )-property up to
a logarithmic factor fails to imply the approximation property. A list of commented
open problems is provided.

Keywords Lipschitz-free spaces · Absolute extendability · Finite-dimensional
decompositions · Bounded approximation properties

1 Introduction

Non-linear concepts and problems constitute a well-established field of research in
functional analysis. The interplay between metric spaces and Banach spaces has been
particularly fruitful. For instance, the Ribe program aims at transferring ideas from the
structured field of Banach spaces to the larger class of metric spaces, and at applying
our knowledge of Banach spaces to metric spaces (see [41]). On the other hand, given
a metric space M, the Lipschitz-free space F(M) over M is a Banach space which
enjoys useful linearization properties, since Lipschitz maps between metric spaces
canonically extend to linear maps between the corresponding free spaces. Hence the

Lipschitz-free arrow provides a canonical way of mapping the quite flexible world of
metric spaces to the realm of Banach spaces, and thus to construct potentially new
Banach spaces without undue pain. Let us observe, however, that the Lipschitz-free
spaces are easier to define than to analyse. This should be expected since the complexity
is transferred from the maps, which are linearized, to the spaces themselves. Although
research on Lipschitz-free spaces has been very active in the last 20 years, a number
of natural questions remain unanswered. The present article fits into this frame.
    The Lipschitz-free space F(M) over a pointed metric space M is the norm-closed
linear span of the set of Dirac measures in the dual space Li p0 (M)∗ of the space
of Lipschitz functions. It is actually a predual to Li p0 (M). Lipschitz-free spaces are
known for more than 50 years and they have been given various names. We will
follow in this article the notation used in [23] and in several subsequent works. Let
us note, however, that the term free space goes back to [45]. We refer the reader to
the authoritative book [51] which plays a crucial role in the development of the field.
Non-linear geometry of Banach spaces as it was in 2000 is displayed in [3]. The book
[2] is an update on this rapidly expanding domain, and we refer to Chapter 5 in this
book for the basic properties of Lipschitz-free spaces. A commented account of Nigel
Kalton’s 2008 graduate course on non-linear functional analysis is displayed in [5].
    Our reference on approximation properties of Banach spaces is [8]. A Banach space
X has the approximation property (A.P.) if I d X belongs to the closure of the space of
finite rank operators for the topology τ K of uniform convergence on compact subsets
of X . The λ-bounded approximation property (in short, λ-B.A.P. or simply B.A.P.
when λ does not matter) means that I d X is in the τ K -closure of the set of finite rank
operators of norm at most λ. The 1-B.A.P. is called the metric approximation property
(M.A.P.). A separable Banach space X has the (commuting) bounded approximation
property if there exist a (commuting) sequence (Rn ) ⊂ L(X ) of finite rank operators
such that lim n x − Rn (x) = 0 for all x ∈ X . If I d X belongs to the τ K -closure of
the set of finite rank linear projections of norm at most λ, we say that X has the (πλ )-
property (or (π ) if λ does not matter). A separable space X has a finite-dimensional
decomposition (F.D.D.) if there exists a sequence (E k ) of finite dimensional subspaces
of X such that for every x ∈ X , there exists a unique sequence (xk ) such that xk ∈ E k
for all k and moreover

    If every E k is one dimensional, the F.D.D. is called a Schauder basis. It is shown
in [9] that a separable Banach space has an F.D.D. if and only if it has the commuting
bounded approximation property (C.B.A.P.) and the (π )-property. Moreover, it is
shown in [10] that a separable Banach space has the C.B.A.P. if and only if it has an
equivalent norm for which the metric approximation property (M.A.P.) holds, namely
the approximation property with a sequence of contractions.
    This note investigates which Lipschitz-free spaces have the (π )-property or an
F.D.D., and why this question is relevant. We will show is particular that if X is a sep-
arable L1 -Banach space, then F(X ) has an F.D.D. If M ⊂ Rn is convex or is a closed
C 1 -smooth manifold, or if a metric space M is doubling and purely 1-unrectifiable,

 then F(M) has a F.D.D. Using a theorem of Lee and Naor [39], we show that if M is
 an arbitrary metric space then F(M) has the (π ) property “up to a logarithmic factor",
 that is, every finite-dimensional subspace of F(M) is contained in a d-dimensional
 subspace which is the range of a projection of norm less than C(log d)/(log(log d)),
 where C is an absolute constant. The existence of separable Banach spaces with the
 (π )-property up to a logarithmic factor but failing the approximation property follows.
 A list of related open problems concludes the note.


 2 Lipschitz-free spaces nearly have the ()-property

 We recall that a pointed metric space M is a metric space equipped with a distinguished
 point called 0, and Li p0 (M) is the Banach space of Lipschitz functions on M which
 vanish at 0. The space F(M) is the closed linear span of the set of Dirac measures
 in Li p0 (M)∗ , and it is an isometric predual of Li p0 (M), hence F(M)∗ = Li p0 (M).
 The space F(M) is separable when M is separable. Let us recall some basic facts (see
 e.g. [23]): if M and N are pointed metric spaces and f : M → N is a Lipschitz map
 such that f (0) = 0, there exists a unique linear operator fˆ : F(M) → F(N ) such
 that fˆ ◦ δ M = δ N ◦ f , where δ denotes the Dirac maps. If 0 ∈ N ⊂ M and i : N → M
 is the canonical injection, then î : F(N ) → F(M) is an isometric embedding and
 thus we may identify F(N ) with a subspace of F(M). If X is a Banach space, the
 canonical inclusion i : X ∗ → Li p0 (X ) satisfies i = β X∗ , where β X : F(X ) → X is
 called the barycenter map. This map β X is a quotient map and if f : M → X is a
 Lipschitz map such that f (0) = 0, then f = β X ◦ fˆ : F(M) → X is a linear operator
 such that f ◦ δ M = f , whose norm is equal to the Lipschitz norm of f . Moreover, the
 map f → f is an isometric linear isomorphism between Li p0 (M, X ) and the space
 L(F(M), X ) of bounded linear operators from F(M) to X .
     Warning: In what follows, metric spaces are always pointed with a distinguished
 point 0, Lipschitz maps f always satisfy f (0) = 0, subsets always contain the dis-
 tinguished point, and the spaces Li p0 (M) are always equipped with the Lipschitz
 norm. Since the space F(M) is isometrically independent of the choice of the dis-
 tinguished point and since the corresponding restrictions are essentially formal, they
 will be ommitted for the sake of shortness, and for instance Li p0 (M) will simply be
 denoted Li p(M).

3 Lipschitz-free spaces with or without the ()-property

The following general result states what happens when good extension operators are
available.
Proposition 3.1 Let M be a metric space. If there exists λ ∈ R such that ae(S) ≤ λ
for every finite subset S ⊂ M, then F(M) has the (πλ )-property. If moreover M is
separable and F(M) has the commuting bounded approximation property, for instance
if F(M) has the metric approximation property, then F(M) has a finite-dimensional
decomposition.
Proof We proceed as in the proof of Proposition 2.1. For any finite subset S ⊂ M,
there exists a Lipschitz map g : M → F(S) extending δ S with g L ≤ λ, and then
g : F(M) → F(S) is a projection with g ≤ λ. Since the union of the subspaces
F(S) where S runs through the finite subsets of M is dense in the Banach space F(M),
the (πλ )-property follows for F(M). If moreover F(M) has the commuting B.A.P.
then by [9] F(M) has an F.D.D. By the main result of [10], any separable space with
M.A.P. has commuting B.A.P. This concludes the proof.
   The first part of the following corollary is implicit in [38].
Corollary 3.2 Let M be a doubling metric space. Then F(M) has the (π )-property.
If moreover F(M) has the metric approximation property, then F(M) has a finite-
dimensional decomposition.

Remark 3.3 We recall that the Nagata dimension of a metric space M is the smallest

an extension operator and G n  = 1. We denote by Rn : Li p(C) → Li p(i n (K ))
the restriction operator. If there exists an extension operator E : Li p(L) → Li p(C),
then Hn = Rn E G n : Li p(i n (Mn )) → Li p(i n (K )) is an extension operator with
Hn  ≤ E, and the (π )-property for F(K ) follows, a contradiction which concludes
the proof.
Remark 3.9 It looks difficult to construct a free space F(K ) with the B.A.P. which fails
the (π )-property. Actually, the only example of a separable Banach space with B.A.P.
and without (π ) has been constructed by Read [48], but unfortunately this example
has remained unpublished (see [8, 10]). Note that Read’s example is reflexive, and
thus has the commuting B.A.P.


4 Open problems

Several natural problems, which are open to the best of my knowledge, are related
with the topics of the present note. We present them with some motivating comments.
Problem 4.1 Let M be a uniformly discrete space, that is, there exists θ > 0 such that
d(x, y) ≥ θ for all pairs of distinct points (x, y) ∈ M 2 . Does the Lipschitz-free space
F(M) have B.A.P.?
   This problem is due to Nigel Kalton ([35], Problem 1). The answer is positive if
and only if every separable Banach space is uniformly approximable in the sense of
[35]. If the answer is negative, there exists an equivalent norm on l1 which fails the
M.A.P. and thus the dual space would have B.A.P. but not M.A.P. which would solve
a problem which goes back to Grothendieck ( [34], Remark after Proposition 4.4). At
present, I would conjecture a negative answer to Problem 4.1.
Problem 4.2 Let M ⊂ R2 be a closed subset of the plane. Does F(M) have the
M.A.P.?
23   Page 10 of 13                                                               G. Godefroy


   We know that F(M) has the (π )-property since M is a doubling metric space
(see Corollary 3.2). I conjecture a negative answer to Problem 4.2. A subquestion
to Problem 4.2 is to know whether F(M) has the commuting B.A.P., from which it
would follow that F(M) has an F.D.D. A positive answer to this subquestion looks
very likely, since no separable space is known with B.A.P. but without the commuting
B.A.P.

Problem 4.3 Let φ : N → [1, +∞) be an increasing function which tends to +∞
at infinity. Does there exist a separable Banach space X failing the approximation
property, which contains an increasing sequence (E n ) of finite-dimensional subspaces
whose union is dense in X , such that for all n there exists a linear projection πn with
πn (X ) = E n and πn  ≤ φ(n)?

   This problem has been formulated by Gilles Pisier. There is hope that the flexibility
provided by metric spaces could provide many examples, in the spirit of the proof
of Theorem 2.4. Note that Theorem 2.4 shows in particular that Pisier’s fundamen-
tal example [46] and all similar spaces with few projections are very different from
Lipschitz-free spaces.

Problem 4.4 Does there exist λ ∈ R such that all spaces F(E) have the (πλ )-property,
where E is an arbitrary finite-dimensional normed space?

    If the answer to this problem is positive, then the proof of Proposition 3.5 shows that
F(X ) has an F.D.D. for every Banach space X with an F.D.D. The proof that F(E) has
the M.A.P. for every finite-dimensional normed space E uses convolution with Fejer
kernels [23]. It would be tempting, for constructing a sequence of projections, to use
partial sums Sn from (−n) to n of the Fourier series on the space of Lipschitz functions,
but in dimension 1 the norms of the corresponding operators diverge like log(n) so
this approach fails. Fourier multipliers could provide proper tools for investigating this
problem. Another approach would be to show that a separable space with M.A.P. and
the (π )-property has the (πλ )-property for some universal constant λ. This question
is apparently open.

Problem 4.5 Is the Lipschitz-free space F(l1 ) complemented in its bidual?

    What motivates this well-known problem is that a positive answer would imply that
any Banach space X which is Lipschitz-isomorphic to l1 is actually linearly isomorphic
to l1 . Indeed, if F(l1 ) is complemented in its bidual, it is also the case for F(X ) when
X is Lipschitz-isomorphic to l1 , and then it follows from the Lifting property that X
itself is complemented in X ∗∗ . But then, ([3], Cor. 7.7) shows that X is complemented
in l1 and thus isomorphic to l1 .
    We may also argue that it follows from ([3], Thm.7.9 and [29]) that if X is Lipschitz-
isomorphic to l1 , then X is linearly isomorphic to an L1 -subspace of l1 . But if Y
is a L1 -subspace of l1 which is not isomorphic to l1 , then Y is not complemented
in its bidual since complemented subspaces of L 1 -spaces with the Radon–Nikodym
property are isomorphic to l1 , and thus the lifting property shows that F(Y ) is not
complemented in its bidual. Note that there exist two L1 -subspaces of l1 which are
uniformly homeomorphic but not linearly isomorphic ([35], Theorem 8.6). It is shown
Lipschitz-free spaces and approximating…                                    Page 11 of 13   23


in [13] that if l2n denotes the n-dimensional Euclidean space, then F(l2n ) is contractively
complemented in its bidual. It is shown in [25] that the space F(l1 ) has a Schauder
basis.

Problem 4.6 For n ∈ N, we denote ae(n) = sup{ae(N ); |N | = n}, where ae(N ) is
defined in Sect. 2. How does ae(n) behave when n increases to infinity?

   This question is considered by Assaf Naor as a major problem. Theorem 2.4 relies
on the deep inequality from [39] that ae(n) ≤ C. log(n)/ log(log(n)).
                                                                       On the other
hand, it is shown in [42] that there is A > 0 such that ae(n) ≥ A. log(n). Of course,
the proof of Theorem 2.4 actually shows the existence of separable Banach spaces
which have the (π ) property up to ae(n) but fail the approximation property.

Problem 4.7 Does there exist a compact metric space K such that the space li p0 (K )
of locally flat Lipschitz functions fail the approximation property?

    We recall that a Lipschitz function f : K → R is said to be locally flat is for any
  > 0, there exists η > 0 such that | f (x) − f (y)| ≤ .d(x, y) if d(x, y) < η. The
compact set K is purely 1-unrectifiable if and only if li p0 (K ) separates F(K ), if and
only if li p0 (K ) is an isometric predual of F(K ) ( [1], see Corollary 2.3). Note that
the Banach–Mazur distance between li p0 (K ) and the set of isometric subspaces of
c0 (N) is 1 ([34], Theorem 6.6), hence if li p0 (K ) has the λ-commuting B.A.P. with
λ < 2, then by ([22], Theorem 4.3), li p0 (K ) and its dual space have M.A.P. Let us
recall that it follows from Enflo’s example [15] that there exists a subspace of c0 (N)
failing A.P. It has been shown by W. B. Johnson and G. Schechtman that there exists
a subspace of c0 (N) which has B.A.P. but fails M.A.P. (Corollary JS in [30]). In turn,
this space has been used to prove the existence of a subspace of c0 (N) with A.P., but
failing B.A.P. (Corollary 1.13 in [16]).
Problem 4.8 Is the set B of separable Banach spaces which have a basis a Borel subset
of the set S B of separable Banach spaces?

    This problem, which was promoted by Bill Johnson, happens to be related with
some issues of the present work. We recall that a proper parametrization turns the
collection of separable Banach spaces into a standard Borel space S B (see [4]) and
that this parametrization appears to be canonical for all practical purposes. A rule of
thumb is that local properties of Banach spaces deliver Borel subsets of S B while
infinite-dimensional properties frequently provide analytic or coanalytic non-Borel
subsets. A very interesting result in this field is due to O. Kurka [37] who showed that
the isomorphism class of c0 is analytic non-Borel. The relation with Problem 4.8 is
that it follows from Kurka’s theorem that having an unconditional basis is an analytic
non-Borel condition, and the same holds true for being isomorphic to a subspace of a
space with an unconditional basis. It is easily checked that B is analytic. On the other
hand, the B.A.P. and the (π )-property both define Borel subsets [18–20]. Hence, if
B is non-Borel, the existence of a space with B.A.P. but without a basis, which is a
theorem due to Szarek [50], follows immediately. Moreover, it would follow that there
is a space with the property (LB S) but no basis, which is an open question (see [50],
Problem 1.8). Indeed, we recall that a separable space X has (LB S) if there exists
23   Page 12 of 13                                                                                G. Godefroy


an increasing sequence (E n ) of finite-dimensional subspaces of X with dense union,
which are uniformly complemented in X and have bases with a uniformly bounded
constant. It is shown in ([12], Subsection 5.3) that (LB S) is a Borel condition. It is
easily seen that the commuting B.A.P. defines an analytic subset, but I was unable to
show that this subset is Borel. If by any chance this were not true, it would of course
follow that B.A.P. is not equivalent to commuting B.A.P. for separable Banach spaces,
and equivalently (by [10, 27]) that there exist separable Banach spaces with B.A.P.
with no equivalent norm for which M.A.P. holds.
