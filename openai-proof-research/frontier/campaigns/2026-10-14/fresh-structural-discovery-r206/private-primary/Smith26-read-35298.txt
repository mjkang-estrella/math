Proof of Theorem 1.3. Using [16], [9, Theorem 8] and Proposition 3.1, the set
                         RBAP := {d ∈ R : F(Md ) has the BAP}
is ∆11 . Let d′ ∈ R and d ∈ RBAP such that Md′ is isometric to a 1-Lipschitz retract of Md .
Again by [30, Theorems 3.6 and 3.7], F(Md′ ) is linearly isometric to a 1-complemented
subspace of F(Md ) and thus d′ ∈ RBAP . For a contradiction assume Rdis ⊆ RBAP . Then
RBAP = R by Lemma 3.10. However, this is false because we can pick d ∈ R such that
Md is isometric to a separable Banach space that fails the AP; as is pointed out in the
Introduction, d ∈ / RBAP by [17, Theorem 3.1].
   Now pick d ∈ Rdis \ RBAP . Again, as stated in the Introduction, F(Md ) has the AP
by [2, Corollary 2.8]. That F(Md ) cannot be isomorphic to a dual space is a simple
consequence of the classical result of Grothendieck which asserts that a separable Banach
space has the MAP whenever it has the AP and is isometric to a dual space.               □
