# Prime-controlled stable-annulus covers

This supplies the monodromy check missing in r59. All root estimates and the underlying metrized-complex construction are those of pinned source019, `04-tame-roots.tex`, Lemmas `lem:annular-unit`, `lem:stable-edge-root`, `lem:etale-collars`. The added assertion is that the covers can be chosen with ell-group Galois closure at the stable-edge step. The group-action lifting criterion is ABBR I, arXiv:1303.4812v3, Theorem 7.4(2), not just the existence theorem 7.7.

Fix an odd prime ell different from the residue characteristic p, and a proper smooth curve C over Omega with stable reduction. A chosen stable node annulus has an analytic ell-th root of its coordinate on its entire inverse image in a finite étale cover whose Galois closure has ell-power degree. The same holds simultaneously for finitely many stable annuli.

## Nonseparating edge

Take an oriented simple circuit containing the edge. At each circuit vertex let P be its incoming mark and Q its outgoing mark. As in the source, choose a function f on the residue curve with divisor P-Q+ell E, and take y^ell=f. Let g send y to zeta y, with the same primitive ell-th root zeta at every circuit vertex. At P a local uniformizer has g-character zeta; at Q it has character zeta^(-1). Indeed one may absorb a local unit into y at P, and use 1/y at Q; the unit roots can be chosen in the complete residue local rings because ell is invertible. The cover is connected, cyclic, totally ramified at P,Q, and unramified elsewhere.

At an off-circuit vertex take ell identity copies, labelled by C_ell, with g acting regularly on the labels. At every non-circuit edge match the unramified marked fibers equivariantly as regular C_ell-sets. Every such edge orbit has length ell. Circuit edges have one lift, expansion factor ell, and are fixed as edges by C_ell. This gives the same connected tame covering of metrized complexes as in the source, now with a specified C_ell-action. Riemann–Hurwitz gives zero augmented ramification at every vertex. No punctures are used.

ABBR 6.18 and Corollary 6.21 lift the residue-curve isomorphisms uniquely to the punctured star-shaped pieces. Hence their group law, including g^ell=1, holds on the analytic pieces. We choose gluing data fixed by this action in the exact sense of formula (7.3.1).

For a circuit edge, write the two base branch coordinates as s,t with st=a. The annular degree-ell maps can be written s=u^ell and t=v^ell after scaling coordinates, using the tame annular normal form in ABBR §7.2. At its tail, g(u)=zeta^(-1)u, since the mark is outgoing; at its head, g(v)=zeta v. Choose b with b^ell=a and identify v=b/u. This gluing commutes with g, since b/(zeta^(-1)u)=zeta b/u. Any scale factors are absorbed into b and do not alter the characters. The character on an analytic annulus is determined by the residue tangent character: an automorphism over u↦u^ell is multiplication by an ell-th root of unity, and reduction identifies those roots because ell≠p.

For each free edge orbit, choose a gluing for one representative and define the gluing for g^j of that edge by conjugation using the two lifted star automorphisms. There is no stabilizer condition, and the definition is consistent at j=ell because g^ell=1. Reverse edge orientations receive inverse gluings. Thus the whole gluing datum is fixed by C_ell.

Following the actual subdivision proof of ABBR7.7, to meet ABBR7.4's no-loop hypothesis, subdivide every loop at an interior rational-length point and attach the standard P1 residue curve with two marked points. This is a strongly semistable triangulation of the same curve; stability at the inserted vertex is not required by ABBR7.4. On an inserted circuit vertex use the cyclic map with divisor P-Q; its two characters are again inverse. Off-circuit insertions use identity copies. The preceding construction applies without loops and gives the same prescribed expansion on both subannuli. The analytic inverse annulus remains a connected tame degree-ell annulus across the subdivision, so its original coordinate has exponent ±ell. Equivalently the subdivision can be forgotten after gluing. No new branch point is introduced.

By ABBR7.4(2), the stabilizer of the chosen gluing datum is the automorphism group of the lifted cover. The C_ell-action is faithful, since it is already faithful on a circuit residue curve. The lifted cover is connected and has degree ell, so this action makes it a cyclic Galois cover. It is finite étale by the source's zero-ramification check and has precisely the inverse node annuli prescribed by its metrized complex. On each of them the original coordinate has exponent ±ell. The source's annular-unit factorization then supplies its ell-th root on the whole inverse annulus.

## Separating edge

Each side of a separating edge in a stable graph contains a circuit or a positive-genus vertex, by the source's valence argument. On each side build a connected cyclic degree-ell cover, with its boundary mark unramified. If there is a circuit, use a connected cyclic graph cover and identity residue maps. Otherwise use a nontrivial cyclic étale degree-ell cover of one positive-genus residue curve (an exact-order-ell Jacobian line bundle); at the remaining vertices use ell identity copies. Match all unramified marked fibers equivariantly. The graph-cover case has a free deck action on vertices; in the residue-cover case the cyclic action on the distinguished residue curve is unramified and all incident marked fibers are regular C_ell-sets. In either case the covered side is connected by lifting paths to the distinguished circuit or vertex.

Identify the two cyclic groups, and match their ell boundary fibers equivariantly across the original bridge. There are ell distinct bridge lifts. Each is nonseparating: removing it leaves the two connected sides joined by the other ell−1 edges. All lifted edges in this preliminary cover are free C_ell-orbits and all expansion factors are one. Choose analytic gluing equivariantly on each orbit as above. ABBR7.4(2) gives a cyclic finite étale degree-ell cover of C. The source checks that its triangulation remains stable.

For each of the ell lifted bridge annuli, use the nonseparating construction. Take a connected component of the fiber product of these cyclic covers. Its Galois group over the preliminary curve is an ell-group; the normal closure over C is an ell-group too. Indeed the compositum of all conjugates over the cyclic preliminary extension has group a subgroup of a product of ell-groups, and the total group is an extension by C_ell. Roots persist under every further pullback. The same finite-compositum argument handles finitely many original node annuli.

## Disk collars and the fixed prime set

Put Sigma={p,ell}. The actual Mochizuki–Tsujimura RNS theorem permits this Sigma: Theorem A/2.17 and the model-domination interface used by the source. Thus the source's disk-to-stable-annulus proof can choose its initial Galois étale cover with Sigma-order geometric group. Above the irrational target level it obtains a finite collection of stable node annuli and a smaller collar whose *entire* inverse image lies in their union. Applying the ell-group construction above to all these annuli and taking a normal closure produces a Sigma-cover. No roots or collars are selected sheetwise; the source's compact-complement argument and annular-unit proof apply unchanged.

Consequently `lem:etale-collars` holds with the additional conclusion that the finite étale cover has Sigma-order Galois closure. This is the exact first-stage interface used in `candidate-proof.md`. The original analytic construction is credited to the source; the action compatibility is elementary once ABBR's stronger classification theorem is used. No independent substantiality claim is made.
