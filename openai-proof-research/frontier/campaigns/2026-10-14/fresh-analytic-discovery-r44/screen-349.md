Likewise, a proposed qualitative isolation theorem would need the familiar
compactness/local-rigidity route. On a Clifford product with factor
dimensions k and m-k, the scalar Laplace eigenvalues are

    (m/k) l(l+k-1) + (m/(m-k)) j(j+m-k-1),  l,j>=0.

The Jacobi kernel corresponds to eigenvalue 2m and consists exactly of
(l,j)=(1,1). These are ambient rotational normal fields: degree two in
either factor alone has eigenvalue strictly greater than 2m. Thus merely
combining a suitable multiplicity-one compactness statement with this
standard kernel calculation and an implicit-function argument would be
a technical transfer, not the intended substantial discovery. This note
does not present that complete gap theorem as established.
