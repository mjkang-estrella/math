# Strongest integrated reductions and contribution question

T177C143 is an ungraded author-proof nominee. Its content is the logarithmic cost of choosing all fillings with one random seed, including arbitrary nonlinear choices, on the full pinned congruence tower. It also gives a universal finite inequality. It is not a new coefficient-uniform expansion theorem, a code puncturing result, or a continuation of r80 or r142.

The source177 construction already provides all complexes, vanishing H^1, expansion, bounded incidences, group actions, finite stabilizer bounds and growth. These operations are used together. There is no claimed new KMS construction, stabilization theorem, field change, or homology theorem.

[Oppenheim–Valentiner-Branth v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05823v2), Theorem1.2 and Proposition2.7, combined with source vanishing and the exact skeleton weights, already give F_2^r expansion uniformly in r. This pays union-support simultaneous filling for every finite family, in the sense of [Kaufman–Tessler v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09495v2), Definition1.2. The initial lead was accordingly not nominated. No failure of coordinatewise estimates is used as novelty evidence.

[Khot–Naor v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0510547v1), Lemmas2.1 and3.3, provide the Fourier support/Poincare method; Lemma3.6 identifies coset distance with transport between uniform coset laws. Their Theorem3.4 and Corollary3.7 obtain nonembeddability from dual-code distance. Those operations are credited. Here the quotient metric itself embeds into L_1 with uniformly bounded distortion. The lower bound concerns lifting into jointly distributed representatives, obtained from the section-dependent character cosets of vertex-pair potentials and their path distances. It does not follow by asserting poor L_1 embeddability of this quotient.

[Li–Anantharam v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01388v2), Proposition39, explicitly formulates the grand-coupling problem as a Lipschitz right inverse of the distribution map. Proposition40 transfers coupling bounds to L_1 embeddings; Proposition42 uses Khot–Naor for a Hamming-cube lower bound over all laws. Theorem15 and Proposition18 give general logarithmic bounds in the number of ambient points or laws. These are stronger than the v1 bounds initially inspected. In this construction both sets can have exponential size in the number of complex vertices; those general bounds do not identify the logarithmic-in-vertices scale here. The explicit lower-bound proof applies despite uniformly bounded L_1 distortion of the particular fibre-law metric. This is a comparison of actual operations, not a claim that all possible uses of their methods have been excluded.

[Kaufman–Oppenheim v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01259v4), Theorem3.8 and Lemmas3.9–3.11, and [Wild's thesis](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/download/11777/11809/finalthesisPascalWildPDFA.pdf), §3.2, already develop random cones/cofillings, conjugation by symmetries, and incidence-load averaging. They pay the averaging framework. The upper construction here is a tree gauge with finitely many edge orbits; it does not claim a novel general random-cone principle.

After all those compatible reductions, the proposed distinct operation is: turn an entirely arbitrary section into an equivariant cut correction; attach to EACH vertex pair a Boolean character-coset function; force every surviving Fourier frequency to connect that pair; and transfer its influence through degree-zero expansion to one distribution-independent sum of syndrome-pair costs. Averaging this sum retains the universal quantifier over stochastic nonlinear sections. The same source symmetry supplies the matching scale from tree gauges. An independent contribution reviewer must decide whether this operation is substantial or a routine composition; the author assigns neither verdict.

Bounded literature/history coverage is recorded separately. No worldwide priority, current-open status, complete absence of antecedents, or exhaustive classical-reducibility conclusion is asserted. The public proof is self-contained for the new finite argument; source construction facts remain explicitly stipulated.

Late evidence: Wild's remarks immediately after Proposition3.13 explicitly allow arbitrary, not necessarily homomorphic, correction maps and note the difficulty of estimating their expectation. Thus neither nonlinear-map terminology nor the general correction framework is proposed as new. The all-sections lower inequality (B) is the specific operation submitted for separate contribution review.
