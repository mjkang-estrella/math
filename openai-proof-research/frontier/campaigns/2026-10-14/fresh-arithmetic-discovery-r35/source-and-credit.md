The companion's encoding.tex315–477 is especially close: action calculations keep original words and polynomial expressions, use minimum-cost labels only in proofs, and never search for representative codes. Its subsequent normalizer uses strict literal dependency 3 floor(n/10)<n. The source explicitly permits finite expansions, but the read passages do not bound their storage or evaluate arbitrarily nested raw/code trees. Our residual proposed operation consists of (i) separating native raw singletons from canonical semantic raw atoms, (ii) quiver and code-boundary protection in arbitrary contexts, (iii) a strict weighted mutual-recursion ledger including short selector matching, and (iv) term-by-term recomputation through mixed stage depths with a polynomial bound in the original group-word length. Whether this is substantial or a technical complexity audit is left to independent contribution review. Absence of a PSPACE statement in the passages read is not evidence of novelty by itself.

## Actual primary comparisons

[Olshanskii, Space functions of groups](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.0118), Proposition 1.1, Theorem 1.2 and corollaries: polynomial-space word-problem groups already embed in finitely presented groups with polynomial space functions. That source pays space-preserving finite-presentation embeddings by a different machine construction. The passages read do not supply simplicity. No new claim is made about that classical embedding operation.

[Darbinyan–Steenbock, Embeddings into left-orderable simple groups](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06183), Theorem 3 and §7.1: effective embeddings into finitely generated simple groups with decidable word problem, with stronger geometric properties in their scope, are already available. The interval multiplication-table encoding, special charts and effective tests are credited. The read theorem does not assert the simultaneous finite-presentation/PSPACE target here; the entire paper was not read.

[Belk–Zaremsky, Twisted Brin–Thompson groups](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.04579), brick/twist definitions, groupoid normal forms and Theorem 3.4: twisted-group construction, canonical embedding and simplicity are paid. Our final finite-table composition and identity check implement these classical operations. [Zaremsky, Finite presentability of twisted Brin–Thompson groups](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18354v2), Theorem A/Corollary B and type-(A) definition: the finite-presentation criterion is paid; no new criterion is claimed.

[Wächter–Weiß, An Automaton Group with PSPACE-Complete Word Problem](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00224-021-10064-7), introduction and Theorem 25 context: ordinary PSPACE word problems do not license a generic compressed-input PSPACE oracle. This is a warning against that inference, not a counterexample to the particular decorated expression family here. The proof uses no generic compressed-word closure theorem.

Finite functional-graph orbit search, exhaustive parity counting over F2, fixed-dimensional matrix expansion and elementary prefix replacement are ordinary algorithms. The originals of Post, Murskii, Kleene, Newman, Thompson, Britton, Krstic–McCool and the cited K-theory results were not newly read; source credit is retained and these are not claimed as new operations.
