# Punctured-region stabilizers cannot in general be normalized to track groups

This is a route obstruction for T253F, not a finiteness obstruction or a separate nominee. It uses the stipulated polygon source and the actual primary reconstruction theorem specified below. All spaces and maps are the Stone cut spaces, not ordinary polygons with an arbitrary convention on boundaries.

## 1. The local area lattice is independent of the region

Let K=Q(sqrt(5)), O=Z[τ]. For a nonempty clopen region W in finitely many tracks, let M_W be the additive subgroup of R generated by the areas of its clopen subsets. Let M be the analogous group for one whole track. For multiple whole tracks the group is still M.

The source's arithmetic lemma puts every allowable vertex in D^(−1)O² for one fixed integer D. The shoelace formula therefore gives

    O ⊂ M ⊂ (2D²)^(−1)O.

For the first inclusion, use the full unit square and the rectangle of width τ−1 and height 1. Thus M is a free abelian group of rank two, a lattice in the two-dimensional rational vector space K. Exact determination of D or M is unnecessary.

We claim M_W=M. Choose an ordinary rectangle R with closure in the interior of a polygonal component of W. Any clopen P⊂W can be subdivided by a sufficiently fine coordinate grid with O-valued levels into finitely many small polygonal pieces P_i. Each τP_i can then be translated by a vector in O² into R: choose the grid fine enough to leave an open set of admissible translations, then use density. Scaling uniformly by τ preserves the allowed directions and O-valued cut levels. Therefore τ² μ(P)=sum_i μ(τP_i) belongs to M_W. This proves closure of M_W under multiplication by τ², and hence under O=Z[τ²]. The translated pieces need not be disjoint from one another, because M_W is an additive group generated by areas, not a claim about one embedded union.

For any clopen polygon P in the whole track, sufficiently large uniform shrinkage τ^(−k)P, followed by an O² translation, fits inside R. Its area τ^(−2k)μ(P) belongs to M_W. Multiplying by τ^(2k)∈O now gives μ(P)∈M_W. This proves M⊂M_W; the reverse inclusion follows by cutting W into tracks. In particular the same fixed rank-two lattice M works for every nonempty reduction.

## 2. Unique invariant measure for all intermediate groups

Write A(W) and F(W) for the alternating and full translation groups of the reduced region. Every group H satisfying A(W)≤H≤F(W) has exactly one invariant probability measure, namely μ|W/μ(W).

First, an A(W)-invariant finite Borel measure ν is invariant under every partial piecewise-translation bijection inside W. Indeed, partition its domain into finitely many polygons each of area <μ(W)/10. This can be done by fine coordinate grids. The source alternating-extension lemma, whose proof explicitly works in any clopen region, extends each restricted bijection to an element of A(W). Invariance gives equality of the measures of the corresponding domain and range pieces, and finite additivity gives the assertion. The argument uses area only to obtain extensions; ν is not assumed beforehand to equal area.

Such a measure extends to an invariant finite measure on the whole track groupoid. Choose a finite clopen partition D_i of an ambient finite track space containing W and partial translation maps t_i:D_i→W, possible by minimality and compactness. Define ν_tilde(E)=sum_i ν(t_i(E∩D_i)). For any translation between clopen sets, refine both the D_i and their translated images. On a resulting piece, t_j g t_i^(−1) is a partial translation inside W, so the just-proved invariance shows that ν_tilde is invariant. To see that ν_tilde restricts to ν on W, refine E⊂W by the D_i and apply the same invariance to t_i:E∩D_i→W. This argument works also when W occupies several tracks: extend to the finite-track groupoid first, then restrict to one whole track; track-switch maps force the same measure on each track.

The source gives a continuous equivariant location map π:X→R²/Z². Any Γ-invariant probability measure pushes forward to Haar measure: Γ=(O/Z)² is dense in the torus, so invariance extends to all translations, for example by continuity of the integrals of continuous functions. Outside the countable union of the allowable lines, π has singleton fibers, since all Boolean membership decisions are determined by the ordinary point. That union has Haar measure zero. Hence the invariant measure on X is unique and is precisely the area measure. Induction and restriction as above prove uniqueness on W. Every intermediate H contains A(W) and preserves area, so the same conclusion holds for H.

## 3. Reconstruction applies to the actual stabilizers

The groups in the preceding paragraph are locally minimal in the exact sense of Nekrashevych, *Simple groups of dynamical origin*, arXiv:1511.08241v2, §3.3, Theorem 3.8 (there attributed to Rubin's Theorems 0.2 and 3.3). This is an actual primary statement read here; Rubin's underlying paper was not read directly.

For any clopen U⊂W and x∈U, the reduced translation orbit is dense in U by source minimality. If y≠x is another orbit point in U, choose a third distinct orbit point z∈U. There are sufficiently small disjoint clopen neighborhoods of x,y,z, related by the corresponding translations. Their 3-cycle lies in A(W), is supported in U, and sends x to y. Thus the subgroup supported in U has dense orbit at every x. This proves local minimality for A(W) and hence for any H between A(W) and F(W). The spaces W are compact Hausdorff Cantor spaces, so the topological hypotheses of Theorem 3.8 hold.

Consequently an abstract isomorphism between two such groups is implemented by a homeomorphism of their reduced spaces. No assumption of affine, polygonwise or piecewise-translation behavior of that homeomorphism is made. This is why one can apply the argument to the exact stabilizer A_m∩F(W), without identifying it with A(W).

## 4. A necessary norm-square condition

Suppose A(W)≤H≤F(W) and H is abstractly isomorphic to A_ell on ell whole tracks. Reconstruction provides a homeomorphism h:W→ell X implementing the isomorphism. Unique invariant measures imply

    μ(h(P))=(ell/μ(W)) μ(P)

for every clopen P⊂W. Since h and its inverse map clopen sets to clopen sets, the local area-lattice calculation gives

    cM=M,  where c=ell/μ(W)∈K.

Multiplication by c on the rank-two lattice M has integral matrix and integral inverse, so its determinant is ±1. Its rational determinant is the field norm N_(K/Q)(c). Therefore

    |N(μ(W))|=ell².                         (* predictor *)

This is a necessary condition only. A negative norm alone is not enough here: a lattice automorphism may have norm −1. The stronger absolute-norm-square test is essential.

## 5. Arbitrarily small holes violating the condition

Take W_b=mX\Q_b, where Q_b is the standard square of side τ^(−b). Then

    A_b=μ(W_b)=m−τ^(−2b),
    N(A_b)=m²+1−m L_(2b),

where L_j=τ^j+bar(τ)^j is the integer Lucas sequence. For sufficiently large b the first real embedding of A_b is positive and the conjugate is negative, so the norm is negative.

For every fixed m, there are arbitrarily large b for which |N(A_b)| is not a square:

* If m=1 and b is positive and even, |N(A_b)|=5 F_b², where F_b is a positive Fibonacci integer, so it is not a square.
* If m>1, choose a prime q≡3 mod4 not dividing m−1. One can choose a prime factor q≡3 mod4 of the positive integer 4(m−1)−1; such a factor exists and cannot divide m−1. In the finite ring O/qO, τ is a unit, since τ(τ−1)=1. Let r be its finite multiplicative order. For arbitrarily large b divisible by r, τ^(2b) and its conjugate both equal 1 modulo q. Thus N(A_b)≡(m−1)² (mod q). Since the norm is negative, |N(A_b)| is congruent to the negative of a nonzero square, a quadratic nonresidue modulo q. It cannot be an integer square.

There is a stronger tail statement for every square track count m=r²>1, enough to rule out simply passing to a subsequence of these square slots. The Lucas identity L_(2b)=L_b²−2(−1)^b gives, once the norm is negative,

    |N(A_b)|=(r L_b)²−(m+(-1)^b)².

The subtracted integer is positive and bounded independently of b. Since r L_b tends to infinity, this expression eventually lies strictly between (rL_b−1)² and (rL_b)². Hence it is nonsquare for EVERY sufficiently large b. Square track counts are available arbitrarily far beyond the source's finite-presentation threshold; this observation does not narrow T253F, which remains quantified over all sufficiently large m.

For any of these nonsquare holes, the actual standard vertex stabilizer

    G_b=A_m∩F(W_b)

satisfies A(W_b)≤G_b≤F(W_b), but cannot be abstractly isomorphic to A_ell for any positive integer ell, by (* predictor *). The same necessity applies to any intermediate group on W_b. This is stronger than r31's obstruction to uniform homotheties.

## 6. Limits and credit

This proves only that one proposed source-group normalization of the Brown stabilizers fails. It does not prove they are not finitely presented, not FP_n, or not F_infinity; it does not refute T253F. The contractible slot telescope may still be useful if finiteness of reduced-region groups is proved directly.

Local 3-cycles, strict comparison and alternating extension are source operations. Spatial reconstruction is the established Nekrashevych/Rubin theorem. The clopen value-group invariant of uniquely ergodic Cantor actions is a standard classification device; this note supplies its elementary application to the source's quadratic area module, without claiming novelty or completeness of the broader literature. Neither this nonisomorphism obstruction nor the local-measure calculation is submitted as a replacement target.
