## Principal compression and the determinant estimate

Let B be an invertible real symmetric m×m matrix, C=B⁻¹, E={x:x_j=0}, and P delete coordinate j. In the coordinate identification E≅R^(m−1), write C_E=C[jᶜ,jᶜ]. Then P Cx=C_E x. The projected graph metric for γ'(x)=(x,PCx) is exactly I+C_E², and the bilinear form xᵀCy is exactly xᵀC_E y. For an orthonormal eigenbasis of C_E with eigenvalues λ, the graph vectors are orthogonal and the representing form has eigenvalues λ/(1+λ²). This remains true if C_E is singular; invertibility of the compression is unnecessary.

For every t>0, Cauchy interlacing bounds each of the positive and negative eigenvalue tails of C_E by the corresponding tail of C. Consequently the total count of eigenvalues with |λ|>t does not increase. Since |λ/(1+λ²)|≤min{1/2,|λ|}, at most k graph-form singular values exceed t whenever at most k inverse eigenvalues exceed t. Applied with t=1/(w√N), the source spectral event gives k≤ξN.

For any s-dimensional subspace, the absolute determinant of the compressed self-adjoint form is at most the product of its largest s singular values, by the exterior-power expression. For sufficiently large N, this is at most t^((s−k)_+), using the bound1/2≤1 on the exceptional values. This applies to the radical complement of the restriction to γ'(F), including indefinite forms and s=0. With the unchanged source lower bound N^(−NJ_B)exp(−O_L(N log log N)) and s≥dim F−ξN, the comparison gives dim F/N≤2J_B+2ξ+o(1), exactly the source's dimension bound. The compression introduces no further spectral loss.

The fixed-bit partition is essential to identify the relevant difference span with a subspace of E. If its bit is1, the points v themselves do not lie in E. Write the image differences as γ'(v−v'), or extend x↦(x,PCx) linearly to all R^m before taking differences. An expression γ'(v) with γ' declared only on E would otherwise be undefined. This is a notation clarification, not a changed estimate.

The first-coordinate projection on the graph difference lattice remains injective and maps the radical lattice into an integer lattice of the same rank. Its covolume is therefore at least1. The finite discriminant pairing pulls back along the same original integer differences. These are the source's graph-lattice operations after the bit-fiber restriction. This check supports their applicability; it does not establish that the adaptation is substantial.

## Tail and fixed-diagonal interfaces checked

After excluding a zero column, a kernel vector with maximal-coordinate anchor j has a nonzero tail. Normalize that tail to norm1. Some A_kj=1, possibly with k=j for a prescribed nonzero diagonal. That row gives |v_j|≤∑_(l≠j)|v_l|≤√(n−1), without dividing by a small random entry. The source's tail/anchor/scalar net can therefore use the same ranges.

For a fixed net vector supported on S containing the anchor, test rows outside S use disjoint off-diagonal cross-block entries. The prescribed diagonal contributes nothing to those tests. In the two-large-coefficient case, choose the interval length smaller than either coefficient magnitude: two bit-square vertices differing in one bit cannot both lie in the interval, so at most opposite vertices remain and their total mass is bounded by p²+(1−p)². The threshold differs by a harmless fixed factor from the sign formulation. In the all-small-tail case, the tail variance is bounded below after normalization, so the source Berry–Esseen estimate applies with changed fixed constants. The separate coordinate-sum approximation handles the rank-one mean.

For a corank-one symmetric matrix, deleting an off-anchor coordinate i with v_i≠0 leaves an invertible principal minor. Its inverse cross column is −v_(iᶜ)/v_i. Deleting the anchor from that vector leaves a tail of norm≤1/|v_i|, hence O(√n) for a significant tail coordinate. No O(√n) bound on the full inverse cross column is claimed.
