## 177: coefficient systems on the same congruence complexes

Pinned source177 constructs, for d>=3 and a sufficiently large fixed odd prime q, the d-skeleton of the coset complex of U/Gamma_N(m), N=2d+1, m>=2. The local groups omit one generator from a cyclic elementary-root system. `geometry.tex`, `lem:patterns`, checks the concrete root-entry patterns, local injectivity and intersections. The source's infinite coset complex is an affine-building opposition complex; spherical opposition links possess **integral** bounded cone functions. Its relative attachment complexes shift those opposition chains.

The actual `homology.tex` argument is stronger than an abstract F2 acyclicity headline. It uses projective permutation modules after averaging finite odd-order stabilizers, identifies low quotient homology with congruence-group homology, combines integral pro-stability and pro-excision with surjectivity from odd-index transfer, centralizes elementary conjugations using two spare coordinates, and averages the resulting finite q-group action. The `expansion.tex` step uses KMS local recognition, distance to cocycles and exact skeleton incidence-weight comparison. These internal operations are all paid inputs.

The strongest coefficient comparator is [Oppenheim–Valentiner-Branth, arXiv:2504.05823v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05823v2), Theorems1.1/1.2, 2.9/2.10, 6.1 and Corollary6.10. It provides coefficient-uniform cosystolic expansion for finitely generated Abelian groups; the cone construction is over Z. It is not merely a binary-field theorem needing a new norm comparison for each group.

A feasibility calculation with R=Z[1/q] illustrates why a coprime coefficient variant is a poor fresh target. Every finite stabilizer and finite-index transfer degree in the source chain is a unit in R. Localization is flat, so the cited integral pro-stability/pro-excision stage is the relevant strongest interface, rather than tensoring an F2 conclusion. If those stages are run over R, the source's two-spare-coordinate centralization and averaging give the same low-degree vanishing. Finite free R-chain complexes with vanishing low homology then give vanishing cohomology with R-module coefficients: over the PID R, boundary submodules are free, and the corresponding short exact sequences split. For a particular cochain, its finitely many labels lie in a finitely generated Abelian subgroup; the primary gives a close cocycle there, and vanishing in the larger coefficient module makes that cocycle a coboundary there. The subgroup itself need not be q-divisible. The same incidence weights finish the comparison.

This is an operation-level feasibility reduction, not a newly certified all-coefficient theorem: the underlying Iwasa/Morrow primary bodies were not freshly read in r117, and no full generalized proof was frozen or nominated. It identifies that scalar extension and averaging, rather than a new combinatorial construction, would do the work. Earlier r12/r14 already screened coefficient changes; actual r80 freezes a different, defining-characteristic all-degree growth target and leaves its upper bound open. No lower-bound-only or degree-one replacement is offered.

A more structural non-Abelian route was compared with [Oppenheim–Valentiner-Branth, arXiv:2510.18351v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18351v1), §3 and Theorems5.1–5.3.
