## Screen 178: negative-cycle extensions and relative high-girth towers

This idea was developed before any scope freeze. It is recorded as a possible classical consequence, not claimed new or substantial.
Let G be a finite simple bipartite graph of maximum degree d>=2. Fix signs on a union of vertex-disjoint cycles, with sign product -1 on each. Choose all other signs independently and fairly. Determinant expansion suggests
p(x)=sum_{S subset cycles} 2^{|S|} mu_{G minus V(S)}(x).
Every summand is positive for x>2sqrt(d-1); MSS Theorem5.1 supplies real-rootedness for arbitrary independent biased edge signs, and conditional interlacing selects a completion with largest signed eigenvalue <=2sqrt(d-1). Bipartiteness gives the matching lower bound. The fixed cycles double in the associated two-cover.
For a bipartite regular Ramanujan base, one could repeatedly kill the length-g lifts of each base shortest cycle (these lifts are vertex-disjoint), then move to the next girth. This preserves the exact spectral bound and suggests relative Ramanujan towers of diverging minimum girth. This is a proof sketch, not a verified packet; no quantitative size/girth claim is made.

## Closing disposition and complete retained argument

Parent concurs with the conservative author screen: no independent review is warranted for an author-identified direct determinant-expansion/interlacing reduction. No nomination; next fresh work is assigned r66. The following spells out the full argument contemplated, rather than promoting it to an independently checked theorem.

For the determinant identity, expand det(xI-A_s) by permutations. Fixed points contribute x; transpositions contribute -1 per edge. Any permutation cycle of length at least three containing a free edge has zero mean, since that edge occurs once and its sign is centered. The only remaining longer cycles lie wholly in the fixed edge subgraph, hence are members of the prescribed vertex-disjoint family. Each of their two orientations contributes minus its sign product, hence +1. After choosing a subset S of these cycles, all remaining nontrivial permutation components are transpositions, producing precisely the matching polynomial of the vertex-deleted graph. This proves the displayed identity. The empty vertex-deleted graph contributes mu=1.

Every matching polynomial in the sum is monic and has all roots at most R=2sqrt(d-1) by the classical matching-polynomial maximum-degree bound. Therefore every summand is strictly positive for real x>R. Independently, the expectation is real-rooted by MSS Theorem5.1, since fixed signs correspond to probabilities0 or1, not correlated conditioning. For any next free edge and every bias in [0,1], the conditional expected characteristic polynomial remains real-rooted by that same theorem. MSS's common-interlacing selection therefore fixes each remaining edge without increasing the largest root. Thus the completion's largest eigenvalue is at most R. A bipartite signed adjacency matrix has symmetric spectrum by conjugation with the diagonal matrix +1 on one side and -1 on the other, giving the lower bound -R. No claim is made for a nonbipartite two-sided extension.

For the relative tower, start with a finite connected simple bipartite d-regular Ramanujan graph, d>=3, and let g be its current girth. List its finitely many g-cycles. At each stage, over the next listed cycle, its inverse image in the current cover is a disjoint union of cycles whose lengths are multiples of g. Its length-g components are pairwise vertex-disjoint. Prescribe negative product on precisely those components and apply the extension argument. Their inverse images double in length. Covering maps cannot create a length-g cycle over an earlier eliminated base cycle: every length-g cycle in a cover projects to a cyclically reduced closed walk of length g in the original girth-g base; such a walk must itself be a simple g-cycle, and monodromy without fixed points cannot gain a fixed point after passing to a further cover. After the finite list, no g-cycles remain. Old eigenvalues are retained and all new ones lie in [-R,R]; bipartiteness and regularity persist. Since d>R, the multiplicity of eigenvalue d remains one, so every selected two-cover is connected. Repeat at each new girth. The tower has diverging girth and retains its cover of the original base. No effectiveness, optimal growth rate, logarithmic girth rate, or source-dependent substantiality is asserted.
