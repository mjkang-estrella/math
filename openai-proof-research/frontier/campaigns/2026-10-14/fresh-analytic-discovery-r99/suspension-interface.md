# What suspension actually supplies

The first assertion of its suspension proposition is genuinely nonhomogeneous: if u is a global minimizer on R^k, then u(x), independent of the extra coordinate, is a global minimizer on R^(k+1). Its proof integrates admissible Sobolev slice competitors and adds a nonnegative transverse derivative energy. The later spherical formula g_tilde(t,y)=sin(t)g(y) additionally requires u to be one-homogeneous.

The corresponding classical stability inequality also lifts nonhomogeneously for cylinders: apply the k-dimensional inequality to each slice of a compact test and add its transverse derivative energy. This moves a GIVEN field from dimension k to k+1. It cannot represent an arbitrary six-dimensional field as a cylinder. In particular, using the five-dimensional Euclidean tensor certificate on a six-dimensional cylinder gives only a subclass.

Restricting a general six-dimensional harmonic u to slices does not repair this: Δ_(x1,...,x5)u=-partial_66 u, so the five-dimensional Hessian is not trace-free unless partial_66u=0. Even that scalar condition does not give independence from x6, nor does it guarantee that the sliced free boundary has inward derivative1; the tangential gradient length is sqrt(1-(partial_6u)^2). Thus the exact source constraints fail before any tensor inequality is used.

A perspective homogenization U(x,s)=s u(x/s) on s>0 is one-homogeneous but does not preserve harmonicity. Writing y=x/s, direct differentiation gives

 Δ_(x,s) U = s^(-1)[Δu(y)+y^T D²u(y)y].

For harmonic u the second term remains. On u(y)=0, grad_(x,s)U=(grad u,-y·grad u), so the squared boundary gradient is1+(y·grad u)^2, generally not1. Therefore this operation does not produce an admissible Bernoulli cone. These are exact obstructions to the two proposed reductions, not a proof that no dimension-six mechanism exists.
