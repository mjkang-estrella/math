# Non-optimal cancellation: examined mechanisms and exact remaining obligation

These are bounded author-side working deductions, not a proposed retraction theorem.

The l1 nearest-path example excludes the natural rule that transports negative charges from available positive charges or an unlimited root reservoir at minimum cost, then retains unused positive charges. The root reservoir is necessary for stability: if all negative off-root charges must instead consume positive off-root charges, delta_a-delta_b with b very close to the root would map to zero despite being very close in KR to delta_a. This comparison has input distance h(b) and output distance h(a), so that forced-total-mass cancellation rule cannot be uniformly stable.

Keeping the signed height moment fixes that last defect but a naive global rule also fails. Consider

    R(nu)=[(sum h nu)_+/(sum h nu^+)] nu^+,

with value0 if the denominator is zero. It fixes positive measures and preserves the positive height moment. However, let the tree be a long interval rooted at0; take a at height H, and x,y at heights2H and2H+eta, respectively. Put nu=delta_a+delta_x-delta_y and mu=delta_a, with 0<eta<H. Then ||nu-mu||_KR=eta. The factor in R(nu) is (H-eta)/(3H), tending to1/3 as eta tends to0. Thus R(nu) tends to (delta_a+delta_x)/3, while R(mu)=delta_a. Writing c=(H-eta)/(3H), the output difference has flow2c-1 on[0,H] and c on[H,2H], and zero farther out. Its exact norm is H(1-c)=(2H+eta)/3. The output/input ratio therefore diverges as eta tends to0 with H fixed. This shows why cancellation must be spatially local as well as height-sensitive.

A harmonic hitting rule has a precise attraction: with a single absorbing positive atom a and an absorbing root, the chance of Brownian motion from b reaching a first is the height of the common ancestor divided by h(a). That ratio would suppress the far-root instability in a two-charge example. No Brownian theorem is imported or relied on here; the ratio is only the solution of the affine Kirchhoff boundary equations on the minimal three-point tree. Turning this into finite-capacity absorption at many positive atoms is not a proved map with a bound. The ordinary electrical obstacle/balayage minimization has quadratic edge-flow energy, and is consequently subject to the parent's fixed-p=2 projection obstruction. This identification can be checked directly. Let x be the signed edge flow, nu_v=x_v-sum_child x_u, and minimize half of sum_e w_e(q_e-x_e)^2 over K. The KKT potential is u_v=sum_(e in root-to-v) w_e(q_e-x_e), with u_root=0. Cone duality gives u>=0 and u_v mu_v=0, where mu_v=q_v-sum_child q_u>=0. The residual relation gives mu=nu+L u, where (L u)_v=(u_v-u_parent)/w_v+sum_child(u_v-u_child)/w_child. These are exactly the finite electrical obstacle conditions u>=0, nu+L u>=0 and complementarity; conversely they imply the quadratic projection KKT condition. Thus this particular obstacle/balayage operation is excluded by the parent's example. No equivalence for every conceivable stochastic cancellation algorithm is asserted.

A non-optimal rearrangement might remain viable. It must fix every positive vertex measure, accommodate arbitrary edge lengths and branching, and bound the joint change between any two signed inputs; bounds only on ||R(q)-q|| or only on ||R(q)|| are insufficient. No such globally controlled operation was derived in this bounded attack. No additional scope was frozen, and no restricted or technical result is nominated.
