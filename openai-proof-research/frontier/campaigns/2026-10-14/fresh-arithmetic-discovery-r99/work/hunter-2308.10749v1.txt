                                                  Abstract. Recently, Alweiss settled Hindman’s conjecture over the rationals. In
                                                  this paper, we provide our own exposition of Alweiss’ result, and show how to modify
                                                  his method to also show that sums of distinct products are partition regular over the
                                                  rationals.

                                        5. Overview

5.1. A digression into yet another Ramsey problem. We will start from a very
general perspective. We note that, despite the generality of this subsection, and we will
still end up needing to work with a modified version of the below (covered in Section 8),
thus the following should be viewed as a ‘toy model’ for what we will do later on.

  Let X be some ground set, and let P be a set of maps p : X → X, which is closed
under composition (i.e., p1 , p2 ∈ P =⇒ p1 ◦ p2 ∈ P). We will think of the elements
p ∈ P as being ‘perturbations’ of our ground set.

   Also, we will often write C to denote some collection of colorings of X. Given integer
r, we write C≤r to denote the set {C ∈ C : C(X) ⊂ [r]}.

     Now, let’s introduce two Ramsey properties.
Definition. Given a collection C of colorings of X and sets P, P ′ ⊂ P, we say P ′ →C P ,
if for every x ∈ X and C ∈ C, we can find p′ ∈ P ′ such that the set
                                     {p ◦ p′ (x) : p ∈ P }
is monochromatic under C.

  We say that a triple (X, P, C) is compactly Ramsey, if for every r < ∞ and finite
P ⊂ P, there exists a finite P ′ ⊂ P where
                                          P ′ →C≤r P.

without placing complex stipulations on C.

  In this section, we briefly show how to modify the work from before to prove Theo-

  To do this, we wish to prove the following version of the stable extension result

Lemma 9.1. Let X be the collection of all new n-family. Write Ω := newpairs(X).

  Then for every r < ∞ and finite Q ⊂ Q+ , there exists some finite Q′ ⊂ Q+ , so that
                                     n−1

  So, here is how to prove Lemma 9.1:
