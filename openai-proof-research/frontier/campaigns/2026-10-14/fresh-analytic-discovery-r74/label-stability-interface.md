# Local prediction under the original-field modification coupling

Live author interface. This note assumes the DH/reference-family bridge of
dh-limiting-coupling.md and the AH6–8 ordered-label construction, with its
AH8.6 use replaced by same-status-lowering.md. That complete construction has
not yet been independently checked at every proof step. The purpose here is
to specify the probabilistic replacement in the actual-read AH9.7–9.8 proof,
not to assert the entire structural theorem by changing its hypothesis label.

Let (s,s') be the cap fields obtained by fixed finite original resampling
above a threshold a, on max_F t<=a. Their marginals are absolutely continuous
with respect to the stationary cap law P_s, and they differ on a finite set.
The preceding reference-family argument gives coalescence of corresponding
old/new reference rays from every root. Uniform cap metric/Busemann estimates
then give AH9.7's unaffected cone: for some finite random translate u, at
every v in a fixed nondegenerate cone K+u, the two bounding reference rays
agree and the cap fields agree throughout the sector between them. This
uses the geometric proof of9.7, now with our established reference stability,
not a presumed arbitrary cap resampling property. The cone has a deterministic
positive asymptotic density kappa in large centered boxes.

Fix a deterministic extremal label alpha and its finite family of neighboring
extremal selectors F={G^-,G_alpha^cw,G_alpha^ccw,G^+} from AH9.8. For any
eta>0 choose a deterministic stub length n and prediction radius N so that
under P_s the following event Q(v) has probability at least1-eta:
(1) the distinct neighboring-label pairs have distinct n-step stubs, and
(2) all four n-step stubs are correctly predicted from cap coordinates
inside v+[-N,N]^2. This is exactly the finite measurable prediction step in
AH9.8. One can use a maximum conditional probability predictor, resolving
finite ties deterministically. There are finitely many n-step paths, so
martingale convergence as N grows makes its error tend to zero.

The crucial existence of a common good stub does not require the joint
old/new coupling to be translation invariant. Ergodicity of the single cap
law gives, on a P_s-full event, asymptotic spatial density of Q(v)^c at most
eta. BOTH marginals inherit this full event by absolute continuity. Hence
the union of their two bad sets has upper density at most2eta. Choose
eta<kappa/4. For all sufficiently large boxes, the unaffected cone contains
more sites than that union (discarding also the finite region whose radius-N
patch meets a changed cap edge). There is therefore a v in the cone where
both fields satisfy Q(v) and their prediction patches coincide. At this v,
all predicted stubs, and thus all actual stubs in F, coincide in both fields.
This replaces the informal common-good-site selection after AH9.8's displayed
probability>=1/2; it remains valid for our nonstationary dominated coupling.

The deterministic planar stub comparison in the actual-read AH9.8 proof can
now be applied in the unchanged reference sector. It bounds the new tail
label of G_alpha^cw below by every neighboring alpha_-<alpha and the new
label of G_alpha^ccw above by every alpha_+>alpha, with the boundary-equality
cases treated by the reference rays. The two extremal comparisons, using
countably many approximating labels as in that proof, preserve label alpha.
Once the AH9.9 limit-to-extremal-selector lemma is available, its geometric
proof and monotonicity give AH9.5(b) for this upward ORIGINAL resampling:
an entirely unchanged old extremal ray remains exactly the new selected ray.

Consequently the conditional selector premise in cubic-wall.md has a concrete

## Symmetry and the all-ray squeezing step in the unified assembly

The common-stub argument after reference tail coalescence is symmetric in the
two fields. Its hypotheses are: both marginals absolutely continuous to the
same cap law, finite cap difference, and corresponding reference rays sharing
tails. None requires that the modification be upward. Thus apply it also with
(s',s) in the opposite order. This does not assume the reversed coupling was
generated by an upward kernel; only the listed geometric/marginal facts are
used. It gives preservation of each fixed extremal label in BOTH directions.

There is a further quantifier step before using AH9.5(b). For any fixed alpha
in the deterministic extremal-label set, every old ray with label alpha lies
between its old clockwise-most and counterclockwise-most alpha rays in the
ordered reference sector. AH9.7's common sector gives a tail of every one of
these rays that is geodesic in the other field, simultaneously; this follows
from finite uniqueness in the unchanged region between the common reference
rays, not from an assertion that a cap ray reattaches to its old root.
The two bounding extremals have new label alpha by the stub argument. The
monotonicity of labels under planar order from AH6 then squeezes the new label
of every intermediate common ray tail to alpha. Apply the same reasoning
with the fields swapped. This is a pathwise statement for all alpha-rays
on the fixed-alpha full event; countable approximating labels used in the
stub proof are intersected first. There is no uncountable intersection of
fixed-alpha events.

Now the actual AH9.5(b) proof applies without a missing reverse-label premise.
An unchanged old extremal is a new ray by upward monotonicity and has label
alpha. If the new extremal differed, its common tail is an old ray of label
alpha by the REVERSED common-sector and squeeze statement. AH9.9 says that
old finite geodesics to its far tail vertices converge to the old extremal.
Consequently for sufficiently far targets those old minimizers agree with
the old extremal through the entire finite changed-cap region and beyond it.
They have unchanged costs under the upward modification: their initial
portion follows the unchanged old extremal, and after a fixed no-return box
they avoid the finite change set. The latter box exists from finite geodesic
uniqueness/coercivity for endpoints in the change box, as in the DH coupling.
Since the new extremal differs, finite uniqueness makes its old cost strictly
larger than that old minimizer whenever their finite paths differ. The new
cost of the extremal path is at least its old cost, while the competitor is
unchanged. This contradicts new minimality. Thus the entire selected ray is
preserved. This supplies the selector premise needed in the cubic wall.

## Tail existence under any finite metric change

Parallel author assistance supplied a useful independent justification for the
tail map just used. Let positive metrics s,s' differ on a finite edge set F,
let V contain its endpoints, and put Delta=sum_{e in F}|s'_e-s_e|. Fix root x
and C=max_{v in V}T_s(x,v), treating F empty as trivial. Let a,b occur in this
order on an old x-ray after its last visit to V. Its a-to-b segment is unchanged.
Any simple competitor from a to b which touches V has new cost at least

 T_s(x,a)+T_s(x,b)-2C-Delta
 =T_s(a,b)+2T_s(x,a)-2C-Delta.

Indeed split at first/last V contacts. Its old prefix and suffix costs are
bounded below by triangle inequalities using x, while its middle cost is
nonnegative. Changing a simple path's metric reduces its cost by at most
Delta. Once T_s(x,a)>C+Delta/2 this lower bound exceeds the unchanged ray
segment. Competitors avoiding V have identical costs and cannot improve the
old minimizing segment. Positive weights let all competitors be loop-erased,
so every sufficiently late ray tail is a new ray. Coercivity gives a finite
cutoff uniformly over all old rays from x; the finite set V is visited only
within a uniformly bounded geodesic prefix. Reverse the roles for the other
direction. This proves tail existence without selector stability, without
reattachment to the old root, and without identifying Busemann functions.
The reference sectors and common-stub argument remain necessary to identify
the labels of these common tails. The formula was checked algebraically and
its hypotheses are supplied by the cap finite-influence/coercivity lemmas.

## Isolated-label endpoint convention

The neighboring-label limit in the stub proof must include isolated labels.
The closed deterministic realized-label set is L_* by AH8.1. If alpha is
approached from below within the relevant reference interval, take a countable
deterministic sequence alpha_- increasing to alpha and use the strict lower
stub comparison. If alpha instead has an immediate predecessor beta there,
choose alpha_-=beta. The comparison gives NEW label>beta, and membership in
L_* (with no point between beta and alpha) implies NEW label>=alpha. If the
lower bounding reference itself has label alpha, use reference equality as
in AH9.8. The upper bound uses an approaching sequence or immediate successor
symmetrically. Thus no nonexistent sequence is assumed at a left/right-isolated
point. The parallel structural author assistance identified this convention;
it does not change the scope or require a new probabilistic hypothesis.
