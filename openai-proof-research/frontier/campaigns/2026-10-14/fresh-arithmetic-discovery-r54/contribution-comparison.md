The classical existence result for odd denominators is credited to Breusch and Stewart, as reported by [Webb1965](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8415D2E364249D16B781F0731569F434/S0008414X00039924a.pdf/sums-of-rational-numbers.pdf)'s introduction. These are existence results, not new assertions of this packet. Their originals/proofs were not all read; the precise reading boundary is recorded separately.

[Elsholtz2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.02117) Theorem1.1 concerns counting expansions of1 with denominators congruent to ±1 modulo a fixed squarefree P. Its proof uses fixed congruence templates, exceptional-case collision repairs, and binary construction of a denominator P^t-1. Its lower counting exponent has k/log k. Congruence-based separation and digit constructions therefore have explicit prior credit; neither is claimed as a new idea in isolation. Its operation does not provide the all-a/b length theorem here.

PNT prime supply, Erdős–Turán discrepancy, additive orthogonality, unique-factorization atom bounds, Markov, Hölder, and elementary greedy estimates are classical. Extending the source random-product range from log v<=S to2S is a checked parameter extension, not a new cancellation theorem. The explicit base5 digit identities are elementary support, not an independently substantial theorem.
