## Integral local calculation

For one 2 by 4 coefficient matrix, assume exactly columns 1 and 2 become dependent, every other pair remains independent, and the determinant crosses zero transversely. Row operations put the special equations in the form a*z1^2+b*z2^2+c*z3^2+d*z4^2=0 and e*z3^2+f*z4^2=0, with a,b,e,f nonzero. The second equation splits into two planes; on each, the first is a smooth conic. They meet in the two distinct points z3=z4=0, a*z1^2+b*z2^2=0. The total surface on a transverse base disc is smooth: at either node the transverse parameter has nonzero derivative in the missing equation. Thus this is a reduced two-node I2 degeneration, not an irreducible one-node degeneration.

A primitive cycle delta of the elliptic fibre contracts at either node. The two parallel vanishing cycles give monodromy T_delta^2. In a symplectic basis (delta,eta), (T_delta^2-I)H1 = 2Z*delta, while the actual vanishing thimble kills Z*delta. The quotient by monodromy relations alone retains Z/2; substituting it for the actual topological kernel loses an integral relation. Rational completion cannot see this distinction.

Shimada arXiv:0906.1074v1 explicitly assumes irreducible codimension-one fibres for its monodromy-relations theorem. His Example 3.5 (xy:C^2->C) shows why a reducible fibre can kill loops with trivial monodromy. That theorem cannot simply be applied to these I2 fibres.

## Stronger curve reduction under examination

Choose a sufficiently ample smooth complete-intersection curve C in X of genus at least two, transverse to D and each one-factor determinant discriminant, missing all codimension-two bad loci and all intersections between those discriminants. Over the root orbicurve R_C, each one-factor total space S_j is a smooth compact surface, and all singular fibres away from D have the I2 local model above. The order-two action on each smooth genus-one fibre over D is free: its fixed loci in P3 are the first and last coordinate lines, avoided when the corresponding pair determinants are nonzero.

On P(O^2+T^2)|R_C, choose positive orbifold metrics for a high base twist and the relative hyperplane bundle. Two successive quadric divisors are positive hypersurfaces in complex dimensions four and three. The actual stack Lefschetz theorem of Halpern-Leistner, arXiv:1008.0891v1, Corollary 2.7, gives pi1(S_j)=pi1(R_C), once smoothness and positivity of these particular successive cuts are checked. This is integral stack homotopy, not coarse-space homotopy. A high-genus root orbicurve admits a finite torsion-free manifold cover C'; base-changing gives the same pi1 isomorphism over the aspherical curve C'.

The remaining concrete topological calculation is to express the full kernel of pi1(E_j)->pi1(S_j') as the subgroup generated by primitive local vanishing cycles, using the contractible universal cover of C', and then to lift every killing disc to the four-factor product: over a disc with a singular j-th factor, the other three factors are smooth bundles and admit sections. This avoids any unsupported global section of the genus-one torsor. The base has pi2=0, so no sphere-boundary relation is available to substitute for a missing local vanishing relation.

For the full map Y->R, the source generic-divisor submersion witnesses should give the orbifold Nori exact sequence pi1(F)->pi1(Y)->pi1(R)->1 by meridian normal generation. Over a common smooth open, this is the fibre-bundle exact sequence. At an ordinary discriminant, a local section kills its meridian; at D a root-chart local section kills the square of its coarse meridian. Every other killed upstairs meridian already maps trivially in pi1(R) because the map factors through R. If all eight elliptic fibre generators die in the restricted product over C, they die in Y.

## Other routes and their limits

Each quadric line on the product of four P3 bundles is pulled back from just one factor and the base. It is not ample on the full product. A naive repetition of ordinary ample-hyperplane Lefschetz is invalid. Counting derivative ranks after three preceding quadric pairs can yield rank zero of the previous projection at isolated points; a single pulled-back metric need not have the three positive directions needed for pi1 isomorphism at every point. This does not rule out a different metric or a stratified/semiample theorem.

An algebraic fibre-dimension approach is stronger than that naive curvature test: for a generic 2 by 4 matrix, rank<=1 has codimension three and rank zero codimension eight. On a fourfold, at most one of three independent factors has a dimension-two fibre, so their product fibres have dimension at most four. A suitable semiample Lefschetz theorem might exploit this. No unverified stack extension of such a theorem is assumed.

The representation theorem for special open varieties gives class-two complex linear images and a rational completion calculation, not an integral identification of this kernel, and is not used to assert one. The full compact theorem and the root-pair theorem already give virtual abelianity; that paid output is not a new target.

## Feasibility closure before freezing

The local-to-global integral interface can be filled by a disc exhaustion, without a global section. Pull a one-factor surface back to the universal cover of a finite manifold cover C' of R_C. The base is an open disc. Over a compact disc with regular boundary, choose disjoint discs about the finitely many critical values and a tree of paths to a regular base point. Outside disjoint node balls the family is a proper submersion with boundary. Its trivialization along the tree, combined with the local model xy=t inside each node ball, gives the regular fibre with one 2-cell per vanishing circle for the purpose of pi1. For two nodes at the same value their balls are disjoint and both cells attach; no irreducibility assumption is used. Enlarging discs exhausts the base, and every null-homotopy has compact image. Consequently the fibre kernel is exactly the normal closure of all transported primitive vanishing cycles. This is the actual local-disc mechanism in ABKP, arXiv:math/9810042v2, Section 2 and the proof of Lemma 3.2; the present use is over a disc, not their sphere with a section.

Since stack Lefschetz identifies pi1(S_j') with pi1(C'), that kernel is all of Z^2. Each of these killing discs lifts to the fourfold fibre product, keeping the other three fibre coordinates along sections over the vanishing disc. Along the chosen path transport those coordinates arbitrarily by their smooth bundles. Hence all four Z^2 summands of pi1(F)=Z^8 die in the product and in Y. The orbifold meridian exact sequence described above then gives the desired isomorphism.

A finite manifold cover of R_C can be constructed explicitly. Write its group relation as product of handle commutators times gamma_1...gamma_m=1. If m is even, send every gamma_i to the nonidentity of C2 and handles to identity. If m is odd, put c=(12)(34), a=(34), b=(13)(24); with rightmost permutation acting first, [a,b]=c. Send every gamma_i to c, first handle to a,b, others to identity, and take the regular covering for the image subgroup. Every marked meridian has exact order two. Filling the punctured covering by local maps w->w^2 produces a smooth compact curve C' and an unramified representable Galois cover C'->R_C. Riemann-Hurwitz gives genus(C')>=2. This avoids an assumed finite manifold uniformization in higher dimension.

For positivity, choose C first; choose the base twist M sufficiently high afterwards. On the finite cover C', the quadric line O_P(V)(2) tensor M has a positive Hermitian metric: relative Fubini-Study is positive vertically and a sufficiently positive base metric dominates bounded horizontal and mixed curvature. Average the logarithms of the metrics over the deck group. The resulting invariant positive curvature descends to the root-stack projective bundle. Its restrictions to the smooth first quadric remain positive. Stack Lefschetz is applied in ambient dimensions four and then three, and both maps on pi1 are isomorphisms.

Genericity is imposed on this one fixed curve as well as the source global construction. Sufficiently high M makes all coefficient restrictions separate the finite jets needed on C. A general tuple has no zero columns or simultaneous pair-minor zeros on C, is transverse to the smooth rank-loss hypersurfaces, has no cross-factor discriminant intersections on C, and avoids every determinant zero at the finitely many marked points. First-row coefficient zeros are simple and disjoint, which makes each first quadric smooth. All are open generic conditions compatible with the source open conditions. Thus the preceding reductions do not require choosing an impossible curve through a fixed bad set.
