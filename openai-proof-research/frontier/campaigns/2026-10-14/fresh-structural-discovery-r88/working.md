## Concrete obstruction to the naive implication
Let Omega=(lambda z:s.z z)(lambda z:s.z z), and E=lambda x:Omega.f x, with x absent from f. E eta-contracts to the beta-eta normal form f. Every beta reduction from E only repeats the self-reducing Omega annotation, so E has no beta normal form. This is a raw-syntax example, not a well-typed counterexample to the target. It rigorously blocks pointwise WN(beta-eta) => WN(beta) on annotated syntax. The system-wide typing hypothesis would have to supply a new argument.

## Source exclusion attempt
Source erasure.tex constructs a labelled proof-mode P:F in a data/proof context, and proves that no beta-normal erasure at any terminal or constant positive telescope can retain proof mode. The argument is a minimal-syntax descent: a proof lambda descends into its body under a data binder; a neutral proof spine must start with a converter and descends into its first proof argument. The source then lifts a beta normalization path preserving the exact labelled type and mode.

A beta-eta normal form is beta normal, so the final syntactic contradiction would still apply IF every erased beta-eta normalization path lifted with a legal labelled judgment at the same exact type. Beta steps are paid. Eta steps preserve the evident result mode syntactically when lambda/application mode labels agree, but typed lifting also needs removal of the discharged variable and conversion of the surviving function type. Its displayed inferred type can depend on that variable through conversion even when the term does not. Barthe Proposition2 proves the needed strengthening only under WN(beta), the missing hypothesis. Merely defining an eta step on the labelled syntax is not a proof of typed lifting.

Even resolving this exclusion obligation would not finish the target: source profiles.tex uses unique beta normal forms and beta-conversion compatibility to build candidates; extensional derivations can use additional conversion judgments. A candidate model respecting beta-eta equality and full annotated reductions is still needed. No claim that replacing beta by beta-eta throughout source245 is valid.

## Persistent-wrapper test (after freeze)
Actual source wrappers.tex1–240 read. Its positive edge for (I,J,K) sends T at J to Pi z:D_I.T and carries t by lambda z:D_I.t, projected by application to e_I. This preserves a payload by beta retraction, but it does not force normalization of every annotation inside t: the original eta contraction remains available inside t. Negative-edge c t uses a neutral converter and preserves t as a syntactic argument, but again eta may discard an annotation internal to t. Therefore an outer wrapper alone does not address the concrete E example. A recursive annotation-persistent transformation would need to move each binder domain into a nonerasable neutral argument.

There is a precise typing cost: to introduce a fresh tester c:Pi a:s.F accepting an annotation A:s, the type of s must itself be available (an axiom s:t) and some product rule (t,u,v) must be available for F:u. The original typing A:s does not guarantee either condition. Even when lambda x:A.M is legal, its formation only supplies a rule (s,r,q) for its codomain, which is a different requirement. Thus this syntactic tester is not justified uniformly over arbitrary specifications. The source's graph-directed wrappers instead require specific feasible triples and paths; they cannot be applied as arbitrary universe-level testers without proving those interfaces. This is an obstruction to this implementation, not a counterexample to the full frozen conjecture.

The missing tester premises occur in an explicit legal specification: sorts {a,s}, axiom a:s, and sole rule (s,s,s). The term lambda x:a.x is legal at Pi x:a.a, but s has no sort by generation, so Pi z:s.F cannot be formed for any F. This witnesses failure of the proposed universe-level tester construction under the full target's quantifiers. It is not a nonnormalizing system or a counterexample to BGK.

## Profile-level alternative
Actual profiles.tex1–180 read after freeze. The feasible-profile closure itself is finite and combinatorial: start with Ax(s) and singleton {s} for Ax(s) nonempty, then close under nonempty Out(I,J). But its completeness for legal types uses reduction to a *legal* normal form, together with subject reduction and context conversion. For the extensional target Barthe gives uniqueness of legal mixed normal forms independent of WN, so a tempting replacement is to define profiles using mixed normal forms. This still requires that an arbitrary raw mixed normalizing path reaches a legal term with the recorded sort, and that typed substitution preserves such profiles. Both require the eta subject-reduction/strengthening bridge already isolated. The finite graph alone does not bypass it.

A precise sufficient intermediate problem is: under system-wide WN(beta-eta), prove eta subject reduction at the exact expected type in every valid context, for arbitrary specifications. Then mixed normal forms would be legal and unique by Barthe, permitting an actual re-check of source profile generation/growth rather than a formal replacement of symbols. Even that lemma would not by itself prove the full candidate theorem: candidate application tests and substitution interpretation must respect eta conversion. No assertion that this sufficient intermediate is necessary, proved, or separately nominatible.

## Parent refinement: unsorted-sort descent (before final seal)
Parent correctly observes that the tester counterexample has no divergent annotation. The following limited reduction addresses that point without assuming eta subject reduction. Use the extensional generation, correctness-of-types and product-compatibility statements already read in Barthe CSL98. If Gamma proves A:s and Ax(s) is empty, A cannot be a variable, lambda or application: correctness of types for these three outer forms gives a sorting of its expected literal sort s, and generation at s then gives an axiom out of s. Thus A is a literal sort or a product. A literal sort is beta normal. For a product Pi x:D.E, raw beta weak normalization is equivalent to beta weak normalization of both D and E: beta cannot alter its outer constructor; projected component paths give necessity, and concatenating normalizing component paths gives sufficiency. Product generation supplies legal sort judgments for each component (in the appropriate context). Therefore, if any legal sorted expression fails beta weak normalization, choose one of least syntax size over all contexts. Its sort must itself have a sort. This descent uses beta-conversion nowhere and eta subject reduction nowhere. It does not prove that the chosen minimal expression is weakly beta normalizing.

This improves the search: a minimal counterexample can be sought at a typable sort. However a typable sort alone does not license a neutral universe-level tester. Example sorts{a,s,t}, axioms(a,s),(s,t), sole product rule(s,s,s): s is typable, lambda x:a.x is legal, but Pi z:s.F cannot be formed because no product rule has first coordinate t. Again this system supplies no divergent annotation. One would need to derive the missing tester rules from the existence of a minimal non-beta-normalizing legal expression, or a different source-graph construction. No such derivation has been supplied.

The supporting product Pi x:A.B of an existing lambda is a legal persistent occurrence of A requiring no additional product rule. A raw beta-eta normalization of this product projects to beta-eta normalizations of A and B, since neither root beta nor root eta can contract a product. This recovers mixed normalization only, already assumed globally. It does not turn either component's normalization into beta normalization; using it as an induction step would require a decreasing measure across beta substitution and subsequent newly created annotations. Syntax size of arbitrary later annotations can increase, so no such induction has been established.
