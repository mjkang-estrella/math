## Feasible conclusion

d_F(R)=5, while d_ZG(R/[R,R])=4. Moreover Def(G)=0, where deficiency
is the maximum number of generators minus relators over finite presentations.

The source identity s t2 s^-1=[t1,a1] shows K=<<t1>> already. Thus the
four old relators and t1 normally generate R, giving a balanced presentation
of G and d_F(R)≤5.

The source character is nontrivial and its four-by-five Fox matrix has
rank four. Hence the presentation complex B has H0(B;Cρ)=H1(B;Cρ)=0:
the first boundary has rank one and the second fills its four-dimensional
kernel. The source finite-plus proposition preserves this twisted homology
in X with π1(X)=G. Twisted H1 depends only on π1 (attach cells in dimensions
≥3 to form a K(G,1)); therefore H1(G;Cρ)=0.

For every finite presentation complex Y of G, H0(Y;Cρ)=H1(Y;Cρ)=0.
The source Characters and torsion corollary forces H2(Y;Cρ)≠0, since x1
has order two and ρ(x1)=-1. Thus χ(Y)=dim H2(Y;Cρ)≥1. Every finite
presentation has generators minus relators at most zero. The displayed
balanced presentation realizes zero, proving Def(G)=0. In particular R
cannot be normally generated by four elements in F5, so d_F(R)=5.

Since R/R0=K is perfect, R=R0[R,R]. The four old relators therefore
generate R_ab as a ZG-module. Conversely, the Cayley-graph relation sequence
0→R_ab→ZG^5→ZG→Z→0 shows that any m module generators extend to a
partial free resolution with degree-two term ZG^m. Tensor with the right
module Cρ. Its first homology is H1(G;Cρ)=0 and the degree-one boundary
has rank one. The image from C^m must fill a four-dimensional kernel,
so m≥4. This establishes the exact module-generator count.
