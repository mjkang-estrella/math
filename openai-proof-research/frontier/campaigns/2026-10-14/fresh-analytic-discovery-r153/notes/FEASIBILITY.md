## First strongest reduction: do not lift to a large cover

The first idea was to remove simple connectivity from the L2 Einstein gap. Lifting the defect would multiply its squared norm by the covering degree, suggesting Gromov Betti bounds and equivariant smooth-limit conjugacy. That is needlessly indirect. EG's actual proof uses simple connectivity in its first paragraph to deduce orientability and chi=2+b2>=2, then again only after the compact convergence embeddings become diffeomorphisms. The estimates between those points work on the base manifold once chi>=1. For a nonorientable manifold the integrated Euler density or the orientation double cover gives the same dimensional bound. A positive-Einstein limit on the base is already a quotient of one of the three models. Thus no deck-group restoration is unpaid here. Gromov and Grove–Karcher were searched before this simplification, but are not needed dependencies.

Finite pi1 in dimension four implies chi>0: the finite simply connected cover has chi=2+b2>0, and Euler characteristic multiplies by covering degree. The stronger natural input is chi(M)>0. No metric-distance rate is asserted; actual original I30 already treated the time-zero metric question, and its full proof was read.

## Second reduction: negative sectional curvature in the critical norm

Let F_g=max(0,-min sec_g), eta=||Ric-Rg/4||_2, sigma=||F_g||_2. Both eta and sigma are invariant under constant rescaling in dimension four. Consider closed connected smooth four-manifolds with Ric_g>=0 and chi(M)>0, and eta+sigma tending to zero. These are exploratory inputs, not a frozen author claim.

For any orthonormal pair, complete it to a four-frame. Since R/2 is the sum of its six sectional curvatures and each is >=-F, every sectional value is <=R/2+5F. With R>=0, equivalence of algebraic curvature norms gives |Rm|<=A(R+F). If R were identically zero, Ric>=0 and eta=0, but the curvature inequality and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet would force chi<=C sigma^2, impossible for small sigma. Thus rescale average scalar to 12.

EG's actual Bianchi–Bochner proof, which requires initial Ric>=0 but not sec>=0, gives ||R-12||_2<=4 eta and ||Ric-3g||_2^2<=5 eta^2. Consequently

  1 <= chi(M) <= C integral |Rm|^2 <= C(144 Vol + 16 eta^2 + sigma^2).

This provides a uniform positive lower volume. The same segment/index-form proof then bounds diameter, hence upper volume, and the same support/truncation argument gives a uniform Sobolev inequality. No fundamental-group order bound is used.

Choose K0=12A (enlarging the fixed algebraic A if necessary). The initial high tail obeys

  (|Rm|-K0)_+ <= A(|R-12|+F),
  ||(|Rm|-K0)_+||_2 <= A(4 eta+sigma).

EG's tail propagation, entropy, noncollapse and common-lifespan proof therefore use exactly their original small parameter, now eta+sigma. Initial R>=0 persists, so there is still no adverse volume term. The homogeneous Einstein-defect equation propagates eta separately. Finally its convex negative-sectional calculation now gives

  integral F(Rm(t)) <= integral F(Rm(0)) + Ct <= V0^(1/2) sigma + Ct.

At each fixed t>0 pass to the compact smooth limit, then let t down to zero in

  (1-6t) integral F(Rm_h) <= Ct.

The limit is a nonnegatively curved Einstein metric on a manifold diffeomorphic to the base. The apparent hypothesis-removal operation is entirely paid by the actual EG proof; it is not evidence of a substantial contribution.
