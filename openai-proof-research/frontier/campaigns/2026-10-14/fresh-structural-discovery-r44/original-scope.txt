# Original scope — leaf-sensitive Euclidean tree signing

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

There exists an absolute finite constant C such that, for every integer d>=1,
every finite nonempty rooted tree T, and every assignment v_t in R^d to its
vertices satisfying ||v_t||_2<=1, there is ONE signing epsilon:V(T)->{-1,1}
with

  max_{u in V(T)} ||sum_{t on the root-to-u path} epsilon_t v_t||_2
      <= C sqrt(d log(1+L)),

where L is the number of rooted leaves (vertices with no children), log is the
natural logarithm, and each path includes both endpoints. C is independent of
d, number of vertices, depths, degrees, unary-chain lengths, L, and all input
vectors. Zero and repeated vectors are allowed, with distinct vertex signs.
For an empty tree or d=0 the corresponding sum and bound are zero. No zero-sum,
arithmetic, bounded-height, binary-tree, genericity, or nonzero-vector premise
is added. This is an existential offline theorem: no efficient algorithm,
online choice, optimal asymptotic dependence on both d and L, or matching
lower bound is claimed.

Scope chronology: recorded after exploratory chain-law calculations and the
parent's heavy-light assembly suggestion, before writing a complete proof or
claiming a completed candidate. The intermediate prefix law alone was treated
as technical support. The tree statement was not an earlier frozen target.
The prior r4 target requires one signing for TWO arbitrary orders of the SAME
indices with bound C sqrt(d); that unresolved target is preserved unchanged.
Tree vertices have one parent and their signs are not identified across
incomparable branches. This is a different incidence system, not a restriction
or a claimed solution of r4. No target was frozen in the preliminary157/184
DP-coloring screen; that screen supplies no nominee here.
