# Live feasibility: nonresolvable ingredients for full LR

No exact theorem has become feasible; no freeze. The full existing LR question is retained verbatim in scope: one finite r-partite r-uniform nonempty SET of edges, r>=2, every distinct pair meeting in exactly one vertex, and every set of at most r-1 vertices missing an edge. No rank/degree/field/construction restriction is added to the target; all old scopes/dispositions remain unchanged.

The old synchronized resolvable-TD inflation pays all pair agreements but has the mixed cover c_out<=m*c_master<=ma=R-b<R. Simply changing class names or schedules cannot remove that cover because cover membership ignores resolution colors. A genuinely different ingredient is a TD(k,m) with no parallel class: its local covering number can exceed m. Such ingredients are classical, e.g. the affine-plane dual TD(m+1,m), whose blocks are pairwise intersecting and hence admit no parallel class for m>1. This removes the premise of the old m-fold cover bound, not the global cover obligation.

Exploratory calculation (not yet a nominee): take an affine-plane master of order q, m points per fiber, one TD(q,m) per master line, and the whole fibers as internal blocks. Pair coverage is exact regardless of how blocks might be globally resolved; every point occurs in R=m(q+1)+1 blocks. The ingredients need not be locally resolvable. Global resolutions could mix blocks from different master directions and internal fibers. Even if such a resolution exists, total block counts may force a class with <R blocks, independently of the old synchronized-cover argument. Need carry this through exactly and compare against actual primary incidence-count bounds before drafting a conclusion.

The most concentrated candidate uses q=m+1 (both prime powers) and local TD(m+1,m) from the smaller affine plane. A tentative analysis of pure cross classes shows their projections omit two parallel master classes; these classes themselves contain mq<R blocks. Rather than declaring failure from this restricted configuration, the next step is a general incidence/class-count argument that allows arbitrary local TDs, arbitrary global resolution, and compatible internal designs. If that broader argument closes, it is classical supporting analysis, not a full LR refutation or contribution evidence.

## Further calculations before extended drafting

Primary FHMW §§2.1 proof and Stinson's entire six-page version now read. Stinson Lemma2.5 expressly gives nonresolvable TD(m+1,m), and Theorem2.4 assembles one parallel class from locally disjoint blocks even when whole local resolutions are absent. Thus lack of a local resolution alone is not an unpaid primitive. The search must include compatible partial-class assembly.

General candidate architecture: master regular PBD on v points, replication a, no singleton blocks, each master block B filled with TD(|B|,m), and internal regular PBDs on fibers of size m, all with replication b. Each output point has R=ma+b incidences. Pair coverage is automatic; a global resolution is not. Let B_M be the number of master blocks and I_z the number of internal blocks. The total output block count is m^2 B_M+sum_z I_z. A necessary condition for any resolution with no class cover of size<R is m^2 B_M+sum I_z>=R^2. This uses all classes, independently of local synchronization.

A second compatible operation is the local TD star. Fix a fiber point x over master point z. In each master block through z, the m TD blocks through x cover all other fibers in that master block. The union of these ma blocks covers every output point except F_z minus{x}. Consequently any internal cover of that residual by <=b-1 blocks yields an output (R-1)-cover, with no global resolution assumption. The correct residual internal-cover number, not just internal minimum degree, must be retained.

Example screen within this SAME family: projective-plane master of order m=13 and internal STS(13), b=6. Raw counts give R=188, N=2379,30927 cross blocks and4758 internal triples, total35685>R^2=35344. Formal class sizes could be157 classes with165 cross+23 internal blocks and31 with162 cross+37 internal. These are only integer margins; no realized resolution or lower-cover proof. The internal block-cover operation can still defeat this model, so these margins are not a candidate.

Replacing the internal STS by a projective plane (e.g. m=13,b=4) keeps its internal residual cover at b but loses the total-block inequality. More irregular internal designs can be made by restricting the point set of a larger projective plane; their incidence replication stays b although block sizes vary. A sufficiently dense subset may retain internal cover b. This is a plausible ingredient mechanism, not a proved complete construction here.

A coherent geometric outer choice is also paid: PG(3,m) minus a point P has fibers the m surviving points on each line through P; master is PG(2,m), and non-P lines in each plane through P give TD(m+1,m). Any projective line spread has one line through P; its m^2 other lines cover everything outside that fiber. Filling the hole with <=b internal blocks gives m^2+b < R=m^2+m+b. Need write and verify the classical field-extension spread construction explicitly. Hence even arbitrary internal replacements cannot repair coherent PG(3,m) cross blocks. Independent local TD identifications might destroy that global partial spread, but they also remove the available resolution mechanism; no conclusion for all such identifications follows.
