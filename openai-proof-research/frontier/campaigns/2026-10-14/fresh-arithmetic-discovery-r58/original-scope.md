# T001U58: one integral multiplier at every good reduction

Full target frozen after source/companion and initial primary feasibility reading, before assembling the proof attempts below. Actual UTC: 2026-10-18T18:06:15.147459+00:00. This is a research question, not a theorem claim.

Fix a number field K with embedding K into C, an abelian variety A/K of dimension g, an integer 0<=r<=g, and a nonempty open S of Spec O_K over which A extends to an abelian scheme. Put L=H^{2r}(A(C),Z(r)) intersect H^{r,r}; this is the integral Hodge lattice. The target is:

There exists one positive integer N=N(A/K,r,S), such that for every finite place v in S, every extension w of v to Qbar, and every gamma in L, there is an integral algebraic cycle z_{v,w,gamma} in CH^r(A_{overline{k(v)}}) whose class is N times the absolute-Hodge specialization of gamma in every prime-to-p rational etale realization and in crystalline cohomology with p inverted and scalar extension to C_w, with p=char k(v). One and the same integral cycle gives all these realizations.

No bound on its degree, field of definition, effectivity or support is required. The integer N is independent of v,w,gamma. It need not be effective or uniform over A. Cycles are signed integral cycles, not necessarily effective. All residue characteristics, including 2, remain in scope. Dependence on a finite enlargement of K is harmless but cannot discard infinitely many places.

The stipulated source001 plus032 pays a rational cycle separately at each place, hence a positive integer N_v separately at each place after taking a finite integral Hodge basis. It does not explicitly supply one N for all places. The full uniform claim is the only target. Neither pointwise N_v, the CM case, divisorial cases, a finite set of places, nor a bound conditional on unknown uniform saturation is a substitute or nominee.
