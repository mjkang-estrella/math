## Complete ordinary amplification — technical only

Put E={1}∪S∪S^{-1}, λ=1+β/2, and choose an integer k with λ^k≥2. For finite nonempty A, a maximizing s satisfies |sA\A|=|sA symmetric_difference A|/2≥β|A|/2, because |sA|=|A|. Thus |EA|≥λ|A|. Iteration gives |E^k A|≥2|A| for every finite A.

Form a bipartite graph with left vertices F×{1,2}, right vertices F, and neighbors of (g,i) equal to E^k g. For a finite set of left vertices let A be its projection to F. Its neighbor set has size≥2|A|, while the left set has size≤2|A|. The locally finite Hall theorem therefore gives an injection j:F×{1,2}→F with j(g,i)=w(g,i)g and w(g,i)∈E^k.

For i∈{1,2} and w∈E^k, put P_{i,w}={j(g,i):w(g,i)=w}. These sets are pairwise disjoint and F is the disjoint union of w^{-1}P_{i,w} for each fixed i. Consequently there is a paradoxical decomposition with at most2|E^k|≤2|E|^k pieces (empty pieces may be deleted). This gives no five- or six-piece bound. It is an ordinary application of Hall to the already-paid expansion, not a proposed new contribution.

The colored Hall operation used here is classical; the primary comparison was Ershov–Golan–Sapir, arXiv1401.2202v4, Lemma2.5 and Theorem2.6 with their proofs. Their Theorem2 also explicitly prevents inferring a uniform Tarski bound from two-generation plus nonamenability. The proof of that theorem was not read in this screen.

## Exact obstruction to the inspected five-piece template

Golan, arXiv1406.2097v1, Proposition2.2 uses a three-generator group, an infinite-order first generator, and group cost at least5/2 to construct translating sets of sizes2 and3. Its forest proof supplies simultaneous finite-set expansion in two colors. The proposition and proof were read in full, together with the rest of this short paper. This sufficient criterion is unavailable for F, for the following elementary reason.

Cannon–Floyd–Parry, Corollary2.6, states that F is generated by two elements A,B. For ANY probability-measure-preserving free action of a two-generated countable group G=<a,b>, take the graphing consisting of the two transformations x↦ax and x↦bx, each defined on the entire probability space. Its connected components are exactly the G-orbits: words in the generators and their inverses give paths, and every edge stays in an orbit. Its cost is1+1=2. The infimum over graphings is therefore at most2 for every such action, and the infimum defining group cost is at most2. Free probability actions exist, for example the Bernoulli action for countably infinite groups; finite groups also have their free action with uniform probability.

Every quotient of F is still generated by the images of A,B. Thus no quotient of F can satisfy cost≥5/2 either. Adding a redundant third generator does not alter this bound. This is NOT a proof that F lacks five-piece decompositions; a different construction could establish them. It also does not exclude applying a cost argument to some subgroup, whose generator number need not be two. No such subgroup construction was supplied here.

## Remaining interface, not a frozen target

To use five total translations one needs specific sets S1,S2 of sizes2 and3 and the two-color Hall condition for every pair of finite sets A1,A2, with consistent left/right conventions. The source controls a maximum boundary over a much larger transport set for a single A. The amplification above loses all small-support control. I found no construction recovering that missing condition from the recursive colors. Neither failure of the cost criterion nor failure to find such a construction is an impossibility theorem.
