Let p be odd, let L be a d-dimensional F_p vector space and let beta:Lambda²L→L be alternating bilinear. Choose a basis and put V=(Z/p²)^d. Define

    x*y = x+y+(p/2) lift(beta(x mod p,y mod p)).

Here p/2 means p times the inverse of2 in F_p; after multiplication by p the chosen lift is irrelevant. Bilinearity modulo p proves associativity: the added term is central and its reduction modulo p is0, so the two parenthesizations have identical cocycle sums. Identity is0, inverse is−x, and x raised to the pth group power is px since beta(x,x)=0. Commutators are p beta(x mod p,y mod p), with the sign determined by the chosen commutator convention but immaterial to the subspace arguments. Hence the group has order p^(2d), exponent p² and class at most2. Its subgroup Z0=pV is central elementary abelian of rank d. All elements of order dividing p lie in Z0. If beta has no common radical then Z(G)=Z0; the bookkeeping below requires only the distinguished subgroup Z0, not that equality.

For H≤G let U be its image in G/Z0=L and let W=H∩Z0, identified with a subspace of L through the pth-power map. Then U⊆W, H^p corresponds exactly to U, and |H|=p^(dim U+dim W). Indeed the pth-power map is a homomorphism because the group has odd p and commutators of exponent p. Its image on H depends exactly on the projection U, and is generated by the individual pth powers. In particular d(H)=log_p|H/Φ(H)|≤log_p|H/H^p|=dim W≤d. Every quotient of H needs at most d generators, while Z0 itself is elementary abelian of rank d. Thus the sectional rank is exactly d.

Consider an arbitrary cyclic subnormal chain from1 toG, omitting trivial factors. Each factor has order p or p², because G has exponent p². If there are s factors of order p² and t of order p, then 2s+t=2d and the length is2d−s.

For a p² step K normal in H with H/K cyclic, choose g whose coset generates H/K. Since g^p∉K, the image of g in L is outside the image of K: otherwise g=kz with k∈K,z∈Z0, and g^p=k^p∈K. Thus U increases by1 at that step. The total order increase is p², so W also increases by1. This argument applies to arbitrary subnormal chains, with no global normality or central-chain restriction.

At the last p² step K normal in H, the subspace W_K is proper, since dim W_H=dim W_K+1≤d. Choose a nonzero linear functional f annihilating W_K. Because H/K is cyclic, [H,H]⊆K; those commutators are in Z0, hence beta(U_H,U_H)⊆W_K. Thus U_H is totally isotropic for the alternating form f∘beta. Also dim U_H≥s, since every earlier p² step increments U. If every nonzero f gives rank at least2r, every such isotropic subspace has dimension at most d−r. Therefore s≤d−r and every cyclic subnormal chain has length at least d+r. If s=0 its length is2d, which already gives that inequality.

For the proposed family L=F_(p^d), d odd and at least3, take beta(x,y)=x y^p−x^p y. Every linear functional is f_a(z)=Tr(a z), with a≠0 for a nonzero functional. Trace duality gives radical condition

    (a x)^(p^(d−1)) − a x^p = 0,

or, equivalently after taking pth powers,

    a x − a^p x^(p²) = 0.

For nonzero x this is x^(p²−1)=a^(1−p). Since gcd(p²−1,p^d−1)=p−1, the right side lies in the image of that power map, and there are exactly p−1 nonzero solutions. Including0, the radical has p elements and dimension1. Thus every nonzero form has rank d−1, giving r=(d−1)/2 and cyclic length at least(3d−1)/2. The common radical of beta is0: for x≠0 choose y/x outside F_p.
