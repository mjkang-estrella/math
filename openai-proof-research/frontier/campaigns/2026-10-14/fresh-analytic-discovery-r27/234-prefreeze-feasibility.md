# Family 234 pre-freeze feasibility work

No target is frozen. This record precedes nomination and contains a plausible deduction, not a completed proof of an earlier frozen claim.

Potential full target: uniformly bounded deterministic real spectra with empirical measure tending to a compact law mu and largest eigenvalue tending to theta >= max supp(mu), without a bottom-edge condition. The zero-field Haar-orthogonal Ising pressure, at every fixed temperature, should have the source variational formula with the inverse Stieltjes branch capped at theta rather than the support edge. Arbitrary bounded lower outliers and any sublinear top multiplicity remain allowed.

The proposed new comparison flattens the top k~epsilon N bulk eigenvalues to the bulk edge, at a cost tending to zero as epsilon decreases. A rank-k positive spike on this flat subspace is the supremum of rank-one quadratic spikes. A deterministic eta-net with at most (1+2/eta)^k points bounds its partition function by a sum of rank-one partition functions, with a strength increase of (1-eta)^(-2). The scalar flat bulk is essential: every net direction then has the same Haar rank-one marginal law. Haar concentration controls the maximum of these correlated pressures by an entropy error O(sqrt(epsilon log(1/eta))); no independence of net points is claimed. The source handles each fixed positive epsilon spike mass. Passing epsilon to zero would yield the isolated-spike formula by a continuous capped-transform argument.

Lower outliers appear removable through convex symmetry of expected pressure as a function of the eigenvalues: derivatives are nonnegative, ordered like the eigenvalues, and sum to 1/2, so raising k bottom coordinates costs at most O(k/(N-k)). Tiny edge deviations need separate operator-norm control. This is a proposed supporting step, not a prior theorem claim.

The source count-comparison lemma uses positive endpoint multiplicities and relative errors; its bound is not small when a rank-one spike is thickened to epsilon N. The source clipped-bin argument assumes no edge outliers. The proposed net comparison addresses precisely this missing interface. The source capped-transform continuity proof is an antecedent to be adapted, not claimed new.

Potential formal support: actual finite projection/net quadratic comparison and finite partition max/sum inequality, plus ordered-gradient counting. Full spin-glass source and Haar concentration remain external mathematical premises.
