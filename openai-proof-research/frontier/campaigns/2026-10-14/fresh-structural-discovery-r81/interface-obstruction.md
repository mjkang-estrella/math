# A limit of the rich/affine interface alone

Author exploration only. This is an abstract finite set-system example, **not** a realization by arithmetic progressions, not a counterexample to the prospective target, and not a theorem nomination. No full target has been frozen.

Fix 0<δ≤1/2 and an even integer k. Let M=ceil(1/δ). Take two disjoint sets V0,V1, each partitioned into M disjoint blocks of size k. Give every point of Vi outer color i and a unique key. The designated affine tests are the 2M blocks. The designated rich tests are every k-element subset R with exactly k/2 elements in V0 and k/2 in V1. All tests have k distinct keys. Every rich test has half its positions in each outer color; every other designated test is one of only 2M affine signatures. Thus the coarse rich/exceptional dichotomy, key multiplicity bound (even multiplicity one), and a subexponential number of exceptional signatures hold with stronger constants than the source interfaces used in the naive perturbation argument.

Nevertheless no binary final coloring balances every designated test to minority δk. Suppose it did. Every affine block would contain at least δk points of final color zero. Each Vi would therefore contain at least Mδk≥k such points. Choose k/2 final-zero points from each Vi. Their union is a rich test monochromatic in the final coloring, a contradiction. Integer rounding only strengthens this argument: requiring at least ceil(δk) in each block gives at least M ceil(δk)≥k.

For clarity, the number of rich tests is binom(Mk,k/2)^2≤2^(2Mk), exponential in k for fixed δ. It fits well below the source's available exp(Θ(k log k)) upper bound on all progression tests. Consequently replacing a union bound by an arbitrary black-box rounding theorem depending only on these three summary properties cannot solve the binary target. The arithmetic/geometric compatibility of the actual rich and affine tests must enter a new argument. This example does not satisfy or claim the source's full coordinate geometry, the actual global-label word bound, or its anchored return-pattern structure.

## Why freezing coarse labels does not repair the issue

A proposed three-state outer coloring could mark a small proportion of labels flexible, leaving two frozen binary colors to protect rich tests under arbitrary inner changes. The source's affine alternative allows a progression with a single full label repeated k times; its key multiplicity bound still permits many distinct keys inside that label. If that label is frozen, this test stays monochromatic. The displayed source interfaces provide no theorem that every such affine test intersects a positive proportion of flexible labels. Therefore this proposal introduces another hitting obligation rather than paying the original one. It is not a proof that the actual source realization has an offending constant-label progression for every frozen-label selection.

## Modular hitting comparison

Fox–Hunter's Lemma2.9 counts primes in [k/101,k] not dividing the step. Its proof has room when log N=O(k). At N=k^(ck), consider the product D_k of all primes in [k/101,k]. The prime number theorem, as used in that primary proof, gives log D_k=Θ(k), hence (k-1)D_k+1≤k^(ck) for every fixed c>0 and all sufficiently large k. This is an actual k-term interval progression step divisible by every prime used in that proof. All residues along this progression are constant modulo each such prime. The proof's guaranteed supply of nondividing primes is zero. This does not disprove a different modular selector or show that every random choice fails; it pinpoints the lost deterministic premise.
