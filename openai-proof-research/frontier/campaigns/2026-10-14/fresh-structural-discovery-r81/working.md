## Prospective full target, not yet frozen

Exist absolute constants δ,c>0 and K such that every integer k≥K admits an integer N≥k^(ck) and a two-coloring of [N] in which every nonconstant k-term arithmetic progression contains at least δk points of each color. Integer rounding must be fixed in any scope freeze. This is a fixed positive proportion independent of k, not a vanishing minority such as k^(19/20). A cyclic version on the source group would suffice but is not automatically required by this prospective interval target. No weakened rescue has been selected.

The source itself gives N=q^D≥k^(10^-5 k), D=ceil(k^.1), q a power of a prime P>k, and no monochromatic k-AP. Its outer coloring has the exact rich-or-affine dichotomy: every progression either has at least γk of each outer bit (γ=.01), or its centered y-coordinates have one global affine lift. Each literal key (y-box, floor(q²||y||²)) repeats at most four times. The number of affine literal signatures has logarithm O(k^.9 log k). The source then flips each key independently with p=k^(-1/20).

## Concrete unpaid mechanism

For affine signatures, constant flip probability p0>0 would give exponential-in-k concentration, sufficient against exp(O(k^.9 log k)) signatures to force a fixed minority. But there are N²=exp(Θ(k log k)) general progressions, and the source's preservation estimate on rich progressions with constant p0 has only exp(-Θ(k)) decay. Thus its displayed union bound fails. Taking p→0 pays preservation via log(1/p)≈log k but only creates o(k) minority on an initially constant affine progression. Replacing exact monochromaticity by o(k) errors and applying a weighted Chernoff bound is a routine adaptation, expressly excluded as a nominee.

The actual new obligation is a selective hitting/rounding construction on keys: achieve a positive fraction of flips on every nearly constant affine signature while bounding damaging flips on every rich progression. Independence plus the existing full progression union bound is insufficient. No such construction is proved.

A four-color pair (outer bit, independent fair key bit) readily separates the two cases: rich progressions cannot concentrate in one pair-color, and affine signatures are balanced in the second coordinate. That routine product is not the binary target and is not proposed as a result. Arbitrary binary merging of four colors can destroy both protections. Majority of repeated sparse perturbations preserves the outer bit and does not create a positive minority on constant affine signatures; parity with enough repetitions makes flips constant-density and restores the rich-family union-bound problem.
