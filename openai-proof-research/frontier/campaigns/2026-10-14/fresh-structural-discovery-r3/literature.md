# Bounded primary-source screen

This is an operation-level comparison, not an exhaustive priority search.
PDF SHA256 and sizes are in `retrieval-literature.json` and the input manifest.
Extractions retain the third-party rights of their source PDFs.

1. Cameron Gordon and Fernando Rodriguez-Villegas, *On the divisibility of
   #Hom(Gamma,G) by |G|*, J. Algebra 350 (2012), 300-307.
   [Versioned primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.6066v1),
   [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2011.09.040).
   Read the full seven-page mathematical body and references, especially
   Lemma 2.1, Proposition 3.1, Corollaries 3.2-3.3 and Section 5 through
   Proposition 5.1. The downloaded bytes have the arXiv v1 margin but an
   internal September 3, 2021 title date; both facts are retained, without
   inventing a publication chronology from that mismatch. The discrete
   semidirect-product restriction map is a torsor, and fixed conjugacy
   classes count the quotient of homomorphism counts. The direct-product
   case already relates subgroup conjugacy classes to exponential-product
   exponents. Proposition 5.1 is the adjacent-index congruence for one
   discrete group with infinite abelianization. These are substantial
   antecedents to the new proposed Lemma 1. The stated result there is not
   universal finite-precision action removal, and does not identify a
   poly-Z_p length residue c_t.

2. H. Kisilevsky, *A Generalization of a Result of Sinnott*, Pacific J.
   Math. 181, no. 3 (1997), 225-229.
   [Publisher primary PDF](https://msp.org/pjm/1997/181-3/pjm-v181-n3-p11-p.pdf).
   Read the complete paper, applying only Proposition 1 and its proof on
   p.225 as comparison. The fixed-point difference for a cyclic p-action
   is divisible by the length of every newly appearing orbit. This is the
   elementary divisibility operation used in our weighted conjugacy-class
   count; it is not new. Number-field/function-field applications are not
   used as premises or claimed consequences.

3. Hirotaka Tamanoi, *Generalized Orbifold Euler Characteristic of Symmetric
   Products and Equivariant Morava K-Theory*, AGT 1 (2001), 115-141.
   [Publisher primary PDF](https://msp.org/agt/2001/1-1/agt-v1-n1-p06-p.pdf).
   Read the title/introduction and pp.139-140 through the complete proof of
   Lemma 5-5 and the surrounding (5-7)-(5-10). The Hermite-type lattice
   basis proves the Z_p^d subgroup count. That count is explicitly credited
   and reproduced, not nominated. The other technical sections were retained
   but not read in full or invoked. No general poly-Z_p congruence was found
   in the selected passages.

4. Hirotaka Tamanoi, *Generalized orbifold Euler characteristics of symmetric
   orbifolds and covering spaces*, AGT 3 (2003), 791-856.
   [Publisher primary PDF](https://msp.org/agt/2003/3-2/agt-v3-n2-p06-p.pdf).
   Read pp.825-828, Proposition 5-4 and Theorem 5-5 with their proofs,
   (5-5)-(5-7), and Lemma 5-6. This is the actual-subgroup versus conjugacy
   normalization and the general automorphism-weighted exponential formula.
   Our proof does not use their full equivariant-principal-bundle structure
   theorem as an additional black box. Other sections were retained but not
   read in full. The first attempted publisher path ended in `p03-p.pdf`
   and returned Frenkel's *Fixed point data of finite groups acting on
   3-manifolds*, pp.709-718. Its title was checked, the filename corrected to
   `frenkel-2003-wrong-retrieval`, and none of its mathematics was used.

5. Ben Davison and Travis Mandel, *Strong positivity for quantum theta bases
   of quantum cluster algebras*, Invent. Math. 226 (2021), 725-843.
   [Versioned primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12915v3).
   For the rejected family-169 alternative, read the introduction distinction
   between positivity/pre-Lefschetz/Lefschetz, the statements and immediate
   context of Conjecture 2.17, Theorem 2.18, Lemma 3.5, Conjecture 3.13 and
   Theorem 3.14. The full long paper was not read; no general Lefschetz
   theorem or acyclic-root identification was inferred. The actual source
   root quiver and the completed I19/I24 objections veto this route.

The search was limited to the three screened families and then to the
selected subgroup-count mechanism. Queries covered cyclic/subnormal length,
pro-p subgroup-count congruences, polyprocyclic subgroup zeta limits,
semidirect-product counts, and the primary references above. Search results
and catalogue abstracts were discovery aids; no secondary web source is
used as a mathematical premise. One publisher XHTML attempt returned an
unsupported-content-type error; the publisher PDFs supplied the actual text.

The contribution review must assess the short elementary proof against
GRV/Solomon-style torsor counts and classical subgroup-growth literature.
Failure to locate the exact displayed theorem in this bounded screen is
not evidence of worldwide priority or of substantiality by itself.
