                          Second-order contact of a ball and a slab
                                  in the cubic three-torus
                                                   Myeongjin Kang∗

                    October 14, 2026 — Draft for independent manuscript review



                                                          Abstract
              Relative to the pinned scalar isoperimetric classiﬁcation of the cubic three-torus, we establish
          a second-order contact law for two nested sets whose volumes approach those of a tangent ball
          and slab. The admissible sets have arbitrary ﬁnite perimeter. If the ball radius approaches the
          ball–tube transition from below, with no restriction on its rate relative to the compression, the
          ball-localized minimum is 2πε2 /(| log ε| + log | log ε|) + O(ε2 /| log ε|2 ), uniformly in both param-
          eters. The lower bound uses the full positive spherical Jacobi inverse and the two face Laplace
          inverses on actual deformed circles. A summed trace cancels relative axial displacement, and im-
          proved convergence of quasiminimizers provides a quantitative nonlinear error by interpolation.
          A tangent logarithmic proﬁle with exact volume corrections gives the matching upper bound.
          The exact competing tube minimum then yields a second-scale crossover law. The logarithmic
          inversion is classical; the assertion here concerns the unrestricted nonlinear minimum and its
          uniform error.

     Any sequence of admissible pairs with parameters tending to (R∗ , 0) and deﬁcit tending to zero
has scalar equality limits, since both deﬁcits are nonnegative. By (1.6), the inner limit is a ball
or coordinate tube and the outer limit an occupied slab of width 2/3. Inclusion persists. A tube
normal to the slab is impossible; a parallel one may have normal center oﬀset at most 1/9. A
ball’s projection has exactly the slab width, so their normal centers agree. The upper bounds in
Lemma 2.1 tend to zero in either localized class, even if g(R)/S diverges. Separation forces every
localized limit into its own orbit, making its distance tend to zero. The constraint is therefore
interior by a ﬁxed margin. Global exact minimizers have the ball upper bound O(S); their limits
lie in one of the two orbits. A counterexample sequence to the asserted identity would contradict
this compactness conclusion.

then δ = O(S) and δ 2 /T2 = O(ε2 / log ℓ) → 0. Hence (g(R) − 2πS)/T2 → (2π/3)α. Taking the
minimum of the two convergent normalized costs proves (1.5), completing Theorem 1.1.
      The proof uses the scalar classiﬁcation and the established AFM regularity theorem at the
exact interfaces stated above. It does not classify the contact set, prove a ﬁnite-parameter unique
crossing, or give regularity to arbitrary near-minimizers. The tangent radius in the recovery is
a construction parameter, not an asserted radius of an unknown minimizer. The second-scale
crossover is a consequence of one contact theorem, not a separate result.
      The accompanying exact-arithmetic program checks coeﬃcient identities for the inverse-kernel
diﬀerence, the tangent-proﬁle energy, and the elementary crossover expansion. These ﬁnite checks
do not certify regularity, measure convergence, uniform asymptotics, or the theorem. No Lean
formalization of this geometric argument is claimed. The earlier leading contact argument and the
