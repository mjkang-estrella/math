# Family 217 pre-freeze feasibility checkpoint

No target frozen. This is a prospective mechanism derived before the source/primary/history screen, not a completed frozen proof. Do not retrospectively describe it as prior frozen work.

Potential full consequence: at every fixed β>1, the Gaussian low-temperature SK limiting law and variance prefactor persist for arbitrary iid centered variance-one real disorder with finite fourth moment, without symmetry or vanishing third moment. A natural stronger formulation permits independent nonidentical triangular arrays with a uniform fourth-moment bound. Exact centering by each model's expectation and its own standard deviation must be retained. Which full formulation is frozen will be decided only after the operation/history comparison.

Let F(J)=log Σσ exp(β n^{-1/2} Σ_{i<j}J_ij σ_iσ_j). For a smooth test φ(F/a), every total mixed derivative of order k is bounded by C_{k,β,φ} n^{-k/2}/a for a≥1 (chain rule and bounded spin cumulants). The ordinary single-edge replacement with first two moments matched has a cubic term involving E∂_e³φ(F/a) at J_e=0, and a fourth-order remainder. Vertex-gauge invariance makes this cubic derivative odd under simultaneously flipping all remaining edges in the star at either endpoint of e. The other edges' law is independent of that star. Compare the star law to its reflected law by a second coordinate replacement: the first two moments still match, and each error is controlled by a mixed derivative ∂_f³∂_e³, order six. A star has O(n) coordinates, so this odd expectation costs O(n·n^-3/a)=O(n^-2/a). Summing over O(n²) outer replacements costs O(1/a); fourth remainders also cost O(n²·n^-2/a)=O(1/a). Thus a diverging scale such as a=n^(1/6) might suffice without third-moment matching.

This cancellation is not the standard direct three-moment Lindeberg bound, whose cubic error at this scale diverges. Whether it is already an available operation in the primary literature remains to be checked.

Variance needs a separate proof. Applying the same mechanism to (F-c)² produces derivative bounds with an extra |F-c| factor. Under intermediate hybrid laws this should give a comparison controlled by C(1+√M), where M is the maximal second moment over all relevant hybrids. One must include reflected-star hybrids and deleted coordinates consistently, avoid circular use of the desired variance bound, and account for finite-fourth-moment integrability in Taylor remainders. A naive supremum over the interpolation point times |J|^4 could demand fifth moments; this is a genuine detail to resolve, not assumed paid. A bounded-truncation argument with a scale chosen quantitatively, or a refined Lipschitz decoupling remainder, may be needed. Distributional convergence alone is not variance convergence.
