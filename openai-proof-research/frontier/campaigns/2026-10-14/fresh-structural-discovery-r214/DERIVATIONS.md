## A concrete limit of an equivariant-polynomial route (feasibility)

For S¹ weights (1,-1) on C², every equivariant holomorphic germ is (z f(u),w g(u)), u=zw. An equivariant polynomial automorphism induces an automorphism u↦u f(u)g(u) on invariant C[u], hence f,g are constants. The volume-preserving entire automorphism (z exp(u),w exp(-u)) has a nontrivial cubic jet, so it cannot have an equivariant polynomial automorphism realizing that jet. Source's nonequivariant volume-jet theorem therefore cannot simply be averaged. This is only a failure of that route, not an impossibility theorem for equivariant uniformization.

The stronger compatible replacement for this special representation is the complete overshear (a z exp P(u),b w exp(-P(u))). Composition yields invariant u_k=D_k u and accumulated exponential sum ΣP_j(D_j u), D_j=∏_(i<j)a_i b_i. If the coefficients grow exp(C Δs_j+o(s_j)) and |D_j|=exp(-(s_j-s_0)/2), sufficiently small macro width makes that series locally uniformly convergent, with all derivatives, on C. Together with contraction of the linear parts, forward maps tend to0 on every compact; source inverse-ball inclusion would then give onto. This removes the polynomial-degree obstruction in the balanced two-weight case. It does not prove the full general equivariant assertion, nor does it establish that this special case is new. For weights (p,-q), p!=q, constant-Jacobian equivariant complete overshears have a further obstruction: the factor exp((p-q)H(u)) in the Jacobian cannot be constant unless H is constant. Do not extrapolate the balanced calculation.
