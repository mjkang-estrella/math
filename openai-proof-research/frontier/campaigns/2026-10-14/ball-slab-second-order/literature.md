## Classical contact logarithm and log–log inversion

Lacasse–Grest–Levine, [Deformation of Small Compressed Droplets](https://arxiv.org/html/mtrl-th/9603005),
arXiv:mtrl-th/9603005v1 (1996): read the introduction and Section III,
especially equations (9)–(31), through the force/energy comparison. Their
axisymmetric parallel-plate calculation has small-contact parameter X and
relations (27), (29), (31). In particular compression is asymptotically
`xi=-2X log X`. Inverting this already produces a log–log correction.
This is stronger historical evidence than the abstract-only read in I346.
The present coefficient mechanism is therefore not claimed as new physics.
Their rigid-plane rotational calculation does not itself give an
unrestricted two-free-interface torus lower bound.

Cunjing Lv, [Effects of geometric confinement on a droplet between two
parallel planes](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.05830v2), arXiv:1704.05830v2
(2017): read the abstract/introduction, Sections 4.1–4.2 portions and Appendix
B.2, especially equations (29), (75), (78)–(84). The small-contact relation
contains the logarithm of contact radius; fixed-volume formulas relate
that radius to geometric compression and energy. These are relevant
higher-order liquid-contact precedents. They are asymptotic calculations
for an axisymmetric confined droplet, not a theorem over the candidate's
full paired BV class. No radius or contact-set theorem for the present
minimizers is inferred from them.
