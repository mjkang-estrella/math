# Exact coverage and exclusions

The intermediate theorem `ray_tail_stable` takes an arbitrary graph, real edge weights, a symmetric triangle function d dominated by every original walk cost, nonnegative original weights, and equality of old/new weights outside finite F. It assumes genuine graph segments between each pair of indexed vertices, original segment cost equal to d, radial additivity from a root, and escape in original distance. Every changed edge has at least one endpoint at root-distance at most C. It concludes eventual minimality of every forward segment against every competitor whose edge list has no duplicates.

Neither eventual avoidance nor new minimality is an assumption. `calibrated_segment_avoids` derives avoidance; `detour_barrier` splits a genuine walk at a visited vertex; `late_segment_minimizes` separates competitors meeting F from those avoiding it. `distance_dominated_by_cost` additionally derives global domination from the zero diagonal, triangle inequality and local adjacent-edge comparison. This helper is used in the concrete control instantiation.

The graph may be infinite. No lattice, measure, stationarity, independence or moments are encoded. The sequence is not represented as a Lean infinite ray; coherence of its segments and existence/coercivity of the original metric are external application obligations. The intermediate conclusion quantifies only edge-simple competitors. `bypass_cost_le` uses Mathlib loop erasure and its edge-sublist theorem to show that nonnegative weighted cost cannot increase. The strengthened `ray_tail_stable_all_walks` then concludes minimality against every finite walk, adding only nonnegativity of the modified weights. No same-root reattachment, uniqueness, Busemann equality, limiting graph, random selector, half-plane splice, cap construction, or all-ray classification is formalized. No conclusion about the full T212M theorem follows from this build alone.

Controls use complete graph on Fin 4, d(u,v)=|u−v|, original edge cost d, and reduce edge {0,1} from 1 to 1/2. The late segment 2→3 meets the theorem hypotheses. Traversing 0→1→0→1 changes cost by 3/2 while Δ=1/2, disproving a no-Nodup budget. Original-minimal unchanged segment 0→2 costs 2, but new shortcut 0→1→2 costs 3/2, disproving preservation without lateness. The new-weight nonnegativity and all-walk late-segment instance are also proved. These are kernel proofs of specified statements, not exhaustive semantic checks.
