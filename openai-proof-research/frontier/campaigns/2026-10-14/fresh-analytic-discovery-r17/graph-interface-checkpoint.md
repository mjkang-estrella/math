## A rigorous obstruction to the naive finite-particle replacement

Suppose a finite metric X has random real functions f_1,...,f_N, every realization L-Lipschitz, and

  E sum_i |f_i(x)-f_i(y)| >= a d(x,y)

for every x,y. Then X embeds into a Hilbert space with distortion at most NL/a. Indeed set F(x)=(f_i(x)-f_i(o))_i in L2(Omega;R^N). Boundedness follows from the pointwise Lipschitz bounds. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

  ||F(x)-F(y)||_2 >= a d(x,y)/sqrt(N),
  ||F(x)-F(y)||_2 <= sqrt(N)L d(x,y).

The normalized variant with average over i has Hilbert distortion at most L/a. Thus any mechanism replacing arbitrary planar bag cut laws by a fixed number of uniformly Lipschitz real particles while retaining a constant lower bound on the entire bag would force a uniform Hilbert bound. Series-parallel diamond graphs obstruct that conclusion. A self-contained diamond proof is being written; the source itself credits Newman–Rabinovich for its Hilbert lower-bound antecedent. This is a restriction on the proposed mechanism, not a counterexample to clique-sum closure.

## Another exact obstruction: subtracting a cut metric from edge lengths

Let a,b,c form a triangle with lengths 1,1,2, so ac is a tight shortest-path edge via b. The valid cut pseudometric D from the singleton {b} has D(ab)=D(bc)=1 and D(ac)=0. For every epsilon>0, residual edge lengths ell-epsilon D violate the cycle inequality at ac: 2 > (1-epsilon)+(1-epsilon). Thus a source nonexpansive cut metric cannot in general be supplemented to make all separator edges exact merely by representing the residual edge lengths. Even arbitrarily small scaling fails on a tight triangle. This again refutes a shortcut, not closure.
