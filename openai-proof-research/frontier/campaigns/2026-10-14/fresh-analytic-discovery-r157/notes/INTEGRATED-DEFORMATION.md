# Integrated circle-domain deformation calculation

## 1. Full objects and normalization

Let Ω be a circle domain in the sphere containing 0, 1 and ∞, with conformally removable boundary. One can impose this normalization by choosing three distinct domain points before applying a Möbius coordinate change. The complement is bounded in the resulting plane; fix R≥2 containing it in D_R. Write its nonpoint complementary components as pairwise disjoint closed disks D_j=overline B(c_j,r_j), j∈J, with r_j>0. J is finite or countable. All remaining complementary components are points, possibly uncountably many. No separation, minimum radius, finite length, or bound on the number of components is imposed.

Let μ be any measurable coefficient on Ω with ||μ||∞<1. There is no compact-support assumption. Fixing the coefficient only almost everywhere is appropriate. Set it equal to zero on the point components. For Γ, the countable reflection group generated by the disk circles, define Eμ on each reflected fundamental cell T(Ω∪P) by (T^{-1})*μ and set it to zero elsewhere, exactly as in Younsi's invariant-extension construction. Positive-area residual sets need not be discarded: zero is assigned there. The construction is invariant on the residual set as well.

The even cells carry a complex-linear phase times μ pulled back to Ω. The odd cells carry a complex-linear phase times its conjugate. Therefore

 E(μ−ν)=Eμ−Eν,   ||Eμ−Eν||∞=||μ−ν||∞,

where E is REAL linear. Norm equality follows already on Ω, and the upper bound on every cell is equality of absolute values under reflection. Neither a sum over the group nor an ℓ1 bound on the number of reflected cells is used. Boundaries and measurable representatives are treated as in the primary construction.

The normalized solution F_μ of

 ∂bar F_μ=(Eμ) ∂F_μ,     F_μ(0)=0, F_μ(1)=1, F_μ(∞)=∞

is a global quasiconformal homeomorphism. The reflected-circle argument in Younsi's Proposition labelled PropInvariant makes Ω_μ=F_μ(Ω) a circle domain. Conjugating each reflection gives an anti-Möbius involution with the image circle as fixed set. A global homeomorphism sends every point component to a point and every original disk to the corresponding closed disk, preserving the labels j. Write their radii s_j(μ)>0. There are no point-to-disk or disk-to-point mismatches in this constructed deformation.

## 2. Where the pinned source enters

Quasiconformal images of conformally removable compact sets are removable; this is Younsi's Corollary InvQC, following his actual measurable-Riemann-mapping argument for equivalence with quasiconformal removability. Thus ∂Ω_μ is removable. The pinned rigidity theorem applies at every μ.

If u:Ω→Ω' is another quasiconformal equivalence onto a circle domain, has coefficient μ on Ω, and fixes 0,1,∞, the composition u∘F_μ^{-1} is conformal on Ω_μ. Pinned rigidity makes it Möbius. Three fixed points make it the identity. Thus the constructed normalized map is the unique normalized circle-domain solution with the prescribed coefficient on Ω. No extension of u across its boundary was assumed.

The existence construction itself, and the quantitative radius calculations below, already work for arbitrary circle domains without removable boundary. Removability plus the source gives intrinsic uniqueness; it does not create a new reflection or Beltrami operation. The broader pinned existence theorem permits starting with an arbitrary planar domain and choosing a circle model, but does not assert that every resulting model has removable boundary. We do not silently infer that stronger premise.

The source's retained-test transfer, mandatory marks, total tail-energy and positive-excess oscillation controls are already proved in its retained-test theorem and scalar corollary. The complete rigidity companion uses those controls to build scalar Sobolev models, conductivity coordinates, saved barriers, common-point masks, and the final flux/area identity. Those are actual source operations, not proposed author innovations.

## 3. All-disk Lipschitz stability, including arbitrary coefficients near infinity

Fix 0≤k<1 and ||μ||∞,||ν||∞≤k. Put δ=||μ−ν||∞. The actual Ahlfors–Bers Theorem8, together with its preceding local uniform bounds, gives for some p>2 depending on k (choose k C_p<1) and every fixed L<∞

 ||F_μ−F_ν||_{W^{1,p}(D_L)} ≤ C(k,p,L) δ,
 sup_{D_L}|F_μ|+sup_{D_L}|F_ν| ≤ C(k,L).

The original theorem uses its equivalent B_{L,p} norm, including the Hölder seminorm and derivatives; normalization at0 controls the missing constant. This is a theorem for coefficients on the WHOLE plane, not merely compactly supported coefficients. In its proof, the normalized spherical bound is localized by a cutoff and the inhomogeneous Beltrami equation; the disk/inversion decomposition pays the behavior at infinity. That distinction matters here.

Set a=∂F_μ, b=∂bar F_μ, c=∂F_ν, d=∂bar F_ν. Almost everywhere, |b|≤k|a| and |d|≤k|c|. Define J_μ=|a|²−|b|² and J_ν=|c|²−|d|². Direct factorization gives

 |sqrt(J_μ)−sqrt(J_ν)|
 ≤ ( |a−c| + k|b−d| ) / sqrt(1−k²).                 (3.1)

Indeed the difference of the squared roots has absolute value at most

 (|a|+|c|)|a−c| + (|b|+|d|)|b−d|,

while their sum is at least sqrt(1−k²)(|a|+|c|). The all-zero case is immediate. This proof does not interpolate through a segment on which the ellipticity cone might fail.

The global area formula and the disk-image property give

 sqrt(π) s_j(μ)=||sqrt(J_μ)||_{L2(D_j)}.

The reverse triangle inequality on EACH fixed original disk, followed by disjointness, yields

 π Σ_j |s_j(μ)−s_j(ν)|²
 ≤ ∫_{∪D_j}|sqrt(J_μ)−sqrt(J_ν)|²
 ≤ (1−k²)^{-1}(||a−c||_{L2(D_R)}+k||b−d||_{L2(D_R)})²
 ≤ C(k,R) δ².                                            (3.2)

There is no interchange of a supremum with a norm and no separate constant for each disk. Finite subsums increase to the countable sum. The point components do not enter the area sum; global homeomorphism preserves their zero radii. The maps send D_R into a fixed bounded disk by AB's normalization bounds, so the entire radius vector itself belongs to ℓ2(J), uniformly on the coefficient k-ball.

Thus arbitrary bounded coefficients, all disks, all accumulations and arbitrary dust are already covered. Discarding tiny disks, assuming compact support, or first proving only componentwise continuity would be unnecessarily weak comparisons.

## 4. An explicit energy check in the compact-support normalization

This is a diagnostic of (3.2), not a scope replacement. Suppose additionally that the coefficient on Ω vanishes off D_R. Every nonidentity reflection cell lies inside an original complementary disk, so Eμ also vanishes off D_R. Use the principal normalization H_m(z)=z+O(1/z), m=Eμ, and similarly H_n. Let C be the ordinary compact-support Cauchy transform, S=∂C the L2-isometric Beurling transform, and

 g_m=(I−mS)^{-1}m,   H_m=z+Cg_m.

The contraction and higher-p regularity/homeomorphism argument are the original AB proof, with a translation from its normalization at0. For A=πR² and ||m||∞,||n||∞≤k,

 ||g_m||2≤k sqrt(A)/(1−k).

Subtracting the equations gives the EXACT resolvent identity

 (I−mS)(g_m−g_n)=(m−n)(1+Sg_n).

Since m−n is supported on D_R,

 ||g_m−g_n||2≤δ sqrt(A)/(1−k)².                         (4.1)

Both derivative differences have this L2 norm: ∂bar difference=g_m−g_n and ∂ difference=S(g_m−g_n). Applying (3.1) and the disjoint-disk reverse triangle inequality gives the explicit bound

 ||s(m)−s(n)||ℓ2
 ≤ R sqrt(1+k)/(1−k)^(5/2) · δ.                        (4.2)

Here s denotes principal-normalization radii, not the0,1,∞-normalized radii from (3.2). Scaling/translation of the normalization must not be forgotten. No claim of optimal constants is made. The 0,1,∞ statement with arbitrary coefficients remains (3.2).

## 5. Why coordinatewise analyticity alone is insufficient

For a fixed disk, its image radius can be read from three image boundary points and is real analytic when the normalized solution depends real analytically on the coefficient. But infinitely many positive radii may tend to zero. Coordinatewise analyticity alone does not imply a locally convergent ℓ2 power series, and taking a square root of an ℓ1 area vector is not a valid blanket analytic argument near such a sequence.

Likewise E(tμ) is not t Eμ for complex t. Write

 E(tμ)=t a+bar(t) b,                                   (5.1)

where a is supported on even cells and b on odd cells, with ||a||∞,||b||∞≤||μ||∞ and disjoint supports. A holomorphic-motion claim for the one-complex-parameter global family based on E(tμ)=tEμ would be false reasoning. Neither issue is retained as an unpaid gap: the classical estimates also pay the correct complexified operation.

## 6. Correct reflected-parameter complexification

Take any μ with ||μ||∞≤1, and consider all t∈C with |t|<1. More generally rescale the parameter disk if ||μ||∞ differs from1. Introduce independent complex parameters (t,s) and solve the normalized global equation with coefficient

 m(t,s)=t a+s b.                                        (6.1)

On every closed bidisk |t|,|s|≤k<1, ||m(t,s)||∞≤k, since the supports in (5.1) are disjoint. AB's analytic-dependence theorem makes F(t,s)(z) holomorphic in (t,s) for each finite z, and in local B_{L,p} spaces. On s=bar(t), it is exactly F_{tμ}. Off that real slice the images of the original disks need NOT be round; they are images under global uniformly quasiconformal homeomorphisms.

Fix t0 with |t0|<1 and let F0=F(t0,bar(t0)). Around each base target disk F0(D_j)=overline B(c_j^0,r_j^0), choose the three points

 z_{j1}=c_j^0−r_j^0,  z_{j2}=c_j^0+r_j^0,
 z_{j3}=c_j^0+i r_j^0.

Let p_{jl}=F0^{-1}(z_{jl}); these are fixed original boundary points for the local calculation. Define holomorphic quantities

 Z_{jl}(t,s)=F(t,s)(p_{jl}),
 d_j=Z_{j2}−Z_{j1},
 w_j=(Z_{j3}−Z_{j1})/d_j.                              (6.2)

Injectivity makes d_j nonzero throughout the parameter bidisk. At the base, d_j=2r_j^0 and w_j=w0=(1+i)/2.

The same parameter neighborhood works for ALL j. To see this, let G=F(t,s)∘F0^{-1}. The coefficient-composition formula yields

 ||μ_G||∞ ≤ ||m(t,s)−m(t0,bar(t0))||∞ /
                  (1−||m(t,s)||∞||m(t0,bar(t0))||∞).

This tends uniformly to zero with the bidisk radius, without any disk-dependent constant. Affinely normalize the input endpoints z_{j1},z_{j2} to0,1 and the output endpoints to0,1. The resulting global map Q_j fixes0,1,∞ and has the same dilatation bound. AB Lemma17 compares every such normalized map to the identity in spherical distance. On the fixed compact neighborhood of w0 this gives

 |w_j−w0|<ε                                              (6.3)

for every j once a common parameter neighborhood is chosen. The estimate also holds uniformly for Q_j and Q_j^{-1} on the fixed standard disk over the diameter[0,1]. This uses only normalized small-dilatation control actually proved by AB, not a geometric separation condition on the source disks.

## 7. Packing pays the infinite holomorphic sum

In the same normalization the base disk is D_* = B(1/2,1/2). Take the common neighborhood small enough that Q_j^{-1}(B(1/2,1/4)) lies in D_* for every j. AB Lemma17, converted from spherical to Euclidean distance on this fixed compact set, guarantees this. Hence

 area(F(t,s)(D_j)) ≥ (π/16)|d_j(t,s)|².                 (7.1)

The image disks in (7.1) may be nonround; the inequality concerns the whole image regions. Their interiors remain disjoint under the SAME global map. All are contained in F(t,s)(D_R), which lies inside a fixed Euclidean disk by AB Theorem7. Thus

 Σ_j |d_j(t,s)|² ≤16 M²                                (7.2)

throughout the common neighborhood, with M finite and independent of j. No theorem about uniform area distortion of an arbitrary measurable tail set is required. In particular no sharp Astala exponent is being used without reading its proof.

Take a symmetric small bidisk U around(t0,bar(t0)), invariant under τ(t,s)=(bar(s),bar(t)). For a scalar holomorphic function h define

 h#(t,s)=overline{h(bar(s),bar(t))}.

This is holomorphic, and on s=bar(t) it equals overline h. From (6.3), w_j stays near w0 and w_j# near bar(w0), uniformly in j. On these neighborhoods define the holomorphic, bounded nonzero factor

 H(w,v)= i sqrt[w v (w−1)(v−1)]/(w−v),                 (7.3)

choosing the square-root branch with H(w0,bar(w0))=1/2. All arguments of the roots stay away from0 and w−v stays away from0 on a sufficiently small common neighborhood. This is one fixed finite-dimensional analytic formula.

The nonzero holomorphic product d_j d_j# has a holomorphic square root on the simply connected bidisk U, chosen positive at the base. Define

 S_j(t,s)=sqrt(d_j d_j#) H(w_j,w_j#).                   (7.4)

For s=bar(t), the square root stays positive by continuity, and (7.3) is the ordinary circumradius of the normalized triangle0,1,w_j. Thus S_j(t,bar(t)) is exactly the radius of F_{tμ}(D_j), not an arbitrary enclosing radius. Orientation is fixed near the base so Im w_j>0 on that real slice.

Equations (7.2) and Cauchy–Schwarz give

 Σ_j |S_j(t,s)|²
 ≤ H_*² Σ_j |d_j(t,s)||d_j#(t,s)|
 ≤ H_*² (Σ_j|d_j(t,s)|²)^(1/2)
          (Σ_j|d_j(τ(t,s))|²)^(1/2)
 ≤16 H_*² M².                                         (7.5)

This is the uniform ℓ2 bound missing from a mere coordinatewise argument. It includes every disk, however small or crowded. Empty and finite J are included without modification.

## 8. Strong analyticity and tails

Each S_j is holomorphic and (7.5) bounds their joint ℓ2 norm locally. For any v∈ℓ2, the scalar series Σ_j overline(v_j)S_j is a locally uniform limit of holomorphic finite sums: the omitted part is at most 4H_*M ||v_tail||ℓ2. Consequently S is weakly holomorphic and locally bounded. One can verify strong holomorphy directly here without an extra theorem: on a smaller closed bidisk use scalar Cauchy formulas for every coordinate; the joint coefficient vector satisfies

 ||c_{mn}||ℓ2 ≤ M_* ρ^{−m−n}.

This follows first by duality against finite unit vectors, then by completion. The ℓ2-valued double Taylor series is absolutely norm-convergent whenever both coordinate increments have modulus<ρ. Its coordinates equal the original scalar functions, hence its value is S. Restricting s=bar(t) proves that

 t ↦ (s_j(tμ))_{j∈J}

is real analytic as an ℓ2-valued map on the entire open parameter disk. Both t and bar(t) occur. On compact parameter sets, tails converge uniformly in ℓ2 by compactness of the image, or directly from local power series and finite approximation. Cauchy estimates give ℓ2 bounds for every parameter derivative on smaller neighborhoods; constants may grow as |t| approaches1.

The same construction applies to any finite-dimensional affine coefficient family inside the unit L∞ ball by introducing independent conjugate parameters for its odd cells. We do not assert complex holomorphy of the reflected family in the original parameters, nor an estimate uniform at ellipticity1. Full arbitrary-coefficient Lipschitz dependence is already (3.2).
