## 1. The Nakai transfer has no unpaid operation

The actual family048 theorem supplies a finitely generated normal integral
complex algebra A of dimension two, singular at a marked point, with
Der_C(A) free of rank two. The affine endpoint is smooth away from that
point. This is stronger than merely a free analytic tangent module.

Let D^{<=q}(A) denote the Grothendieck differential operators of order at
most q. The following standard argument, restated to check the exact
global interface, already turns that theorem into a Nakai counterexample.
It is not a proposed new theorem for this campaign.

For a normal finite-type characteristic-zero domain A with free derivations
delta_1,...,delta_d, the ordered monomials

    delta_1^{a_1} ... delta_d^{a_d},  sum a_i <= q,

form an A-basis of D^{<=q}(A). To see this, let U be the smooth locus.
Normality makes its complement have codimension at least two. On U,
the principal symbol of an order-q operator is a section of Sym^q T_U.
The restrictions of the delta_i are a tangent frame. Expanding this symbol
in their degree-q monomials gives coefficients regular on U; each extends
uniquely to A by normality. Subtract the corresponding ordered differential
operator with these coefficients. The difference has order at most q-1
on U, and therefore globally: every q-fold commutator with multiplication
vanishes on U, and its values in the domain A must vanish everywhere.
Induction proves spanning, starting with order zero equal to A.

For independence, a relation's highest-degree symbols vanish on U.
Their coefficients vanish there and thus in A. Descend the order to prove
all coefficients zero. No commutativity of the delta_i is presumed;
their commutators are first-order derivations, and ordering only fixes
the choice of lifts of symmetric symbols.

Consequently D(A) is generated by A and its derivations, and its order
associated graded is the polynomial algebra Sym_A Der_C(A). The supplied
singular A therefore violates the proposed regularity implication in
Nakai's conjecture. Freeness of all filtered pieces and their ranks are
part of the same argument, not additional findings. This does not say
that the modules of principal parts themselves are free: their A-duals
being free does not justify that inference.

The closest primary proof is C. J. Rego, *Remarks on differential operators
on algebraic varieties*, Osaka J. Math. 14 (1977), 481–486,
[Proposition 2 and its complete proof, pp.485–486](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/10502/ojm14_03_04.pdf).
Rego uses ordered derivation monomials on the regular locus, establishes
their independence, and extends equality across the singular set by
reflexivity and local cohomology. He explicitly derives the implication
from Nakai to Zariski–Lipman. Thus the proposed counterexample is the
contrapositive of an existing bridge. The global coefficient extension
above repays that same operation rather than introducing a new one.

This rejection rests on the actual primary proof body, not a search
snippet or an assumption that all short corollaries are technical.
No manuscript, Lean work, new conjecture scope or independent PASS is
appropriate for this transfer under the supplied campaign standard.

## 2. One-stable coordinates give an equivalent relative-action example

The actual family049 theorem constructs a specific f in
R=C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4] that is not a coordinate, while R[t] is a polynomial
algebra over C[f]. It also proves every fiber R/(f-lambda) is a polynomial
ring in three variables. Its construction and stabilization proof were
read, including the locally nilpotent derivation, exponential shift and
unimodular change that eliminate the stabilized equation.

Write S=R[t] as C[f][y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4] using those supplied coordinates.
The original derivation partial_t becomes a C[f]-linear locally nilpotent
derivation delta of S, with slice t and invariant ring R. On every fiber
f=lambda it is a translation on
(R/(f-lambda))[t] = C^{[3]}[t]. Nevertheless it cannot be conjugate to a
coordinate translation by a C[f]-automorphism of S. Such a conjugacy would
identify its invariant ring with C[f]^{[3]}, making f a coordinate in R,
contrary to the stipulated theorem.

This gives a family whose individual additive actions are translations
but which is not relatively conjugate to a translation over the base.
It is a direct restatement of the source's stable-coordinate failure:
the only additional step is taking the kernel of partial_t. It does not
produce a globally nonlinearizable additive action on affine five-space;
the action on S is globally conjugate to the original partial_t. Nor does
it produce an exotic affine fiber. Those tempting stronger descriptions
would be false for this construction. The precise equivalent statement is
therefore rejected before freeze, without a claim of new contribution.
