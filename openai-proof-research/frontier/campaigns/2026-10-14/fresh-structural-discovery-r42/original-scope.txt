Original full target selected in structural discovery r42, after the family166 introduction/reduction and the primary APST/Solymosi–Vu introductions and operative pinned recurrence were read, but before any complete candidate proof. This is not a claim that scope was frozen before exploratory reading.

For every integer d>=3 there exists a constant c_d>0 such that, for every finite set P of N>=2 distinct points in Euclidean R^d, there exists a point p in P satisfying

    |{ ||p-q||_2 : q in P, q != p }| >= c_d N^(2/d).

The constant depends only on d. All finite configurations are included: no separation, aspect-ratio, algebraicity, generic-position, homogeneous-distribution or nonconcentration assumption. The pin must be an actual point of P. The lower bound has a constant factor, not N^(-epsilon), subpolynomial or logarithmic loss. Empty and singleton sets impose no positive-distance assertion. No algorithmic efficiency or explicit numerical formula for c_d is required.

This is the sharp-order finite PINNED distance statement. The stipulated family166 theorem counts the union of all distances, so does not already assert it. The developed I124/I127 target is planar continuum pinned distance positivity and is not this target. No version of either prior target is being revived or narrowed.

The contemplated new interface is common-pin sphere/bisector concentration that preserves the sharp scale while replacing the source's global-palette equal-distance graph. Local radius polynomials are available, but the source's Omega(N^4/M) four-point count is not supplied by a bound M on each pinned palette. A full proof is not currently established. This scope remains fixed if these routes fail; no restricted configuration or weaker exponent will replace it.
