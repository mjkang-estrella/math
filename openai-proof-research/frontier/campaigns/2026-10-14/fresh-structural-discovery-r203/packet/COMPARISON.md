# Strongest-reduction comparison and research limits

The nominee is the exact finite-witness theorem and its quantitative J-convexity corollary in FROZEN.md. The author proof is DERIVATION.md. It is offered for the parent's independent correctness and separate substantial-contribution reviews. No verdict or count is assigned.

The source already pays for a strong obstruction, not merely for the qualitative word “superreflexive.” Its ordered norm, chart transport, two-level cocycle, and odd-label lattice lift give maximal-order finite-time Markov growth for nonsuperreflexive spaces. Ordinary renorming consequences, direct-sum stability, bounded-increment variants, weak-type variants, and the compression estimate in NOTES.md are therefore not offered as discoveries.

The strongest compatible classical reduction also has to be credited. The full forward/reverse martingale proof in the author-hosted 2004 version of [Naor–Peres–Schramm–Sheffield](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~naor/homepage%20files/Mtype.pdf), Section 4, produces Markov inequalities from a specified smoothness inequality. It does not permit reading a same-p smoothness modulus or a distortion bound backwards from a Markov-type hypothesis. The proof uses the power-smoothness parameter in its martingale estimate; r203 does not silently identify that parameter with K.

The group cocycle and bounded-increment proofs in [Naor–Peres, arXiv:0708.0853v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.0853v3), Sections 2 and 5, already implement the relevant speed/compression comparison. They are why the nonlinear compression calculation in NOTES.md is retained as a paid composition rather than a separate nominee. Those proofs also distinguish a stationary finite-chain inequality from a walk started at the identity of an infinite group. No unsupported stationarity transfer is used here.

The actual ESA preparatory argument in [Ostrovskii–Randrianantoanina](https://facpub.stjohns.edu/ostrovsm/OstRandESA.pdf), Section 2.1, identifies spreading, subadditivity, and equal-sign additivity and explains the nonreflexive finite-representability construction. [Pisier's author-hosted notes](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~gilles.pisier/ihp-pisier.pdf), complete Section 3.3, give the subsymmetric/additive construction, its subdivision limit, and the J-convexity relationship. These are actual proof-body comparisons. The 1975 Brunel–Sucheston original could not be downloaded from either of two publisher PDF routes (HTTP 403); the full canonical appendix and the specified accessible treatments were read, but this does not replace an original-primary contribution review.

The quantitative operation proposed here is a finite replacement for the source's limit, with a different interface. A finite approximately spreading array is sent linearly into a finite ℓ_p sum of X by averaging Q^j-subdivisions. Varying subdivisions differ by at most (T^(−1/p)+η)||a||_1, because shifting the finite average loses just one endpoint. This estimate yields approximate adjacent merging even when the two coefficients cancel. It is then propagated through every block compression in the actual chart proof. Finally a finite reflected lattice and coordinate selection recover an X-valued map, preserving an explicit one-time cost ratio and a chain-size bound depending only on n.

This does not follow by applying the source's exact-spreading proposition to an approximate norm. Nor does it claim that an ℓ_p sum is finitely representable in X. Both invalid shortcuts are avoided by the explicit merging estimates and by coordinate selection from an actual finite-chain inequality. All of this is proved for the entire frozen scope, including approximate triangular entries, zero-sum merges, finite/incomplete/nonseparable spaces, stationary masses, state count, and original-norm normalization.

The strongest routine compactness comparison is worth making explicit. For fixed (p,K), coordinatewise Markov inequalities show that an ℓ_p sum of spaces with that bound again has the same bound. The source conclusion then prevents an unbounded family of finite triangular obstructions in such a sum. This gives a qualitative uniform exclusion by contradiction. It provides no displayed finite array length, finite chain size, or error budget. The frozen candidate supplies those functions, but their enormous Ramsey growth is a material weakness. It does not establish a useful polynomial rate, an optimal rate, or quantitative renorming parameters. Whether this constructive finitarization goes beyond a technical consequence of the paid source and standard operations is unresolved pending separate contribution review; difficulty or formula size is not offered as evidence of substantiality.
