## Watanabe, 2403.14065v2

Title: *Fano varieties of middle pseudoindex*. Version 2, September 20, 2024. Actual PDF has seven pages; extracted text lines 1–393 were read completely, including all mathematical arguments and references.

Theorem 1.4 assumes 2ι=n+1 and ρ>1 and gives four types: the blowup of P^n along a linear P^(ι−2), the product P^(ι−1)×Q^ι, P(TP^ι), and P^(ι−1)×P^ι. Its blowup is the projectivization of O(2)⊕O(1)^(ι−1), equivalent after twisting to our O(1)⊕O^(ι−1) bundle. This already covers the rank-two defect-one part of AGM063. Small quadric conventions affect actual Picard rank, as in our theorem. No new credit is claimed for these already classified examples.

Proposition 2.2 starts with a projective-space bundle. It uses the Fano-base pseudoindex inequality, rank-one characterization, and Fujita/Sato bundle classification. Section 3.1 treats birational contractions: a minimal covering family is unsplit by the degree cutoff; one-point locus bounds and Ionescu–Wiśniewski exclude small contractions; another ray meets the exceptional divisor positively; intersections of distinct contraction fibers and dimension inequalities force a projective-space bundle, then a blowup. Section 3.2 treats the all-fiber-type case by comparing two contractions and their fiber dimensions, yielding a projective-space bundle in the remaining cases. The final classification is again Proposition 2.2.

These are one-point loci and pairwise contraction-fiber intersections. The paper does not form a composed virtual correspondence, infer its two-endpoint support dimension, use a full saturated chain quotient to put all exceptional pairs in E×E, exclude a curve base using a compact divisor disjoint from numerical representatives, or supply bounded competitors at every stage of an arbitrary minimal horizontal construction.

## Watanabe, 2411.00379v2

Title: *Smooth Fano varieties with pseudoindex equal to half of their dimension*. Version 2, October 21, 2025. Actual PDF has fourteen pages; extracted text lines 1–716 were read completely, including all mathematical arguments and references. This is the revised title and exact revised statement, not a search synopsis.

Theorem 1.3 assumes ι=n/2≥2 **and a birational extremal contraction**. It classifies eight types: blowups of P^n along P^(ι−1) or P^(ι−2); blowups of Q^n along P^(ι−1) or a quadric Q^(ι−1) not contained in a linear subspace; P over P^(ι+1) of O(2)⊕O^(ι−1); P over Q^(ι+1) of O(1)⊕O^(ι−1); P over P^(ι+1) of O(1)^2⊕O^(ι−2); and P^1×P over P^2 of O(1)⊕O. The theorem is not an unrestricted classification of all Fano manifolds with ι=n/2, nor the unrelated large-pseudoindex ranges discussed in its introduction.

Section 2 gives extremal contraction inequalities, projective-bundle criteria, unsplit locus estimates, and blowup pseudoindex computations. Proposition 2.10 estimates Locus(M)_Y and requires irreducibility of Y; Remark 2.11 explicitly explains why this cannot be omitted. Section 3 reduces larger Picard number using a known theorem, recalls a blowup classification, and proves splitting classifications for projective bundles on projective space or quadrics. Proposition 3.7 treats the equal-pseudoindex base case through minimal curves and a trivial uniform bundle.

Section 4.1 treats a small contraction. A minimal dominating family has degree strictly below 2ι and is unsplit. Its locus from a large exceptional fiber fills X. Numerical spanning forces another extremal ray, and a jumping-fiber intersection contradiction makes that contraction equidimensional; the maximal-length theorem gives a projective bundle. Section 4.2 treats a divisorial contraction. Positivity on the exceptional divisor forces the second contraction to be fiber type. Fiber dimension alternatives give either an already classified blowup or a projective bundle. The explicit bundle results finish. Section 4.3 assembles these cases.

The paper has no nonzero composed virtual-cycle support argument and no full saturated quotient/divisor contradiction. Its use of an unsplit locus filling X comes from the dimensions and existence of a specific birational contraction. It does not prove that a finite bounded chain-connecting collection bounds every minimal horizontal competitor in arbitrary dimension and actual Picard number.
