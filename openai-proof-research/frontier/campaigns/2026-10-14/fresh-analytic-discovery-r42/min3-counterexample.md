# The feasible minimum-of-three resolvent does not prove T332

Myeongjin Kang, no affiliation. Original prose CC BY4.0. Author-side continuation of the unchanged T332 target. The graph/star construction and the proposed obstruction were supplied by the parent; the finite local proof and elementary graph-existence argument below complete that author-side route check. No independent gate, novelty, or nomination is claimed.

## The operation and its feasibility

On a finite rooted weighted tree write p_i(e)=mu_i(T_e). Let A be reversible with stationary law pi, t>=1, p=1/(t+1), q=t/(t+1), and G=p sum_(s>=0) q^s A^s. For each i let J1,J2,J3 be independent with law G_i and put

    z_i(e)=E min(p_J1(e),p_J2(e),p_J3(e)).

The coordinatewise minimum of feasible probability flows is feasible. Indeed, for every parent vertex v and each a in {1,2,3}, the sum of the childwise minima is at most the sum of the a-th child's flows, which is at most the a-th parent flow. Taking the minimum over a proves the parent constraint. The same argument gives the root budget<=1. Convex mixtures preserve feasibility, so z_i describes a probability nu_i, with leftover mass at vertices. Constant families are fixed.

Nevertheless there is no universal cotype constant for THIS specified operation, even when the target is a finite unit star. This does not refute metric Markov cotype of the star-probability space, any other operator, or full T332.

## Arbitrarily high-girth cubic graphs, without an imported existence theorem

For an integer g>=3 consider all nonempty reduced words of length<=g in letters a,b,c, where reduced means consecutive letters differ. For each such word w=s1...sl, use the finite set {0,...,l}. For each letter s, define an involution tau_s that swaps j-1 and j whenever sj=s, and fixes all other points. These swaps are disjoint because consecutive letters of w differ. Applying the letters in the order s1,...,sl sends0 successively to1,...,l, so the corresponding permutation word is not the identity (reverse the written multiplication convention if necessary).

Take the product of these permutation representations over all reduced words of length<=g. The subgroup Gamma generated by the three resulting involutions is finite. Every nonempty reduced word of length<=g remains nonidentity, because its own factor detects it. In particular the three generators are nontrivial and distinct. The undirected Cayley graph of Gamma for these generators is therefore finite, connected, simple and3-regular. A graph cycle of length<=g would give a nonempty reduced word of that length equal to the identity; consecutive cancellation cannot occur on a simple cycle. Thus its girth exceeds g. This is the standard finite-permutation proof of the needed residual finiteness, included to avoid relying on an unchecked graph-existence citation.

Choose a sequence of these graphs with actual girths g tending to infinity; denote the number of vertices by n. Let A be simple random walk, pi uniform, and t=floor(sqrt(g)). Set R=floor(g/8). All estimates below are for sufficiently large g, so R>=1. In particular 2(R+1)+1<g, and the radius-(R+1) ball about any vertex is isomorphic to the corresponding ball in the infinite3-regular tree, including all edges needed by walks of length<=R from that vertex or a neighbor.

## Two elementary infinite-tree resolvent estimates

Let A_infty be simple random walk on the infinite3-regular tree, with the same independent geometric stopping time S, P(S=s)=p q^s.

First, the probability of ever hitting a specified vertex from a neighbor is1/2. Until hitting that vertex, the distance chain moves down with probability1/3 and up with probability2/3. Stopping the harmonic function2^(-r) at0 and M gives

    P_r(hit0 before M)=(2^(-r)-2^(-M))/(1-2^(-M)).

Letting M increase proves the assertion for r=1. Therefore the expected total number of visits to a specified vertex, starting there and counting time0, is2 by the renewal decomposition into returns. Starting anywhere, the expected total number of visits is at most2, by the strong Markov property at the first hit. Thus

    sum_(s>=0) P_x(X_s=y)<=2                         (1)

for all x,y.

Second, let x,y be adjacent and let U,V be the two components of the tree after deleting their edge, with x in U and y in V. Put alpha=P_x(X_S in V), and let tau be the first hitting time of y from x. The geometric memoryless property and strong Markov property give

    alpha=E_x[q^tau 1_(tau<infinity)] P_y(X_S in V).

An automorphism swapping x and y also swaps U and V, so P_y(X_S in V)=1-alpha. The discounted hitting probability a=E_x[q^tau 1_(tau<infinity)] is at most1/2. Hence alpha=a/(1+a)<=1/3. Therefore

    P_x(X_S in U)-P_y(X_S in U)=1-2alpha>=1/3.       (2)

This bound holds for every t. It does not require convergence to an end of the tree or a heat-kernel asymptotic.

## Transfer to the finite high-girth graph

Write epsilon=q^(R+1)=P(S>R). Couple finite and infinite walks by the local tree isomorphism through time R. For any finite graph vertex i and endpoint k, the contribution from s<=R to G_ik is bounded by p times the infinite-tree Green sum in(1); if k is outside the local ball, that contribution is zero. The remaining geometric tail is at most epsilon. Uniformly in i,k,

    G_ik<=2/(t+1)+epsilon.                          (3)

For adjacent i,j, use the radius-(R+1) ball about i. The infinite-tree component U in(2), intersected with this local ball, corresponds to a subset C of finite vertices; define C arbitrarily outside the ball, for instance by excluding all other vertices. Endpoints of walks of length<=R from either i or j match under the local isomorphism. Each side probability therefore differs from its infinite-tree counterpart by at most epsilon. Equation(2) implies

    G_i(C)-G_j(C)>=1/3-2epsilon,
    ||G_i-G_j||_1>=2/3-4epsilon.                    (4)

The factor2 in the second line is the finite probability total-variation identity. Since t=floor(sqrt(g)) and R=floor(g/8),

    epsilon=(t/(t+1))^(R+1)
       <=exp(-(R+1)/(t+1)) ->0.

Together with(3), for all sufficiently large g we have

    max_ik G_ik<=1/2,     ||G_i-G_j||_1>=1/3

on every graph edge. All constants are uniform over edges and vertices; no expansion or spectral gap of the finite graph is assumed.

## Star inputs and failure of the cotype estimate

Take a target unit star with root o and n leaves indexed by the graph vertices. For input i let mu_i put mass1/(n-1) at every leaf except i, and mass0 at i and at o. Its leaf flow is

    p_i(k)=(1-delta_ik)/(n-1).

Distinct inputs have W1 distance2/(n-1). The minimum of three sampled input values at leaf k equals1/(n-1) exactly when no sampled index equals k. Therefore the proposed output has leaf masses

    z_i(k)=(1-G_ik)^3/(n-1).                        (5)

Any deficit in their sum is mass at o, so these are indeed probability outputs. On[0,1/2], the absolute derivative of s↦(1-s)^3 is at least3/4. From(3)–(5) and the exact unit-star W1 cut formula, for every query edge ij,

    W1(nu_i,nu_j)
      =sum_k |z_i(k)-z_j(k)|
      >=[3/(4(n-1))]||G_i-G_j||_1
      >=1/[4(n-1)].                                (6)

Consequently the nonnegative variation part alone of the cotype left side is

    t sum_ij pi_i A_ij W1(nu_i,nu_j)^2
       >=t/[16(n-1)^2],                            (7)

because sum_ij pi_i A_ij=1. The Cesaro endpoint cost of the inputs is at most

    sum_ij pi_i [(1/t)sum_(s=1)^t A^s]_ij
               W1(mu_i,mu_j)^2
       <=4/(n-1)^2.                                (8)

A cotype inequality with this prescribed output and squared constant C² would force C²>=t/64. Since t tends to infinity, no universal C works for the minimum-of-three operator. The approximation term cannot repair the failure because it is nonnegative.

## Exact limitation

The endpoint-flatness issue in the pinned source is substantive. Minimum-of-three acts as s³ on a binary layer and has nonzero derivative at1; the complement-of-singleton star inputs expose that endpoint. The source's median cubic3s²−2s³ is flat at both0 and1, but its uncorrected flow output can violate probability constraints. This result rules out min3 as the missing feasible replacement; it gives no lower bound on the best feasible cotype witnesses. The full unchanged target remains open in this work, and direct constrained minimization or another feasible two-end-flat construction remains to be investigated.

## Corollary: even adaptive COMMON positive minimum mixtures fail

The same family excludes every operator whose output on these inputs is

    z_i(k)=F(1-G_ik)/(n-1),

where F:[0,1]→[0,1] is convex with F(0)=0,F(1)=1 and is COMMON to all indices and coordinates in the instance. In particular this includes every probability mixture of positive-order minimum samplers,

    F(s)=sum_(m>=1) a_m s^m, a_m>=0, sum a_m=1,

including countable mixtures. F may depend on t and on the entire input family. This result does not cover index-dependent or coordinate-dependent mixing rules, nor other operations.

Put H(x)=1-F(1-x). Then H is concave, H(0)=0,H(1)=1, and H is increasing for the stated minimum mixtures (monotonicity is unnecessary for the secant estimates below once F is convex with range[0,1]). Let M=max_ik G_ik. The finite estimates give M→0 and ||G_i-G_j||_1>=1/3 on query edges.

If H(M)<=1/2, concavity says that every secant slope on[0,M] is at least the slope from M to1, namely

    [1-H(M)]/[1-M]>=1/2.

Hence every query-edge output distance is at least1/[6(n-1)]. Comparing its variation energy with(8) forces C²>=t/144.

If H(M)>1/2, concavity between0 and M gives H(x)>=x H(M)/M for0<=x<=M. Since every row of G sums to1,

    L_i:=sum_k H(G_ik)>=H(M)/M>1/(2M).

For the complementary-star input, the exact approximation cost is

    W1(mu_i,nu_i)=[L_i+1-2H(G_ii)]/(n-1)
                   >=[L_i-1]/(n-1).

When M<=1/4 this is at least1/[4M(n-1)]. Averaging its square and comparing with(8) forces C²>=1/(64M²). Both alternatives diverge along the high-girth sequence: t→infinity and M→0. Thus no common convex F in this class, even chosen adaptively per instance, yields a universal cotype bound.

The parent suggested the fixed-mixture corollary and author-checked the strengthened dichotomy and constants. This remains technical elimination of an operator family, not a new target or a claim against constrained cotype itself.
