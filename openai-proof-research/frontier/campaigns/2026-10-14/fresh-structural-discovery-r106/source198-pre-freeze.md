## A paid positive grading
Choose integer heights h(v) increasing strictly along the directed order of D. Give e_v D e_w degree h(v)-h(w). The diagonal corners are scalar and all other nonzero D corners have positive degree. Choose N greater than max(h)-min(h), and give e_v X e_w degree N+h(v)-h(w). Multiplication by either D action adds degrees; X squared is zero. Thus A is positively graded with semisimple degree-zero part. This needs no new homological mechanism and is not a substantial candidate.

## The genuinely unpaid quadratic question
A homogeneous quadratic/Koszul realization cannot be inferred from the above grading. X may need bimodule generators in different degrees, and its relations and the differential encoded by the C arrows are not asserted linear. The source B is quadratic but that does not imply the final A is quadratic. Replacing high-degree generators by paths must preserve both finite-dimensionality and finite projective dimension of every test module, not merely represent the same module category after localization. No such bridge is proved here. No quadratic or Koszul target has been frozen.

## Why the naive substitutions do not close the interface
Introducing y for a long path ab produces y-ab, which is not homogeneous quadratic in ordinary path length. Padding with a new arrow t gives ty-ab, but recovering the original module category then requires t to act invertibly. This is a localization interface, exactly the kind already used in the source, and does not itself preserve finite projective dimensions over the unlocalized finite algebra. Nor does passage to an associated graded algebra supply the required upper bounds on projective dimension of the particular test modules. These are gaps in proposed routes, not a proof that quadratic realization is impossible.
