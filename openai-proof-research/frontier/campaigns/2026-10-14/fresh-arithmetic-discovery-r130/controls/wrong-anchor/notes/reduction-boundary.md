## Varying fields: the actual additional cost

For m selected real algebraic approximants x_i of individual degrees at most d0, the correct product-formula field is F_m=Q(x_1,...,x_m), not an arbitrary one of their individual fields. Its degree D_m can be as large as d0^m. The arithmetic lower bound proved in the working calculation has main cost D_m(1-bbar), with additional coefficient errors also proportional to D_m. The source parameter design would need

nu[A(1-eta)-theta]>D_m(1-theta).

Since A<sqrt(theta),

(A-theta)/(1-theta) < sqrt(theta)/(1+sqrt(theta)) < 1/2.

Thus the gap in this particular comparison requires nu>2D_m, already before the error budget is paid. One cannot fix a bounded-degree exponent nu and then run the source's growing-dimension selection while pretending D_m=d0. This observation is a limitation of this reduction. It is not a proof that no choice of finite parameters or different method can work, nor a lower bound for the true approximation exponent of pi.

A finite family of permitted fields causes no such problem: either work in their fixed compositum or, for a statement with separate thresholds, use pigeonhole and the fixed-field calculation. That is another routine composition, not the missing operation. Bounds on individual degrees alone do not imply a bounded compositum degree.

## Tested improvement: average analytic savings over embeddings

Taking a norm is not necessarily wasteful if every conjugate determinant also has the small analytic bound. Therefore it would be premature to stop at the compositum factor without checking whether conjugate structure saves it.

For an exploratory square-root model x_i=sqrt(a_i), a_i rational near pi^2, a conjugate sends r_i=2i*x_i to s_i*r_i, s_i in {+1,-1}, while fixing the rational coefficients of G_i(t). The conjugate row samples

P(1+t, j*s_i*r_i+G_i(t)+u_i).

A putative exact-period reference row on the same single z variable would use

f_{a,s,P}(z)=[u^a]P(e^z,s_1*z+u_1,...,s_m*z+u_m),
z=j*2pi*i+log(1+t).

The transverse error is exactly

j*s_i*(r_i-2pi*i) + G_i(t)-s_i*log(1+t).

When s_i=+1, the logarithmic remainder has order T_i, as required. When s_i=-1 and T_i>=2, it equals 2t+O(t^2), not a high-order tail. The source bound sum_i d_i*w_i < H/F0 came from the order T_i>=F0*w_i/v0. It cannot be applied to this first-order remainder. Many low-t-order copies can consume large transverse weights without any exp(-nu*w_i) saving. Merely replacing z by -z in the entire function does not repair the row identity with its original coefficient budget.

This is an exact incompatibility of the suggested conjugate averaging, not an unresolved sign in the source or a truth audit. The source itself remains unchanged.

## Tested normalization of slopes

One may try to choose algebraic slopes lambda_i=r_i/r_0 so that sign conjugation changes the logarithmic tangent too. At a fixed embedding, scaling X_i=lambda_i X'_i reduces interpolation to the source with centers j*r_0, so full rank is not the main obstacle. But the matrix now factors column/row powers of lambda_i and all centers depend on the same r_0. Two costs must be faced:

* If r_0 is an early approximant, lambda_i-1=(r_i-r_0)/r_0 has the accuracy of r_0 for later, much larger w_i; this does not give independent exp(-nu*w_i) errors.
* If r_0 is a very late accurate approximant, its height occurs in every ratio r_i/r_0. A crude product-formula charge is h(r_0) times the sum of degrees in those ratios. It is not the separated sum of the individual w_i costs needed for MH(1-bbar). Cancelling the scaling factors algebraically leaves a determinant whose centers all depend on r_0 and does not recreate m independent good-approximation scales.

These calculations do not prove that every slope construction must fail. They show why the two obvious integrations do not close the source budget. No degree-independent norm/jet package with both arithmetic subtraction and simultaneous analytic savings was constructed in this attempt.
