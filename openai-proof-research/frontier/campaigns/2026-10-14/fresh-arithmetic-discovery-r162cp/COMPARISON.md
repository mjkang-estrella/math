## Paid transport, Fourier and tree operations

Khot--Naor Lemma 3.6 identifies the transportation distance between uniform coset laws with the quotient metric by translating a minimizing pair. This exactly pays the identity W_1(mu_z,mu_z')=Q_m(z+z'). The factor-three coboundary norm bound and Source177 coisoperimetry then show that this entire metric family is already uniformly bi-Lipschitz to the weighted Hamming metric of its syndromes. In particular it has an ordinary L1 embedding of constant distortion. An L1 nonembedding argument for a different quotient cannot establish our lower bound merely by changing notation.

Khot--Naor's actual Walsh identities, translation-invariant Fourier support in a dual code, minimum dual distance argument, and cut-cone Poincare comparisons are paid. In a quotient by the graph cut space, invariant characters are supported on Eulerian edge sets; the graph has triangles, so minimum nonzero cycle size does not give log V. Our proof instead constructs characters from a rooted potential of f+s(delta f). These characters transform by prescribed endpoint signs. The resulting support is the affine boundary class partial A=u+v, whose minimum size is the distance between the actual vertices u,v. The same section supplies these characters for every pair and every edge-cube input. Applying original weighted vertex expansion to their common potential increments closes the average sensitivity inequality in PROOF.md (14). This works for every nonlinear section and every probability law on sections, without choosing a pair after observing the seed.

Li--Anantharam's primary v2 explicitly treats the same general pairwise multi-marginal transport optimization and its formulation as a distribution-preserving lifting. Their finite ground-space upper bound is O(log |ground space|), and their finite family upper bound is O(log |family|), using a sequential Poisson construction or coupling along a random tree, respectively. Here the ground edge cube has size 2^E, E=O_(d,q)(V), while the syndrome family has size 2^(E-V+1). These general bounds yield at best O(V) by direct application, not the logarithm of V. Their Proposition 42 gives an Omega(E) lower bound for ALL probability laws on an E-cube through Khot--Naor's L1 nonembedding. This does not specialize to the prescribed source fibers: the latter have the constant-distortion L1 representation just described. The fact that this transport optimization exists, the lifting interpretation, and general obstructions to simultaneous couplings are therefore fully paid.

Our upper construction is ordinary tree gauging plus source symmetry: tree rows have O(log V) length, and source edge-type orbit masses turn their total weighted row budget into expected column bounds. Adding one common uniform cut enforces every marginal without altering pair differences. These operations are elementary compatible comparisons and are not independently presented as substantial contributions. No finite-dimensional field construction, tabulation, inverse map or random-sampling cost is hidden; the theorem itself is existential, and PROOF.md records explicit-input implementation and expected-only exact sampling.
