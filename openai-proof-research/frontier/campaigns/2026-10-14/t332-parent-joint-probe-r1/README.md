# Joint finite retraction probe: no universal theorem

Author: Myeongjin Kang. Prose CC BY 4.0; code MIT.

This is parent-side research for the unchanged T332 target. It is not an independent review or an accepted result. The accepted campaign total remains 13/20. No uploads were made.

## Mathematical formulation

For a finite rooted tree, index edges so parents precede children. For positive edge lengths w, put N(x)=sum_e w_e |x_e| and

K={q: q_e >= sum_{f child of e} q_f for every e}.

The leaf inequalities imply nonnegativity throughout the tree. Choose finite anchors A in K and finite ambient points X. The unknown output q_x for each x in X must belong to K; set q_a=a for anchors. Require N(q_x-q_y)<=L N(x-y) for every pair in A union X, with L>=1. Introducing nonnegative auxiliaries above both signs of each output-coordinate difference makes this a linear program. Its feasibility is exactly feasibility of these finite assignments. Anchor-anchor inequalities hold automatically.

The optimization chooses all unknown outputs jointly. It imposes no nearest-point, entropy, electrical, or other predetermined projection formula. Thus a verified infeasible instance at a fixed L could obstruct an L-Lipschitz retraction for that tree. None was obtained here. The broader T332 extension target is not equivalent to this sufficient retraction route; a retraction obstruction would not itself refute T332.

For a fixed finite tree and fixed L, feasibility for **every** finite subset of rational ambient points, fixing its cone points and including zero, would imply a global L-Lipschitz retraction onto K. To see this, enumerate rational points and take assignments on increasing finite sets. Their outputs are bounded by L N(x) because zero is fixed. Diagonal subsequence compactness in finite-dimensional space gives compatible outputs on the whole rational set. All pair inequalities survive the limit. The map extends uniquely to the ambient space by completeness, with values in the closed cone. Rational cone points are dense in K: K is generated by the rational root-path incidence vectors with nonnegative coefficients. Consequently the extension fixes K. The converse follows by restriction. This elementary compactness observation does not replace the universal finite-feasibility premise.

## Outcomes

`results.jsonl` records 36 successful floating-point optimizations: four stem-star controls, eight path cases, eight seven-edge binary-tree cases, eight fifteen-edge binary-tree cases and eight nine-edge comb cases. Random choices use seed 3321743. The star controls have numerical optima 2k/(k+1) for k=2,3,4,8; the path cases have numerical optimum one. No exact dual certificates were generated.

`structured-results.jsonl` records three full seven-edge cube configurations, each with a 45-second solver timeout. `cutting-plane-results.jsonl` records a second attempt on these same configurations using separated norm inequalities; all three also timed out under a 60-second case budget. These are unresolved computations, not infeasibility certificates.

After these failures, `reduced-results.jsonl` records successful optimizations for reproducibly selected subsets of 24 unknown points from each cube, retaining every anchor. Seed 3322026 and the selected indices are recorded. These three successes apply only to the subsets. They do not resolve their corresponding full cubes.

`exact_check.py` reconstructs rational outputs from the 39 successful floating solutions, clips negative rounding noise and repairs any cone-rounding error upwards. It then checks every cone constraint and every pair inequality with exact `Fraction` arithmetic at L=2. **All 39 finite assignments pass.** A deliberately negative leaf output is rejected in every case. The rational outputs and exact maximum pair ratios are in `exact-check-result.json`.

The exact check establishes existence of the displayed finite assignments only. It does not establish their optimal constants, feasibility of untested configurations, a uniform all-tree retraction, or the full T332 theorem. Optimization status is not used as a mathematical proof of feasibility: the returned rational assignments are checked directly.

For reference, the stem-star controls have an elementary exact lower bound. At x=(k-1,1,...,1), use anchors v=(k,1,...,1) and a_j obtained from x by zeroing leaf j. Each is distance one from x. For any cone output (t,q_1,...,q_k), write S=sum q_j<=t. The sum of its distances to these k+1 anchors is at least 2k+(k-1)(t-S)>=2k, by summing the coordinatewise signed lower bounds. Hence L>=2k/(k+1). The feasible output t=k^2/(k+1), q_j=k/(k+1) attains it. This familiar finite control is not a campaign contribution.

## Replay and reading limits

Run `python3 exact_check.py` from any directory and compare the JSON with `exact-check-result.json`. It uses only the standard library. The optimization scripts require NumPy and SciPy; these runs used SciPy 1.18.1 in the existing `/tmp/agvenv` environment. `probe.py`, `structured_probe.py`, `cutting_plane.py`, and `reduced_probe.py` print JSON lines. Timing results are environment-dependent and need not reproduce exactly.

The parent read the r39 original T332 scope, the r42 constrained-optimization and path/branching notes, and source332 main text lines 180–410 while developing this probe. Earlier source-authentication records remain in the campaign; this packet makes no new source-body authentication claim. Bounded web searches produced possible literature leads (Godard; Mathey–Prevot–Valette; Hajek–Medina; Ambrosio–Puglisi), but no body from those leads was read or imported here. There is no literature-completeness or novelty assertion. No third-party bodies are included in this packet.

Next mathematical work would have to prove uniform finite feasibility, find an exact obstruction, or develop a different full-scope extension mechanism. More positive samples alone cannot close the target.
