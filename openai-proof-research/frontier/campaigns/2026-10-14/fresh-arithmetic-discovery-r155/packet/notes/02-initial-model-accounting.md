# Initial-model accounting: the strongest closed feasibility reduction

These are prefreeze derivations from the fully read generalized-canonical source, its independent analytic counting inputs, and the primary mechanisms identified in Note 01. They are retained as supporting mathematics, not a frozen substantial theorem or a replacement for an old target. No general-lc lifting, existence assertion, real-boundary extension, or moving-nef-data assertion is made.

## 1. Fix the objects before counting

Use EXACTLY the hypotheses and diagrams of generalized-canonical thm:termination, spelled out in Note 01. Thus the boundary is effective rational with coefficients below one; all global discrepancies are positive and exceptional log discrepancies are at least one; all models are normal globally Weil Q-factorial compact Kähler fourfolds; the rational analytically nef b-divisor and actual canonical representatives are fixed; both sides of each nontrivial small diagram are projective with connected fibres and the prescribed opposite ample signs. No prescribed base, relative-rank-one rule, pseudo-effectivity, or flip existence is added.

Fix m making mM' Cartier and clearing all boundary coefficients. Let b_1>...>b_s>0 be the DISTINCT positive coefficients. Put

    C = c_2(X_0),
    R_j = sum_{H component of B_0 with coefficient b_j} c_2(H^nu).

Here c_2(V) is the real dimension of the span of compact analytic surface classes in H_4(V,R); H^nu is the finite normalization of the reduced prime component. These are finite integers. Importantly, R_j uses normalizations, not the raw possibly nonnormal component.

Choose ONCE the projective smooth nef-carrying log resolution used in the source low-place proof, with positive strict boundary components smooth and disjoint and exceptional locus divisorial. Let F be the finite collection consisting of its exceptional primes and all below-two forced-chain places over each intersection of a positive strict component with an exceptional prime. It is harmless to include unused or above-threshold primes already on the resolution. Write f=|F|. This collection is attached to the initial data and chosen resolution, independently of the program. If e is the number of exceptional primes and I_d the number of irreducible intersections with positive strict components of coefficient d, the construction gives the elementary finite upper bound

    f ≤ e + sum_d (ceil(1/(1−d))−1) I_d.                    (1)

No uniform estimate for e or I_d has yet been inserted.

The source proof gives slightly more than its lemma's stated threshold: outside F, any exceptional E with a_0(E)<2 has centre an initial surface S in a unique coefficient-d boundary component, the chosen carrier is an isomorphism at its general point, and E is one of the forced generic blowup-chain divisors. Since the nef data descend there, they cancel on every higher local blowup, so

    a_0(E)=1+k(1−d),  k=1,2,..., k<1/(1−d).               (2)

To see that no extra choices occur, blow up S at its generic smooth point. If a place below two is not yet extracted, the differential estimate forces its next centre to be the intersection of the positive strict boundary and the newest exceptional divisor. The coefficient of that divisor is −k(1−d). Every other possible centre has discrepancy at least two. Iteration ends at the strict cutoff, exactly as in source eq:forced-chain-cutoff. Blowing up global closures realizes these generic places globally. A place over the same generic centre cannot give two different global primes with that same local divisor: its global closure is unique.

For 0≤b<1 define

    J(d,b) = max(0, ceil((1−b)/(1−d))−1).

This counts the positive integers k satisfying 1+k(1−d)<2−b. It vanishes for d≤b. In particular J(d,0)=ceil(1/(1−d))−1. The endpoint is STRICT: for d=2/3 the k=3 place has discrepancy two and is not included. Since d is a positive multiple of 1/m and d<1, J(d,b)≤m−1.

## 2. Exact rank accounting on normalized threefolds

The needed stronger statement is a standard negativity/localization consequence, the analytic counterpart of AHK Lemma 1.6. Let g:V→W be a projective bimeromorphic morphism of normal compact complex threefolds. Then

    c_2(V)−c_2(W) = number of g-exceptional prime divisors. (3)

Here the spaces in our application admit projective resolutions; all are finite normalizations of compact analytic subspaces of the source diagrams.

First suppose V is smooth. Choose an analytic A⊂W of dimension at most one off which g is an isomorphism, containing the exceptional image. Such a set exists by normality and bimeromorphicity. Its inverse image E has dimension at most two. Localization identifies H_4(W) with H_4^BM(W\A), since H_4(A)=H_3(A)=0. Pushforward maps surface spans surjectively: every surface downstairs meets that open and has an analytic strict transform. Its kernel is the image of H_4(E), generated by the fundamental classes of the two-dimensional irreducible components of E, precisely the exceptional primes.

These exceptional divisor classes are linearly independent. Any real homological relation among the integral classes would give a nonzero rational relation (compute the kernel over Q of the integral homology classes modulo torsion). The resulting rational divisor D on the smooth compact V has zero homology class. By Poincaré duality its first Chern class vanishes, so D has degree zero on every g-contracted curve. Both D and −D have zero pushforward. Apply analytic negativity to both signs; each is effective, whence D=0. This proves the independence and (3) for smooth V. No algebraic ample hyperplane on the ambient compact space was used.

For normal V take a projective smooth resolution h:T→V. Apply the smooth case both to h and to gh. Every h-exceptional prime is gh-exceptional, and the other gh-exceptional primes are exactly the strict transforms of the g-exceptional primes. Subtracting the two identities proves (3). This avoids assuming the Weil exceptional divisors on V are individually Cartier.

For a boundary component H_i of a given flip, its normalizations on the two sides map projectively and bimeromorphically to the normalization of their common image in Z_i. Normality gives factorization through that normalization; projectivity is preserved by the finite base change and the closed graph of the factorization. The exceptional divisors of those maps are exactly the prime branches above surfaces in H_i∩L_i or H_{i+1}∩L_i^+. Indeed the exceptional image downstairs has dimension at most one; elsewhere the maps are isomorphisms. Applying (3) twice and summing over components of coefficient b_j gives

    r_j(i)−r_j(i+1)=e_j^−(i)−e_j^+(i),                 (4)

where r_j(i)=sum c_2(H_{i,alpha}^nu), and e_j^± count prime NORMALIZATION branches, not surfaces with multiplicity ignored. This is an exact signed identity, valid also when both sides have exceptional surfaces.

At a flipped surface S the ambient target is smooth generically, and the source witness formula gives

    sum_l b_l mult_S(H_l) ≤ 1−1/m <1.                    (5)

The number of prime normalization branches of a reduced H_l above S is at most mult_S(H_l). For completeness, take a transverse smooth two-dimensional slice at a sufficiently general point of S. Each analytic branch of the resulting plane curve contributes a positive integer to its multiplicity. Global prime branches, including those whose maps to S have residue degree greater than one, give at least one such local branch apiece. Thus counting global prime branches is bounded by the sum of these multiplicities; ignoring residue degrees only weakens the bound. This is the same local branch-versus-multiplicity comparison used in AHK Lemma 2.6.

Consequently, if

    K_j=ceil(1/b_j)−1,

then e_j^+(i) ≤ K_j t_j(i), where t_j(i) counts the DISTINCT flipped surfaces contained in at least one coefficient-b_j component. A surface in multiple such components is counted once by t_j and all its branches are counted by e_j^+. The strict inequality in (5) justifies K_j; also K_j≤m−1.

Let A_j(N) count distinct surface occurrences on the SOURCE sides of the first N steps that lie in at least one coefficient-b_j component. Each such surface has at least one prime branch on a normalization. Summing (4), using nonnegativity of r_j(N), yields

    A_j(N) ≤ sum_{i<N}e_j^−(i)
           ≤ R_j + K_j T_j(N),
    T_j(N)=sum_{i<N}t_j(i).                              (6)

No independence of arbitrary codimension-two classes on the fourfold is asserted. Independence was proved only for exceptional DIVISORS after passing to a smooth resolution of a normal threefold.

## 3. Charge every later witness to an initial object

Choose the source's unique generic-blowup witness for EVERY target surface, not merely one per step. Witnesses of different surfaces in the same step are different places because they have different target centres. A fixed place can occur at most m times across all these occurrences: its target discrepancies at uses strictly increase in the same m-element grid. The source finite-use proof therefore applies unchanged to the total incidence count.

Fix a target surface counted in T_j(N), with witness E. Then a_0(E)<2−b_j. If E∈F, all such uses together cost at most mf. Otherwise (2) attaches E to a unique INITIAL good surface S in a boundary coefficient d>b_j, and to one of J(d,b_j) chain indices.

There must be a first step, no later than this witness use, at which the current strict transform of S is a flipping surface. Until such a step, source lem:persistence transports the centre of E across the common open at every step. Its target centre consequently meets that common open and cannot equal a flipped surface. This proves existence of the first loss; it is not assumed from the mere fact that the program is finite.

Distinct initial surfaces whose first loss occurs at the same step give distinct source surfaces there: the composite map is an isomorphism at their generic points up to that first loss, and strict transform is injective on such surfaces. Thus the number of initial coefficient-d surfaces first lost during this finite prefix is at most A_d(N). Each carries at most J(d,b_j) potential witness places and each place has at most m uses. We obtain

    T_j(N) ≤ m [ f + sum_{h<j} J(b_h,b_j) A_h(N) ].       (7)

Possible overlap of F and the charged chains only overcounts; a target surface lying in more than one boundary coefficient may occur in multiple T_j, which also only overcounts. A witness need not have the same centre after its first loss. That causes no gap: the charging is to its unique initial place, and the global m-use bound applies through every later centre change.

Equations (6)–(7) are triangular in the DISTINCT boundary coefficients. Define initial-data numbers recursively by

    U_j = m [ f + sum_{h<j} J(b_h,b_j) V_h ],
    V_j = R_j + K_j U_j,     j=1,...,s.                  (8)

Induction gives T_j(N)≤U_j and A_j(N)≤V_j for EVERY finite prefix, with no tail choice. At threshold b=0 all flipped surfaces, including those outside the boundary, satisfy the same charging argument. Their total number of occurrences P(N) obeys

    P(N) ≤ P_* := m [ f + sum_{j=1}^s J(b_j,0)V_j ].      (9)

This is the integrated reduction: the finite-place sets need not be recomputed on later models. The inputs f, R_j and m are explicit invariants of one initial resolved datum, though not furnished as uniformly bounded numbers over a family.

## 4. Close the ambient rank resets, rather than assume monotonicity

Let c_i=c_2(X_i). For one flip restrict the two surface spans to H_4^BM(U_i), where U_i is their common open. The images coincide, because strict transforms identify all nonexceptional surfaces and exceptional surfaces restrict to zero. Let k_i^± be the kernel dimensions on the source and target. Localization says each kernel is spanned by the classes of exceptional surfaces on that side. Hence

    c_i−c_{i+1}=k_i^−−k_i^+,
    0≤k_i^+≤p_i^+,

where p_i^+ is the number of target exceptional surfaces. These statements do not require the boundary to be normal and do not claim any isomorphism of full H_4 groups.

At a step with p_i^+=0, the source graph dimension inequality forces a source surface, and Kähler positivity makes its class nonzero. Thus k_i^−≥1. Summing the exact difference over a prefix, the number Q(N) of target-surface-free steps satisfies

    Q(N) ≤ sum k_i^− = C−c_N+sum k_i^+ ≤ C+P(N).

Every other step contributes at least one target surface, so its count is at most P(N). Combining with (9) gives the fixed-resolved-input estimate

    N ≤ C + 2P_* .                                      (10)

All finite prefixes obey it. This does not invoke König's lemma, finite branching of rays, or an implication from pointwise termination to bounded lengths. The derivation explicitly pays both multiple target surfaces per step and possible rank INCREASE at those steps.

If there is no positive boundary, s=0 and (10) becomes C+2mf. A coarser recurrence uses only m, f and the initial R_j: with S_0=0 and

    S_j = [1+m(m−1)^2]S_{j−1}+R_j+m(m−1)f,

one has sum_{h≤j}V_h≤S_j and hence

    N ≤ C+2m[f+(m−1)S_s].                               (11)

This is a bookkeeping bound from the paid source/primary apparatus. We do not claim it is sharp, algorithmically computable from an arbitrary analytic presentation, new to the literature, or substantial enough for nomination. In particular, providing f as an unrestricted input does not close the proposed family-uniform operation in Note 03. No theorem was frozen or author-proof nominee developed.
