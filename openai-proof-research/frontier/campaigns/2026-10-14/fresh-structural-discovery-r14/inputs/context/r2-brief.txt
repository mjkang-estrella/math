## A sharper junction criterion for a fixed combined root basis

Suppose the combined roots of one fixed two-segment system form an
independent basis B of R^n, with endpoint equation Bm=w in Q. Let F
denote its first-segment columns. Its junction obeys

    z-(a+b)=(u_k b-b)-B_F m_F.

Thus z is in the required coset exactly when B_F m_F is in Q.
The signed graph of B is a disjoint union of unbalanced unicyclic
components. An odd component means that the coordinate sum of w on
that component is odd. The exact criterion is:

1. The first-segment columns contain all or none of the cycle edges
   of every odd component.
2. The number of odd cycles wholly in the first segment is even.

To prove it, prune trees to obtain integral coefficients there. The
unbalanced cycle equations have determinant two, giving integral cycle
coefficients on even components and half-odd coefficients on odd ones.
Modulo integers, twice the selected odd-cycle contribution is its edge
incidence boundary over F2. Only the empty and full subsets of a cycle
have zero boundary, including a signed two-edge cycle. A fully selected
odd cycle has odd integral coordinate sum, since subtracting its
integral tree contributions from w subtracts only even-sum roots.
These facts prove both conditions. The signed-graph arithmetic is
credited to the source; this is its application to the junction projection.

Two odd cycles in the SAME segment can therefore give a correct
junction with fractional switches. Integral coefficients everywhere
are stronger than Q4 needs. This is a conditional criterion, not a
claim that every input has a feasible combined basis. Separate basic
schedules may have up to 2n roots, and a global basis reduction is unpaid.

The additional operation selected here is coefficient-preserving removal of all torsion for arbitrary torsion-free G and arbitrary nonsingular systems.

## An exact equivalent gap, with proof

Identify G with its embedded image in U. Set T_0=1 and, recursively,

           T_{s+1}=<<u in U : u^k in T_s for some k>=1>>_U,
           T_omega=union_{s>=0} T_s.

This construction is already in Chiodo, [arXiv:1107.1489v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.1489v3), **Definition 3.9 and Proposition 3.10**, crediting the torsion-free radical of Brodsky--Howie. It is not an original mechanism.

Each T_s is normal and the sequence increases. If (u T_omega)^k=1 then u^k belongs to some finite T_s, so u belongs to T_{s+1}. Thus U/T_omega is torsion free. For any homomorphism f:U->H with H torsion free, induction shows T_s subset ker f: a k-th power in ker f forces its root into ker f, and normal closure preserves inclusion. Hence T_omega subset ker f.

Consequently full TFS for this input is equivalent to

                          G intersect T_omega(U)=1.          (*)

If (*) holds, U/T_omega itself is a required solution group. Conversely every torsion-free solution factors the presentation map through a homomorphism U->H injective on G, and that map kills T_omega. Equivalently, one must establish G intersect T_s(U)=1 at every finite stage. This reformulation retains all frozen quantifiers and is not a proof of (*).

Suppose v in G*F_n represents torsion in U, with v^k in <<w_i>>. A finite normal-closure expression and the exponent homomorphism E:G*F_n->Z^n give

                       k E(v)=sum_i d_i E(w_i),  d_i in Z.

Thus E(v) is in the rational row span of A. Adjoining v=1 adds a dependent row, so Theorem 1 cannot be invoked again. Inductively the same is true for every word adjoined at a later finite radical stage: all earlier new exponent vectors lie in the original row span, and taking a root preserves membership in that rational span. Nothing here proves that a coefficient is actually lost. It proves that the proposed iteration has left the source theorem's hypotheses exactly where a new preservation theorem is needed.

The rational abelianized part is paid: write V=G_ab tensor Q. Then

 U_ab tensor Q = (V direct-sum Q^n)/span_Q{(c_i,a_i)},

where c_i is the coefficient class of w_i and a_i its exponent row. The map V into this quotient is injective, since a combination with zero variable component has sum_i lambda_i a_i=0, forcing all lambda_i=0. The target is a torsion-free abelian group and therefore kills T_omega. It follows that G intersect T_omega(U) lies in ker(G->G_ab tensor Q). The hard obstruction can remain in the rational derived subgroup; this calculation does not eliminate it.

## A checked primary example: torsion removal can create torsion

Chiodo's discussion after Proposition 3.10 gives

                     P=<x,y,z | x^2,y^3,xy z^{-6}>.

Here is the full relevant calculation. In A=C_2*C_3, xy has infinite order by reduced-word normal form. Thus P=A*_{<xy>=<z^6>}<z>, with both edge maps injective, and z has infinite order. A finite-order element of an amalgam is conjugate into a vertex group: a cyclically reduced word of length at least two has powers of positive length and cannot have finite order. Applying the same observation in A shows all torsion of P is conjugate into <x> or <y>. Hence

                 T_1(P)=<<x,y>>,  P/T_1(P)=C_6,  T_2(P)=P.

The quotient really is C_6 because setting x=y=1 leaves exactly z^6=1, with a surjection onto a cyclic group of order six. Thus z acquires torsion only after the first removal.

For any torsion-free G, the same three equations with trivial coefficients have matrix rows (2,0,0),(0,3,0),(1,1,-6), determinant -36. Their universal group is G*P, while its first and second radical quotients are G*C_6 and G. This actual TFS input disproves automatic torsion-freeness of U and sufficiency of a single torsion-removal step. It has a torsion-free solution x=y=z=1 in G and is not a counterexample to TFS.

If instead C=<z> is the coefficient group and x,y the two unknowns, the same relations preserve the infinite cyclic C in P, but every torsion-free solution forces x=y=z=1. This is an actual failure of coefficient-preserving de-torsion for an unrestricted system. Its exponent matrix has THREE rows and only two columns, with rank two. It violates the frozen nonsingularity hypothesis and is explicitly not offered as a TFS counterexample. The corresponding nonsingular root-adjunction repair is recorded in `torsion-tower-checkpoint.md`, with its limited scope.

# Checkpoint 1: the exact torsion-free quotient operation

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

Fix the entire frozen TFS input and put U=(G*F_n)/N, N=<<w_1,...,w_m>>. The stipulated Theorem 1 embeds G in U. Define normal subgroups

T_0(U)=1,
T_{s+1}(U)=<< u in U : u^k in T_s(U) for some integer k>=1 >>_U,
T_omega(U)=union_{s>=0} T_s(U).

This is the standard torsion-free radical construction, not a new operation: Chiodo, arXiv:1107.1489v3, Definition 3.9 and Proposition 3.10, explicitly give it and identify it with the radical of Brodsky--Howie. We reproduce the proof to expose what still has to be established relative to the coefficients.

Every T_s is normal, the sequence increases, and its union is normal. If (u T_omega)^k=1 in U/T_omega, then u^k lies in some T_s, hence u lies in T_{s+1}. Thus U/T_omega is torsion free. If f:U->H has torsion-free target, induction gives T_s subset ker f: if u^k is in ker f, then f(u)^k=1 and f(u)=1; normal closure preserves this inclusion. The union is also in ker f. Consequently every torsion-free image factors through U/T_omega.

It follows, with no changed quantifiers, that TFS for this input is equivalent to

                     G intersect T_omega(U) = 1.             (R)

Indeed (R) makes U/T_omega the required torsion-free solution group. Conversely any torsion-free solution induces f:U->H injective on G and killing T_omega, forcing (R). Since the union is increasing, (R) is equivalent to G intersect T_s(U)=1 for EVERY finite s. This is a full-strength reformulation, not a proof of TFS. No transfinite iteration beyond omega is necessary.

## Why a second appeal to the pinned theorem is unavailable

At stage zero let a word v in G*F_n represent an element of finite order k in U. There is an equality

v^k = product_l c_l w_{i_l}^{epsilon_l} c_l^{-1}

in G*F_n. Apply the exponent homomorphism E:G*F_n->Z^n killing G. One obtains

k E(v)=sum_i d_i E(w_i),  d_i in Z.

Thus E(v) is in the Q-row span of A. Adjoining the relation v=1 gives an augmented exponent matrix with a DEPENDENT new row. Theorem 1's full-row-rank hypothesis has been lost. This is not repaired by the fact that v had finite order, or by the fact that G remains torsion free.

The same obstruction persists at every finite radical stage. Inductively all words adjoined earlier have exponent vectors in the original rational row span. If v^k is in their normal closure together with N, then E(v) lies in that span as well. Thus iterative removal of torsion never gives a new independent exponent row to which the pinned theorem directly applies. This calculation does not imply that removal actually kills a coefficient; it identifies precisely why the proposed induction has no available theorem.

One would need to prove coefficient preservation under these dependent torsion consequences. Simply asserting that a quotient of a coefficient-injective group is still coefficient-injective is false, as the explicit singular example in the next checkpoint shows.

## A small paid detection bound

Let V=G_ab tensor_Z Q. Rational abelianization gives

U_ab tensor Q = (V direct-sum Q^n) / span_Q{(c_i,a_i)}.

Here c_i is the total coefficient class of w_i and a_i its exponent row. The map V into this quotient is injective: an element of the span with zero Q^n component has coefficients lambda_i satisfying sum lambda_i a_i=0, so every lambda_i=0 by rank_Q A=m. The rational vector group U_ab tensor Q is torsion free, hence every map to it kills T_omega. Therefore

G intersect T_omega(U) subset ker(G->G_ab tensor Q).

This is a supporting restriction, not the missing theorem: a torsion-free group can have a large rational derived subgroup, including the whole group when it is perfect.

# Checkpoint 2: an actual torsion tower, and its exact rank boundary

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

The following example is credited to the discussion immediately after Chiodo, arXiv:1107.1489v3, Proposition 3.10. It is NOT an original counterexample or a solution of TFS. The normal-form calculation below checks the operational point without a finite search.

Let

                   P=<x,y,z | x^2=1, y^3=1, xy=z^6>.

Write A=C_2*C_3=<x,y|x^2,y^3>. The word xy has infinite order: its positive powers are reduced words of length 2k, and the inverse powers are likewise nontrivial. Hence

                P = A *_{<xy>=<z^6>} <z>

is a free product with amalgamation along an infinite cyclic subgroup, with both edge maps injective. Normal form embeds both vertex groups. In particular z has infinite order in P.

A finite-order element of an amalgam is conjugate into a vertex group. For completeness, conjugate a word to cyclically reduced normal form. If its amalgam length is at least two, all positive powers retain positive length, so it cannot have finite order. Words of length zero or one lie in a vertex group up to conjugacy. In A the same free-product argument places every finite-order element in a conjugate of C_2 or C_3; <z> has no torsion. Consequently

T_1(P)=<<x,y>>_P,
P/T_1(P)=<z|z^6>=C_6,
T_2(P)=P.

The displayed C_6 is exactly cyclic of order six: sending x,y to 1 and z to a generator of C_6 is a surjection, and those are all remaining relations. Thus removing all original torsion creates new torsion represented by the previously infinite-order z.

For any torsion-free G, the three equations over G with these words but no nonidentity coefficients have exponent matrix

                 [2 0  0]
                 [0 3  0]
                 [1 1 -6],       determinant -36.

Their universal group is U=G*P. Finite-order elements of this free product are conjugate into P, because G is torsion free. Hence

U/T_1(U)=G*C_6,       U/T_2(U)=G.

This is a genuine nonsingular system within TFS's scope, and it has the torsion-free solution x=y=z=1 in G. It refutes both the claim that its universal group is automatically torsion free and the claim that one torsion-removal step must suffice. It does NOT refute TFS or coefficient preservation at all stages.

There is also a concrete warning about omitting the rank hypothesis. Regard the embedded infinite cyclic subgroup C=<z> of P as the coefficient group and x,y as the two unknowns. The same three equations now have exponent rows (2,0),(0,3),(1,1). The rank is 2 with 3 equations, so the system is NOT nonsingular. It is solvable in P, preserving C. In every torsion-free solution, x^2=y^3=1 forces x=y=1, whence z^6=1 and then z=1. Thus it has NO torsion-free solution preserving C. This is an actual failure of arbitrary coefficient-injective de-torsion, located precisely outside TFS. No singular counterexample is offered as the frozen target's counterexample.

As a consistency check on that boundary, add a third unknown u and replace xy=z^6 by xy=z^6 u^k, k!=0. The three rows now have determinant -6k and are independent. This particular system has a torsion-free solution: set x=y=1 and adjoin a k-th root of z^{-6}. If z!=1, amalgamate G with an infinite cyclic root group over the two infinite cyclic subgroups; normal form preserves G and torsion-freeness. If z=1 choose u=1. This elementary repair pays this family only, not general nonsingular systems.

# Checkpoint 3: full-scope attempts and remaining obstruction

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

## Finite coefficient reduction

Every finite system uses only a finitely generated subgroup C of the torsion-free coefficient group G. If it has a torsion-free solution over C in H_C, then G*_{C}H_C is a torsion-free solution over G. Both vertex groups embed by normal form, and a finite-order element is conjugate into a vertex group, as in checkpoint 2. Conversely a theorem for every G includes finitely generated G. Thus it suffices to settle finitely generated coefficient groups, but not merely finitely presented, recursively presented, solvable, or orderable ones. This is an elementary reduction, not a restricted success.

Rectangular systems can be reduced to square systems by retaining m columns of a nonzero m-by-m minor and setting the other variables to 1. The resulting exponent matrix is nonsingular. This standard reduction is already stated in the pinned source's historical discussion of Gerstenhaber--Rothaus. It is credited, not nominated.

## Attempt A: diagonalize and solve successive equations

Nielsen operations on the variables realize GL_n(Z) on exponent columns while fixing G. Inverting, permuting and multiplying relators realize elementary unimodular row operations and preserve their normal closure. Smith normal form therefore diagonalizes the exponent matrix, with nonzero invariant factors d_i. This says nothing about the actual occurrence of other variables inside commutators. For example x [a,y]^{-1} c^{-1} and y [b,x]^{-1} d^{-1} have identity exponent matrix, although both relators contain both variables. No assertion that this specific system cannot be transformed further is needed or proved.

Forester--Rourke Theorem 1 has an actual layered hypothesis: each new relation involves only previously adjoined variables and its one new variable. In the square unimodular case its diagonal exponents are +/-1, and their relative-asphericity argument propagates torsion-freeness by Proposition 1. Smith normal form of augmentation rows is not such a layering of words. Applying that theorem after matrix diagonalization would silently add an unproved word-level operation. For d_i>1 even the exponent-one hypothesis is absent. TFS allows arbitrary d_i and has not been proved this way.

## Attempt B: the source's planar proof after torsion removal

The pinned Proposition 4.1 pairs EVERY variable slot inside a planar union of signed relator disks. Its boundary components carry coefficients only. The balance equations are

                  sum_i (p_i-m_i) a_ij = 0.

Row independence gives p_i=m_i for every i. The positive and negative arrays of equal size permit the involution J; Theorem 2 then supplies the fixed-space bound. Those are actual hypotheses of the proof, not cosmetic notation.

For a torsion relation v^k in N, the outside word v^k usually has variable slots. The exponent balance is instead

                  sum_i d_i a_ij = k E_j(v).

It need not vanish. The balanced arrays and coefficient-only exceptional boundary of Proposition 4.1 are missing. Substituting a dependent additional relator v=1 fails the rank condition by checkpoint 1. A new punctured-surface or quotient-preservation theorem would have to pay these changes; the existing fixed-space bound alone does not. The example G*P also prevents proving torsion-freeness of the unmodified universal quotient as an unnecessarily strong replacement for TFS.

## Attempt C: residual solvable detection

Let G_r^(0)=G and G_r^(j+1)=ker(G_r^(j)->(G_r^(j))_ab tensor Q). Each quotient G/G_r^(j) has a finite subnormal series with torsion-free abelian factors. Klyachko--Mikheenko--Roman'kov (KMR), arXiv:2303.13240v1, Main theorem and its explicit all-torsion-free-factor corollary, already supply a torsion-free solution over every such quotient. Compose the induced U->H_j with the coefficient quotient. By checkpoint 1 it kills T_omega(U), so

                 G intersect T_omega(U) subset intersection_j G_r^(j).

If this intersection is trivial, this proves TFS for G, but this is a standard residual closure of the KMR operation (their Proposition 2 supplies the quasivariety framework). No new substantial result is claimed, and this is not substituted for the frozen all-group assertion. For a perfect torsion-free G the right side is G itself, so this argument supplies no detection.

A perfect torsion-free group exists without any appeal to the pinned Artin family. Let F_j be free on 2^(j+1) letters and embed F_j in F_{j+1} by sending its l-th generator to the commutator of the (2l-1)-st and 2l-th new generators. These maps are injective: powers of each cyclically reduced commutator remain reduced, and consecutive distinct generator blocks use disjoint alphabets, so a nonempty reduced word cannot vanish. Their direct limit L is nontrivial and torsion free, since each element and any finite power relation occur in a free stage. Every stage generator becomes a commutator at the next stage, so L=[L,L]. Thus every map from L to a nilpotent group is trivial: its image is both perfect and nilpotent, and a perfect group's lower central series is constant, which is incompatible with nontrivial nilpotence.

Consequently a blanket embedding of all torsion-free groups into a separated, integer-degree Magnus group (whose degree truncations are nilpotent) is false. This observation does not rule out other filtered constructions, the more general real-degree monoids in Ramirez-Cote--Wise, or solving finite systems over L. Indeed finite coefficient subgroups of L lie in a free stage. It is a limitation of that particular global completion shortcut, not a TFS counterexample.

Ramirez-Cote--Wise Theorems 9--10 already solve square nonsingular systems for groups EMBEDDED into their specified 1+K power-series groups, inverting det(A) in the scalar ring. Their embedding assumption cannot be dropped just by invoking nonsingularity. KMR's Main theorem with K equal to the class of all torsion-free groups would also be circular: its premise that K is already a Howie quasivariety is precisely the unsolved assertion here.

## What would finish the target

For every original nonsingular system, prove G intersect T_s(U)=1 for every finite s, or construct one system for which that intersection is nontrivial. Neither has been done. All reductions preserve the original quantifiers; none supplies the coefficient-preserving dependent-relator step. The known constructions and explicit torsion tower above are technical support. No complete proof or genuine counterexample to full TFS is proposed.
