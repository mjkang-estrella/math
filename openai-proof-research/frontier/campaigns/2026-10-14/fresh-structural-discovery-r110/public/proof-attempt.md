## 1. A general obstruction to retaining the source's baseline gate

This is a proof about a specified source architecture, not an impossibility theorem for T111I110 or all one-sample rules.

Fix the actual source kappa=2^100, B=2^32, and an integer m>2 kappa. For arbitrary integers r>=2 and q>=1 construct a multigraph as follows. There are vertices x,y, v_1,...,v_r and u_1,...,u_q. Include e=xy; m parallel copies of each xv_i (class A_i) and each yv_i (class B_i); and bridges z_j=xu_j. Also add an auxiliary loop of weight0 at x. Let M be its labelled cycle matroid. Give z_j weight3/2, e weight5/4, and every spoke copy weight1. Break rounded-weight ties by putting all z_j first, then e, then the branches, with fixed orders inside branches. Every positive rounded weight is1. The auxiliary zero-weight loop never participates in an independent set or any candidate group.

Every optimum includes all bridges and e. With e, choose one spoke at each v_i, obtaining weight3q/2+r+5/4. Without e, a forest can use at most r+1 spokes and has weight at most3q/2+r+1. Thus e belongs to every optimum. Fix one optimum I* by choosing the preferred spoke of each branch. Since r>=2, the total weight of optimum elements lighter than e exceeds the heaviest single weight3/2. Consequently e is outside the standard FSZ light tail. In the literal Definition5.2 threshold convention, the auxiliary zero-weight loop ensures that threshold0 is available and the empty light tail is well-defined: threshold1 already includes total optimum weight r>3/2, so L(OPT) is empty.

Consider the source main branch. Write H,D for its independent masks of rates1/2 and1/4. Define the branch event G_i that all2m spoke copies in branch i have H=0 and D=1. Its probability is 8^(-2m), independently between branches.

On G_i, every spoke in that branch is a candidate: no higher-priority H-element has a v_i component (equivalently, no H-edge touches v_i), so it cannot span either spoke. This remains true even if higher-priority H-edges elsewhere already connect x to y. Hence all2m spokes belong to the sole true group U_0 and its density sample D_h; the group is listed.

Let F_i be the rank-two flat generated by A_i union B_i. It contains e. If a flat Q omits e, it cannot contain both spoke classes of this branch, since either one edge of each class spans e. Thus at least m sampled spokes lie outside Q. Adjoining F_i increases rank by at most2. For the actual density objective f(Z)=|D_h intersect Z|-kappa r_M(Z),

    f(cl(Q union F_i))-f(Q) >= m-2 kappa > 0.

Therefore every density maximizer, in particular Q_h(L), contains e. If H_e=1, the rule already sacrifices e. If H_e=0, e passes the candidate filter because its only higher-priority labels are the independent bridges; on G_i its nominal birth is exactly a_h, the baseline birth rejected by the source eligibility test. No modification of post-arrival greedy decisions or discarded selections can make this e pass that unchanged pre-arrival gate.

It follows for the main branch that

    P(e is ever proposed) <= (1-8^(-2m))^r.

For the maximum branch, e cannot pass its sample maximum if any of the q higher-priority bridges is revealed. Therefore, even if that branch is generously allowed to continue proposing after discarded proposals,

    P(e is ever proposed) <= 2^(-q).

For the source's fair branch mixture the upper bound is

    [ (1-8^(-2m))^r + 2^(-q) ] / 2.                 (1)

For fixed m and the actual source constants, (1) tends to zero as r,q tend to infinity. All instances are finite; this argument uses no feasible-size claim and no numerical simulation of the enormous source constants.

Now give a proposed weak-opt extension the switching adversary that discards *every* proposal, so T1 is empty and T2 is the proposed set. Its required pathwise inequality reduces to

    w(T2 intersect O) >= w(O).

All weights in OPT are strictly positive (the loop is not independent), so O is a subset of T2. Hence P(e in O) is at most (1). No positive universal per-element weak-opt constant can hold for extensions retaining these pre-arrival gates and the sample-maximum restriction. This even defeats the weaker non-full-seed weak-opt interface for this architecture; it does not rely on exchanging an expectation and minimum.

There is no contradiction with source111. It asserts aggregate welfare, and the distinguished e is an arbitrarily small part of the optimum as r,q grow. The source's baseline loss is explicitly permitted and paid by population/rank accounting. The construction neither audits nor disputes a stipulated source claim.

## 2. A limited repair, with the full target still open

Allow baseline births b=a_h in the source main branch, retaining candidate tests, sacrifice, test thinning, parity, the final-flat exclusion, and greedy independence within each layer. This is a modification of the attempted algorithm, not a change to the frozen target.

The source's feasibility proof remains applicable: every eligible e lies in X_h(b) subset Fhat(b), each layer is selected independently over Fhat(b-2), and earlier layers lie in that base. Baseline b=a_h is a legitimate auxiliary endpoint, and paths have their given finite range. Measurability is unchanged.

The original welfare lower bound is also preserved by the *proof*, not by a false claim that greedy reward is monotone under extra arrivals. Keep the original lambda_h statistics on original valid births only. For any expanded execution, the selected set in a layer is still a basis of its enlarged eligible set. Every selected lower-weight element born at b, including a newly allowed baseline element, is already in the source comparison set

    K_(h,b)=union_(j<h) [T_j intersect (X_j(b) minus X_j(b-1))].

That set was defined without removing baseline births. Therefore the proof of `lem:all-orders` still gives N_(>=h)>=lambda_h. The source's unchanged thinning, rank-cost and weighted-sum estimates yield the original 2^-293 fixed-vector welfare guarantee. This is a technical reuse of paid operations, not a nomination.

For the one-group obstruction above, Fhat(a_h-2)=L. When e is at the baseline and is unsacrificed, tested and of the chosen parity, the repaired rule may now propose e. Thus Section1 must not be advertised as an obstruction to this repaired rule.

What remains unproved is a full-seed weak-opt credit system for the repaired rule. The old estimates give, for n_i>=kappa,

    E[lambda_i | H] >= (t/kappa) [gamma |Y_i|
      -(2^-23+t kappa)n_i -(1+t kappa) sum_(l>i)n_l].    (2)

Replacing the independent comparison subset Y_i by S subset Y_i in the safe-count proof retains the same n_i and later-group debts. It does not turn (2) into a rank bound for the exchange-neighborhood of S. For small S the right side can be negative. A capacitated assignment of uniformly probable individual credits needs simultaneous inequalities for those subsets (and compatible pointwise charging under arbitrary discards), which (2) does not state. Ordinary basis exchange or a fractional matching theorem cannot supply missing capacity inequalities.

I also considered recording a credit whenever a selected element spans an optimum element. This is not a valid allocation by itself: one selected direction may lie in many fundamental circuits; such credits compete for the same weight capacity. Counting each circuit separately double-counts the selection. Assigning all later-generator losses once globally, as the source correctly does, likewise does not bound their concentration on a specified credit subset. No localized capacity estimate or charging rule closing this gap was proved.

## 3. Why two simple substitute formalisms do not close it

The rank of a common independent set is not a matroid rank. On the three edges a,b,c of a path, the two endpoint partition constraints give matching rank. For A={a,b}, B={b,c}, one has r(A)=r(B)=r(A intersect B)=1 and r(A union B)=2, violating submodularity. Thus the source supermodular density proof cannot be applied after replacing rank by common-independent-set rank.

Taking an alternating closure under the two matroids also does not give the source generator budget. On a path with2s edges, take the two partition matroids imposed by alternating internal vertices. Starting from the end edge, alternating closures propagate along the path and eventually include every edge, although their common independence number is s. This holds for every s>=1. One generator can therefore cause unbounded common-rank growth in that closure. It refutes this particular common-closure surrogate, not paired flat paths with a new argument.

Keeping two separate flat paths avoids that algebraic error but creates a genuine intersection problem inside their two-dimensional array of layers. Separate total ranks do not lower-bound common rank. In the simplest free-matroid example, two permitted ground sets can be disjoint and each have half of the full rank. A lower bound requires common-set/cut information, not the two marginal rank totals. The source has no such two-path Hall-cut assertion.

## 4. Exact remaining interface

A sufficient interface would be a distribution-independent fixed-vector selection rule for each matroid, with its mask chosen before weights, whose seed determines a set F_R subset OPT, independent of arrival order and discard decisions, with P(e in F_R)>=a>0 for every e outside the standard light tail. For every switched execution it would satisfy

    w(F_R minus proposed) <= b w(T1 minus F_R),

with absolute b<infinity, while keeping T1 feasible. This is stronger than the printed FSZ weak-opt quantifier; it is stated as a sufficient *unproved* interface, not as a supplied source theorem or an equivalent reformulation of T111I110.

If that interface existed with compatible masks, independent seeds would give independent F_R membership conditional on the fixed filtered weights. FSZ's actual greedy-relevance overlap supplies factor1/16. Its light-tail argument loses at most factor2 on the common optimum. Its deterministic two-algorithm charging then gives factor1/max(2b,1). Thus the resulting hidden-vector fraction would be at least a^2/[32 max(2b,1)]. Merging the seed-determined sacrificed masks and applying the source gluing identity would give the frozen one-sample target. The final composition is a credited technical step. The new work needed is the uniform full-seed credit interface or a different complete two-matroid construction.

No such interface has been established. Neither the baseline-inclusive welfare lemma, the graphic obstruction, nor this conditional composition is nominated or counted. T111I110 is UNRESOLVED at its original scope.
