## 1. The exact lifting input

K permits a countable characteristic-zero field k containing all roots of unity, a finite central division algebra A/k, and a finite tuple X of free series over A in two noncommuting variables. The constant tuple must generate A; the coefficients of the two letters must be independent modulo the space of simultaneous inner derivations of that constant tuple. The theorem increases the division degree, preserves every polynomial identity of the full series over the old field, algebraizes a selected series, and restores generation and both directions. The fields can grow transcendently. The finite division algebras do not form an embedding tower.

The question examined here was whether fixed low degrees of the two generators could survive this contract, including its two-direction condition. Merely inserting equations of the constant tuple would not work. The following constructs the identities on the full series.

## 2. An explicit split witness for the quadratic/cubic seed

On a six-dimensional vector space with basis e_i,f_i, i modulo 3, take weights (a_0,a_1,a_2)=(2,3,5). Define

    S e_i = a_i f_i,       S f_i = a_i^(-1) e_i,
    T e_i = e_(i+1),       T f_i = f_(i-1).

Then S²=1 and T³=1. Put r_i=a_(i+1)/a_i, so (r_0,r_1,r_2)=(3/2,5/3,2/5). For R=(ST)²,

    R e_i = r_i e_i,       R f_i = r_(i-1) f_i.

The two diagonal operators R and SRS have respective joint eigenvalue pairs

    (r_i,r_(i-1)) on e_i,    (r_(i-1),r_i) on f_i.

These six ordered pairs are distinct. R alone has only three distinct eigenvalues; it does NOT by itself provide six coordinate projections. For each coordinate j and every other coordinate l, choose whichever of R,SRS separates their eigenvalues, subtract the l-eigenvalue, and divide by the nonzero difference. Multiplying these five factors produces the j-th coordinate projection. Thus the algebra generated by S,T contains every diagonal matrix unit.

The directed monomial support of S,T is strongly connected: T cycles each triple and S joins corresponding coordinates in the two triples. Sandwiching a word along a directed path between two coordinate projections yields a nonzero multiple of the corresponding matrix unit. Hence S,T generate M_6 over the ground field. This is a symbolic argument, with an exact rational diagnostic in seed_check.py; the diagnostic is not a substitute for generic descent.

## 3. Realizing the witness inside a division algebra

Over k_0=Qbar(alpha,beta), form the generic symbol algebra A_0 of degree 6 with

    U^6=alpha, V^6=beta, VU=zeta_6 UV.

The source's `lem:symbol-division`, including its skew Laurent proof, makes A_0 division and keeps it division under pure rational extensions. Take a basis b_1,...,b_36 and independent central variables t_1,...,t_36. Over k=k_0(t_1,...,t_36), let A=A_0 tensor k and g=sum t_j b_j. This nonzero element is invertible. Put

    u=U^3,       v=g V^2 g^(-1).

Then u²=alpha and v³=beta. These are separable minimal equations of degrees 2 and 3: irreducibility follows, for example, from the alpha and beta valuations before the rational extension. After splitting A, u has eigenvalue multiplicities (3,3), and V² has multiplicities (2,2,2).

The split witness above shows that some pair in these two conjugacy classes generates all M_6: scale S and T by roots of alpha and beta, and simultaneously conjugate the first matrix to the specified u. Consequently some determinant of 36 word-coordinate columns is nonzero as a rational function of the entries of g. The split images of b_j form a matrix basis, so the t_j really give independent matrix-entry coordinates; this determinant remains nonzero at the generic g. Its nonvanishing descends by scalar extension. Thus u,v generate A over k. We did not assume that an arbitrary pair in those conjugacy classes generates, or that arbitrary algebraic scalar extensions preserve division.

Let C_u,C_v be the centralizers in A. Their dimensions are 18 and 12, by the split eigenvalue multiplicities. Their intersection is k, since u,v generate A. Therefore

    dim_k A/(C_u+C_v) = 36-(18+12-1)=7.

Choose h_1,h_2 whose classes in this quotient are independent. In the free-series algebra set

    H=1+h_1 z_1+h_2 z_2,
    X=(u, H v H^(-1)).

H is invertible by its constant term. Crucially,

    X^(1)²=alpha,       X^(2)³=beta

hold as full noncommutative formal identities. The two letter coefficients are (0,[h_j,v]). If a linear combination is ([c,u],[c,v]), its first coordinate gives c in C_u and its second gives a h_1+b h_2-c in C_v. Independence in A/(C_u+C_v) forces a=b=0. This verifies exactly K's two-direction hypothesis. No assumption about a first-kind involution, matrix algebraicity, or effective equality has been inserted.

## 4. What the strongest source construction already pays

Start K's countable schedule from this seed. Every old full-series identity is retained, so all later constants and the final marked elements satisfy x²=alpha and y³=beta. The source schedules every polynomial over every coefficient field, constructs inverses by the least-degree algebraic relation, proves infinite dimension using finite word spans, and descends finite multiplication tables to the actual center. These operations apply unchanged to the new seed: they require generation, two directions, countability and increasing degrees, all supplied above.

Consequently this calculation produces a countable characteristic-zero division ring D, algebraic over its actual center F, with D=F[x,y], [D:F]=infinity, and x²=alpha, y³=beta. Both elements are noncentral: otherwise the algebra they generate over F is commutative. Their minimal degrees over F are exactly 2 and 3. For the cubic assertion, F contains all cube roots of unity. If t³-beta is reducible, it has a root in F and splits completely; a root in a division ring of a polynomial split into central linear factors must lie in F. That would make y central. The quadratic assertion is the same argument in degree 2.

This is not submitted as a substantial nominee. The only initial input missing from K's headline theorem was the seed, and its construction above is compatible ordinary conjugacy-orbit linear algebra and the source's own symbol construction. Le Bruyn's actual primary body, arXiv:1003.1610v1, Section 1, Theorem 2, describes the same (3,3;2,2,2) modular-group component; its dimension formula is 1+36-18-12=7. Section 2, Theorem 4 and its proof, explicitly constructs normal-direction matrices; Theorem 5 supplies rational dense families. The historical Westbury 1995 preprint it credits was not obtained. No exact infinite-dimensional theorem in that primary is claimed, but absence of that full statement would not make the compatible composition nonroutine.

For comparison, two quadratic generators cannot yield this conclusion. In characteristic different from 2, translate two quadratic generators to u,v with central squares. The element uv+vu commutes with u and v and hence is central in the generated algebra. Using vu=(uv+vu)-uv and the two square relations, every word reduces to the span of 1,u,v,uv over the actual center. Thus the generated algebra has dimension at most four. This elementary boundary is not a separate finding or a nomination based on a small example.

## 5. The actual center is the union of stage fields

There is a stronger useful reduction inside this same construction. It does not prescribe a center in advance.

Let k_infty be the union of K's increasing stage fields, D the generated algebra inside its division-ring ultraproduct, and c in Z(D). Represent c by a polynomial in x,y with coefficients in a single stage field. On an ultrafilter-large set, its coordinates c_i commute with both marked constants x_i,y_i. Those constants generate A_i over k_i, so c_i belongs to Z(A_i)=k_i on that set.

Let q be the monic minimal polynomial of c over k_infty, which exists by the source schedule. All its coefficients lie in some k_h. On another ultrafilter-large set, q(c_i)=0. Intersect these sets with i>=h and select one stage. There q has a root c_i in k_i, a subfield of k_infty. Irreducibility of q over k_infty forces degree 1. Hence c belongs to k_infty, and

    Z(D)=k_infty.

The fields k_i still depend on all the lifting choices and may include transcendental extensions. This does not solve the earlier prescribed-center or computable-presentation questions. The argument also does not identify the center of the entire ambient ultraproduct.

## 6. Normalized traces and rationalized norms

Put F=k_infty=Z(D). For 0!=a in D, write its monic minimal polynomial as

    q_a(t)=t^e+c_(e-1)t^(e-1)+...+c_0.

All its coefficients lie in one stage field. At ultrafilter-many later stages q_a(a_i)=0, and q_a is still irreducible over k_i, because k_i is contained in F. Thus q_a is the minimal polynomial of a_i there. The equal-multiplicity calculation in K's `formal-generic.tex` gives e dividing n_i=deg(A_i), and

    Trd_i(a_i)/n_i = -c_(e-1)/e,
    Nrd_i(a_i) = ((-1)^e c_0)^(n_i/e).

Only finite-dimensional reduced trace/norm is used in these equations. Define

    tau(a)=-c_(e-1)/e, tau(0)=0,
    nu(a)=(1/e) [(-1)^e c_0] in F^* tensor_Z Q,

using additive notation for the last group. For any finite set of elements, their scalar coefficients, minimal-polynomial identities, and algebraic identities hold at one common stage in the ultrafilter. Ordinary matrix trace there proves tau(a+b)=tau(a)+tau(b), tau(ca)=c tau(a) for c in F, tau(ab)=tau(ba), and tau(1)=1. The pairing tau(ab) is nondegenerate: for a!=0 choose b=a^(-1).

For multiplicativity of nu take nonzero a,b,ab, with degrees e,f,g, signed constant coefficients A,B,C, and L=lcm(e,f,g). Finite-stage determinant multiplicativity gives

    (A^(L/e) B^(L/f) C^(-L/g))^(n_i/L)=1

at a common stage. All scalars in parentheses already belong to F, and n_i/L is a positive integer. Thus the scalar in parentheses is a root of unity. Its class in F^* tensor Q is zero, proving nu(ab)=nu(a)+nu(b). For a central scalar c, nu(c)=[c]. No uniform bound on algebraic degrees, no embedding of the A_i, and no inverse limit of their norm maps is required.

The central-commutator step admits an even shorter compatible proof. If c in F^* is a finite product of multiplicative commutators in D^*, represent all elements and inverses by scalar polynomials in the marked generators. The identity holds at a common stage, where determinant multiplicativity gives c^(n_i)=1. Hence F^* intersect [D^*,D^*] consists of roots of unity. The order may depend on c.

The primary comparison is precise. Hazrat–Mahdavi-Hezavehi–Motiee, arXiv:1302.0596v1, Lemma 5.2 and its displayed proof, expresses a^e as its field norm modulo multiplicative commutators using Wedderburn factorization. Lemma 5.10 and its proof gives the compatibility of the three normalized field norms up to a CENTRAL commutator. Theorem 5.9 uses that same defect in the valuation-extension criterion. K's finite-stage determinant argument kills exactly this defect after rationalization, at compatible inputs. Thus the normalized-norm construction is also already paid by a source/classical composition; the calculation is retained, without an independent contribution verdict or an assertion that every algebraic division ring has this norm. The classical factorization theorem's original proof and the 1994 valuation paper were not newly read; the coauthor survey supplies the relevant displayed norm proofs, and our stage argument independently proves the norm compatibility needed here.

## 7. A precise limit on adding a mixed radical relation

For the quadratic/cubic construction, suppose in addition that (xy)^r=c in F^* with r a positive integer coprime to 6. Applying nu and multiplying by 6r gives

    [alpha^(3r) beta^(2r) c^(-6)]=0 in F^* tensor Q.

Hence alpha^(3r) beta^(2r) c^(-6) is a root of unity. Every root of unity is a square in F, since F contains Qbar. The other displayed factors except alpha^(3r) are squares, and 3r is odd. Therefore alpha is a square in F. But x²=alpha then makes x central, contradicting the infinite-dimensional two-generator conclusion.

This calculation blocks that additional relation for these source-generated rings. It is not a universal impossibility assertion about algebraic division rings, and no full triangle-group construction was frozen or nominated. Split representation dimension counting alone could not repair it: a division realization and the surviving scalar identities must also match.
