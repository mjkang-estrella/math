## A cross-model statement is also a short paid transfer

Suppose G and H are two groups in the source bounded-height theorem, and f:G→H, g:H→G are quasi-inverse maps. Their product is again in that theorem, with height W_G⊕W_H. The swap map F(x,y)=(g(y),f(x)) is a self quasi-isometry of G×H. Its bounded-height linear part A decomposes into blocks. Holding y fixed and varying x shows A_GG=0, because pi_G g(y) is independent of x and pi_G is surjective. Similarly A_HH=0. Thus

    pi_H f(x)=b_H+B pi_G(x)+O(1),
    pi_G g(y)=b_G+C pi_H(y)+O(1).

Since A is invertible, B and C are inverse-dimensional linear isomorphisms. Applying g∘f and f∘g, which are uniformly close to the identities, also gives CB=I and BC=I. Consequently the exponential-height dimension and bounded affine cross-model control are already consequences of the source by the standard product-swap device. This is technical, not a nominee. It does not classify the horizontal nilpotent geometry.

## Exact failure of the algebraic shortcut

Fix a prime p and let L=(Q_p⊕Q_p)⋊Z, where the generator t acts by (x,y)↦(px,p^(-1)y). This group is compactly generated: Z_p² and t,t^(-1) generate it. It is solvable and amenable, and unimodular because the two p-adic module factors multiply to one. The height homomorphism L→Z⊂R has locally elliptic kernel: every compact subset of Q_p² is contained in a compact subgroup p^(-M)Z_p². Hence every compactly generated subgroup of the height kernel is compact, and has bounded, in particular polynomial, growth in its own compact word metric.

Nevertheless L has no nontrivial compact normal subgroup. The projection of any such subgroup to discrete Z is a finite subgroup, hence zero. Its image lies in Q_p² and is invariant under all powers of t. If an element has nonzero first coordinate, negative powers make that coordinate unbounded in p-adic norm; if its second coordinate is nonzero, positive powers do so. Compactness therefore forces both coordinates to vanish. L is nondiscrete and totally disconnected, hence not a real Lie group. It cannot be made a Lie group by quotienting by a compact normal subgroup.

This is NOT claimed quasi-isometric to the source Lie model or to a virtually polycyclic group. It refutes only the shortcut from amenability+unimodularity+bounded height with locally polynomial kernel to a compact-by-Lie conclusion. The full geometric/cohomological information would have to exclude this expanding compact-subgroup phenomenon. No proof of that exclusion is currently supplied.
