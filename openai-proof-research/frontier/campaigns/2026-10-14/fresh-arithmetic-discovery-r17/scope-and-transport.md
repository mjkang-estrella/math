# Smooth transport, with the full normal target kept separate

This is a technical proof record, not a nomination. It concerns **smooth** connected compact Kähler manifolds in arbitrary dimension. It is not the full normal-space extension recorded in checkpoint.md. All unmodified source operations are credited. No independent proof gate is claimed.

For a smooth compact Kähler manifold X whose universal cover U has a semialgebraic projective-open presentation, the following source/primary assembly gives U≅D×C^a×F, with F smooth simply connected projective. The point case is included. The source's Borel-embedding construction proves the converse for these products, so only this forward implication is discussed.

1. **Presentation and local pseudoconvexity.** Because U is smooth, normalize and resolve its projective ambient along the complement, leaving U unchanged. It is thus a smooth semialgebraic open in a smooth projective Y. Ivashkovich2008 Corollary2(a) applies to a domain in an arbitrary complex manifold with a free properly discontinuous group and compact Kähler quotient. It gives local pseudoconvexity. The actual theorem was read; projectivity of X is not its hypothesis.

2. **Whole-leaf quotient.** Apply the pinned `win:construction`, whose exact statement only requires this ambient presentation and local pseudoconvexity. Apply `win:tower-properties`. In `quotient.tex`, the complete argument from “Whole projective fibres in a window” through “Exhaustion and the inverse maps” supplies a surjective holomorphic submersion f:U→M with connected entire leaves, a contractible Stein base M homeomorphic to Euclidean space and biholomorphic to a bounded domain, and a compatible Γ=π1(X) action. Each leaf has a smooth projective compactification with analytic complement. The projection's local full incidence charts extend to proper projective families before removing analytic divisors. The source's source-resolution descent is not needed because X is smooth. The guards, compactness, inverse maps and entire-leaf property are paid source operations, not new lemmas of this packet.

3. **Transverse discreteness and symmetry.** In the full read `groups.tex`, the countable-core-level proof applies to the compact Kähler X: the core is available after a projective *modification* of X, its base is projective, its smooth general fibers are compact Kähler special, and the source057 theorem makes their full groups virtually abelian. The source's Liouville lemma already has a Kähler premise. Pulling the core composition back to each full incidence family and applying core extension gives exactly its locally finite parameter sets. The closure-of-image argument forces Λ⊂Aut(M) discrete and cocompact. The adjoint/Selberg argument removes finite stabilizers. Its final projective algebraization of the cover of X is omitted: the finite unramified analytic cover is compact Kähler by pulling back a Kähler form. The source's Bergman positive metric makes M/Λ projective independently of projectivity of X. The source's Nadel–Frankel/spanning-vector argument then identifies M with a bounded symmetric domain D. No new symmetry theorem is used.

4. **Fiber group.** After this finite covering replacement, X→D/Λ is a proper holomorphic submersion with connected fibers. Ehresmann gives a smooth bundle. Since D/Λ is aspherical, its homotopy sequence identifies K=ker(Γ→Λ) with the entire group π1(N) of a fiber N. Its universal cover is the leaf U_d: this follows equally by pulling the bundle back to contractible D and then taking universal covers. The leaf's projective compactification supplies the analytic compactification in the source057 corollary `cor:compactifiable-covers(i)`, so K is virtually abelian. It is finitely generated because N is compact. Intersect the finitely many subgroups of K of index≤[K:B], where B is a free-abelian finite-index subgroup, to obtain a characteristic free-abelian finite-index K'⊂K. This is exactly the source's `cmp:characteristic` operation.

5. **Relative Albanese torsors in the smooth Kähler setting.** The family 𝓧=U/K'→D is smooth proper with compact Kähler fibers N_d and π1(N_d)=K'. Pullback of a Kähler form on X gives a global Kähler form on 𝓧. Smooth proper Kähler Hodge theory makes R¹p_*Z and F¹H¹ a weight-one variation with constant rank. The usual integration-of-relative-one-forms construction gives a holomorphic family of Albanese torsors 𝓐→D and a map α:𝓧→𝓐. The integral Albanese lattice for a smooth compact Kähler manifold is H1 modulo torsion; here H1=K' is torsion-free, so it has rank2a, and α is an isomorphism on each fiber fundamental group. Neither algebraicity nor a rational polarization of these tori is asserted. This is the classical relative construction used by the source, restricted here to the smooth case so no singular Albanese theorem is silently assumed.

   Every N_d has its universal cover analytically Zariski-open in a smooth projective variety. CH2013 Theorem1.5, or source057's explicit corollary, makes its Albanese map a fiber bundle after a finite étale cover. It is already a fiber bundle before that cover in the present torsion-free abelian situation. Indeed if N'→N_d is the supplied finite cover, its fundamental subgroup is finite-index in K'. The map Alb(N')→Alb(N_d) is an isogeny: both have dimensiona and the induced lattice map is this finite-index inclusion. The natural map N'→N_d×_Alb(N_d)Alb(N') is the isomorphism of connected covers classified by that subgroup. Smooth local triviality descends through this étale base change. The fiber is the same simply connected compact manifold as in CH's theorem. Thus α is a proper submersion with simply connected fibers; submersivity follows from its fiberwise submersivity and the submersivity of 𝓧 and 𝓐 over D.

6. **Universal affine base.** Let T→𝓐 be the universal cover. Since α induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups, its pullback is connected and simply connected, hence is U. This gives a proper submersion j:U→T with connected simply connected compact fibers. Exactly as in `cmp:affine-base`, T→D is an affine bundle whose linear part is the relative Lie bundle. Cartan B kills its translation cocycle and Oka–Grauert trivializes the vector bundle over contractible Stein D, so T≅D×C^a. The Γ action lifts uniquely through j and is proper and cocompact on T because j is proper and surjective. Its stabilizers are finite. These are the source's operations; nonisotriviality of the Albanese tori causes no problem and is not excluded.

7. **Compact factor.** Pull a Kähler form from X to U. It is Γ-invariant, closed, and positive on the fibers of j. The compact fibers are projective because U is an open subset of Y. Apply the complete lemma in `real-class-marking.md` to obtain a single isomorphism class. Fischer–Grauert makes j a holomorphic bundle; the complex Lie group Aut(F) and Oka–Grauert over T give U≅T×F. The fibers are simply connected from step5. This proves the smooth technical implication under the explicit classical interfaces used above.

## Why this does not prove the full normal target

A normal compact Kähler space need not be smooth. The proper quotient fibers in step4 are then only normal Kähler spaces. Step5 used precisely the smooth Kähler Albanese lattice and CH's **manifold** fiber-bundle theorem. Resolving a normal fiber gives a surjection of fundamental groups, not an asserted isomorphism; exceptional fibers need not be invisible to its Albanese geometry or to product descent. The source's projective normal step uses KP Theorem20 with a normal projective source. That premise has not been recovered here. None of steps1–4 or the real-class marking lemma fills this gap.

A possible full proof would adapt KP's normal bundle argument using Kähler cycle spaces and carefully retain connectedness, normality and flatness for all fibers, or establish a suitable separate normal Albanese theorem. It would need an actual complete proof and primary comparison. This packet supplies neither and does not claim the normal target solved.

## Contribution boundary

Even the complete smooth transport is assembled from source windows/guards/transverse rigidity, source057 compactifiable covers, CH's smooth Kähler Albanese theorem, and classical graph-volume/marking/bundle/Oka operations. The new bookkeeping identifies which projectivity hypotheses can be removed. There is no currently identified unpaid substantial operation in that smooth transport. It is therefore not nominated as a smaller substitute for the normal target.
