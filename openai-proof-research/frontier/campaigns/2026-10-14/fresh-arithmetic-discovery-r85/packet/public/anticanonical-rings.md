## Same-space calculation

Every rational function representing a section of mD has polar divisor bounded by mD. Its polar divisor is null, hence that function belongs to k. If k=C, each H0(X,mD) is exactly the constant functions in this rational realization, so R(X,D) is a polynomial ring in the canonical degree-one section. This branch requires no field model.

For dim Y>0, let Q range over prime divisors of Y and let E range over prime divisors of W mapping onto Q. Put

    P=sum_Q min_(E->Q) [coeff_E(p^*D)/ord_E(f^*Q)] Q.

This is an effective rational divisor with finite support. Equidimensionality ensures that every vertical prime maps onto a base divisor. Horizontal coefficients of p^*D are nonnegative, so p^*D-f^*P is effective. Smoothness of X and Y permits the indicated rational Cartier pullbacks even if W is singular.

For a,c>=0 rational, use only multiples where the divisors in question are integral. For h in C(Y),

    div_Y(h)+aP+cA0>=0
      => div_W(h)+a p^*D+c f^*A0>=0
      => div_X(h)+aD>=0.

The second implication is pushforward by p and uses p_*f^*A0=0. Conversely div_X(h)+aD>=0 pulls back effectively. Taking the minimum of its inequalities above each Q yields div_Y(h)+aP>=0, which implies the first inequality because cA0>=0. Thus these are the SAME rational section spaces, with no choice of generators or multiplication change. The fact that the returned functions are in k was checked before comparing them; it is not inferred from equality of dimensions.

Apply `thm:correction-ring` to P. Choose r>0 so q=r k0 epsilon is a positive integer and all required divisors are Cartier. For every j>=0 the preceding calculation gives, inside C(Y),

    H0(Y,jr B)=H0(X,jq D),  B=k0(A0+epsilon P).

Here c=jr k0, a=jq. Multiplication is ordinary multiplication of those very functions in every degree. Consequently the rings agree after regrading. Finite generation of R(Y,B) passes to R(Y,rB). Conversely finite generation of the Veronese R(X,qD) passes to R(X,L), since D is linearly equivalent to m0L. This is exactly the classical section-ring/Veronese operation, with a compatible rational trivialization for D.

The inspected primary is [Corti–Lazic, 1005.0614v3, Lemma3.1 and Theorem3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0614v3). The displayed proofs were read: finite-index Veronese uses finite invariants and integrality/normalization; the adjoint result uses log resolution and the big/klt theorem. The source already invokes these same interfaces. No new theorem about arbitrary subalgebras being finitely generated is used.
