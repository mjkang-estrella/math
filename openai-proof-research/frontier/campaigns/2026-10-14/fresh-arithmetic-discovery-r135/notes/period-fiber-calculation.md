## A concrete field-of-definition invariant after rational trivialization

At the stage where the rational tensor torsor has been trivialized (as in the Kisin–Zhou proof), write rho:Gamma_K->G(Q_p) for the continuous representation and C for its compact image. A geometric point of the level-K_p period fiber is a coset gK_p. Its stabilizer in Gamma_K is the inverse image of C cap gK_p g^(-1). Its orbit size is

    d(g)=[C:C cap gK_p g^(-1)].                         (P1)

This is finite: the intersection is open in the compact group C. The corresponding finite fixed field defines that point. Consequently the minimum finite extension degree needed to obtain a point in this fiber, with this rational trivialization, is

    delta(rho,K_p)=min_(g in G(Q_p)) d(g).              (P2)

The nonempty set of positive integers has a minimum. This is a usable exact reduction to an orbit problem, not a claim that delta is uniformly bounded in the source parameters. A field bound for a flag determines neither the image C nor the orbit minimum solely from that degree.

There is a stronger compatible classical operation in the general linear case. Any compact C subset GL_n(Q_p) stabilizes a Z_p-lattice. Choose a lattice Lambda0. Boundedness gives p^s Lambda0 subset c Lambda0 subset p^(−s) Lambda0 for every c in C. The Z_p-submodule

    Lambda=sum_(c in C) c Lambda0

is contained in p^(−s)Lambda0, contains Lambda0, is finitely generated by noetherianity, and is C-stable. Hence for K_p=GL_n(Z_p), some conjugate of K_p contains C and delta=1. Thus lack of a specified initial stable lattice is NOT a genuine residual even before allowing a field extension.

With polarization and defining tensors one must retain the stipulated integral G-model. The arbitrary stable GL_n-lattice need not have the required self-duality or integral tensor stabilizer. Fontaine–Rapoport's actual §3(a),(b), printedpp83–84, specifically distinguishes admissible-filtration existence from group-compatible/self-dual lattice construction and explains why the maximal adapted lattice is not tensor compatible. SOURCE001 and Kisin–Zhou/PR already repair EXISTENCE by their integral shtuka machinery. The note does not claim that the elementary GL_n argument replaces it or that such tensor-compatible lattices do not exist.

## Degree of a period point and size of its marking are different

Even a point defined over the original field can have an arbitrarily rescaled quasi-isogeny marking. On the level fiber, multiplying the marking by a central power p^s leaves the Hodge filtration unchanged but changes the Dieudonne lattice by a homothety and the height by a multiple of s. A stable lattice stays stable under that homothety, so its field of definition need not grow at all.

This is not a counterexample to a possible optimized height bound: the minimum over all compatible markings may be small. It shows exactly why a bound for a flag or even a chosen period point's residue degree cannot be substituted for a bound on the actual integral lattice position. A genuine quantitative arithmetic refinement must minimize over compatible integral lifts, rather than estimate every deliberately rescaled marking.

For example, fixing the reference crystalline lattice, one can measure the position of a marked lattice Lambda_z by the least h>=0 with

    p^h Lambda_ref subset Lambda_z subset p^(−h) Lambda_ref.

An optimized bound would have to exhibit some z in the appropriate period fiber with controlled h, keeping the polarization and every defining tensor. The current construction provides a point and hence finite h for each input; it has not proved uniform control in a new full arithmetic scope. No impossibility follows.
