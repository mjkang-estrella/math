# Convex sections do not repair the q-sibling product expectation

This strengthens the earlier arbitrary-measurable-hole diagnostic to ACTUAL convex sections satisfying the source's small-hole contract. It does not refute the full existence target or the possibility of jointly selecting a different horizontal lattice.

Fix m>=2, u>1, eta=exp(-u), Lambda_h=Z^m, and

 J=[eta,1] x [0,L]^(m-1),   L=(u/(1-eta))^(1/(m-1))>1.

J is a compact full-dimensional convex body, of volume u. Its image on T^m misses precisely U=(0,eta) x T^(m-1), up to null boundary sets. Thus its hole proportion is eta=exp(-u)<=exp(-u/2), exactly within the source common-horizontal-lattice conclusion at normalized load u. The load u can be anywhere in sqrt(m)<=u=o(m), so this is not a small-load exception.

Let K=J x [-R,R] in R^(m+1); all its sections at vertical heights in[-R,R] are exactly J. Choose q>=3 such section labels. For one Haar-uniform shear Z in T^m, the corresponding q hole sets are U+jZ, j=0,...,q-1. Taking Z's first coordinate in(0,eta/(2q)) and a target first coordinate in(eta/2,eta) gives

 E_Z |intersection_j(U+jZ)| >= eta^2/(4q) = exp(-2u)/(4q).

All other coordinates are unrestricted. The independent-product prediction is eta^q=exp(-qu). Their ratio is at least exp((q-2)u)/(4q), which diverges exponentially in u. For a proposed estimate with a pattern factor exp(o(u)), this is still a contradiction. Convexity, identical neighboring sections, and the source exponential small-hole bound therefore do not by themselves restore the q-fold product estimate.

Qualification: this K is not asserted to be the source's specific Gaussian-marginal normalization; its role is to disprove an inference from convex sections plus the hole contract alone. If a new proof exploits additional global Gaussian-marginal information or the probability law of Lambda_h, those are extra hypotheses and are not contradicted here. The example also does not prevent finding a deterministic successful shear for these intervals; it refutes the proposed averaging inequality.
