## Frobenius calculations inside the actual construction

Let char(k)=p and q=p^e. In a commutative polynomial algebra, the span of qth powers of linear forms is contained in span{x_1^q,...,x_r^q}; over a perfect field it equals that space. Thus the characteristic-zero spanning argument for Sym^q(U) and the nonzero f_i coefficient in assembly cannot be applied to arbitrary forms. For general b, the multinomial coefficient attached to x^alpha in (sum t_i x_i)^b is nonzero modulo p exactly when the base-p additions alpha_1+...+alpha_r=b have no carries. Over an infinite field, independence of polynomial functions in the t_i shows that the span of powers has exactly these permitted monomial positions. This is a precise image calculation, not merely failure of one polarization formula. Finite fields may be extended; extending them does not restore vanished multinomial coefficients.

The source's annihilator-membership equivalence itself is not what fails: a vector belongs to the ideal piece exactly when all annihilating linear functionals vanish, even if evaluation lambda -> (ell -> lambda(ell^b)) is noninjective. The actual lost facts are full spanning/nonzero generic coefficient and the simple-factor/smoothness conclusions. When b=q, every nonzero w_lambda is geometrically a qth power of a linear form, so has no multiplicity-one factor and has zero ordinary gradient. A divided-power dual can restore a perfect pairing, but its qth divided power is not the ordinary qth power in Delta[x]. Without a multiplicative evaluation respecting the actual triangular identities this changes the problem.

There is also a rigidity inside binary addition. Suppose u,v,z pairwise commute in D[x], where D is a division algebra of characteristic p and the variables are central. If z^q=u^q+v^q, then (z-u-v)^q=0. The polynomial algebra D[x] is a domain (use a compatible monomial leading term), so z=u+v. More generally, for b=q c with p not dividing c, the same calculation gives z^c=u^c+v^c. This proves rigidity only when all three elements lie in one division-coefficient polynomial ring. ## Failure of the generic-root curve safeguard in the wild case

For an algebraically closed field Omega of characteristic p, any number m of homogeneous degree-q forms g_r(x_1,...,x_n), q=p^e, and n>=2, consider

    X = V_+(s_r^q-g_r(x): 1<=r<=m) in P^{n+m-1}.

Unlike the characteristic-zero independent-square-root safeguard, X always contains an integral curve of degree at most q. Choose a projective line in the x-space. On it put x_1=u^q,x_2=v^q and the other x_j=0 after choosing linear coordinates. Write the restricted g_r as sum_{a=0}^q c_{r,a} x_1^a x_2^{q-a}. Since Omega is perfect, set h_r(u,v)=sum c_{r,a}^{1/q}u^a v^{q-a}. The morphism

    [u:v] -> [u^q:v^q:0:...:0:h_1(u,v):...:h_m(u,v)]

is everywhere defined and lands in X. Its image is a reduced integral curve: the x-projection is nonconstant. The pullback of O_X(1) has degree q because the coordinates have common degree q and no common zero. For the finite map onto its image, q=degree(map)*degree(image), so the image degree is at most q. This holds for arbitrarily many independent generic coefficient blocks; adding such roots does not make the covering degree grow exponentially along a curve. In particular at p=2, q=2 the source's exact initial Q=(s_r^2-g_r(x)) cannot meet its no-curve-of-degree<=D condition when D>=2. Its source choice D=(max a_i)^2 is >=4.

This is a supporting obstruction to the exact unchanged initialization over characteristic2, not a theorem against LPP and not a substantial nominee. A tame-root initialization of a different shape is not ruled out. It must still supply degree<=2 relations and a division representation with all the commuting forms, and it must handle subsequent p-divisible row degrees. The source's Veronese encoding is an available low-degree encoding operation but uses 1/b and primitive bth roots for its particular binary relation; these must be replaced at wild b.

Thus the calculation about powers in Sym(U) does not rule out noncommuting coefficient linearizations in Delta[x]. The form coefficients may be noncommuting even when the resulting linear forms commute with the central variables. These are plausible replacement building blocks.

## Exact zero-generic test

Work over an infinite field k of characteristic2, with I=(x^2,y^2) in k[x,y]. This contains the full regular sequence of two quadrics. Every linear coordinate change keeps its quadratic span equal to span{x^2,y^2}, so gin_k(I)=I. In characteristic zero, a generic coordinate transform of this same monomial ideal has degree-two leading monomials x^2 and xy: the corresponding 2 by 2 pivot minor is a nonzero polynomial in the change-of-coordinate entries. Its Hilbert function is (1,2,1), hence its initial ideal is exactly (x^2,xy,y^3). Thus by the actual definition, gin_0(I)=(x^2,xy,y^3), also viewed over k.

Every quadratic in this zero-generic ideal is divisible by x. No two quadrics in it form a regular sequence. It does contain a regular sequence of degrees(2,3), but applying the characteristic-zero source with those changed degrees changes the comparison ideal and cannot prove the original(2,2) bound by that route. The original I is already a pure-powers example and satisfies LPP; this tests preservation of the needed hypothesis, not the theorem. Together with the actual limits of Proposition2.2, this blocks a direct use of zero-generic ideals while leaving other degree-preserving constructions open.

## Lifting with every finite invariant retained

For a fixed full degree list a=(a_1,...,a_n), put D=sum(a_i-1). Every quotient S/I in the working problem vanishes above degree D. Its ideal can therefore be specified by subspaces I_d of S_d for 0<=d<=D+1, with I_(D+1)=S_(D+1), and S_1 I_d subset I_(d+1). There are finitely many possible dimension sequences. Parameterize the subspaces by Grassmannians over Z and the f_i by their coefficient affine spaces; impose containment and multiplication equations. Impose the open condition that the f_i form a regular sequence, equivalently in this setting that their degree-(D+1) ideal piece is all S_(D+1). In affine charts this is a finite union of nonvanishing-minor conditions. The quotient graded pieces are vector bundles of the prescribed ranks.

For each such Hilbert function H, its degreewise lex-plus-P vector spaces are completely determined by exponent combinatorics. Ideal closure is a finite check through D+1. If closure fails, the whole corresponding parameter locus is bad. If it is an ideal J, form the Koszul complexes of the universal quotient and of Z[x]/J. All possible quotient Betti entries occur for 0<=i<=n and 0<=j<=D+n. In a fixed (i,j), beta is the dimension of the Koszul term minus the ranks of the two adjacent differentials. The locus beta(I)>beta(J) is constructible by finitely many rank strata. This also retains any characteristic dependence of the fixed monomial ideal J: do not silently identify its Betti numbers over Q and F_p. All resulting bad loci are finite-type constructible loci over Z.

The pinned characteristic-zero theorem makes every bad locus empty over Qbar. Chevalley then makes its image a constructible subset of Spec Z missing the generic point, hence a finite set of closed primes. Alternatively use finitely many affine rank charts and encode each required nonzero minor by adjoining its inverse. A resulting finite-type Z-algebra A has A tensor Q=0, so some nonzero integer N maps to zero in A. Each chart is supported at primes dividing its N. There are finitely many charts. This gives a uniform finite exceptional prime set depending only on the degree list, not on the coefficients or extra generators.

Consequently the supplied characteristic-zero conclusion, ordinary finite-type algebra and the all-ideal Artinian reduction give the full LPP assertion in every sufficiently large characteristic for each fixed degree list. The same Artinian list of length c handles arbitrary ambient n>=c through Caviglia–Kummini. This is a routine transfer consequence, not a nominee, not an explicit bound, and not an assertion that the finite exceptional set is empty. In particular it does NOT prove the working full-scope question over every positive-characteristic field. Taking a union over all degree lists does not provide one uniform finite exceptional set. No scope is frozen around this supporting consequence.

The finite-type argument is stronger than a chosen arbitrary coefficient lift: it retains all Hilbert and Betti strata simultaneously and excludes every large-prime bad point. But it does not eliminate a stratum supported only over a small prime. Such vertical support is exactly what an all-prime transfer would have to exclude.

## Why flatness and semicontinuity alone do not close the small-prime case

Over R=Z[t], take B=R[x,y]/(xy,y^2-t x^2,x^3). The displayed generators are a monic Groebner basis for lex order y>x: the S-polynomials reduce using x^3, xy, and y^2-tx^2. Thus B is R-free with basis 1,x,y,x^2 and Hilbert function(1,2,1). Both fibers contain the same regular sequence(x^3,y^3) of degrees(3,3), since y^3=y(y^2-tx^2)+t x(xy).

When t is nonzero in a field, the ideal equals(xy,y^2-tx^2); these are relatively prime quadrics, and its quotient Betti entries are beta_(0,0)=1,beta_(1,2)=2,beta_(2,4)=1. At t=0 the ideal is(xy,y^2,x^3). Its minimal syzygies are y*(xy)-x*(y^2) and x^2*(xy)-y*(x^3), of degrees3 and4. The extra entries beta_(1,3)=beta_(2,3)=1 appear, giving totals(1,3,2). The common LPP comparator for the prescribed cubic degrees and H is(x^2,xy,y^3), with the special fiber's Betti table. Both fibers satisfy the conjecture; this is a concrete warning about the comparison direction, not a counterexample.

One can specialize t to a prime p in Z_(p) to have a flat mixed-characteristic family whose generic and special Betti numbers differ. Knowing the characteristic-zero source bound for the generic fiber and the usual inequality beta_special>=beta_generic does not produce an upper bound on beta_special. A Betti-preserving lift of EVERY original quotient, or another controlled comparison compatible with the fixed degrees, would suffice; none has been constructed here. The uniformly finite exceptional set above is not removed by this flat example.

## Late stronger initialization test: Artin–Schreier quadrics

This is an additional operation within the SAME family, read/derived before sealing, after the provisional final-status paragraph. The no-small-curve calculation above concerns pure Frobenius-root equations; a general quadratic initialization in characteristic2 need not have that form.

Over k=F_2(a,b,c), use the cyclic quaternion division algebra with u^2+u=a, v^2=b and vu+uv=v. It is division: in the completion F_2(a,c)((b)), the Artin–Schreier extension u^2+u=a is unramified quadratic (a is not AS in F_2(a,c), as its simple pole at a=infinity shows), so norms have even b-adic valuation and b cannot be a norm. The norm criterion for a quadratic cyclic algebra proves nonsplitting, hence division; a split algebra over k would stay split over the completion. This is classical cyclic-algebra arithmetic, not a new result.

For central x,y put s=u x+(v+c)y. Direct multiplication in characteristic2 gives

    s^2+x s = a x^2+c xy+(b+c^2)y^2.

Thus a generic binary quadric has a separable quadratic relation of the low-degree form s^2+x s=q(x,y), represented by a division-coefficient linear form. Away from x=0 the corresponding cover has nonzero derivative x with respect to s. The pure-root parametrization by [u^2:v^2] no longer proves its cover is purely inseparable. This demonstrates a real compatible replacement ingredient and prevents claiming that characteristic2 forbids every low-quadratic initialization.

However even a successful many-variable no-small-curve construction using Artin–Schreier relations would not restore the source's sum-of-ordinary-bth-powers parameter construction at b=2. In the formal commutative algebra Sym U the map ell -> ell^2 is still Frobenius-linear; its dual equations have zero derivative. Replacing it by ell^2+L ell yields mixed terms, but the output equation is then t_i^2+L t_i=... . The extra term L t_i is at the SAME row, not a multiple of a later t_d, and so the original least-exponent proof does not imply containment of y_i^2. One would need to prove this term is later in a common compatible order or otherwise redesign the full monomial transfer. In particular the special binary identity is not a solution of the arbitrary regular-sequence LPP problem. Neither the p-power rigidity shortcut nor failure of pure-root initialization is used as a universal impossibility argument. The full unresolved obligation above remains.
