# Formal identity limits for the source201 construction

This is prefreeze feasibility work, not a nominated theorem or a contribution rating. Fix one entire sequence of choices in the pinned lifting construction. The calculation removes dependence on the final ultrafilter; it does not remove dependence on the lifting choices, construct an effective field, add an involution, or prove stable algebraicity under scalar extension. ## Exact available contract

Source201, *A Counterexample to Kurosh's Division Ring Problem*, `sections/lifting.tex`, `thm:lifting`, starts from a countable characteristic-zero field containing all roots of unity, a finite central division algebra, a generating constant tuple, and two independent free-series directions modulo inner directions. It constructs a larger-degree finite central division algebra over an extended countable field, preserves EVERY old polynomial identity of the tuple and chosen series, imposes a monic algebraic relation for that series, and restores generation and both transverse directions. The finite algebras are not an embedding tower.

The actual `division-realization.tex` does more than a generic matrix existence argument. It uses a symbol factor and an eleven-block cyclic factor, embeds in a skew Laurent division ring, and descends finite affine linear correction systems by equality of ranks after field extension. Evaluation at small constant inputs involves convergent coefficient sums in a central Laurent field. The actual `generation.tex` translates by a second Laurent variable and retains the countably many scalar coefficients of all series and structure constants. It does not state that these scalar fields have decidable equality.

`application.tex` schedules every polynomial over every coefficient field. Its final construction uses constants in an ultraproduct, proves algebraicity, and uses finite word spans plus actual-centre finite descent to prove infinite dimension. The following calculation uses this full identity-preservation interface, not merely the headline existence theorem.

## Direct limit and eventual kernels: full elementary calculation

Write the actual sequence as k_i ⊂ k_(i+1), A_i finite central division over k_i, and X_i=(X_i1,X_i2) in A_i⟨⟨z_1,z_2⟩⟩². Let a_i be its constant tuple. The a_i generate A_i over k_i, and dim_(k_i) A_i tends to infinity. For each p over some k_h, a later stage imposes q(p(X_i))=0 for a monic q of positive degree over that stage's scalar field; the identity persists at every later stage.

Let I_i be the kernel of substitution k_i⟨x,y⟩→A_i⟨⟨z_1,z_2⟩⟩ and B_i=k_i⟨x,y⟩/I_i. Identity preservation gives unital maps B_i→B_(i+1), extending the specified scalar inclusions and fixing the two marked generators. They are NOT asserted injective.

Each B_i is a domain. For completeness, the free formal-series ring over a division ring, with the variables commuting with coefficient scalars but with each other freely noncommuting, is a domain: choose the least nonzero homogeneous degree in each of two nonzero series; in their product the lexicographically least word at the sum degree comes from the uniquely split least words of those two lengths, with nonzero product coefficient. This proves the required nonvanishing even for noncommuting coefficients.

Set B=colim B_i and k=∪k_i. The central field k embeds in B: a nonzero scalar cannot become zero in a later unital algebra. In particular B is nonzero. It is a domain even though the transition maps need not be injective. Represent two elements and a putative zero product at a common stage; equality to zero in a filtered colimit is witnessed at a later stage. That later B_j is a domain, so one of the factors is zero there and therefore in B.

Every element of B is a polynomial in x,y with coefficients in k and is treated by the schedule. Its retained monic relation shows it is algebraic over k. A nonzero element b of this domain has a monic annihilator of least degree with nonzero constant term: a zero constant term would allow cancellation of b and yield a smaller annihilator. Writing q(b)=0 expresses a two-sided inverse of b as a polynomial in b with central coefficients. Thus B is a division ring and B=k[x,y]. No fraction localization or Ore hypothesis was inserted.

B is infinite-dimensional over k. Suppose instead that words w_1=1,w_2,...,w_d in x,y form a k-basis. For every w_a and g∈{x,y}, express w_a g as a k-linear combination of this basis. There are finitely many coefficients and finitely many identities. They all originate, and all equalities are witnessed, at one sufficiently late stage j. Identity preservation makes them formal identities at every i≥j, so they hold after constant evaluation in A_i. The k_i-span of w_1(a_i),...,w_d(a_i) contains 1 and is stable under right multiplication by both generators. It therefore contains every word, hence all A_i by constant generation. This would give dim_(k_i) A_i≤d for all i≥j, a contradiction. This finite multiplication-table argument is exactly compatible with the source's own generation-and-dimension argument; it is not a new compactness obstruction.

Let F=Z(B). Every element of F is algebraic over k. If B had finite F-dimension, choose a finite F-basis containing1 and the finitely many central coefficients of its multiplication table and of x,y. They generate a finite extension k' of k inside F. The k'-span of the basis is a subalgebra containing x,y and k; since B=k[x,y], it equals B. Then B is finite over k, the contradiction just proved. This is the source's actual-centre descent, also matching the finite multiplication-table proof in Deo–Bien–Hai, [1510.08711v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.08711v2), Theorem5 (read in the actual proof). It is not a new assertion that arbitrary algebraic scalar extensions preserve division.

There is a stronger description of the constant evaluations. Fix p∈k_h⟨x,y⟩. If its image in B is zero, it belongs to I_j at a later stage and so p(a_i)=0 for every i≥j. If its image is nonzero, its inverse in B is represented by a polynomial r with coefficients in some k_l. The equality pr=1 is witnessed formally at a later stage j. At every i≥j, p(a_i)r(a_i)=1, so p(a_i) is nonzero. Thus, for EACH fixed scalar polynomial, its zero/nonzero status under constant evaluation is eventually constant. The stage is allowed to depend on p and the fixed construction; no computable or uniform bound is supplied.

For every nonprincipal ultrafilter U, send B to the source's marked algebra D_U in ∏_U A_i using the scalar embeddings and the constant tuples. Every formal relation of B holds eventually on constants, so the map is well-defined. It is a unital map from a division ring to a division ring and hence injective. Its image is precisely k[x,y]=D_U by definition. Therefore all these marked k-algebras D_U are isomorphic through their common direct-limit presentation. One can equivalently deduce this directly from eventual kernels. The conclusion concerns the generated algebra, not an isomorphism of the whole ambient ultraproducts.

## The stronger effective avenue remains incomplete

Before any freeze, I investigated whether the proof could produce a computable presentation of such a division ring. Countability, a countable list of Laurent coefficients, and coefficientwise recursion do not themselves give effective equality for the scalar field or for the formal identities defining I_i. The stronger source realization was read in full after this question arose. It solves finite linear systems OVER the full Laurent coefficient field and chooses its minimal polynomial there; these operations still require equality of the relevant series/field elements if they are to be used algorithmically.

Here is the precise general warning, not a counterexample to the source. For a machine index e, define a formal series whose t^n coefficient is1 exactly if machine e first halts at step n, and0 otherwise. Its coefficients are uniformly computable by finite simulation, but deciding whether the entire series is zero decides nonhalting. Even the fact that each such series happens to be either0 or a monomial does not provide a uniform degree bound. This does not prove that the particular source series have undecidable equality; it identifies a missing effective-interface proof.

If a recursive presentation of a nonzero simple algebra over a computable field were supplied, equality could be decided by dovetailing a proof that p=0 with a finite two-sided-ideal certificate that1 lies in (p). The latter exists for every nonzero p by simplicity. This elementary conditional procedure does NOT supply the required recursive presentation here. Nor does embedding a countable scalar field into a standard algebraically closed field preserve the division/algebraicity construction.

Earlier actual history excludes other shortcuts: I28 already isolates the first-kind involution problem, the exponent obstruction to the eleven-block factor, the non-domain tensor product with the opposite ring, and the failure of a sixteen-block graph change to fix the Brauer class. Arithmeticr76 already distinguishes matrix algebraicity and external transcendental scalar extension from the finite-centre descent. None is reopened under a new name.

The integrated comparison is decisive: formal identity preservation, scheduling, algebraic inversion, finite word closure, and actual-centre descent all match without a residual compatibility problem in the direct-limit calculation. No candidate is nominated. The stronger effective presentation remains unproved at the exact equality/construction interface; no weaker countable, ultrafilter-free or conditional result is substituted for it.
