A precise diagnostic rules out one naive return-time application. For the ordinary one-dimensional Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck kernel relative to its invariant Gaussian, put a=exp(-t), 0<a<1. Then

    p_t(x,y)=(1-a^2)^(-1/2) exp(-(y-ax)^2/(2(1-a^2))+y^2/2),
    p_t(x,x)=(1-a^2)^(-1/2) exp(a*x^2/(1+a)).

The diagonal grows without bound, and continuity gives open positive-measure rectangles of arbitrarily large kernel value. Hence ||P_t||_(L1->Linf) and ||P_t-E||_(L1->Linf) are infinite for every finite t. This is a failure of the tested return-time route, not a counterexample to Gaussian complete LSI (which has stronger curvature structure), not a new theorem nomination, and not an impossibility claim for the full matrix question.
