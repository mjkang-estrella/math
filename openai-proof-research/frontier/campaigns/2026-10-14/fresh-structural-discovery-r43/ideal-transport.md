# Technical calculations for the family301 screen

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Author-side work, not an independent review or nominee.

Let P=A⊗W⊗K and Q=B⊗W⊗K, for separable nuclear C*-algebras A,B. The pinned source's structure theorem identifies traces with Cuntz-semigroup functionals and supplies the finite-rank-cut criterion. Let g:Cu(P)→Cu(Q) be a Cu morphism: it preserves zero, addition, order, increasing sequential suprema, and compact containment. Write F_g(τ) for the trace characterized by

    d_{F_g(τ)}(x)=d_τ(g(x)).

Existence of this trace uses the source's exact-algebra trace/functional identification; it is not a new representation theorem here. Only this identity, not an unproved continuity assertion about F_g, is used below.

For an ideal I⊂P let J_g(I) be the closed ideal of Q generated by positive representatives of g(x), x∈Cu(I). Stability allows representatives in Q itself. If a is strictly positive in I and g([a])=[b], then

    J_g(I)=Ideal_Q(b).

Indeed Cu(I) is the semigroup ideal generated by [a], namely the classes bounded by sup_n n[a]. Apply g to this inequality. The reverse inclusion is immediate from [a]∈Cu(I). In particular the definition is independent of a and b.

**Ideal transport.** J_g is order preserving, preserves arbitrary joins, and preserves compact containment of ideals. Here compact containment is the way-below relation in the lattice of closed ideals, not compactness of a subset of a primitive spectrum.

For joins, the Cu ideal associated to the closed ideal generated by a family I_λ is the Cu ideal generated by their union. To check that g carries it into the Cu ideal generated by the g(Cu(I_λ)), take a class x in the former ideal and a rapidly increasing approximation x_n≪x. Each x_n is bounded by a finite sum of classes from the component ideals (equivalently apply positive-cut approximation to a finite part of the algebraic ideal sum). Its image is therefore in the target generated ideal. Supremum preservation puts g(x) there too. The reverse inclusion follows by monotonicity. This argument also applies to an uncountable family: each individual cut uses only finitely many members; no uncountable supremum axiom for g is assumed.

For compact containment, if I≪I', choose strictly positive a∈I' and ε>0 such that I⊂Ideal((a−ε)_+). This follows because the ideals generated by cuts of a increase to I'. Write g([a])=[b]. Since

    g([(a−ε)_+]) ≪ g([a]) = [b],

some δ>0 satisfies g([(a−ε)_+])≤[(b−δ)_+]. Consequently

    J_g(I) ⊂ Ideal((b−δ)_+) ≪ Ideal(b) = J_g(I').

The last compact containment follows by norm approximation in a directed ideal union. The zero case is included. Likewise a source class compactly Cuntz supported in I has image compactly Cuntz supported in J_g(I); choosing the deeper cut before applying g gives a fixed target controller. The source's common-controller lemma then supplies finite multiplicities. Neither a new absorbing representation nor a new analytic estimate is obtained this way.

**Finite and zero domains.** For a lower semicontinuous extended trace σ, Fin(σ) is the largest ideal on which σ is densely finite, and Ker(σ) its zero ideal. For every ideal I⊂P and every τ∈T(Q),

    I⊂Fin(F_gτ)  iff  J_g(I)⊂Fin(τ),
    I⊂Ker(F_gτ)  iff  J_g(I)⊂Ker(τ).

Here is the finite-domain proof. If a∈I_+ and I⊂Fin(F_gτ), the finite-rank-cut criterion gives d_{F_gτ}((a−ε)_+)<∞ for every ε>0. A positive representative of g([(a−ε)_+]) has finite τ-rank, hence finite τ-value and belongs to Fin(τ). Since g([a]) is the supremum of these cut images, it belongs to Cu(Fin(τ)). Taking image generators proves J_g(I)⊂Fin(τ).

Conversely suppose J_g(I)⊂Fin(τ). For a∈I_+, represent g([a]) by b∈Fin(τ)_+. For each ε>0 the way-below relation gives a δ>0 with g([(a−ε)_+])≤[(b−δ)_+]. The latter class has finite τ-rank by the same finite-rank-cut criterion. Thus every cut of a has finite F_gτ-rank, and a∈Fin(F_gτ). This proves I⊂Fin(F_gτ). For kernels, zero rank is equivalent to membership in the trace-zero ideal, so the same generator argument, without finiteness cuts, proves the second equivalence.

These are right-adjoint preimage identities: Fin(F_gτ), respectively Ker(F_gτ), is the largest ideal I whose J_g-image lies in Fin(τ), respectively Ker(τ). They do not assert that J_g is injective, surjective, or intersection preserving.

**Overlapping labels are already allowed.** Let D be a nonzero stable nuclear W-absorbing algebra. Two orthogonal isometries in M(D) give the map D⊕D→D, (a,b)↦v_1av_1*+v_2bv_2*. Each summand generates D although the two domain summand ideals intersect trivially. Thus J_g need not preserve intersections. This is not an obstruction to the source's stated uniqueness machinery: source05 explicitly allows an order-preserving target ideal assignment without requiring preservation of intersections. The original suspicion that this would require a new absorption theorem was withdrawn after reading that passage.

**Status and debt.** These arguments are technical bookkeeping from the Cu axioms, standard ideal correspondence, and the pinned structure lemmas. They do not establish existence or approximate-unitary uniqueness of a homomorphism realizing every g. Such a claim would require transferring every rough tracial model, controller estimate, labelled correction, norm uniqueness and point-extraction step of source03–08 without use of an inverse cone map. That work was not completed. No new full theorem or literature-priority assertion is made.
