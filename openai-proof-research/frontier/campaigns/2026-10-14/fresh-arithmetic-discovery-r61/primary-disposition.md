## Exact operative primary

Kôji Uchida, *Homomorphisms of Galois groups of solvably closed Galois extensions*, Journal of the Mathematical Society of Japan 33 (1981), 595–604, DOI 10.2969/jmsj/03340595. Actual retained publisher PDF: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/33/4/33_4_595/_pdf/-char/ja .

The Corollary on p.604 permits algebraic number fields k1,k2, only k1 initially assumed finite over Q. If Omega_i/k_i are solvably closed Galois extensions with isomorphic topological Galois groups, it concludes that k2 is finite over Q. The surrounding notation explicitly relaxes finite degree for this corollary (p.595). This is stronger than the finite-base isomorphism theorem quoted in the pinned source.

Apply it to Omega1=Omega2=F^sol, k1=F and k2=E=(F^sol)^H, where H is the closed image of the continuous injective endomorphism alpha. Compactness makes alpha:P_F→H a topological isomorphism. The field F^sol is solvably closed and is Galois over E, with Galois group H. The corollary makes E a number field, so H is open in P_F. Uchida's 1979 isomorphism theorem then gives an isomorphism F^sol→F^sol taking the fixed field E to F (opposite orientation to alpha). Hence [E:Q]=[F:Q]. Since F⊂E, E=F and H=P_F. This proves the entire frozen target without the new pinned open-homomorphism theorem.

## Credit and reading boundary

The p.604 proof uses Neukirch's 1969 Theorem 1 to recognize decomposition groups under the abstract topological isomorphism with a group over a possibly infinite base, and to obtain local openness. It then invokes Uchida's Theorem 2 (pp.600–604), whose hypothesis is local openness at every finite prime and whose conclusion includes global openness. That is precisely the missing interface in the preliminary attempts. No new reconstruction is supplied here, and no claim is made that the source's character-detection argument is needed.
