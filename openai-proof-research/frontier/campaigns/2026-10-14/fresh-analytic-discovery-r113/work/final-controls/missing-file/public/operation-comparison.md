## 095: a derivative construction tested before claiming a new obstruction

The source's `prop:hyperbolic` applies to every PSD-valued quadratic Q. Its pointwise Gram factor depends on y and is not a joint LMI. `prop:epigraph` identifies the exact affine-section image including the singular range condition. `lem:polynomial-patch` uses rank-N A=M M^T and equality of the trace constraint. `prop:no-lift` compares a negative order-epsilon^4 residual with an order-epsilon^16 lift approximation. Adding a positive term delta|y|^2 I destroys the old range inclusion on that patch. I198 and I200 already inspect this operation and its full-rank repairs; that unresolved target is not resumed.

Here is the derivative feasibility calculation, with every k retained. Let p(x) be homogeneous hyperbolic of degree d in direction e, normalized by p(e)>0, with closed cone K. For integer k>=0 define

    Q_k(t,x) = D_(1,e)^k [ t^(k+1) p(x) ] = t R_k(t,x),
    R_k(t,x) = sum_(h=0)^k binom(k,h)(k+1)!/(h+1)! t^h D_e^h p(x).

Terms h>d vanish. Products and directional derivatives preserve hyperbolicity in direction E=(1,e). The cone of Q_k is contained in t>=0, since t is a factor positive at E. To identify its face at t=0, note for 0<=j<=d that

    D_E^j R_k(0,x) = C_(k,j) D_e^j p(x),
    C_(k,j) = (k+1)! sum_(h=0)^min(k,j)
                   binom(k,h) binom(j,h)/(h+1) > 0.

This follows by differentiating each t^h term exactly h times in t; h!/(h+1)!=1/(h+1). Both sides vanish for j>d. For a hyperbolic polynomial f with f(E)>0, x belongs to its closed cone exactly when every coefficient of f(x+sE) is nonnegative. Indeed its roots are real; if the roots are all nonpositive then the coefficients are nonnegative, and conversely nonnegative coefficients and a positive leading coefficient exclude a positive root. Applied to R_k and p, the displayed positive factors show

    Lambda_E(Q_k) intersect {t=0} = {0} times K.

The remaining factor t has cone t>=0, so this really is an exposed face. If Lambda_E(Q_k) had an affine semidefinite lift, intersecting it with t=0 and projecting would lift K. Consequently the source nonliftable cone propagates to these derivative examples by the ordinary product/derivative/face construction. It does not yield an irreducible example, a smooth example, or a new obstruction to lifts. None is nominated.

The stronger smooth reduction is Netzer–Sanyal [1208.0441v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.0441v1), Lemma 2.4, Theorem 3.1 and proof of Theorem 1.1. The proof obtains local strictly quasi-concave defining inequalities, invokes the stated Helton–Nie theorem, takes a finite boundary cover and its convex hull, then a conical hull after removing lineality. Their final Nuij paragraph explains why arbitrarily close polynomial approximations can have liftable cones. This blocks the naive inference that singular source nonliftability is automatically stable under smoothing. It does not settle I200's particular Q_delta family, whose defining-polynomial smoothness was not checked here.
