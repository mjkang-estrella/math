# Route A: finite-field transport and local Hilbert unitaries

Author mechanism under examination; the full bounded-net target remains open. This route is intended to avoid the invalid general inference from formal Lie commutators to unitary group integration.

## Proposed local construction

For each finite seed family, use grade-floor.md to close its centered tower. Source equation R:centered-Ward expresses every conformal variation as a first-order spacetime differential operator plus a smooth finite-dimensional fiber matrix. Its Lie relation is an exact flatness relation on this finite family, modulo actual field identities. On a compact spacetime chart avoiding the poles of a prescribed near-identity conformal transformation, solve the characteristic ODE and finite matrix ODE. This is an ordinary finite-dimensional transport problem; nilpotent lowering terms terminate, and all coordinate coefficients are smooth on the chosen compact chart. Fiber transforms respect adjoints and all field identities. The resulting local transformation is invertible and composes locally because the flat first-order system has unique solutions.

Integrate the source vacuum Ward identities along this transformation. For each finite joint test kernel, all transformed supports stay within a fixed compact chart. The derivative of the pulled-back Wightman distribution is zero by the exact Ward identity, so its value is constant. Thus all finite vacuum-word inner products are preserved, including cross inner products after combining the two finite seed families and their adjoints.

For an open double cone O in the chart, let D_O be the algebraic span of all compact joint vacuum words supported in O, with the finite descendant families admitted as needed. It is dense by original-field Reeh–Schlieder. Mapping each word to its transformed word therefore gives an isometry on D_O. Its range contains the analogous word space in gO: the finite fiber transformation is invertible, and applying the inverse to any finite target list uses another finite admitted family. That range is dense. The isometry extends to a unitary U_O(g).

For overlapping O_1,O_2, coincidence on words in their open intersection identifies the unitaries; the intersection contains a double cone, so those words are dense. Chains of overlapping small double cones give consistency throughout a connected coordinate neighborhood. For a fixed base cone, shrink a neighborhood of the group identity so that products g,h,gh are defined on a larger common compact chart. The word-level local composition law then gives U(g)U(h)=U(gh) on a dense word space. Strong continuity follows first on words from smooth test transport, then on the Hilbert completion by uniform boundedness. A genuine strongly continuous local unitary homomorphism integrates to the simply connected group by path subdivision and homotopy, rather than by an assertion about closures of infinitesimal generators. Its affine subgroup agrees with the given physical subgroup on a dense word space and hence on all H; positive translation energy is retained.

## What must still be checked

The preceding argument is more than a formal Lie-algebra claim, but its field-family coherence, local integration conventions and domain identification require independent checking before treating it as an established global Hilbert representation. In particular the coordinate chart and group neighborhood depend on the compact supports; equality of local unitary germs must be proved on overlaps before integrating. No analytic-vector estimate is being silently assumed.

Even if this Hilbert integration succeeds, it does NOT prove the original bounded-net conclusion. On localized words it gives covariance of field insertions on a dense polynomial domain. The source explicitly does not assert that this domain is a graph core for a chosen closed affiliated extension. Therefore one cannot conjugate arbitrary spectral projections or identify every original M(O) merely by taking closures of the field identity. Nor does the source assume the bounded net is generated by these fields. The explicit Jacobi extension example in extension-obstruction.md illustrates precisely this failed inference; it is not a QFT counterexample.

## Possible generator-identification refinement

Source C:K-domains supplies Dom(K_n^r) for compact polynomial vectors whose supports lie strictly on one side of a null plane, locally uniformly in placement. The preceding charge calculation identifies its action with the null-current flux. A small conformal convolution of such words remains supported on that side and, if the chart construction is valid, is again a finite joint polynomial (include translation variables to obtain smooth joint test kernels). Small-group Gårding convolutions of a dense word set can furnish a graph core for the derived representation generator. This could identify the source's self-adjoint null K_n with the integrated generator, without assuming the source domain is already its core.

This refinement is not yet claimed proved: one must verify uniform graph integration, exact agreement of the source charge identity on these undamped words, and the joint-test-kernel representation of the convolution. Moreover identifying null generators still does not automatically show their geometric action on every bounded double-cone algebra. The source's actual time-Möbius group is reconstructed from cone–diamond inclusions and must be compared explicitly, not identified merely because it has the same affine subgroup.
