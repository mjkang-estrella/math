## Exact quadratic-scale implication

Define G2(k) as in arithmetic r1: the maximum avoidance gap for at most k distinct primes, at most two forbidden residue classes per prime, and proper forbidden sets. Suppose, hypothetically, that for one absolute C and every k>=1,

    G2(k) <= C k^2/(log log(3k))^2.

Then for every sufficiently large real z there are twin primes n,n+2 with z<n<z^2. Indeed take all primes p<=z and forbid {0,-2} modulo p (only one class at p=2). Put k=pi(z) and H=ceil(C k^2/(log log(3k))^2). Apply the hypothetical bound to the interval {2,...,H+1}. By the prime number theorem, H+3=o(z^2). A survivor n has n,n+2>1 with no prime divisor <=z, and both numbers are <z^2 for large z. Every composite positive integer below z^2 has a prime factor <z, so both numbers are prime, necessarily >z. Arbitrarily large z yields infinitely many twin primes. The same implication already follows from an O(k^2) bound. This is an elementary implication, not a proof of the hypothesized bound or an impossibility theorem.

The classical square-root-sieve interface is explicitly present in Tao's actual primary notes opening, lines16–35: two residue classes at each prime count twin primes when the cutoff reaches the square root. The present k-to-z conversion uses PNT and is routine. It is not a substantial contribution.
