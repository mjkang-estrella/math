       Abstract. We study the stability of optimal transport maps for the
       quadratic cost c(x, y) = ∥x − y∥2 on Rd with respect to Wassersein-1
       perturbations of the target measure. Existing results establish Hölder
       stability estimates, but the best-known exponent (1/6) does not match
       the theoretical lower bound (1/4). In this paper, we close this gap by
       proving a sharp 1/4-Hölder stability estimate when the source probabil-
       ity distribution has a bounded density. Our approach relies on analyzing
       the strong convexity of the Kantorovich functional through its associ-
       ated Bregman-Kantorovich and Jensen-Kantorovich divergences. We
       show that quantitative bounds on the singularities of convex functions
       yield meaningful lower bounds on these divergences involving distances
       between transport maps. Furthermore, these methods also yield quanti-
       tative convergence rates for the entropic Brenier map towards the unreg-
       ularized Brenier map as the regularization parameter vanishes, requiring
       only mild assumptions on the measures.




                                1. Introduction
   The stability of optimal transport maps has been the focus of significant
recent research, driven in part by applications in numerical analysis, sta-
tistics, and sampling theory, as well as the need to better understand the
“linearized optimal transport” framework. From a PDE perspective, this
stability question amounts to quantifying the well-posedness of the (real)
Monge-Ampère equation associated to optimal, and to establish continuous
dependence on the data even when solutions lacks regularity. In this in-
troduction, we summarize the stability question and existing results in the
quadratic case—that is, for the cost c(x, y) = ∥x − y∥2 on Rd .
   Given a probability density ρ supported on a compact set X of Rd and
a target probability measure µ supported on another compact set Y of Rd ,
Brenier’s theorem guarantees the existence of a unique quadratic optimal
transport map, i.e. a unique minimizer in the problem:
                                   Z
                          inf         ∥x − T (x)∥2 dρ(x).
                    T ∈L2 (ρ,Rd ),T# ρ=µ

We will call this optimal map the Brenier map and we will denote it Tρ→µ .
This map takes the form Tρ→µ = ∇u for a convex potential u : Rd → R.
While the potential u may not be unique, its gradient is uniquely defined
in L2 (ρ). When ρ satisfies a connectedness assumption, such as a Poincaré
inequality, the potential u is then also uniquely determined (ρ-a.e.) up to
an additive constant.

    The question of stability focuses on establishing bounds of the form

where the constants C and α may depend on ρ and Y . In practice, existing

deduced from the inequality W1 ⩽ Wp . The first stability result of this form
is due to Berman [2], who showed that if ρ is bounded away from zero and
infinity on a compact set, such inequalities hold with a dimension-dependent
α. Delalande and the author [11] later improved to α = 1/6, under the same
assumptions on ρ, using convex analysis and functional inequalities.
   The introduction of a gluing technique by Letrouit and the author [19]
broadened the scope of the stability theory, enabling its extension to much
more general source measures, cost functions, and ambient spaces. In par-
ticular, [19] established the inequality (1.1) for source measures ρ whose
support lacks convexity, or whose densities tend to 0 or +∞ at controlled
rate. We refer to the PhD thesis of Alex Delalande [10] and to the short
book of Cyril Letrouit [17] for a detailed account of the existing stability
theory, as well as to Remark 1.2. Finally, it is worth noting that this sta-
bility literature is rapidly growing, with active research exploring entropic
optimal transport [8, 9, 12], other cost functions [22], Riemannian manifolds
and more general metric spaces [16, 23], or Schrödinger bridges [25].

1.2. Lower bound for the stability exponent. Gigli [14] provided a
counter-example demonstrating that it is not possible to achieve a bound
of the form (1.1) for α > 1/2 even if ρ is the restriction of the Lebesgue
measure to a convex set. More recently, Letrouit [18, Theorem 1.2] proved
that for dimensions d ⩾ 2, there exist a bounded open set X ⊂ Rd and a
compact set Y such that, if ρ denotes the uniform probability measure on X,
there exists a bounded and compactly supported source measure ρ such that
the stability inequality (1.1) cannot hold for any exponent α > p/(2p + 2).
In particular, for p = 1, the exponent in (1.1) cannot be larger than 41 for a
general bounded and compactly supported source measure ρ.

1.3. Contributions. To quantify the strong convexity of the Kantorovich
functional Kρ , we introduce its associated Bregman and Jensen divergences
in §2. By leveraging recent quantitative results on the singularities of con-
vex functions [4] alongside elementary arguments from convex analysis, we
establish tight lower bounds on these divergences, see Theorem 2.2. In
Appendix A, we provide a second proof of Theorem 2.2.(ii) with slightly
different techniques, which are subsequently applied in §4.
   From these estimates, we deduce a proof of the stability inequality (1.1)
in §3 for p = 1 with the optimal exponent α = 1/4 for bounded densities ρ.
Theorem (Theorem 3.1). Let X, Y be compact and convex subsets of Rd ,
let ρ ∈ P ac (X) ∩ L∞ (X). Then (1.1) holds for p = 1 and α = 14 .

Remark 4.1 (Comparison with existing convergence rates). Prior to this
work, quantitative L2 (ρ) convergence rates for the entropic Brenier map
Tε → T0 were heavily dependent on the regularity of the solution to the
unregularized problem.
   At the highest level of regularity — assuming for instance C 3 regularity
of the unregularized map T0 and finite integrated Fisher information along
the Wasserstein geodesic — Pooladian and Niles-Weed [24, Corollary 1] es-
tablished a fast rate of ∥Tε − T0 ∥L2 (ρ) = O(ε). At a lower regularity level,
but still assuming that the unregularized map T0 is Lipschitz, Malamut and
Sylvestre [21] recovered a slower O(ε1/2 ) rate by combining their primal gap
bound (Theorem 4.1) with the stability framework of Li and Nochetto [20].
Beyond these smooth settings, Divol et al. [12, Section 4] recently estab-
lished the same O(ε1/2 ) rate for semi-discrete optimal transport problems.
This rate could also be recovered by combining Corollary 2.2 from [9] with
Theorem 3.5 from [7].
   Compared to these results, Theorem 4.3 makes very little regularity as-
sumptions — assuming only compactness of the supports and an L∞ bound
on the source density. The upper bound only depends on the primal gap Pε ,
so that any progress in the estimation of this gap will automatically lead to
tighter estimates for the stability of the entropic Brenier map.

Remark 4.2 ((Non-)optimality of the exponent). We do not know whether
the exponent 41 in Theorem 4.3 is optimal. However, simple examples even
in dimension d = 1 show that we cannot expect this exponent to better than
1/2 in general.
