## 2. Algebra and notation used in the audit

The exact identity X^-3 P(X)=(X+X^-1)^3-4(X+X^-1)-1 reduces the algebra to
q(y)=y^3-4y-1. Its rational-root candidates +/-1 are not roots; sign changes
give its three roots in (-2,-1), (-1,0), (2,3). For the last root y0,
X^2-y0 X+1 has real roots beta,beta^-1. Its discriminant has negative images
at the other two real embeddings of Q(y0), so it cannot be a square in this
field. Thus beta has degree six, is a unit, and its other roots are the two
circle pairs z_r, conjugate(z_r), r=1,2. Also P(7/5)<0<P(3/2), and the unique
real root >1 is in (7/5,3/2). All this is algebra, not a floating root test.

For a general Salem number of degree 2d, use the same notation with d-1
circle pairs. Let A in O_K, A>=1, A'=sigma'(A), c_r=sigma_r(A),
Delta=sum_r |c_r|, L=log(1/Delta), R=beta/(beta-1), and

    f(t)=-log|cos(pi t)|,
    Lambda_L=sum_{j<=0} f(A beta^j),
    Lambda_R=sum_{j>=1} f(A' beta^-j), Lambda=Lambda_L+Lambda_R,

## 3. Reconstruction of Theorem S

### Norm separation, with equal atoms merged

Let X_N=sum_{k<N} eps_k beta^-k and H_N its Shannon entropy on distinct atoms.
For distinct atoms write their difference as 2u, where
v=beta^{N-1}u=sum_{l<N} d_l beta^l is a nonzero algebraic integer, d_l in
{-1,0,1}. Its nonzero integer norm and the bounds |sigma'(v)|<=R,
|sigma_r(v)|<=N give

    |x-x'| >= 2(beta-1) beta^-N N^-2(d-1).                 (3.1)

No lower bound is asserted for a polynomial that is zero modulo P.

### Positive product, Lipschitz estimate and cell entropy

Fix Q>2, 0<a<=1/2, integers M,N>=1, and real frequencies
xi_1>=pi, xi_{j+1}>=Q xi_j, xi_M<=beta^N, |nu-hat(xi_j)|>=a.
Define

    Psi(a)=((1+a)log(1+a)+(1-a)log(1-a))/2,
    c_Q=(Q-2)/(Q-1),
    D=2a/[pi c_Q(1-(1+a)/Q)], B=aQ/[(1-a)(Q-1)],
    C_sep=1+(1+2R)/[2(beta-1)],
    C(a)=log[(2R+2+D)C_sep]+B.

Symmetry makes the Fourier transform real. Choose signs sigma_j so that
E[sigma_j cos(xi_j W)]>=a and set P_a(x)=prod_j(1+a sigma_j cos(xi_j x)),
F=log P_a. The chord inequality for log(1+at) gives E F(W)>=M Psi(a).
Also Psi''(a)=1/(1-a^2)>=1, so Psi(a)>=a^2/2.

Expand the positive product into exponentials. A nonconstant term with
largest selected index j has frequency modulus at least c_Q xi_j. Its total
absolute coefficient, summed over such terms, is a(1+a)^{j-1}. Therefore,
for every bounded INTERVAL I,

    |integral_I P_a-|I|| <= sum_j 2a(1+a)^{j-1}/(c_Q xi_j) <= D.

Direct differentiation gives Lip(F)<=a/(1-a) sum_j xi_j<=B xi_M.
Put eta=beta^-N and K=floor(W/eta). If U is the union of occupied cells,
positivity gives the essential containing-interval step

    0<Z_U=integral_U P_a
       <=integral_{[-R-1,R+1]} P_a<=2R+2+D.               (3.2)

The interval oscillatory-integral bound was NOT applied to arbitrary U.
For an occupied cell C_k, set p*_k=Z_U^-1 integral_{C_k} P_a. The Lipschitz
bound implies p*_k>=eta exp(F(y)-B)/Z_U for every y in that cell. Relative
entropy of K against p* then gives

    H(K)<=N log beta-M Psi(a)+log(2R+2+D)+B.

Since W=X_N+eta W' with |W'|<=R, conditional on K=k the possible X_N atoms
lie in an interval of length (1+2R)eta. By (3.1) there are at most
C_sep N^{2(d-1)} of them. The entropy chain rule proves

    H_N <= N log beta-M Psi(a)+2(d-1)log N+C(a).          (3.3)

This is the primary quartic entropy argument with separation exponent
2(d-1). That exponent change is technical; it does not pay T.

### Independent block cover

Fix N. Group the independent signs into blocks B_i with the merged law of
X_N; W=sum_i beta^{-Ni}B_i. By the strong law for -log p_{B_i}, almost every
block sequence eventually has prefix probability >=exp[-m(H_N+epsilon)].
There are at most exp[m(H_N+epsilon)] such prefixes of length m. Each maps
to an interval of diameter 2R beta^{-Nm}. Their eventually-always union is
a Borel full-measure set. For s>(H_N+epsilon)/(N log beta), the sums of the
s-powers of these diameters tend to zero. A countable union over eventual
starting times and then epsilon down to zero gives

    dim_H nu <= H_N/(N log beta).                       (3.4)

### Trace and Taylor constants

Trace integrality gives A beta^j+A' beta^-j+e_j in Z for every integer j,
where e_j=sum_r 2 Re(c_r z_r^j), |e_j|<=2Delta. Thus for j>=1,
f(A beta^j)=f(A' beta^-j+e_j). If |cos(pi v)|>=gamma and
|e|<=gamma/(2pi), absolute cosine stays >=gamma/2 on the segment,
f''<=4pi^2/gamma^2, and |f'(v)|<=pi sqrt(2f(v))/gamma.
Taylor and Cauchy–Schwarz therefore give, when Delta<=gamma/(4pi),

    -log|nu-hat(pi A beta^n)|
    <= Lambda+(2sqrt(2)pi/gamma)Delta sqrt(n Lambda)
              +(8pi^2/gamma^2)n Delta^2, n>=0.          (3.5)

These constants follow from the segment bound and Taylor remainder; no
unspecified small-error asymptotic is being used.

For any integer q>=1 with Q=beta^q>2 and M>=1 put n*=q(M-1),

    a=min(1/2, exp[-Lambda-(2sqrt(2)pi/gamma)Delta sqrt(n*Lambda)
                         -8pi^2 n*Delta^2/gamma^2]),
    N=ceil(log_beta(pi A)+n*).

The frequencies pi A beta^{q(i-1)} satisfy all hypotheses of (3.3). Hence
M Psi(a)>2(d-1)log N+C(a) implies dim_H nu<1 by (3.4), and singularity.
This reconstructs EXACT Theorem S as a sufficient statement. It establishes
no converse, and does not give a successful element for beta6.

## 4. Corollary repair and O1/O2 boundaries
