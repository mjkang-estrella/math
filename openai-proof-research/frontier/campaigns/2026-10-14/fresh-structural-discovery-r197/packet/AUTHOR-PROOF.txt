Write h_i(T;V)=dim_C H_i(T;V). A representation is a homomorphism R from
the marked fundamental group to GL_r(C). Row vectors form the right
module v.g=v R(g). Universal-cover cellular chains carry the left deck

1. The exact source input and a small ordinary-coefficient calculation

The source presentation complex B has one vertex, edges x1,a1,x2,a2,s,
and the four relators

 r1=x1 a1 x1^{-1} a1^{-4},
 r2=x2 a2 x2^{-1} a2^{-3},
 r3=x1 a2^{-13},
 r4=s x2 s^{-1} a1^{-5}.

Let P=pi_1(B), K=<<x1^2,x2^3>>_P and G=P/K. Source lem:perfect proves
K perfect; prop:plus produces the fixed X by adjoining two 2-cells,
E with attaching word x1^2 and E' with attaching word x2^3, and two
3-cells. Its universal cellular boundary has a left ZG-linear splitting

                 q_X d3_X = id_{(ZG)^2},                    (1.1)

where q_X projects onto E,E'. Its relative complex over the G-cover of
B is the identity in degrees 3 and 2. Thus for EVERY right ZG-module N,
H_*(X;N)=H_*(B;N) via the inclusion. This is source prop:plus(iii), not
an assertion that B and X are homotopy equivalent.

Fix a primitive third root zeta. The source character is

 rho(x1)=-1, rho(a1)=zeta, rho(x2)=zeta^2,
 rho(a2)=-1, rho(s)=1.

It factors through G, and source lem:twisted-vanishing proves
H_2(X;C_rho)=0. The proof is the full-column-rank Fox boundary, with
minor -4(1-zeta^2). In particular the marked x1 has order exactly two.

We also need H_2(X;C)=0. In the rational abelianization of P the first
two relations say 3[a1]=0 and 2[a2]=0; the last two say [x1]=13[a2]
and [x2]=5[a1]. Consequently P_ab tensor Q is generated freely by [s].
It is precisely Q, since s->1 and the other generators->0 defines a
homomorphism P->Z. Therefore b1(B;Q)=1. The cell counts of B give
chi(B)=1-5+4=0, so 0=1-1+b2(B;Q), hence b2(B;Q)=0. Extension to C
and the source coefficient-preserving inclusion give

                         H_2(X;C)=0.                        (1.2)

The fixed X has cell counts (1,5,6,2). We retain both pairs even though
source commutator identities show that K is normally generated by either
of x1^2,x2^3 alone. Reducing that count is not part of this statement.

The second source input used at the end is cor:torsion: for a finite
connected CW complex Y of dimension at most two, H_2(Y;C_chi)=0 implies
chi(g)=1 for every finite-order g. The entire proof thm:tower, its
cyclic-cover lemma, triangulation, finite-image argument, and Moore-space
specialization were read and are accepted as source operations.

2. Actual finite-cell construction and D(2) splitting

Fix m>=3 and put n=2^{m-1}, d=2^{m-2}. Adjoin based edges a,t to X and
then two 2-cells F,J with attaching words

                 f=a^n x1^{-1},   j=t a t^{-1} a^{-5}.        (2.1)

These are actual finite edge loops. No algebraic realization theorem or
unspecified 3-cell attachment is required. Let Z=Z_m and Gamma=pi_1(Z).
Van Kampen gives the marked relative presentation

             Gamma=<G,a,t | a^n=x1, t a t^{-1}=a^5>.          (2.2)

There are exactly (1,7,8,2) cells and chi(Z)=0. In Gamma we have
x1^2=1 and therefore a^{2n}=1. Nontriviality of x1 and exact order 2n
of a will follow from the explicit representation below, without needing
an unproved injectivity assertion about G->Gamma. The map Gamma->Z
sending s to 1 and all other generators to 0 respects all relators and
is onto. Thus Gamma is infinite.

Put S=Z Gamma and R=ZG. Choose lifts of the old cells compatibly with
the inclusion X->Z. The chains of the preimage of X in the universal
cover of Z are S tensor_R C_*(universal cover of X). This description
is valid also if the homomorphism G->Gamma is not injective: the
preimage is the induced covering whose connected components correspond
to the kernel of that homomorphism. Equivalently, the old boundary
matrices are obtained by applying R->S to their coefficients.

There are no new 3-cells, and the old 3-cell attaching maps lie wholly
in X. Extend S tensor_R q_X by zero on the new F,J 2-cell summands.
This gives q_Z:C2(universal cover of Z)->S^2 with

                             q_Z d3_Z=id.                    (2.3)

Tensoring a split identity needs no flatness assumption. Thus d3_Z is
injective, proving H_i(universal cover of Z;Z)=0 for i>2. For every
left S-module M and every S-linear b:C3->M, the cochain b q_Z has
coboundary b. Consequently H^3(Z;M)=0 for ALL such M, with no finite
generation restriction. Tensoring (2.3) with any right S-module also
shows that H_3(Z;V)=0 for every coefficient system V. Higher homology
vanishes by dimension. This proves all assertions in clause (1).

3. The relative homology identity for every representation

Let R_V:Gamma->GL(V) be ANY finite-dimensional complex representation.
It need not be unitary, semisimple or finite-image. Set

       A=R_V(a), T=R_V(t), U=R_V(x1)=A^n,
       S_k(A)=I+A+...+A^{k-1}.

The group relations give

              A^{2n}=I, U^2=I, T A T^{-1}=A^5.              (3.1)

The relative complex C_*(Z,X;V) has V^2 in degree 2, V^2 in degree 1,
and zero in all other degrees. With the 2-cells ordered F,J and the
new edges ordered a,t, its boundary sends row vectors (u,v) to

 D(u,v)=(u S_n(A)+v(T-S_5(A)), v(I-A^5)).                   (3.2)

For completeness, apply the source's left Fox derivative convention
partial(uv)=partial(u)+u partial(v). The derivatives of f along a and
x1 are S_n(a) and -a^n x1^{-1}, respectively. After evaluation they are
S_n(A) and -I. For j, the a derivative is t-S_5(a) because t a t^{-1}
=a^5 in Gamma, and the t derivative is 1-a^5. No other old edge appears
in j. Tensoring the left cellular boundary with the right row module
therefore gives exactly (3.2). The old-edge part of the absolute
boundary of (u,v) is -u on the edge x1 and zero on every other old edge.

Because A has finite order 2n over C, it is diagonalizable. Write

 E_0={w:w A=w},
 E_* = direct sum of row eigenspaces for lambda^n=1, lambda!=1,
 E_- = direct sum of row eigenspaces for lambda^n=-1.

Then V=E_0 direct-sum E_* direct-sum E_-, and Fix(U)=E_0 direct-sum E_*.
Since gcd(5,2n)=1, v(I-A^5)=0 is equivalent to v in E_0. Relation
T A=A^5 T implies that T preserves E_0. Thus for v in E_0,

                     v(T-S_5(A))=v(T-5I) lies in E_0.

On E_0 the operator S_n(A) is nI; on E_* it is zero; and on a lambda
eigenspace in E_- it is (-2)/(lambda-1), which is nonzero. Consequently
D(u,v)=0 holds exactly when

 v is arbitrary in E_0,
 u=u_* - n^{-1} v(T-5I), with u_* arbitrary in E_*.            (3.3)

In particular u belongs to Fix(U) for EVERY relative 2-cycle, and

                 dim ker D = dim E_0+dim E_* = dim Fix(U).    (3.4)

This uses semisimplicity of the single finite-order operator A only.
It imposes no semisimplicity on the Gamma-representation or on T.

The connecting map H_2(Z,X;V)->H_1(X;V) is zero. Indeed, a relative
2-cycle (u,v) has old boundary -u on x1, and u U=u by (3.3). The old
2-cell E has attaching word x1^2, so its twisted boundary is I+U on
that same edge. Adding the coefficient u/2 on E gives boundary +u,
which cancels the old boundary. Thus every relative 2-cycle has an
explicit absolute 2-cycle lift:

               u F + v J + (u/2) E.                         (3.5)

There are no relative 3-chains. The homology sequence of the cellular
pair, which is degreewise split before tensoring, now gives

 0 -> H_2(X;V|_G) -> H_2(Z;V) -> ker D -> 0.                 (3.6)

In fact (3.5) splits this sequence as complex vector spaces; its
projection is (u,v), so no nonzero such lift can be an old 3-boundary.
It follows that, for every R_V,

              h_2(Z;V)=h_2(X;V|_G)+dim Fix(U).              (3.7)

The relative kernel alone would not have implied (3.7). Killing the
connecting class using the ACTUAL x1^2 cell is essential. Both the
source splitting and that cell remain present in this construction.

4. The universal rank-dependent lower bound

Let r=dim V and let b=dim E_-. On E_-, A's eigenvalues lambda satisfy
lambda^n=-1, so they are exactly some primitive 2^m-th roots of unity.
Conjugacy of A and A^5 implies that the multiplicities of these roots
are constant on multiplication-by-5 orbits of their odd exponents
modulo 2^m. Equivalently T permutes the associated eigenspaces (possibly
in the inverse order), so they have equal dimensions around each orbit.

Each such orbit has length d=2^{m-2}. Here is the elementary order
calculation. For j>=0,

                       v_2(5^{2^j}-1)=j+2.                  (4.1)

For j=0 this is 5-1=4. Inductively, 5^{2^j} is 1 modulo 4, so its
successor has 2-adic valuation 1; factoring a difference of squares
proves the next case. For an odd positive k, the quotient
(y^k-1)/(y-1) is odd whenever y is odd, since it is the sum of an odd
number of odd terms. Therefore if ell=2^j k with k odd, then
v_2(5^ell-1)=j+2. The least positive ell with 5^ell=1 modulo 2^m is
2^{m-2}. Multiplying an exponent by an odd residue does not change
that condition. This proves the orbit assertion.

It follows that d divides b. Set r=q d+s with 0<=s<d. Because
0<=b<=r, r-b is nonnegative and congruent to s modulo d, hence

                  dim Fix(U)=r-b >= s.

Combining with (3.7) proves

                h_2(Z;V) >= s = r-d floor(r/d)              (4.2)

for EVERY rank-r complex local system. Arbitrary nonsemisimple
representations are included. In particular if 1<=r<d, the bound is
h_2(Z;V)>=r. This is stronger than merely excluding a particular
rank-one character.

5. Attainment in every rank by finite-image unitary systems

Choose lambda=exp(2 pi i/2^m). On C^d, with column basis e_j indexed
by j modulo d, define

             A e_j=lambda^{5^j} e_j,    T e_j=e_{j-1}.       (5.1)

Thus T A T^{-1}=A^5, including the cyclic wrap because
5^d=1 modulo 2^m. Also A^n=-I. Define R_m on the old generators by

 R_m(x1)=-I, R_m(a1)=zeta I, R_m(x2)=zeta^2 I,
 R_m(a2)=-I, R_m(s)=I,

and on a,t by (5.1). The old generator matrices satisfy every relation
of G by the source character. The two new relations hold by (5.1)
and A^n=-I. We therefore have an ACTUAL representation of Gamma.
Every matrix is unitary. Its image is finite: it is generated by
A, T and zeta I, with A^{2^m}=I, T^d=I, T normalizing <A>, and zeta I
central of order three. Every image element has the form
(zeta I)^i A^j T^k with 0<=i<3, 0<=j<2^m and 0<=k<d.
This also proves that a has order exactly 2^m and x1 order exactly
two in Gamma.

The restriction of R_m to G is the direct sum of d copies of the
source rho. Therefore h_2(X;R_m|_G)=0. Since U=-I, equation (3.7)
gives h_2(Z;R_m)=0. Alternatively both S_n(A) and I-A^5 are invertible,
so (3.2) is an invertible relative boundary and source plus preservation
computes the same vanishing. This second calculation checks that no
unstated topological realization of a matrix has been assumed.

For the trivial one-dimensional representation 1, equation (1.2) and
(3.7) give

                           h_2(Z;1)=1.                     (5.2)

For any r=q d+s as above, take the direct sum of q copies of R_m and
s copies of 1. The representation has dimension r, is unitary with
finite image, and homology commutes with this finite direct sum. Thus
its second Betti number is s. Combined with (4.2), this proves the
exact minimum in clause (2) and attainment for every r>=1.

The positive ranks with H_2=0 are precisely the positive multiples
of d. In fact whenever H_2(Z;V)=0, (3.7) implies Fix(U)=0. Since
U^2=I, this means U=-I. Hence H_0(Z;V)=0: coinvariants include the
relation vU-v=-2v for every v. We already proved H_i(Z;V)=0 for i>=3.
The finite cellular Euler identity sum_i (-1)^i h_i(Z;V)=r chi(Z)=0
now forces H_1(Z;V)=0. Thus every H_2-zero local system here is
acyclic in all degrees, proving the remaining assertion of clause (2).

6. Sharp finite-cover degree and the two-dimensional obstruction

First recall the right-module form of the classical covering identity.
If H<=Gamma has finite index e and W is the corresponding connected
cover, and L is a right CH-module, put

                     Ind L=L tensor_{CH} C Gamma.

Its dimension is e dim L. Associativity of tensor products, using the
common universal cover, gives the chain isomorphism

 (L tensor_{CH} C Gamma) tensor_{C Gamma} C_*(universal cover of Z;C)
       = L tensor_{CH} C_*(universal cover of Z;C)
       = C_*(W;L).                                         (6.1)

The cellular bases on the left and right match cosets and lifted cells,
so this is a chain isomorphism with the specified monodromy, not only
an equality of Euler characteristics. This is the Eckmann-Shapiro
operation, explicitly recorded with proof in Friedl--Nagel, Lemma 2.7;
their Lemmas 2.1 and 4.5 also make the finite-image/unitary transfer
explicit, in left-module conventions.

If dim L=1 and H_2(W;L)=0, (6.1) gives a rank-e representation with
H_2(Z;Ind L)=0. Clause (2) therefore proves d divides e. This conclusion
holds for every connected finite cover and every rank-one local system,
not only for the cyclic cover used to attain the minimum.

For attainment, define phi:Gamma->Z/d by phi(t)=1 and phi=0 on every
other marked generator. It is a well-defined epimorphism by (2.2).
Let H=ker phi and W the corresponding connected d-sheeted cyclic cover.
The permutation part of the monomial representation R_m is precisely
phi (up to choice of cyclic orientation). Every h in H acts diagonally.
Consequently the row line C e_0 is H-invariant and defines a character
chi:H->C^* by e_0 R_m(h)=chi(h)e_0. Its image is finite. In particular
x1 belongs to H and chi(x1)=-1.

There is an explicit right Gamma-module isomorphism

 C_chi tensor_{CH} C Gamma -> C^d,
                         e_0 tensor g |-> e_0 R_m(g).        (6.2)

It is well-defined because e_0 R_m(h)=chi(h)e_0. The coset representatives
1,t,...,t^{d-1} map to e_0,e_1,...,e_{d-1}, since the row action of T
cycles in that order. Thus (6.2) is bijective and intertwines the full
Gamma-action. Equations (6.1), (6.2), and h_2(Z;R_m)=0 imply
H_2(W;C_chi)=0. The marked x1 loop closes at the chosen lift of the
basepoint, has square one, and has nontrivial chi-value. It therefore
has order exactly two in pi_1(W). This proves clause (3), including
minimality of d among all possible finite covers.

Suppose now that Z were homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex Y
of dimension at most two. Y is connected. Choose a based homotopy
equivalence and transfer the subgroup H along the induced isomorphism
pi_1(Z)->pi_1(Y). Its corresponding d-sheeted cover Y_H is a finite
connected complex of dimension at most two. Lifting the homotopy
equivalence and an inverse gives a homotopy equivalence W~Y_H (with
compatible basepoint choices; deck changes only conjugate the marking).
Transfer chi along this equivalence. Homotopy invariance of local
homology gives H_2(Y_H;C_chi)=0, while the transferred order-two element
still has character value -1. This contradicts source cor:torsion.
Therefore no such Y exists. Clause (4) follows.

7. What is being nominated, and what is already paid

The full nominee is the explicit family with the exact all-rank minimum
profile (4.2), its attainment, and the sharp cover-degree consequence,
while retaining the source finite D(2) conditions. No independent new
counterexample to Wall's problem is claimed: that is the stipulated
source input. No finite-group example or asphericity conclusion occurs.

The strongest compatible reduction is stronger than a new vector-valued
tower theorem. The witness (5.1) is induced from a rank-one system on a
finite cyclic cover, so SOURCE cor:torsion plus classical Shapiro already
supplies the geometric obstruction once the construction is done. The
rank-one tower can also be extended to finite rank by the inequality
r chi<=dim H0<r, but that routine extension is unnecessary here and is
not offered as the result.

Finite plus construction, relative presentation realization, cyclic
spectral orbits, coefficient induction and stable sphere cancellation
are all credited to source/classical operations. The additional derived
identity is (3.7), valid for every representation, with its explicit
connecting-map cancellation (3.5). Combining that identity with the
spectral orbit bound and trivial-block attainment determines the entire
rank profile. Existence of a single induced representation alone would
not establish that profile or the lower bound over every cover.

Hambleton's Lemma 2.1 applies to Z and gives a finite two-dimensional
model after adjoining two 2-spheres, since Z has two 3-cells. This is a
paid stabilization. Its twisted H2 increases by 2r and so does not give
the unstabilized profile in the statement. Likewise the source itself
has a rank-one H2-zero system, whereas every rank-one system on Z_m
has H2 of dimension at least one for m>=3. No sphere wedge is the
operation producing this profile.

The sharp number d grows with m, but the word a^n has growing length.
Eighteen is an upper cell count of the displayed structure, not a
minimal-cell theorem or a bounded description-length assertion.
