## What Dloussky–Teleman already pays

Published Section 2 treats simply exceptional divisors, their local deformation and uniqueness, and the non-minimality divisor He in the base. Theorem 4 extends the exceptional line-bundle class to a section of the associated Picard line bundle. Its norm is exponential of minus Gauduchon degree. Area explosion is used positively to extend that section by zero, via Radó.

Theorem 5 does not merely prove a fiberwise existence assertion. It takes a 2-connected base, a lifted class and strictly pseudoconvex nonseparating spheres on all fibers. It lifts exceptional divisors over the nonempty non-minimality complement, uses a smoothly varying sphere as a common one-sided barrier, applies Remmert–Stein to the closure, and proves flatness. Remark 7 extends the operation to families each of whose fibers admits a small deformation containing the exceptional class; in particular, arbitrary families of GSS surfaces over a ball are included even if every displayed fiber is minimal. Theorem 2 in the introduction records that stronger family conclusion. Theorem 6 permits an exceptional codimension-two set; its proof uses the forward deck semigroup and local finiteness to bound the closures towards the pseudoconvex end. Neither an all-fibers GSS flat family nor removing its formerly unknown central GSS case is a distinct nominee after Source060.

Section 4 actually constructs the universal cover by an infinite chain of elementary blown-up ball pieces. With b=b2, its standard compact homology basis (u_j) and compact curves (C_j) satisfy

    H2(Y,Z)=direct_sum_{j in Z} Z u_j,
    u_j.u_k=-delta_jk,  K.u_j=-1,
    [C_j]=u_j-sum_{d=1}^{s_j}u_{j+d},
    1<=s_j<=b+1,  s_{j+b}=s_j,
    f_*u_j=u_{j+b},  f_*C_j=C_{j+b},

where f moves towards the pseudoconcave end. These are actual construction formulas, not guesses from a dual graph. Proposition 3 defines the positive integer fundamental series E_i=sum_{j>=0}a^(i)_j C_{i+j} by forward triangular inversion. Remark 12 proves one-sided uniqueness in Borel–Moore homology using compact test classes. Lemma 5 excludes open curves bounded towards the pseudoconvex end by contracting the compact curves into a capped ball and applying Remmert–Stein. Theorem 7 identifies the flat exceptional limits with the fundamental series. Both compactness and canonical coefficient recovery are therefore already paid once a shell is known.

The b=1 Enoki series has all coefficients 1. The b=1 Inoue–Hirzebruch series is Fibonacci. At b=2 the intermediate pattern yields powers of 2 per period; the one-cycle Inoue–Hirzebruch pattern gives a 2 by 2 matrix with eigenvalues 1±sqrt(2); the two-cycle pattern again uses Fibonacci coefficients. These examples are explicit in the primary paper, not new examples or new classifications. Its earlier introduction also describes curve-class changes and area explosion within minimal GSS families. This rules out treating individual rational curves as a fixed proper family without proof.
