## The different operation pursued

restriction functors. It changes the starting object to one over F_2 whose
minimal syzygy dimensions are 2n+1. This avoids both a moving scalar orbit

the triangular conversion and ordinary symmetric trivial extension, including
negative complete-Hom calculations; it fails for an exact mathematical reason.

Put k=F_2,

    E=k[x,y]/(x^2,y^2), B=k[u]/(u^2), X=E/(x,y), T=B/(u).

The coefficient-of-xy and coefficient-of-u forms make E and B symmetric.

and regular right E action. Each is right free of rank one and left free
of rank two. Set F=L_x direct-sum L_y. The exact functors L_i tensor_E -
are the two restrictions, preserve projectives, and send X to T as actual
modules. Their positive cohomology maps are

    k[alpha,beta] -> k[t],
    (alpha,beta) -> (t,0) and (0,t).

algebras. It cannot be silently interpreted as a zero/identity pair of square
maps on the source's original polynomial pieces.

resolution of X with E^{n+1} in degree n. Its syzygy dimensions are exactly

# Frozen target LR: a linear intersecting partite covering obstruction

There exist an integer r>=2, pairwise disjoint finite sets V_1,...,V_r,
and a nonempty SET E of subsets of V=union_i V_i, such that:

1. For EVERY e in E and EVERY i in {1,...,r}, |e intersect V_i|=1.
2. For EVERY two DISTINCT e,f in E, |e intersect f|=1.
3. For EVERY C subset V with |C|<=r-1, there exists e in E with
   e intersect C empty.

and tau(H)=r. The upper bound follows because any edge covers E.
No bound on r, side sizes, degrees, edge count, coordinates, or construction

# Checkpoint: no intersecting linear detachment of the balanced source

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.
This is a construction obstruction, not a proof or refutation of LR.

## Exact geometric hypotheses

Let A be the affine plane F_q^2, q>=4, and D its q+1 directions.
For each d choose two distinct parallel constituent lines M_d^0,M_d^1.
Choose two other split lines of direction d, with four retained designated
points on each. Let S be the collection of split lines. Put
R=A minus union S, and X=R union all designated point sets. Assume each
split line meets X in exactly its four designated points.

The d-partition P_d of X has ordinary blocks X intersect L on lines L
other than the two constituents and two split lines, one merged block
X intersect (M_d^0 union M_d^1), and two opposite-pair blocks on each
split line. Assume at most B constituents pass through any affine point.
Assume EVERY nonsplit line L contains more than 2B points of X.
Assume every constituent contains a point of R. Finally assume that some
split line has two designated points in different opposite-pair blocks
and that the original partitions give every pair of distinct X-points
at least one agreement. These assumptions are all literal properties
of the balanced source's deterministic construction.

A refinement consists of partitions Q_d of the SAME X with every Q_d
block contained in a P_d block. There is no bound on the number or sizes
of refined blocks. Claim: no such refinement gives exactly one shared
block for every pair of distinct points of X.

## 1. Forced connectivity on every nonsplit line

For a nonsplit line L of direction d, join a,b in X intersect L when
their original edges agree in exactly one part. They already agree in
part d. In a different direction h, their h-lines are distinct, since
their actual joining line is L. Therefore an additional agreement can
only come from the merged h-block, with a and b on different h-constituents.

Fix a. It lies on constituents in at most B directions altogether. For
each h!=d among those directions, the partner constituent intersects L
in at most one point. Thus a has at most B nonneighbors in this graph
(on distinct vertices; a is not counted). The graph on n=|X intersect L|
vertices is connected when n>2B. Indeed, if a component C were proper,
for any vertex in C all n-|C| vertices outside C are nonneighbors, so
|C|>=n-B. Two distinct components would then give n>=2(n-B), contrary
to n>2B.

In any refinement preserving intersection, a pair with a unique original
agreement must retain that agreement in the same part: refinement cannot
create an agreement in another part. Transitivity along the connected
graph therefore puts ALL of X intersect L in one refined d-block.
This holds for every nonsplit line, including each constituent separately.

## 2. A merged cross-identification forces a duplicate

Suppose a refined d-block contains a point of M_d^0 and a point of M_d^1.
By the preceding connectivity, it contains all of X on both constituents.
Choose x in R intersect M_d^0 and y in R intersect M_d^1. Their joining
line J is nonsplit: either endpoint in R lies on NO split line. Its
direction h differs from d because the endpoints lie on different
d-parallel lines. Connectivity on J forces x,y together in refined
part h as well as in refined part d. Thus the resulting edges have at
least two common vertices. A linear refinement cannot make ANY such
cross-identification.

## 3. A split pair forces precisely such an identification

Take the stipulated designated pair a,b separated by its split partition
in direction g. Its actual joining line is that split line. It cannot
agree in any ordinary block in a different direction, and refinement
cannot restore its lost g-agreement. Intersection in the refinement
therefore requires a,b together in a merged block of some direction
d!=g. They lie on different constituents in that direction, since their
joining line has direction g. This is the cross-identification prohibited
by step2. The contradiction proves the claim. No uniqueness of the
source's adjacency generator was assumed or needed.

## 4. Application to the actual source, with no new existence hypothesis

In balanced construction.tex, eq:det-core and eq:det-split-intersection
give exactly X,R,S above. Compatibility gives the designated-point
property. The section Partitions and intersecting edges proves pairwise
intersection. Any consecutive designated pair is separated by its two
opposite-pair blocks.

The occupancy hypotheses of prop:deterministic-reduction give at least
rho*q points of X on EVERY nonsplit line: regular lines and M_d^0
have that many points in R_0 subset R; M_d^1 has that many in R.
The source's eq:det-size-condition is rho*q>2(1+B), which implies n>2B
and supplies points of R on both constituents. Hence EVERY configuration
meeting that proposition's hypotheses has no intersecting linear
simultaneous partition refinement. This is not a probabilistic claim or
an assumed numerical property of one uncomputed source example.

The source thm:main proves existence of its configurations for sufficiently
large primes with B=ceil(log q), fixed rho>0 and the displayed inequality.
We accept that theorem, not audit its probability estimates. Our deduction
uses only its deterministic interface, which has been read in full.

## 5. Why copying edges or cloning vertices does not evade the obstruction

More generally there is no r-partite linear intersecting hypergraph K
with a part-preserving vertex map pi to such a source H whose induced
edge map is onto E(H). To see this, choose one preimage edge f_x for each
source edge e_x. Different x give different f_x because their images are
different. In each part, declare x,y equivalent when f_x,f_y use the same
K-vertex. These are partitions refining the source partitions. Pairwise
intersection and linearity of K give exactly one agreement between every
pair, contradicting steps1-3. K may have arbitrarily many vertices and
additional edges; selecting these finitely many edges suffices.

This is the natural cover-preserving lift: if a cover of K had t vertices,
its images would cover H with at most t vertices, so tau(K)>=tau(H).
The contradiction rules out this entire lift for THIS source, including
edge-surjective clone constructions. It does not rule out other source
hypergraphs, non-surjective constructions, changing the number of parts,
or genuine trades that introduce new agreements rather than refining old
ones. It certainly does not prove tau<=r-1 for all linear hypergraphs.

## Limits and contribution

Forced pairs and equivalence closure are elementary operations. The
source-specific all-configuration obstruction is supporting work; no
worldwide novelty or substantial-result nomination is asserted. Its value
is to reject a real cover-preserving operation with explicit hypotheses,
rather than declaring arbitrary pair-color choices a valid linearization.

# Checkpoint: one-fresh-coordinate affine pencil extensions cannot work

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.
This is a separate attempted construction, not a subclass replacement for LR.

Let q>=3 be a prime power and D the q+1 affine-plane directions. In part d
make one old vertex [d,L] for each affine d-line L, and any finite set T_d
of fresh vertices, all labels in all parts disjoint. For any chosen subset
X of F_q^2 use the ordinary edges

 e_x={ [d,x+d] : d in D }, x in X.

Allow any finite set of exceptional edges, each of the precise form

 f(p,g,t)={t} union { [d,p+d] : d in D, d!=g }, t in T_g.

Here p is any affine point. Centers, omitted directions, fresh labels,
ordinary points, and their numbers are arbitrary. Equal edges are retained
only once. Suppose the resulting nonempty hypergraph H is linear and
intersecting. Then EVERY part has at most q nonisolated vertices, and
therefore tau(H)<=q=r-1. Thus this whole construction cannot witness LR.

Proof. Fix an exceptional edge f=f(p,g,t), and write L=p+g.
No ordinary edge e_x can have x on L. For x=p, its intersection with f
has q old vertices, violating linearity. For x!=p on L, the only direction
in which the two pencils agree is g, and that label is omitted from f;
they are disjoint. Consequently the old label [g,L] occurs in no ordinary
edge.

It occurs in no exceptional edge either. An exception with omitted
direction g has a fresh vertex in that part. An exception f(p',h,t') with
h!=g could contain [g,L] only if p' lies on L. If p'=p, the two exceptions
share q-1 old labels, at least two since q>=3. If p'!=p, their unique
possible common old label would have direction g, omitted from f; they
have no common fresh label, because their fresh labels lie in different
parts. This would make them disjoint. Both cases are excluded. Thus
[g,L] is an UNUSED old vertex of part g in the entire H.

For each used fresh label t in part g, all centers of exceptions using t
lie on one g-line L(t). Otherwise two such centers have joining direction
h!=g, and the two edges share both t and [h,p+h], contradicting linearity.
The preceding paragraph shows that [g,L(t)] is unused.

The assignment t -> L(t) is injective on used fresh labels in part g.
If two different fresh labels had centers p,p' on the same g-line, then
p=p' would give q common old vertices, and p!=p' would give no common
old or fresh vertex. Either violates the hypotheses. Hence each used
fresh label pays for a DISTINCT unused old label. If u_g old labels are
unused, then |T_g used|<=u_g, and the total number of nonisolated vertices
in part g is at most (q-u_g)+u_g=q. If no exceptions use part g, that
bound is immediate. Selecting all nonisolated vertices of any part covers
all edges and proves the assertion. The same argument includes X empty.

The motivation was to use the q old labels of a punctured pencil to give
exactly one intersection with ordinary point-edges while adding a fresh
label. Using two or more exceptions with the same omitted line avoids
a degree-one fresh label, but the injection above shows why it still
cannot enlarge the usable part. This is an all-parameter obstruction,
not a finite search. It applies only to exceptions with ONE fresh
coordinate and an ordinary pencil in every other coordinate. Exceptions
with several fresh coordinates or old labels that are not one pencil,
and other linear-space constructions, remain outside the claim.

The elementary finite-plane and covering operations are established
background, not a substantial contribution claim. This theorem does not
assert anything about arbitrary linear intersecting hypergraphs beyond
its explicit construction class.

## 1. Model and the operation attempted

    f(p;a,b;z_a,z_b) = {z_a,z_b} union {[d,p+d]: d notin {a,b}}.

coordinates. The six-edge gadget below is a nonpencil partition trade:
its new triple blocks are not old line blocks, and it changes pair agreements.
The theorem is about this precise ambient model, not arbitrary exceptions.

Choose four distinct directions d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3, fresh labels z_i in these
parts, and six centers p_ij indexed by 0<=i<j<=3. Write f_ij for the

for two DIFFERENT centers, their old pencils agree only in their joining
direction, and retain that agreement if and only if neither edge omits it.
Fresh agreements are counted separately. This proves pair conditions
directly; it does not select colors without transitivity.

The intended mechanism was to increase all four affected part sizes by

* h=0: the first two h-lines have y=0. The last row then forces x=0,

# Resolvable designs: exact translation and the synchronized inflation attempt

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. 2026-10-16.
Original prose CC BY 4.0; original code MIT. Third-party rights retained privately.

## Exact dual formulation, with singleton blocks permitted

For a finite nonempty edge set E of an r-partite hypergraph, put X=E.
In part i, partition X according to its incident vertex; omit isolated
vertices and use a separate block label for every part. Every pair of
distinct X-points is in exactly one common block across all partitions
if and only if the original hypergraph is linear and intersecting.
Selecting hypergraph vertices covers edges if and only if their blocks
cover X. This preserves EVERY mixed-part cover, not just whole classes.

Conversely, given r partitions of a nonempty finite X with precisely one
common block per distinct pair, take one vertex per block, labelled by
its partition, and one edge per point. Distinct points give distinct
edges: otherwise they would share all r>=2 vertices. These operations
are inverse up to labels after isolated vertices are discarded. Thus LR
is exactly the existence of such partitions with block cover number r.
Singleton blocks may appear in this general translation. Excluding them
would narrow the target. An LR witness cannot have them, because if {x}
is a block through x, the other r-1 blocks through x cover ALL points:
each other point has its unique agreement with x somewhere else.

The partitions automatically impose equivalence transitivity. Merely
partitioning the PAIRS of X into colors does not provide this structure.

## A restricted assembly that genuinely realizes pair coverage

Let a>=2 and let P_1,...,P_a be partitions of a finite master set Z,
|Z|>=2, with every distinct pair in exactly one common block. Assume all
master blocks have size at least two. Write c_M for the minimum number
of master blocks covering Z. Fix m>=2. For each z take a disjoint fiber
F_z of m points. For each master block B require an ACTUAL resolvable
TD(|B|,m) on the fibers F_z, z in B: every block meets every fiber once,
every pair in different fibers occurs once, and its m^2 blocks are
partitioned into m parallel classes, each of m blocks. The existence of
these ingredients is a hypothesis, not inferred from divisibility.

On each F_z take b>=1 internal partitions having exactly one common
block per distinct pair. Require the same number b of internal classes
on every fiber. Internal class j on the output is the union of internal
class j over all fibers. For each i and each t in {1,...,m}, an output
cross class is the union of local TD class t over B in P_i. Blocks in
these unions are disjoint and cover all fibers, since P_i partitions Z.
There are exactly

                         R=ma+b

output partitions. A same-fiber pair occurs precisely once internally
and never in a cross block. A different-fiber pair projects to a unique
master block, then occurs in a unique block of its TD, and nowhere else.
Thus this is a proved resolvable pairwise balanced design, not necessary
incidence equations or an assertion that a general WFC preserves resolutions.

## The full cover estimate for this operation

For every selected master block B, one local TD parallel class consists
of m OUTPUT blocks covering the entire union of its fibers. Select a
minimum master block cover, of size c_M, and do this for each of its
blocks. The output has a cover by at most m c_M blocks. Choices for
different B can belong to different output classes; this is a valid
mixed-class cover. The blocks through any fixed master point cover Z,
by pair coverage, so c_M<=a. Therefore

                c_output <= m c_M <= ma = R-b <= R-1.

No cover estimate from the pinned source is used. Even a hypothetical
master with c_M=a would not make this synchronized inflation an LR
witness. The additional internal classes increase R without preventing
the m-fold lifted cover. The result applies to nonuniform master blocks
and arbitrary ingredient resolutions satisfying the stated hypotheses.

The ingredients are not vacuous. Take a master consisting of the three
two-point partitions of a four-point set. For m any prime power, a local
TD(2,m) is given on two copies of F_m by pairs (s,s+t), with t fixed
within a parallel class. Put the whole fiber in its one internal block,
so b=1. This explicitly realizes the output on 4m points with 3m+1
partitions and a cover of at most 2m blocks, by lifting a master parallel
class. This example is illustration of the operation, not a proposed LR
witness or a meaningful new construction family.
