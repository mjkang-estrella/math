# Frozen target CDF098: a doubling rectifiable curve with no finite-dimensional embedding

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0, original code MIT.

Frozen after the preliminary source/history/primary and construction screen, before writing the full proof packet or any diagnostic. There exists a finite universal integer Lambda such that, for every infinite-dimensional real Banach space B, there exists a Lipschitz map gamma:[0,1] -> B whose image Gamma satisfies both:

1. With the ambient norm distance, Gamma is Lambda-doubling: every intrinsic Gamma ball of radius r>0 has a cover by at most Lambda intrinsic Gamma balls of radius r/2, with centers in Gamma.
2. Gamma admits no bi-Lipschitz embedding into any finite-dimensional real normed space, at any finite distortion.

Thus Gamma is one compact path-connected set of finite one-dimensional Hausdorff measure, chosen independently of the target dimension and distortion. The Lipschitz constant of gamma need only be finite, not prescribed. No injectivity, simple-arc topology, intrinsic-geodesic metric, uniform quasiconvexity, local Ahlfors regularity, or quantitative finite-witness cardinality is asserted. The metric throughout is the ambient Banach norm restricted to the image.

The proposed additional operation is a dimension-independent ambient doubling bound for a connected finite polygonal thickening of any finite doubling Hilbert set, obtained by lifting its nets to dyadic heights before joining parent edges. Direct star filling may create unbounded local branch counts; filling all sparse source coordinates continuously creates high-dimensional boxes. Finite length alone does not bound the doubling constant. The new operation must supply the ambient covering bound at every ball, including balls whose centers lie inside edges. Summable finite cluster lengths and one spine then give the whole stated curve, without replacing the norm metric by graph distance.
