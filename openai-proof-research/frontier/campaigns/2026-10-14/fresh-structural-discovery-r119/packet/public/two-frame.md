# Two-frame tensor feasibility calculation

This is a preselection compatibility calculation, not a frozen candidate, a contribution rating or a mathematical gate. The orthogonal version was derived first; the Lorentz/projective version below was noticed while checking its strongest compatible reduction. Both occurred before any freeze. No target was frozen at any point.

## Exact scope tested

For any integer d>=1, let A,B be finite subsets of the unit sphere in R^d whose coordinates are real algebraic numbers. Either set may be empty. Put N=d+1 and J=diag(-1,1,...,1). Let M1,M2 be arbitrary real N-by-N matrices satisfying Mi^T J Mi=J. For x on the sphere write

    f_M(x) = spatial(M(1,x)) / temporal(M(1,x)).

The denominator is nonzero: an invertible Lorentz map sends a nonzero null vector to a nonzero null vector, and a null vector with zero temporal coordinate is zero. The tested conclusion is that f_M1(A) union f_M2(B) is Euclidean Ramsey: for every finite number r>=2 of colors there is a finite dimension D in which every coloring of R^D contains a monochromatic congruent copy, at its original distances. There is no stated quantitative bound for D, no measurability condition, no proximity-to-identity condition, no commutativity assumption and no bound on the cardinalities. This calculation is relative to the stipulated source172 classification. It does not assert that arbitrary unions of Ramsey sets are Ramsey.

The complete construction below checks this proposed interface in its full stated scope. It is retained to explain why no further unpaid compatibility operation was identified. It is not offered as a new eligible result.

## Exact algebraic calculation

Let K be a real number field containing the coordinates of A and B. Work in E tensor_Q E, where E is the real field generated by K and all entries of M1,M2. Subscripts L and R denote its two scalar embeddings; m is multiplication. Matrix transposes below are ordinary transposes, not tensor flip.

First there is an idempotent e with m(e)=1 and e k_L=e k_R for every k in K. Indeed write K=Q(theta), with separable minimal polynomial f. Over K put g(t)=f(t)/(t-theta), and define

    e = g_L(theta_R) / f'(theta)_L.

The denominator is a unit because it lies in one field factor. The identity (theta_R-theta_L)g_L(theta_R)=0 proves equality of the two theta actions on e. Polynomial and inverse operations give equality for all k in K. Consequently e g_L(theta_R)=e g_L(theta_L)=e f'(theta)_L, giving e^2=e; m(e)=1 follows directly. Injection into the larger tensor ring follows from freeness of field extensions as vector spaces. This is the usual separability projector, with its needed properties proved explicitly here.

It suffices initially to handle A union f_M(B), where M=M1^{-1}M2. Set

    U=M_R M_L^{-1},    delta=det(I+U),    C=adj(I+U),    Q=2 J C.

Both copies of M preserve J. Also det(M) is the real number +1 or -1, so det(U)=1. The following identities hold over this commutative tensor ring, even if delta is a zero divisor:

    C^T J = J C U,
    Q+Q^T = 2 delta J,
    Q U = 2 delta J-Q.                                      (1)

To see the first, I+U^T=J U^{-1}(I+U)J, because J^{-1}=J. Take adjugates, using adj(AB)=adj(B)adj(A), adj(U^{-1})=U and similarity covariance. This gives C^T=J C U J. For the other two use C(I+U)=delta I; adjugates commute with their original matrix. No density argument or cancellation by delta is used.

For every algebraic null vector p=(1,a), equality of its two scalar actions on e and characteristic zero give

    e p_L^T Q p_R = e p_L^T Q p_L
                    = e delta p_L^T J p_L = 0.              (2)

For p=(1,b), use M_R=U M_L and (1). The matrix T=M_L^T Q M_R satisfies

    T=2 delta J-M_L^T Q M_L,
    T+T^T=2 delta J.

Thus e p_L^T T p_R=0 by exactly the same null-vector calculation. Dividing by the two separate, nonzero temporal coordinates shows that eQ kills the actual affine columns of f_M(B), not merely their unnormalized homogeneous representatives.

Under m, U becomes I, delta becomes 2^N and Q becomes 2^N J. Hence

    P=2^{-N} e Q

has m(P)=J and kills all the evaluation columns of A union f_M(B). It never inverts delta.

Finally a certificate with full multiplied matrix J is covariant under any Lorentz projective map H: replace it by H_L^{-T} P H_R^{-1}. Each image evaluation differs from the old evaluation by two invertible temporal scalars, and the multiplied matrix remains H^{-T} J H^{-1}=J. Apply H=M1. This is a certificate for the originally specified two images, including noncommuting M1,M2.

For the affine-spanning hypothesis of the source, enlarge A by the algebraic sphere points +/-e_j. Their augmented columns span R^{d+1}; applying M1 and individually dividing by the nonzero temporal coordinates preserves their rank. The enlarged image therefore affinely spans R^d. Its spatial multiplied block is I_d. The exact source172 `alg:equivalence` already permits coefficients in R tensor_Q R, symmetrizes by tensor flip-transpose and descends to the field generated by the actual output coordinates. It uses free-module expansion and a finite linear system over that smaller field, not an illicit numerical specialization. Apply `thm:classification` to the enlarged set and then take its subset. Empty or singleton original unions cause no exception.

## Strongest compatible reduction and credit

This is a Cayley calculation, rather than a missing new positive Ramsey forcing theorem. When I+U is invertible,

    Q/delta = 2J(I+U)^{-1}
            = J + J(I-U)(I+U)^{-1}.

The second summand is skew-symmetric for a J-isometry. Cayley's original [1846 paper, collected-paper reprint pp.332-334, equations (1)-(16)](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/119100/PDF/WA35_143388_12807-1_Art52.pdf) explicitly obtains the orthogonal transformation through cofactors of an identity-plus-skew matrix. These three scanned pages were visually read, after the extracted text dropped the equations. The modern [Lu–Ye v1, introduction, equation (1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02071v1) explicitly includes arbitrary quadratic matrix groups. Its later representation-theoretic classification is not used or claimed read in full. The short identities (1) provide the indefinite and zero-divisor interface directly, retaining the classical cofactor operation.

The pinned source's `cons:quadratic` already clears a determinant before tensor specialization and normalizes only by its nonzero multiplied value in a single field factor. `alg:equivalence` already handles actual-coordinate-field descent and flip symmetry. Here the multiplied determinant is simply 2^N, so the apparent singular mixed-matrix obstruction is removed by exactly that existing operation. The separability projector makes the algebraic coordinate blocks compatible. The displayed calculation verifies all these interfaces together: two different maps, arbitrarily many points, indefinite homogeneous coordinates, actual output fields, and the original-scale Ramsey conclusion. No additional unpaid operation was found after doing that integration. This is the reason for not freezing; neither the number of elementary identities nor absence of an identical theorem in a search is being used as a gate.

Actual historical comparison matters. I17's long original six-point proof (read in full late in this round, after the initial two-frame feasibility calculation) constructs a local Mobius comparison, residual ideal generators and a diagonal Jacobian. The actual r3 review gives a stronger compatible reduction: multiply two three-point bilinear adjugate interpolants, clear denominators, and normalize after multiplication. That review's rating does not decide this round; its explicit operation does exclude presenting a planar two-frame polynomial factorization as an untouched bridge. Both readings occurred before any freeze, and the late full-author reading is not backdated.

The positive primary [Ivan–Leader–Walters, 2606.13472v1, Theorem1 and Lemmas2-3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13472v1) uses a common finite transitive group and a nonzero perpendicular prism height. Its proof constructs interpolated orbit sets and a wreath-product action, then increases height by a product with a two-point set. It does not directly supply this coplanar/projective statement: algebraic sets need not come with a common finite group, and its height is explicitly nonzero. This failure alone gives no contribution, because the Cayley/source reduction above is compatible.

The negative primary [Palvolgyi, 2609.23327v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23327v1), Theorem1, has an explicit seven-point derivation obstruction. Appendix A.10's Mobius/algebraic discussion excludes a particular derivation certificate, not all negative colorings; Appendix A.17's positive conclusion concerns colorings invariant under algebraic translations. Neither is silently promoted to the arbitrary-coloring conclusion here. The author's warning that the appendix is unchecked is retained.

There is no proposed arbitrary-three-frame extension: source172 `cons:twelve` already gives a non-Ramsey union of three rotated rational squares. This source example is an exclusion, not a new counterexample and not a refutation of the two-frame calculation. No quantitative dimension estimate, subtransitive embedding, arbitrary algebraic dependency algorithm or general multi-frame criterion is obtained.
