# Focused operation screen and contribution limit

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0.

The target was unchanged full R throughout. This round did not perform
a broad new-family discovery campaign or duplicate the analytic/crossing
work. It examined actual cyclic Hom transport and the normalizer route.

The decisive primary statement is Digne–Godelle–Michel,
[arXiv:2407.07459v2, Lemma 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07459v2#S2).
It already supplies an arbitrary-type twisted product formula for the
parabolic set retraction on inputs whose Coxeter images normalize W_I.
The zero fiber in the actual Artin normalizer is therefore a subgroup;
the candidate proves this with products, inverses and a representative
of every quotient coset. Stipulated torsion-freeness then applies to an
embedded subgroup, and stipulated parabolic closure supplies the reduction
from a root to its normalizer. These are explicit short operations, not
a new finite-orbit height theorem.

Consequently the proposed full proof is a source composition. It may
resolve the parent research gap relative to the stipulated premises,
but no substantial frontier contribution is nominated. A new formula or
absence of an identical theorem title would not change that assessment.
No independent contribution PASS is claimed.

The r11 antecedents survive: CMV pay large-type root closure with systolic
finite-orbit fixation; HMS credit two-dimensional root closure; GHLY's
spherical arrangement model does not become an arbitrary-type theorem
through its atomic Matsumoto result. The quotient embedding here is
weaker than GHLY's specific model and does not prove Conjecture 1.10.

Heng–Licata–Yacobi's finite-type periodic-elements theorem was inspected
because a stability-space fixed-point or phase argument might have paid
the cyclic problem. Its central-power hypothesis, full-twist shift and
stability covering are actual extra interfaces, so it was not imported
into arbitrary infinite type. That route was abandoned when the exact
retraction formula supplied the normalizer argument.
