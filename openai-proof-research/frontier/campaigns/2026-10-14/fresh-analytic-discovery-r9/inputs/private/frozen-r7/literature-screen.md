# Bounded primary-literature comparison

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0. Read on 2026-10-16. Private PDFs/extractions retain their original rights. No worldwide novelty certificate is claimed.

Searches included: “random conductance critical Galton Watson tree spectral gap heavy tailed resistance Frechet traps”; “random planar maps q greater than 4 random walk conductances spectral gap heavy tail”; “spectral gap heavy-tailed conductances tree”; “spectral Frechet random conductance”; “conditioned trees heavy resistances spectral”; “Galton fringe additive sublinear Janson 2016”; “heavy tailed random conductance trees low lying eigenvalues point process finite critical trees”; “random conductances finite tree smallest eigenvalues Frechet”; and the named primary papers below. Nonprimary search hits were discovery aids only.

1. Svante Janson, [arXiv:1312.1211v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.1211v1), introduction through Theorem 1.5; extraction lines 730–820 (Lemma 5.1 and start of 5.2), 930–965 (Remark 5.3). The exact cyclic expectation formula and the integrable-toll truncation principle are already there. Remark 5.3 permits unbounded tolls with conditional mean o(sqrt(size)); our h_s=o(1/s) criterion is precisely of this type by the size local limit. No novelty is claimed for it. The weighted-child estimate and shape-uniform resistance payment verify the criterion for this problem. We prove the required operations explicitly. We did not read the full CLT proof.

2. Franziska Flegel, [arXiv:1608.02415v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.02415v3), model and results extraction lines 145–468, proof sections 5.4/5.5 lines 2718–2838; abstract/contents also read. Theorem 1.8 and Corollaries 1.10/1.11 already establish spectral localization and a Weibull minimum law for lattice boxes with Dirichlet killing. Extreme-value spectral localization is credited. The local statistic is a sum of incident conductances at a site, with finite-range dependencies. F uses the inverse mean-zero operator on a finite random map and bridge branch masses. Its weighted fringe tails and nonbridge trace payment are not supplied by the cited lattice statements. The percolation and path-analysis proof sections were not read in full.

3. Flegel, [arXiv:1801.05684v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.05684v1), extraction lines 50–235: model, Assumption 2.1, main theorem, Corollary 2.3. The first k lattice modes and their extreme-value law are already obtained, with an auxiliary spectral problem/Bauer–Fike method described. We claim no new general principle that extremes lead to localized eigenvalues and no all-mode point process. Full proof not read.

4. Yuyang Feng, [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-025-01424-2), private PDF obtained via https://d-nb.info/1385594543/34; extraction lines 150–270, especially Theorem 1.3 and Remark 1.7. Infinite q>4 geometry converges locally to infinite CRT; Remark 1.7 discusses resistance additivity and ordinary walk convergence. This reinforces rejection of the ordinary-conductance lane. It does not assert the finite all-edge Pareto-disorder gap law F. Proof body not read.

5. Alessandra Faggionato, [EJP 17 (2012), paper 15](https://doi.org/10.1214/EJP.v17-1831), exact model/results extraction lines 336–359 and 548–588, with introduction source description. Theorem 2.1 proves interval spectral convergence for generalized differential operators; Theorem 2.5 treats heavy-tailed barrier models on a line via inverse stable-subordinator spectra, with eigenvalue scale n^{1+1/alpha}. It is an important antecedent for heavy barrier spectra. It does not supply branch-mass spectral separation for conditioned critical decorated trees. Full proofs not read.

The actual local candidate operations are (i) energy-orthogonal inverse decomposition over articulation blocks, (ii) two independent edge-disjoint path tails converted to a block-shape-uniform alpha moment, (iii) weighted child-fringe conditioning at only finite second offspring moment, and (iv) asymptotic orthogonality of finitely many extreme bounded-mass bridge cuts plus small-trace truncation. All are proved in candidate.md. Classical finite-dimensional electrical identities and the general fringe principle are credited, not offered as new findings. Whether the entire bridge is substantial requires a separate contribution review. No exact duplicate was identified in this bounded screen; that is not a priority claim.
