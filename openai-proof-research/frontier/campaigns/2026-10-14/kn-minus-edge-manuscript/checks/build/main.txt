1    Introduction
An ordinary drawing of a ﬁnite simple graph places its vertices at distinct points of the plane
and its edges as simple arcs joining their endpoints. Edge interiors avoid the vertices, and two
edges meet in ﬁnitely many proper crossings, with no tangencies or triple points. The number
c(D) of crossings of a drawing D counts every crossing point, so two edges that meet several
times contribute several crossings. The crossing number cr(G) is the minimum of c(D) over all
ordinary drawings of G. No restriction is placed on vertex positions or edge routes. This is the
convention of [7, Section 1], and all statements below use it.

1.1    History and what is new
Zheng, Lin, Yang and Cui [10] gave the upper bound (1.1) for cr(Kn − e), conjectured equality,
and proved it for n ≤ 8. In the same paper they proved cr(K6 □ Pn ) = 15n + 3, proved the lower
bound cr(Km □ Pn ) ≥ (n − 1) cr(Km+2 − e) + 2 cr(Km+1 ), and conjectured (1.3). We were not
able to obtain the text of [10]. These attributions follow Ouyang, Wang and Huang [8, Section 1,
display (1.1)–(1.2), and Lemma 2.3] and the survey of Clancy, Haythorpe and Newcombe [4,
Theorem 2.19, Conjecture 2.20, Theorems 3.12–3.13, Conjecture 3.14].
    Ouyang, Wang and Huang [8] proved two lower bounds for cr(Km − e) in terms of complete-
graph crossing numbers [8, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2]. They deduced the even case of the conjecture
from the Harary–Hill formula for the preceding odd order [8, Theorem 3.3], and settled m ≤ 12
using the then-known values of cr(Km ) [8, Theorem 3.4]. Chia and Lee [3] also obtained
equivalent results: the upper bound [3, Lemma 1], the counting lower bound [3, Theorem 1],
the even case under the odd Harary–Hill hypothesis [3, Theorem 2], the values for n ≤ 12 [3,
Corollary 1], and the conjecture [3, Conjecture 1]. Ouyang, Wang and Huang also proved the
product identity [8, Corollary 5.1] and deduced (1.3) for m ≤ 10 [8, Corollary 5.2].
    Once the Harary–Hill formula is available for every order [7], the following parts of Theo-
rem A are classical consequences and are not claimed as new:

• the upper bound for every n [10, 3]; Section 5 recounts it in the two-page drawing of [7];

• the lower bound for every even n, by [8, Theorem 3.3] or [3, Theorem 2]; Section 4 reproduces
  the proof of [8, Theorem 3.2];

• the lower bound for odd n ≤ 13, from [8, Theorem 3.1] (equivalently [3, Theorem 1]) and
  integrality; see Section 7.

(Proposition 3.7). There the counting bound of [8, Theorem 3.1] falls short of the target by
exactly (s−1)(s−2)

The OpenAI preprint [7] proves the odd lower bound cr(K2s+1 ) ≥ 2s as follows. A skew bilinear
form built from signed crossings and rotation orders vanishes on pairs of cycles. Weighting

    For Kn −e the same polynomial chains put nonzero weight on the missing pair. Four changes
are needed, and they are the content of Section 3.

 (i) Missing-pair tests (Lemma 3.1). A test polynomial gives a valid identity whenever it satisﬁes
     two polynomial symmetry conditions at the missing pair. The conditions are suﬃcient, not
     necessary (Remark 3.2).

(ii) Split spaces and mixed orthogonality (Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4). The   
                                                                           full space of [7] is not
     admissible. We use two subspaces Sa , Sb , each of codimension 2s , vanishing on the missing
     pair in the ﬁrst or in the second edge slot,and show that their images are orthogonal to
     each other. Their intersection would lose 2s − 1 further dimensions per side.

(iii) Twisted detector (Lemma 3.5). The localization tests must carry extra factors (y − ta )
      and (v − ta ). The resulting pairing is controlled by the rectangular nonsingularity in [7,
      Lemma 3.3], not by its square consequence.

(iv) Localization away from the missing edge (Lemma 3.6). Only the 2s−1 vertices with complete
     stars are used for localization. This still exceeds the degree 2s − 4 of the relevant diagonal
     polynomial, and the degree descent preserves the vanishing condition at the missing pair.

    Sadhu [9] recently determined cr(Km,n − e) for complete bipartite graphs, a family also
treated by Chia and Lee [3]. That argument is stated to be inspired by the same algebraic
framework, in its bipartite form [6]. That preprint appeared before the present work. Its stated
new ingredients are exact node-deletion maps, a balanced star-compression rank inequality with
dimension losses, an extension of the bipartite order detector to unequal rectangles, and a signed
three-edge completion that replaces the missing edge. In the passages we compared (Section 7),
these operations diﬀer from ours. Neither theorem implies the other. We therefore claim the
speciﬁc argument for Kn − e, not the general idea of adapting the framework of [7] to a deleted
edge.
    Asiri, Burdett, Chimani, Haythorpe, Newcombe and Wagner [1] gave a general computa-
tional framework for cr(G □ Pn ) and applied it to graphs of order ﬁve and six. It does not treat
complete graphs of arbitrary order.

Status before this work. The surveys [4] (Section 2.2.1 and Section 3.2) list the minus-edge

Comparison with Sadhu’s bipartite theorem. Sadhu [9, Theorem 1.1] proves an exact
formula, written there as cr(Km,n − e) = Z(m, n) − tm tn , for all positive m, n and every edge e,
and states that the argument does not assume the Zarankiewicz formula. We read the preprint
in the text rendering of its ResearchGate page, because the PDF link was not accessible to us.
The passages compared are the abstract, the contribution paragraph, the balanced compres-
sion inequality (Lemma 3.2) and the beginning of its proof, the rectangular bipartite detector
(Lemma 4.1), the signed three-edge completion of the missing edge in the proof of Theorem 1.1,
the closing rank comparison, and the closing questions. In those passages the missing edge is
replaced by a signed combination of three present edges and the intersection identity is run on
a completed matrix. A multi-index compression inequality absorbs the dimension losses. Our
argument instead forces the test chains to have zero coeﬃcient on the missing pair, pairs two
diﬀerent vanishing subspaces of one four-variable space, quotes the already proved rectangular
nonsingularity for complete graphs, and never localizes at the incomplete stars. We have not
re-proved Sadhu’s lemmas, and our comparison is limited to the passages listed.
