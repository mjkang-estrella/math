# T047N: a thickness family of cancellation fourfolds

## 1. The theorem and notation

For each integer n>=2 define

    P=C[p,s,u,F,J], x=s²+u³+p^n F,
    H_n=x²F−(1+2sx)J−p^n J²−pu,
    A_n=P/(H_n), X_n=Spec A_n.

Then X_n is a smooth integral affine fourfold, X_n×A¹≅A⁵, and X_n≄A⁴. Moreover X_n≄X_m whenever n≠m, under arbitrary complex variety isomorphisms. No marking, grading, fibration or action is required to be preserved.

Fix n during the proof and suppress its subscript. In A set

    y=s+x(x−u³), z=sx+p^n J.

The auxiliary ring and its special fiber are

    S=S_n=C[p,x,y,z,u]/(xy−z(z+1)−p^(n+1)u),
    R=S/pS=C[x,y,z,u]/(xy−z(z+1)).

In R put

    s_R=y−x(x−u³), f=x−s_R²−u³, g=z−s_R x, I=(f,g).

The Derksen invariant DK(B) of a complex affine domain B is the subalgebra generated by the kernels of all nonzero locally nilpotent C-derivations (LNDs). It is intrinsic under arbitrary algebra isomorphisms.

## 2. Integrality and the polynomial cylinder

The identity

    xy−z(z+1)=p^n(H_n+pu)                                      (2.1)

holds already in P. After p is inverted, (x,y,z,u) are coordinates over C[p,p^−1], with inverse

    s=y−x(x−u³), F=(x−s²−u³)/p^n, J=(z−sx)/p^n.

In these coordinates H_n=p^−n(xy−z(z+1))−pu, a coordinate because its coefficient of u is a unit. Consequently A[p^−1]=C[p,p^−1,x,y,z]. The polynomial H_n is irreducible after localization and is not divisible by p: modulo p it is x_0²F−(1+2sx_0)J, where x_0=s²+u³. Unique factorization in P implies H_n is prime before localization. Thus A is a domain of dimension four and p is nonzero.

Here is an explicit generalization of source047 §2; all divisibilities are recorded, rather than invoking residual-coordinate stability as a new result. On P[p^−1], let Δ=−p^n∂/∂u with p,x,y,z fixed. In original coordinates

    Δp=0, Δu=−p^n, Δs=−3p^n x u²,
    ΔF=(6sx+3)u², ΔJ=3x²u².

Thus Δ preserves P and is locally nilpotent there. It satisfies ΔH_n=p^(n+1), Δ²H_n=0. Extend Δ by Δw=0 and put Φ=exp(wΔ). Then Φ(H_n)=H_n+p^(n+1)w and

    A[w]≅T=P[w]/(H_n+p^(n+1)w).                              (2.2)

Use the determinant-one linear change

    L=x_0²F−(1+2sx_0)J,
    M=(1−2sx_0)F+4s²J,
    F=4s²L+(1+2sx_0)M, J=−(1−2sx_0)L+x_0²M.

Write B=C[p,s,u,M], so P=B[L]. With F,J regarded as linear polynomials in L,

    H_n(L)=L−pu+p^n Q(L)+p^(2n)F(L)³,
    Q(L)=2x_0 F(L)²−2sF(L)J(L)−J(L)².

Let C=Q(0), Q_1(L)=(Q(L)−C)/L and L_*=pu−p^n C. Then

    H_n(L_*)=p^(n+1)[(u−p^(n−1)C)Q_1(L_*)+p^(n−1)F(L_*)³].   (2.3)

In B[L,w] set

    h=u−p^(n−1)Q(L)−p^(2n−1)F(L)³−p^n w,
    e_0=−w−p^(n−1)F(L)³−Q_1(L)h.

Direct substitution, using H_n+p^(n+1)w=L−ph, gives

    p^(n+1)e_0−(L−L_*)=(p^n Q_1(L)−1)(H_n+p^(n+1)w).         (2.4)

Its image e in T satisfies L=L_*+p^(n+1)e. By (2.3), the expression

    W(Z)=−H_n(L_*+p^(n+1)Z)/p^(n+1)

belongs to B[Z]. The relation in T implies w=W(e), since T is a domain and p≠0. Hence T=B[e]. After inverting p, T[p^−1]=B[p^−1][L] and e=p^−(n+1)(L−L_*), so e is algebraically independent over B. This proves T≅C^[5] and therefore (2.2) proves the asserted cylinder. Smoothness of X follows: tangent dimension and local dimension both increase by one on taking its product with A¹, and that product is smooth. Equivalently smoothness descends along this smooth faithfully flat projection.

## 3. Exact valuation filtration

The displayed map S→A is injective, because S is a domain and becomes the same Laurent polynomial ring after inverting p. The domain assertion follows exactly as in §2 by localizing its relation, which is linear in u, and observing that p does not divide that relation. R is a smooth domain: xy−z(z+1) is primitive and linear in y, while its partial derivatives cannot vanish simultaneously on its zero locus.

The local ring S_(pS) is a DVR with uniformizer p. Its order ord_p satisfies, for b∈S and i>=0,

    ord_p(b)>=i iff b∈p^i S.                                (3.1)

For i=1 this follows by contracting the prime pS from S_(pS); iterate after division by p. Finite order follows from the DVR, or from the ascending-chain argument on repeated principal quotients. For h∈S[p^−1], (3.1) gives

    ord_p(h)>=0 iff h∈S.                                    (3.2)

Set deg(h)=−ord_p(h) on A. In particular the nonpositive filtration subring is exactly S, not merely a subring containing it.

We claim the exact associated graded ring is

    G=gr A=R[τ,I/τ^n]⊂R[τ,τ^−1], deg τ=−1.                  (3.3)

Here p,s,u,F,J have initial forms τ,s_R,u,f/τ^n,g/τ^n and degrees −1,0,0,n,n. The elements f,g,s_R are nonzero, as can be seen by specializing x=z=0 or x=0,z=−1. Under presentation weights (−1,0,0,n,n), put

    H_top=x²F−(1+2sx)J−p^n J²; H_n=H_top−pu.

The map from P to R[τ,τ^−1] given by these initial forms has image R[τ,I/τ^n]: one recovers x,y,z from the displayed formulas. Its kernel is (H_top). Indeed, after p is inverted this is the prime relation xy−z(z+1); H_top is not divisible by p and is irreducible, so its ideal is p-saturated before localization.

For completeness this also controls cancellations. If a polynomial Q representing a nonzero a∈A has highest presentation weight M>deg(a), its homogeneous top part lies in (H_top), so is H_top C with C homogeneous of weight M−n. Replace Q by Q−H_n C. The same element is represented and its maximum weight decreases: the new term puC has weight M−n−1<M. Repeating terminates at the finite lower bound deg(a). Hence every element has a representative of weight precisely its valuation degree, proving (3.3), not just inclusion.

The ring G=P/(H_top) is smooth. Away from τ=0 it is R[τ,τ^−1]. At τ=0 its F,J partial derivatives are x_0² and −(1+2sx_0), which cannot both vanish because

    (1−2sx_0)(1+2sx_0)+4s²x_0²=1.

This proves all hypotheses needed for the source's normalized line-bundle lift.

## 4. No positive-degree LND invariant

We prove a stronger assertion than nonpolynomiality: for every nonzero LND D of A, ker D⊂S.

Any nonzero D induces a nonzero homogeneous LND D_0 on G. Namely take the largest shift deg(Dh_i)−deg(h_i) on the five generators. The exact-weight representatives from §3 bound that shift on every element. Associated graded passage respects iterates, so local nilpotence descends and the initial form of any nonzero invariant remains invariant. This is the classical filtered-LND operation, implemented in source047 §3. Consequently, an invariant outside S would supply a nonzero positive-degree homogeneous invariant of D_0.

Let

    R~=C[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac−bd−1), x=ab, y=dc, z=db,
    v=a³b−a²u³−d².

Source047 Proposition principalization gives a faithfully flat line-bundle torsor R→R~, with I R~=(v). This identity does not depend on n. It follows either from the two charts D(z+1),D(z) or the source's polynomial certificate. Thus

    G~=G⊗_R R~=R~[τ,V]/(τ^n V−v)⊂R~[τ,τ^−1],
    deg τ=−1, deg V=n, deg R~=0.

The separate fiber weights are wt(a)=wt(d)=1, wt(b)=wt(c)=−1, wt(u)=wt(τ)=0, wt(V)=2. The presentation is exact: the relation is τ-saturated because v is a nonzerodivisor, and localization eliminates V.

The smoothness established in §3 and source047 Proposition linearization lift D_0 to a nonzero LND δ on G~, preserving fiber weights and homogeneous of the same valuation-degree shift j. This uses a line bundle over Spec G, not an unsupported assertion that D_0 preserves R. Normalization makes the additive lift unique; conjugating by the grading action therefore gives homogeneity.

The homogeneous pieces, by reducing τ^n V=v, are

    (G~)_e=τ^(−e)R~                              for e<=0,
    (G~)_e=τ^(nq−e)V^q R~, q=ceil(e/n),          for e>0.    (4.1)

Every positive-degree element is divisible by V. The kernel of an LND on a characteristic-zero domain is factorially closed: if ν(t)=max{k:δ^k(t)≠0}, then ν(ab)=ν(a)+ν(b), by the nonzero top binomial term. Hence the assumed positive invariant forces δV=0.

If δτ=0, localize at τ and write δr=τ^(−j)E(r) for r∈R~. The resulting E is a nonzero LND of R~, of fiber shift zero, and E(v)=0. Local nilpotence follows by iterating with the invariant multiplier τ^(−j).

Suppose δτ≠0 instead. LND order implies δτ∉τG~. By (4.1) its degree j−1 must be nq for an integer q>=0. Thus j=nq+1, and for each r∈R~ there is a unique E(r)∈R~ such that

    δr=τ^(n−1)V^(q+1)E(r).                                  (4.2)

Cancelling this common multiplier in the product rule makes E a derivation, with fiber shift −2(q+1)<0. Let m=ν(τ)>=1. Since ν(V)=0, (4.2) gives whenever E(r)≠0

    ν(E(r))=ν(r)−1−(n−1)m.                                  (4.3)

All iterates remain in R~, so E is locally nilpotent. Differentiating v=τ^n V shows E(v)≠0, and ν(v)=nm gives ν(E(v))=m−1. If E²(v) were nonzero its order would be

    (m−1)−1−(n−1)m=(2−n)m−2<0,

impossible for n>=2. In both cases we have produced a nonzero LND E of R~ with nonpositive fiber shift and E²(v)=0. This contradicts source047 Proposition rigidity, whose ring, cubic v and fiber weights here are EXACTLY unchanged. Its Mason–Stothers and nonsquare argument is a stipulated source dependency, not a new theorem here. This proves ker D⊂S.

## 5. The full Derksen invariant

The preceding section gives DK(A)⊂S. The opposite inclusion is essential; an upper bound alone would not support the later intrinsic argument.

On A[p^−1]=C[p,p^−1,x,y,z], take ∂/∂x, ∂/∂y, ∂/∂z. Each fixes p and is locally nilpotent. Since A is finitely generated, a sufficiently large power p^N times any one of these derivations maps all generators of A into A, and hence preserves A. The multiplier is invariant, so the restriction remains locally nilpotent and nonzero. Their invariant kernels together contain p,x,y,z. This denominator clearing is legitimate before every iterate because preservation of A has already been checked on its generators.

To obtain u, use on S the derivation

    δ_1p=δ_1x=δ_1u=0, δ_1z=x, δ_1y=2z+1.

It preserves xy−z(z+1)−p^(n+1)u and is locally nilpotent on the five generators. In A[p^−1] it has

    δ_1s=2z+1,
    δ_1F=−2s(2z+1)/p^n,
    δ_1J=−2xz/p^n.

Consequently p^n δ_1 preserves A, is a nonzero LND, and fixes u. Therefore every generator of S belongs to DK(A), proving

    DK(A_n)=S_n.                                            (5.1)

In particular S_n is proper in A_n because deg(F)=n>0. A polynomial algebra in four variables has full Derksen invariant (each coordinate is fixed by a different nonzero coordinate partial derivative). Thus A_n is not polynomial.

## 6. Recovering the divisor intrinsically

The inclusion DK(A_n)⊂A_n is canonical, so an abstract isomorphism A_n≅A_m induces an isomorphism of the corresponding morphisms

    π_n:Spec A_n→Spec S_n.

The maximal open subset of Spec S_n over which π_n is an isomorphism is exactly D(p). The inclusion D(p) is already proved. To verify the converse at EVERY point of V(p), reduce the identities p^nF=x−s²−u³, p^nJ=z−sx and s=y−x(x−u³). The image of π_n over V(p) lies in the center V(I)⊂Spec R. At a point outside V(I) the fiber is empty. At a point of V(I), its residue-field fiber is

    κ(q)[F,J]/(x(q)²F−(1+2s_R(q)x(q))J)≅A¹_(κ(q)).

The coefficients cannot both vanish; the same determinant-one identity used in §3 proves this. Thus no point of V(p) belongs to an open set with isomorphic inverse image. This proves the intrinsic characterization, including nonclosed points.

Since pS_n is prime, its reduced ideal is recovered from this closed complement. An isomorphism S_n≅S_m induced by A_n≅A_m therefore maps (p) onto (p). Generators differ by a unit of S_m, which is also a unit of A_m. Thus every contracted ideal (p^r A_n)∩S_n and its image modulo pS_n are intrinsic up to the induced isomorphism. If desired, units of S_n are exactly C*: localization makes them c p^k, and their orders and inverse orders must both be nonnegative.

## 7. The exact contraction staircase

For r>=1 let J_r=(p^r A_n)∩S_n and let Jbar_r=(J_r+pS_n)/pS_n⊂R. Then

    Jbar_r=I^ceil(r/n).                                     (7.1)

For the upper inclusion, take b∈J_r. If its residue in R is zero there is nothing to prove. Otherwise ord_p(b)=0, and b=p^r a with a∈A gives deg(a)=r. Its initial form is bbar τ^(−r)∈G. In (3.3) the degree-r coefficient ideal is exactly I^ceil(r/n): a monomial τ^i(I/τ^n)^k can contribute only for nk−i=r, hence k>=ceil(r/n), and the sum of the ideals I^k over such k is the first of them. Conversely that first power is obtained by i=n ceil(r/n)−r>=0. This is an exact homogeneous-piece computation, based on the cancellation-controlled graded equality of §3.

For the lower inclusion put k=ceil(r/n). The elements p^nF and p^nJ belong to S_n and reduce to f,g. Every degree-k product in these two elements belongs to p^(nk)A_n⊂p^r A_n and to S_n. Multiplying by arbitrary lifts in S_n of coefficients in R proves I^k⊂Jbar_r. This proves equality, including all cancellation and saturation issues.

The ideal I is nonzero and proper. Its quotient is explicitly C[s,u]: set x=s²+u³, z=sx, y=s+x(x−u³); these substitutions and s=s_R are inverse maps. The element f is nonzero, as shown in §3. Thus I²≠I. Indeed, localize at a maximal ideal containing I; I remains a nonzero finite ideal in a local domain. If I²=I, then I=mI and Nakayama's lemma would force I=0, a contradiction.

It now follows from (7.1) that

    n=max{r>=1:Jbar_r=Jbar_1}.                              (7.2)

For r<=n the power is one; for r>n it is at least two, hence contained strictly inside I. Formula (7.2) recovers n from the unmarked algebra A_n using §§5–6. Therefore A_n≅A_m implies n=m, proving the full theorem.

## 8. Scope and credit boundary

This proof constructs the stated full all-n family and proves arbitrary abstract nonisomorphism. It does not classify all complex cancellation fourfolds, all embeddings of A² in Q₂×A¹, or all possible deformations of u³. The earlier moving-polynomial idea was never frozen and is not silently solved by this theorem.

Source047 pays the original n=2 construction, cancellation filtration, line-bundle principalization and lifting, and the decisive cubic rigidity obstruction. The polynomial cylinder is a direct adaptation of its explicit calculation and of classical residual-coordinate stabilization. Ghosh–Gupta (2023), Theorem4.1 and Corollary4.4, and Ghosh–Pal (2024 preprint), Theorem3.2, already recover exponents in principal linear modifications by DK/ML invariants and contraction ideals, and obtain infinite nonisomorphic positive-characteristic cancellation families. They must be credited for that strategy. Their displayed classes require polynomial coefficient rings and one principal linear modification; (5.1) here has the specific nonpolynomial S_n and the graded center I has two generators. No direct application of their theorem is asserted. The proof above supplies its own exact reduced staircase rather than importing a two-generator contraction formula for arbitrary r.

For the coefficient-ring distinction, S_n is indeed not a polynomial algebra: compactly supported complex Euler characteristic is additive over the strata p!=0 and p=0. They are G_m×A³ and Q₂×A¹, respectively. The former has Euler characteristic zero; Q₂ has Euler characteristic two by its strata x!=0 (G_m×A¹) and x=0 (two affine lines). Thus χ_c(S_n)=2, whereas χ_c(A⁴)=1. This comparison is ancillary and is not used in the theorem proof.

Whether the passage to this source-specific center and the full complex family is substantial rather than a technical adaptation remains an independent contribution gate. No count or novelty verdict is asserted by this author proof.
