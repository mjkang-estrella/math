# Family 137: fixed multitape two-fifths simulation remains unresolved

Author: Myeongjin Kang, no affiliation. Original prose CC BY 4.0. This is a discovery screen, not a nomination or a space lower bound. All pinned source results are stipulated true.

## Full scope retained

For each fixed deterministic finite-control machine with any fixed finite number m of ordinary one-head writable tapes, fixed finite alphabets, and a fixed finite number of read-only heads, seek a deterministic simulator using O(T^(2/5) log^C(T+2)) work bits. All simulated heads start at fixed origins and move at most one cell per step. Initial writable contents and read-only symbol functions are independent of the supplied cap. They satisfy the source's access condition: a fixed accessor, given the public input, tape/head tag, and signed displacement of magnitude at most T, returns the symbol in polylog(T+2) work bits. Initial configurations may have more than T nonblank cells; no whole-configuration storage allowance is assumed. The simulator's public input is read-only and endmarked, with its physical input head confined between endmarkers. The binary cap T>=2 is supplied on that public input. Reading/access conventions are exactly the source's; no random-access word-RAM or unit-cost unbounded integer operation is introduced.

The answer is the finite-control state and halting status at or before T, with a timeout outcome carrying the state at T if no halt has occurred. A halt exactly at T takes priority over timeout. Terminal behavior is padded absorbingly. Final writable tapes are not output; the finite output and any simulated head coordinates, binary counters, controller buffers, accessor storage, interpolation state and suspended frames all count toward work space. Machine, m, alphabets and accessor are fixed, so constants may depend on them. This does not assert uniformity in a variable encoded machine description, a nondeterministic simulation, or a simulation-time bound. These quantifiers were specified before pursuing a proof and have not been narrowed to synchronized heads or a special transition relation.

## What the actual source pays

The pinned manuscript is *Simulating One-Tape Time in Two-Fifths-Power Space*, September 25, 2026. All five substantive sections were read in full. `source-receipts.json` pins their bytes and the accompanying main/references/figure/README bodies.

The proof's relevant contracts are `lem:local-replay`, `lem:factored-history`, `lem:weighted-evaluation`, and `lem:epoch-weight`. An offset gives O(T/b) visits. For a fixed short controller transcript, replay on a single block is independent of every other block. Successful transcript validation therefore factors into single-block predicates. In an epoch of b visits, group about sqrt(b) visits per round. The history rule sums over a candidate suffix Y, with length O(sqrt(b) log T), times a product of local block predicates. Homogeneous interpolation is ordered so that a descent into the previous history does not retain Y, whereas a descent into a block does. Each dependency path makes only one such costly block descent per epoch. This produces soft-O(b + T/b^(3/2)), balanced at b=T^(2/5).

The full `multitape.tex`, `prop:multitape`, already handles arbitrary fixed m by a different construction. Joint visits update at most m blocks. It enumerates and retains a complete O((T/b) log T)-bit controller list. A node Q_(t,h) depends on the last earlier update of each required block. These indices always have s<t, even for malformed lists under total parsing. Ordinary fixed-fan-in evaluation gives soft-O(b+T/b), hence soft-O(sqrt T). The source explicitly identifies the two-bit equality nonfactorization and explicitly limits that observation to this method. Neither product-state bookkeeping nor a constant number of additional input slots supplies the missing epoch argument. The source's one-tape quadratic-time conversion gives soft-O(T^(4/5)), not the target exponent.

## Exact stronger factorization obstruction, with its limits

A fixed two-tape machine can scan two independently supplied b-bit words simultaneously, retain a finite equality flag, and halt after the common endmarker. It does not halt early on a mismatch. Every pair has the same elapsed time and final head coordinates; only the final flag distinguishes equality. The words and markers fit in one represented block of width b+O(1) on each tape for the selected offset. Thus one joint visit can have acceptance relation

E_b = {(x,x): x in {0,1}^b}.

If a nonempty rectangle A times B is contained in E_b, choose (x,x) in it. Any other (y,y) in it would also put (x,y) in it, so y=x. Every accepting rectangle covers at most one diagonal pair. Consequently a union of accepting rectangles needs at least 2^b rectangles.

There is also an algebraic version, valid over every field, including characteristic two: the matrix of E_b is the 2^b-dimensional identity. If it is represented as sum_(y in Y) f_y(x) g_y(z), each summand has rank at most one. Thus |Y|>=2^b. A full g-bit witness index therefore needs g>=b. Cancellation does not evade this rank argument.

This is a lower bound on this factorization's number of witness values. It implies b retained bits only when the proposed evaluator stores a full witness identity across the recursive call. It is **not** a lower bound on the work space of arbitrary evaluation, streaming, recomputation or multitape simulation. In particular, ordinary streaming equality uses constant data storage beyond head/counter bookkeeping. The parent raised this distinction and it is part of this final statement. The finite b=2 rectangle enumeration in `checks.py` is a diagnostic; the preceding argument proves the general factorization fact.

## The investigated alternative and the unpaid interface

For a fixed proposed controller suffix, within-epoch block updates form a fixed-fan-in DAG ordered by visit number. Total parsing plus last-earlier-update navigation makes this DAG acyclic even on malformed proposed data. Ordinary Cook–Mertz evaluation applies to that fixed object. This does not by itself produce the required history rule: node semantics and navigation refer to the same suffix whose storage one wants to release. A restored numerical register is not a reconstructed controller suffix.

A genuine bridge would provide a uniform operation which (i) evaluates a coupled epoch on independent start blocks, (ii) translates a target register by the required fixed semantic value while restoring the other shared registers for arbitrary initial register values, (iii) supports total navigation with strictly decreasing visit indices on all candidate encodings, and (iv) proves a weighted-path bound that does not charge the same long suffix repeatedly on same-epoch descents. No such operation or bound was obtained. Treating an entire epoch as a single ordinary high-arity function instead materializes up to the number of touched blocks times b bits; grouping the m current blocks into a product state does not remove different revisit histories. Extending a local predicate to constant fan-in is routine and does not solve this interface.

## Primary-operation comparison and remaining scope

Ryan Williams, *Simulating Time With Square-Root Space*, February 25, 2025, [author PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/time-vs-space.pdf), reduces multitape simulation to an implicit bounded-fan-in tree. In §3.2, head movements encode the candidate computation graph in O(T/b) bits, its edge relation is obtained by scanning that stored encoding, local functions use O(b) space, and every edge decreases the time block. The final bound is O(b+(T/b)log b). This is an actual operation-level antecedent to the source's multitape comparison, not an absent-statement search.

James Cook and Ian Mertz, *Tree Evaluation Is in Space O(log n · log log n)*, STOC 2024, DOI [10.1145/3618260.3649664](https://doi.org/10.1145/3618260.3649664), §6.2 Theorem 7 and its proof explicitly handle arity d: O(h log(d log k)+d log k). Their register construction shares the large value storage and accounts separately for the program position. Oded Goldreich, *On the Cook-Mertz Tree Evaluation procedure*, ECCC TR24-109 revision 2, December 27, 2024, [revision PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/109/revision/2/download), gives the shared-global-storage and interpolation exposition. These read operations pay the fixed-function evaluation step; none of the passages examined gives the proposed release/reconstruction of a globally defining unknown epoch suffix. This is bounded comparison, not a literature-wide absence claim.

Status: **UNRESOLVED**, no candidate, no claimed new simulation exponent, no contribution credit. A feasible Lean support fragment would formalize the diagonal-rectangle lemma and rank bound or the already-known exponent balance. Neither would formalize the missing evaluator, so no formal artifact is presented as a full proof.
