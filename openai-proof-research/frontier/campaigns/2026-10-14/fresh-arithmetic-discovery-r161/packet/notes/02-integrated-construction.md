# Defect two: full families, quotient saturation, and the residual operator

These are prefreeze feasibility derivations, not a proof of the exploratory target. Write n=r(i-1)+2, i>=3, M=i+2. In cohomology H_k means complex codimension k. The canonical S operators lower that grading.

## 1. Construct the finite proper connecting population

A stable map of degree d<=M has d<2i. It therefore has exactly one nonconstant component, of covering multiplicity one: every rational image contributes at least i. After forgetting all markings, a contracted tree would have a leaf violating stability. Thus the unmarked stable map has smooth domain P1 and is birational to its irreducible image. This is the actual source equality argument at its correct strict degree threshold, already used in r18.

For every d<=M retain ALL full irreducible RatCurves components of that degree. Bounded anticanonical degree bounds projective degree and numerical coordinates; the proper finite-type unmarked stable-map spaces have finitely many irreducible components. Their images and finite normalizations give a finite population V, and every member of V is proper. No chosen divisor subfamily or preferred component replaces this population. Its one-step relations are closed proper images of the twofold universal-curve square. Composing finitely many such relations gives closed ordered endpoint loci, including special endpoints.

The source supplies r independent degrees gamma_j, with nonzero P=S_gamma1...S_gammar, d_j>=i and s=sum(d_j-1)<=n. Hence

    sum(d_j-i)<=2,       d_j<=i+2=M<2i.

All their rational images occur in V. Their numerical classes span N1(X). Compose the actual algebraic virtual correspondences by the refined diagonal operations. The resulting class T has dimension n+s in X x X, is supported on the closed ordered endpoint locus Z, and acts as P (with the compatible input/output convention). Nonzero action implies a nonzero class with a representative supported on Z, hence dim Z>=n+s. Positivity of the virtual class is unnecessary and is not inferred.

## 2. Saturate BEFORE estimating the base

Let U->B be the proper saturated quotient for the equivalence relation generated by the ENTIRE V. Shrink the base to obtain pure fibers; its preimage remains saturated. Put E=X\U and b=dim B. If an endpoint of a V-chain is in U, every point of that chain is in the same entire compact quotient fiber. Therefore the exact inclusion is

    Z subset (U x_B U) union (E x E),
    dim(U x_B U)=2n-b,       dim(E x E)<=2n-2.            (2.1)

If b=0, properness makes the dense open U closed in X, so U=X and every pair is equivalent. The proper-prerelation theorem bounds the number of curves by 2^n-1. That would settle the target.

Suppose b>0. ACO Lemma 6.5 and Corollary 6.6 supply a minimal horizontal dominating full family W with d(W)<=b+1. If d(W)<=M, then W is itself a member of V. Every one of its curves meeting U is entirely contained in a quotient fiber by saturation, contradicting horizontality. Consequently

    d(W)>=M+1=i+3,            b>=M=i+2>=5.             (2.2)

This uses the full low-degree population, not only the r source degrees. It closes the possible curve and surface bases and much more. Numerical spanning alone would not do that. In particular, the curves of V wholly contained in E are not automatically numerical classes of curves in actual compact fibers; that mistaken identification would contradict the actual hypothesis of the primary horizontal-family lemma.

For r=1, n=i+1<b, already impossible. For r=2 the target is also paid by the actual Novelli--Occhetta high-pseudoindex construction: i>=(2i+3)/3 for i>=3. If one of its successive minimal families were non-unsplit, its proof forces k=1 and then rho=1, contradicting r=2. Thus k=r=2 and both families are unsplit; their degree budget gives each degree <=i+2. Their Chow families connect X. This is credited prior payment, not a separate nominee or restriction of our target.

## 3. Only an all-minimal word can survive failure

If s>=n-1, its support has dimension >=2n-1. Equation (2.1), together with b>=5, bounds that support by 2n-2, a contradiction. Equivalently the paid r18 connectivity lemma already settles this branch.

Thus under failure necessarily s=n-2. Since sum(d_j-1)>=r(i-1)=n-2, this forces

    d_1=...=d_r=i.                                           (3.1)

Moreover every (2n-2)-dimensional irreducible cycle in a supported representative of T must lie in E x E: the part meeting U x U has dimension <=2n-b<=2n-5. Write D_1,...,D_t for the prime divisorial components of E. Every irreducible subvariety of E x E of dimension 2n-2 is precisely D_a x D_b for some a,b. Therefore, with rational coefficients which need NOT be positive,

    T = sum_(a,b) c_ab [D_a x D_b] in CH_(2n-2)(X x X)_Q.     (3.2)

The equality is obtained by choosing a representative on the actual closed ordered-chain support; a term with nonzero coefficient has its entire divisor product contained in that support. Artificial divisors created by shrinking the quotient base do not create such a product of independently varying base points. At least one product remains after collecting cycle coefficients, and the induced cohomology tensor is nonzero since P is nonzero. Relations among divisor classes can cause cancellations; no coefficient is individually claimed nonzero in a preferred cohomology basis.

For input alpha of codimension k, the action is

    P(alpha)=sum c_ab (integral_X alpha*[D_a]) [D_b]
                    when k=n-1,
    P(alpha)=0       when k!=n-1.                            (3.3)

In particular P(pt)=0 and P vanishes on H_(n-2). The transpose also vanishes on the point. This is compatible with, and substantially more specific than, the source's merely nonzero word. It is not contradicted by the source spectral selector A_0: the nonzero trace may close this middle block by an operator of opposite grading.

Choose a divisor basis L_1,...,L_r, and its Poincare-dual basis alpha_1,...,alpha_r of H_(n-1). These bases exist in the Fano setting: H^2 is algebraic, and hard Lefschetz identifies H^(2n-2) with H^2 times an ample class to the (n-2)nd power. Write D_a=sum_l v_(la)L_l. The residual matrix is explicitly

    B_(l j)=sum_(a,b) c_ab v_(j a) v_(l b),
    P(alpha_j)=sum_l B_(l j)L_l.                              (3.4)

B is nonzero. Its image is in the span of the boundary divisors, its kernel contains their annihilator, and its rank is at most the dimension of that span. It need not be symmetric: an ordered word is not generally self-adjoint even though each S_gamma is. Reversing the word gives the adjoint. Nothing here supplies positivity or identifies the gamma_j with extremal rays.

## 4. Extra source compatibility, without a false commutativity shortcut

There is a further exact constraint at each degree gamma_j=i. Let L_D mean cup product by D. In the coefficient of q^gamma in [A_D,A_F]=0 there cannot be a sum of two positive degrees: that would have degree >=2i. Hence

    (F.gamma)[L_D,S_gamma] = (D.gamma)[L_F,S_gamma].           (4.1)

If D.gamma=0, choose F.gamma!=0 to get [L_D,S_gamma]=0. With divisors dual to the independent gamma_j this gives

    [L_j,S_gamma_k]=0 for j!=k.                              (4.2)

This is a real restriction retained for a future attack on (3.4). It does not assert that S_gamma_j commute with one another: their pairwise commutators occur at total degree 2i together with other quantum coefficients. Nor does (4.2) state that P commutes with every divisor. Moving a cup operator across its own S_gamma_j leaves exactly the unpaid commutator. It is therefore invalid to derive P(pt)!=0 from the nonzero middle block by hard Lefschetz alone.

## 5. What connectivity would actually buy

If the target closes, the original r18 bounded-competitor argument applies to every step of Fujita Construction 3.5: a finite connecting collection of degree <=M provides an initial dominating family and a horizontal dominating competitor over every positive-dimensional intermediate quotient. Thus each chosen minimal family has degree <=M<2i and is unsplit. Numerical generation gives exactly k=r such independent families.

Writing e_j=d(V^j)-i>=0 and c_j=codim Locus(V^j)>=0, Fujita's actual dimension-drop estimate gives

    sum_(j=1)^r (e_j+c_j)<=2.                                (5.1)

Consequently at least r-2 are covering. The remaining possibilities distribute two units among excess degrees and locus codimensions. One cannot upgrade this to r-1 covering families, or apply Theorem 6.1 at defect two: BOTH its count and its numerical hypothesis are different. Even with r-1 covering families, its first complementary-span calculation here gives dim F<=i+1, not <=i-1. This permits fiber-type, divisorial, and codimension-two small-contraction possibilities under the fiber-locus inequality. No such possibility is classified here.

The desired low-degree chain construction is already the complete exploratory target. Its failure has been reduced to the actual nonzero divisor tensor (3.2)--(3.4) with all-minimal independent degrees, the constraints (4.1)--(4.2), and a proper low-degree quotient of base dimension >=i+2. Closing that residual interface would settle the target without assuming extremality, a product decomposition, or smoothness of the boundary divisors. It has not been closed.
