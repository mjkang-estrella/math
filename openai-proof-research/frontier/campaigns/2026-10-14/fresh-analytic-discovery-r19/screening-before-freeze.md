## Proposed unpaid operation

The target is the zero-field radial deficit SV-J, not a chosen magnetic
component. An absolute finite-volume partition comparison is needed:
pressure-density asymptotics permit errors diverging on this scale.
CGS's printed proof loses factors from both the SU(2) multiplicity and
the free zero mode, harmless for pressure but fatal for fluctuations.

The proposed route retains a positive auxiliary field lambda/L^2 down
to lambda=beta^(-1/20). A finite-torus two-particle Nash estimate and the
actual low-energy cutoff control the interacting partition function with
absolute logarithmic error o(1), uniformly throughout that shrinking
field interval. The proposed second step uses the exact SU(2) character,
the Casimir deficit cutoff, and a vanishing auxiliary field to cancel
the zero-mode factor and normalize the zero-field highest-weight trace.
It then restores the physical multiplicity weights. This must be proved;
it is not licensed by convergence of the bulk pressure.

The concrete window beta^(501/1000) <= L <= beta^(51/100) is chosen to
close both operations, not asserted optimal. Its worst interaction bound
is beta^(-0.18) times a fixed logarithmic power; the tiny-field character
error is beta^(-0.02) times a logarithmic power. The ideal-gas limit is
then paid by the classical geometric/exponential calculation.

This is a proposed substantial mechanism, not a contribution verdict.
The independent reviewer may find a prior sufficient theorem or judge
the finite-volume reconstruction an adaptation of CGS. The proof may
not logically need the new thermodynamic theorems in pinned271; the
family supplies the question and precise comparison, while the old CGS
operations carry much of the analytic input. This dependence is stated
openly rather than describing all ingredients as enabled by the pin.

The earlier fixed-temperature Goldstone heuristic and beta~L^2 finite
occupation regime were prefreeze screens only. Neither was frozen, and
neither is being claimed as proved by the proposed infrared-window law.
