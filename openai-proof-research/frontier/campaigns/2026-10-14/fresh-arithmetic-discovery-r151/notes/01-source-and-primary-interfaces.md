## What the sources actually supply

The rationally connected manuscript's `prop:box` holds for any smooth projective X with a specified tangent splitting, even before integrability. For every g:P1→X the formal Cauchy equation along the diagonal of P1×P1 uses the given projectors. Formal section counting using O(−2) as the diagonal conormal bundle reconstructs rational coordinate ratios. Complementary tangency annihilates the derivative on every exceptional curve of the surface resolution. Thus the result is a global holomorphic tangent box, including critical points.

Its `prop:transport` pulls back the projected-bracket partial connection, with no inverse differential. Curvature vanishes along a fiber of P1×P1→P1 because that fiber is a curve. Simply connectedness of P1 then provides path independence; finite chains of ODE charts give holomorphic parameter dependence. Neither assertion says that the ambient partial connection is flat in two independent directions. On a factor curve one input summand has its original pullback, while the other is trivial. The two-point variation construction supplies sections vanishing at one mark and spanning the summand at the other. Applying the bracket tensor produces a constant section of a trivial bundle, hence zero. This proves both specified summands integrable when X is rationally connected. The compatible projective product and the general-fiber rational-quotient consequences are already in the source.

For a compact Kähler X with both specified summands integrable, the universal-cover manuscript gives the ordinary universal-cover product with equality of the lifted distributions and factor tangents. The local product charts depend on Frobenius at the first step. The Hartogs proof removes exceptional graph fibers using separated coordinates. The mixed-boundary proof uses bounded projections, the closed Kähler form and Stokes, then small-area annuli to build a shell wider than the distance to the boundary. It is not an ambient curvature-flattening estimate. Full polydisk axes retain their original parameter domains. Finite grids then continue both product germs and their inverses along arbitrary continuous paths; matched covering lifts and the identity principle give the global inverse maps and summand matching.

The alternative canonical route does not remove that prerequisite. `canon:parallel` derives the integrated second-fundamental-form bound from the special Ricci equation and Chern-class cancellation for nef and big K_X. Its limit supplies parallelism, hence integrability, before the crossing argument. The rational-tree and neighborhood-crossing appendices assume integrable summands. The projective collapsed fiber is both rationally chain connected and ordinarily simply connected; these are separate inputs. Stable-map routes continue ambient germs; monodromy identifies them; the real-analytic product metric gives separate nonexpansion. These mechanisms do not construct a new integrable splitting from an arbitrary nonintegrable one.

A deck transformation preserves a splitting lifted from X. Once the compatible product exists, vanishing wrong-factor derivatives gives a diagonal action. This is classical bookkeeping, and neither discrete factor images nor finite-cover product descent follows. It is not a proposed contribution.

## The recovered working question

The operation tested is whether the embeddings of a specified splitting T_X=E⊕F on a compact connected Kähler manifold can be replaced by integrable embeddings of the SAME abstract bundles. A precise sufficient formulation is the existence of a holomorphic vector-bundle automorphism A of T_X over id_X with A(E), A(F) both integrable. This is a working question, not a frozen theorem or a proved universal assertion. It is stronger than merely finding an abstract product on the universal cover. The saved r1 files contain no exact frozen target to amend.

If A exists, the pinned universal-cover theorem applies to A(E),A(F). Composing dΦ with the lifted A gives abstract bundle identifications of π*E,π*F with factor tangents. The original nonintegrable subbundles cannot themselves be images of factor tangents under a biholomorphism. A bundle automorphism is not generally the differential of a map. Notes02–03 carry the curvature and global-existence reductions without conflating these conclusions.
