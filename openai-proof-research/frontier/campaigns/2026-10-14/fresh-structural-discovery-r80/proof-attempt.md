# T177 growth target: unified proof attempt, not a completed theorem

Scope is exactly scope-freeze.md, SHA256 0ef8e3f0c6575bc093f8ef71178a6942f0de91d3644cff4725ae0071ea8c544b. In particular the upper estimate in every degree 1≤j<d is mandatory. The facts below do not by themselves establish the target and are not nominees.

## 1. Coefficient comparison and finite quotient

The pinned source uses F2 in its statement of acyclicity of L, but its geometric proof factors through integral cone functions on the spherical opposition complexes. Oppenheim–Valentiner-Branth2504.05823v2 Definition2.3, Proposition2.7 and Corollary6.10 explicitly give the integral cone and reduction to every finitely generated abelian coefficient group. Use that integral chain identity with Fq coefficients. Each source attachment (F*O,∂F*O) has relative chains equal to the augmented chains of O shifted by |D|, including orientation signs if an order is chosen on the type set. These relative homology groups vanish below n=2d. The exact sequence and filtered union with the contractible building therefore give H̃_a(L;Fq)=0 for a<n−1. This repeats a paid source lower operation with its actual coefficient interface; it is not a new geometric argument.

The source checks Γ_N(m)∩gH_i g^{-1}=1 and identifies Γ_N(m)\L with T_m, retaining intersections. Its action preserves types, so there are no inversions and its simplicial chain modules are free Fq[Γ_N(m)]-modules. The augmented chains, exact through degree n−2, can be extended to a free resolution without changing the modules and differentials needed in degrees ≤n−2. Consequently H_j(T_m;Fq)=H_j(Γ_N(m);Fq) for 0≤j≤n−2. The d-skeleton has the same chain modules and differentials needed in degrees j<d, so the same equality holds for X_m in the full target range. This step uses free congruence stabilizers, not averaging over the nontrivial q-group stabilizers of U. The latter averaging would be invalid over Fq.

For size comparison, reduction SL_N(Fq[t])→SL_N(Fq[t]/t^m) is onto: the target local ring's SL_N is generated by elementary matrices and each elementary entry lifts to a polynomial. Over Fq[t]/t^{r+1}, a lift of a determinant-one matrix modulo t^r can have its last error in the determinant removed by a diagonal correction 1+t^r a (r≥1). The determinant-one kernel of reduction between these two rings is exactly I+t^r sl_N(Fq), of order q^{N²−1}. At m=1, the group U has image the unitriangular group of order M=q^{N(N−1)/2}. It follows that |G_m|=M q^{(m−1)(N²−1)} and the source vertex count gives

    |X_m(0)| = N q^{(m−1)(N²−1)}.

Thus m is comparable with log |X_m(0)|, uniformly for m≥2 and fixed d,q.

## 2. Exact finite elementary-abelian retraction

Write R=Fq[t], I=t^mR, and V_m={f∈I:deg f<2m}. This is an m-dimensional Fq-vector space, with the zero polynomial allowed. Let

    E_m={I_N+Σ_{b=2}^N f_b E_{1b}: f_b∈V_m}.

For b,c>1 one has E_{1b}E_{1c}=0. Therefore multiplication in E_m is exactly coefficientwise addition, inverses negate coefficients, determinants equal1, and E_m≤Γ_N(m) is a finite elementary abelian group of rank r=m(N−1), since q is prime. In particular no high-degree term is created in this subgroup; a degree cutoff by itself would not make an arbitrary family of polynomial matrices into a subgroup.

For g∈Γ_N(m), put

    ρ_m(g)=I_N+Σ_{b=2}^N [g_{1b}]_[m,2m) E_{1b},

where brackets retain precisely degrees m,...,2m−1. Write g=I+A and h=I+B with every entry of A,B in I. Then gh=I+A+B+AB and every entry of AB lies in I²=t^{2m}R. Hence the retained entries add, proving ρ_m(gh)=ρ_m(g)ρ_m(h). The output always belongs to E_m. On E_m the map is the identity, so this is an actual group retraction, not just a linear splitting of a quotient.

Functoriality of group homology now gives an injection H_j(E_m;Fq)→H_j(Γ_N(m);Fq), split by ρ_m. For a cyclic group of odd prime order the periodic free resolution has one basis element in each degree, and both alternating differentials become zero after tensoring with the trivial Fq module. Tensoring r such resolutions and applying Künneth gives

    dim H_j(E_m;Fq)=binom(r+j−1,j).

Equivalently its Poincaré series is ((1+z)/(1−z²))^r=(1−z)^{-r}. Therefore, throughout the target range,

    dim H^j(X_m;Fq) ≥ binom(m(N−1)+j−1,j) ≥ (N−1)^j m^j/j!.

No upper bound is contained in this calculation. Lopez1001.2071v2 §5 already uses inclusion of commuting root subgroups, a first-order congruence quotient and cohomology detection to get lower bounds. The characteristic and finite-rank specialization here make the splitting particularly transparent, but the lower-bound mechanism is classical.

## 3. First failed upper route: source pro-stability and transfer

The source proves pro-isomorphisms for stabilization and then uses transition surjectivity. Its surjectivity is obtained from inclusion after transfer multiplying by the index. Over F2, [Γ_a(m):Γ_a(m')] is odd and hence a unit; over Fq, for m'>m the index is a positive power of q and hence zero. Thus the displayed source composite becomes zero, not identity. A pro-zero system can have nonzero terms and zero transition maps. Taking V_m=Fq with every strictly later transition zero is an elementary counterexample to inferring levelwise zero from pro-zero without surjectivity.

Even a direct integral pro-isomorphism does not bound the dimensions of its nonzero finite-level terms. Neither the source cohomology vanishing nor its stabilization-surjectivity lemma is imported at characteristic q.

## 4. Second failed upper route: congruence-filtration spectral sequence

The extension

    1→Γ_N(2m)→Γ_N(m)→Q_m→1

has Q_m an elementary abelian group (the first-order trace-zero matrix quotient). Its dimension is m(N²−1), by the same lifting argument as above. The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence has

    E²_{a,b}=H_a(Q_m;H_b(Γ_N(2m);Fq)) ⇒ H_{a+b}(Γ_N(m);Fq).

The b=0 row has polynomial size O(m^a). But that row alone does not bound the abutment: already E²_{0,j} is the coinvariant space of the unknown H_j(Γ_N(2m);Fq). There is neither a smaller-level induction nor a proved contraction factor. The action on those higher homology groups cannot be discarded: source trivial-conjugation relies on the unavailable finite-level stabilization surjections in characteristic q. Counting Q_m's dimension and assuming the spectral sequence collapses would therefore assume the missing conclusion.

## 5. Third upper route: compressed resolutions

A possible independent route would construct partial resolutions of Γ_N(m) whose j-th numbers of generators are O(m^j), for every j<d. The source quotient chain resolution has exponentially many cells, of order |G_m|, and therefore only gives an exponential upper bound. Reidemeister–Schreier rewriting from a fixed finite-index ambient presentation has the same problem.

The nonunital ideal t^mFq[t] is generated as an algebra by t^m,...,t^{2m−1}. This does not automatically give a controlled presentation of Γ_N(m). One must retain conjugates of elementary root matrices, prove all relative Steinberg relations, control the kernel of the Steinberg-to-matrix map, and then control higher syzygies rather than just relator counts. For example, multiplying polynomial generators has relations connecting different lengths: (t^m)^3=t^{m+1}t^{2m−1} for m≥2. Treating all relations as homogeneous quadratic relations would already be wrong. No bounded partial resolution or higher-degree dimension bound has been constructed here.

## Checkpoint before further depth calculations

The lower estimate and the three precise upper-bound obstructions are recorded. The matching upper estimate remains unpaid. This is a blocked full research target, not a proof of it and not a nomination of its lower half. No narrowed degree range, subsequence, different tower or weaker growth assertion replaces the frozen target.


## 6. Exact first degree at every depth (continued author derivation)

This does not replace the frozen higher-degree target. Put D=N²−1, O=Fq[[t]], and K_a=ker(SL_N(O)→SL_N(O/t^a)). The actual primary Capdeboscq–Lubotzky–Rémy, arXiv1508.00526v5, Theorem1.3 and Lemma2.1, identify the profinite completion of SL_N(Fq[t]) with the product of its local groups and identify the pro-q completion of its upper-unipotent preimage with the corresponding local pro-q group. Apply the identical finite-index/product argument to Γ_N(a): its profinite completion is K_a times the factors at all other irreducibles. Those other factors have no nontrivial q-group quotient. Hence its pro-q completion is K_a. This is an application of the actual CSP interface; it is not an assertion of cohomological goodness.

Here is a complete computation of the closed Frattini subgroup of K_a. For every s≥1, K_s/K_{s+1}=sl_N(Fq), via the coefficient of t^s. Surjectivity follows from elementary off-diagonal matrices and diagonal determinant-one matrices diag(1+t^s c,(1+t^s c)^{-1},1,...,1), using permutations. For s≥2a, the commutator of elements with leading coefficients X at depth a and Y at depth s−a has leading coefficient [X,Y] at depth s. The Lie algebra sl_N(Fq) is spanned by such brackets: E_ij=[E_ik,E_kj] for distinct i,j,k, and E_ii−E_jj=[E_ij,E_ji]. This also works when q divides N. Both matrix lifts lie in K_a. Therefore the closed subgroup C generated by commutators in K_a surjects onto every layer K_s/K_{s+1}, s≥2a. Successively matching the leading coefficient of an arbitrary element of K_{2a} by elements of C, the residual tends to identity in the t-adic topology. Closedness gives K_{2a}⊂C. The reverse inclusion follows from matrix multiplication modulo t^{2a}, so C=K_{2a}.

Moreover (I+A)^q=I+A^q in characteristic q, since I commutes with A. Thus qth powers in K_a lie in K_{qa}⊂K_{2a}. The closed Frattini subgroup is consequently K_{2a}. The quotient K_a/K_{2a} is additive, of dimension aD. Every homomorphism Γ_N(a)→Fq has finite q-group image and extends uniquely to its pro-q completion. Therefore

    dim_Fq H¹(Γ_N(a);Fq) = aD.

By the free-chain comparison in §1 this gives dim H¹(X_m;Fq)=mD. No comparison of continuous and discrete cohomology in degrees ≥2 was used or proved.

## 7. Degree-two five-term calculation (continued author derivation)

Let Q=Γ_N(m)/Γ_N(2m), of rank r=mD. The degree-one calculation identifies H_1(Γ_N(2m);Fq) with the additive trace-zero coefficient matrices in degrees 2m,...,4m−1. Conjugation by I+t^m X+... changes such coefficients by [X,Y] at degree at least3m. Therefore its Q-coinvariants are precisely the coefficients in degrees 2m,...,3m−1. To check the word “precisely”, all changes lie in degrees≥3m, while for each s in [3m,4m), the leading commutator calculation with depths m and s−m spans sl_N at degree s. Eliminate coefficients from low to high, as in §6; this time it is a finite filtration. Thus the coinvariant space has dimension r.

The homology five-term exact sequence is

    H_2(Γ_N(m);Fq) → H_2(Q;Fq) → H_1(Γ_N(2m);Fq)_Q
      → H_1(Γ_N(m);Fq) → H_1(Q;Fq) → 0.

The last map is an isomorphism by §6, and the inclusion map immediately preceding it is zero (all its coefficients start at degree2m). Exactness makes the transgression onto the r-dimensional coinvariant space. Since dim H_2(Q;Fq)=r(r+1)/2, its kernel has dimension r(r−1)/2. Consequently

    dim H²(X_m;Fq) ≥ binom(mD,2).

This stronger quadratic lower bound still does not contradict the target. It proves that the first-order quotient does account for many more degree-two classes than the one-row retract detects, but the kernel of H_2(Γ_N(m))→H_2(Q) remains unbounded by this argument. A finite-quotient calculation cannot bound that kernel without an additional theorem.

## 8. Why the completion presentation does not close the proof

CLR Theorem0.2 bounds a maximal pro-q Sylow presentation as the field degree and Lie rank vary. Its parameter a in q=p^a is not the congruence depth m. Substituting m for that a is invalid. Reidemeister–Schreier for the open subgroup K_m has exponential index and does not yield the desired polynomial bound. Even a polynomial presentation bound for K_m would only bound continuous H²; an upper bound on the discrete H² of Γ_N(m) requires a comparison argument. Completion identifies all finite quotients and degree-one characters, not all discrete central extensions or higher classes. No such comparison is asserted here.

The new calculations are compatible with the unchanged full target. They are neither a counterexample nor a complete proof. The remaining obligation is a polynomial bound for all higher discrete classes, j=2,...,d−1, including classes invisible in the first-order quotient. No full-target obstruction has been established and no r81 switch is justified by these calculations alone.


## 9. Exact limitation of the finite elementary-abelian-retract attack

Suppose E≅(C_q)^s is any finite elementary-abelian retract of Γ_N(m), not necessarily the one-row subgroup in §2. The induced map H_1(E;Fq)→H_1(Γ_N(m);Fq) is injective because the group retraction splits it. Section6 therefore forces s≤mD. Its entire degree-j homology has dimension binom(s+j−1,j)≤binom(mD+j−1,j)=O(m^j). Thus no elementary-abelian retract, however its polynomial support is chosen, can give a super-polynomial-in-this-degree obstruction to the frozen target. The infinite root subgroup does not evade this bound: its projection to the first-order quotient has only m coefficients per entry, and a retraction onto an elementary-abelian group of larger rank would contradict the exact H¹ calculation.

This is a rigorous closure of that proposed obstruction mechanism, not a disproof or proof of the target. The possibility of genuinely new higher discrete classes remains.
