                   Fourth-moment comparison of zero-ﬁeld SK
                            free-energy ﬂuctuations

                                                 Abstract
           We compare zero-ﬁeld Sherrington–Kirkpatrick log partition functions with independent,
       possibly nonidentically distributed, centered variance-one couplings under a uniform fourth-
       moment bound. The mean error is bounded independently of system size, the variance error is
       bounded by a constant times one plus the Gaussian standard deviation, and bounded smooth
       tests at scale a ≥ 1 have error O(a−1 ). Third moments need not vanish. The cubic term in
       coordinate replacement is controlled by a second replacement over a reﬂected vertex star. An
       expansion of the square and a ﬁnite family of hybrid laws give the variance estimate without
       ﬁfth moments. Assuming the explicitly identiﬁed Gaussian low-temperature theorem, these
       comparisons transfer its exact variance prefactor and its limiting law with each model’s own
       mean and variance. The Gaussian theorem is an input, not a result proved here.

Contribution and antecedents. Carmona–Hu [1] use approximate integration by parts and
interpolation to compare free energies; their Section 4.2 obtains an absolute O(1) comparison with
a vanishing third moment and a ﬁnite fourth moment. Coordinate replacement and bounded-
spin log-partition derivative estimates are classical; see Chatterjee [2]. Cheng–Liu–Shao–Xu [3,
Proposition 3.2] give a particularly close scaled smooth-test comparison under matching of the ﬁrst
three moments.
    There is an earlier conceptual precedent for controlling a mismatched cubic coeﬃcient by an
extra cancellation in its expectation. In edge Green-function comparison, Erdős–Yau–Yin [4, end of

                                                      2
Section 6.1 and Lemmas 6.5–6.6] expand after deleting a matrix row and column; local-law estimates
isolate a centered linear term independent of the minor. That principle is credited here. Our
implementation for SK uses deterministic gauge oddness and comparison with a reﬂected product
law, together with uniform Gibbs cumulant bounds. It does not import a resolvent expansion or
local-law estimate.
    The contribution considered here is the integrated comparison (3)–(5) with unrestricted third
moments. Gauge symmetry and ordinary matched-moment replacement are antecedents. The
square-remainder identity and ﬁnite-maximum bootstrap are a technical part of the argument, not
a separate contribution. The law and prefactor in Corollary 1.3 are inherited from the Gaussian
input. This is a bounded comparison with the primary operations cited above, not a worldwide pri-
ority assertion. No external-ﬁeld, varying-temperature, critical-window or ground-state extension
is claimed.

its variance vanishes. A nonzero external ﬁeld would break (11), and is excluded. At a = 1 the
smooth-test estimate is merely bounded, not a claim of convergence.
    Under only a fourth moment, the pressure need not have all higher moments or exponential
moments. The properties of the already deﬁned limiting measure νβ remain those supplied by
the Gaussian input, but the proof does not transfer convergence of every ﬁnite-size moment. The
assumptions permit asymmetric heavy tails with no ﬁfth moment: for example, center and normalize
a Pareto variable with tail t−9/2 for t ≥ 1. The proof uses none of the absent moments.

6    Provenance and formal support
The ﬁnite-size estimates form one integrated result. The variance-square algebra, restoration and
ﬁnite-hybrid bootstrap received author-side assistance during pre-freeze feasibility; that assistance
is recorded in the accompanying research provenance and is not represented as an independent
proof review. The scope was ﬁxed after feasibility work and retained through the subsequent proof
and contribution reviews. Those reviews are internal project gates, not external peer review. A
separate independent manuscript review is still required.
    The primary comparison is bounded to the operations cited in Section 1. In particular, the
EYY passage was read at the end of Section 6.1 and throughout Lemmas 6.5–6.6, not treated as
an exhaustive survey of Green-function comparison. The complete precise reading record, input
identities and source map accompany this manuscript. Third-party primary bodies are private
review inputs and are not bundled in the manuscript payload.

Scoped formal support. The separate Lean package o217-formal-v1 proves 28 theorems
across 43 public support declarations. It constructs the actual ﬁnite zero-ﬁeld spin model and ver-
tex permutation, proves partition-function and log-partition gauge invariance, proves smoothness
of the one-coordinate log partition, and derives oddness of the deleted cubic derivative, including
observables of the log partition. It also proves the exact square identity and its fourth-order scalar
remainder bound from supplied lower-order remainder inequalities; the scalar quadratic bootstrap
and mean-square correction; and the edge count and both bounds in (15). The arbitrary-observable
sign identity uses Lean’s total derivative convention; it does not assert C 6 regularity or quantitative
derivative bounds for an arbitrary observable.
    The probability spaces and independent product laws, moment cancellation, Gibbs cumulant
and mixed-derivative bounds, Taylor’s theorem supplying the scalar remainder hypotheses, inner
and outer replacements, integrability and deleted-coordinate restoration, and construction of the
ﬁnite hybrid maximum remain human arguments. In particular, the bootstrap inequality is a
premise of the scalar formal theorem; its probabilistic derivation is not formalized. The Gaussian
source, characteristic-function argument, Lévy/Slutsky transfer and eventual positive variance also
remain outside the formal coverage. Thus neither Theorem 1.1 nor Corollary 1.3 is claimed fully
formalized. Exact declaration types and axiom reports accompany the sealed support package. The
separate independent formal audit and its dependency bindings are recorded in the accompanying
provenance.
