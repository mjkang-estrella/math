## Stipulated family 256

**Nonsingular systems of equations over arbitrary groups**, October 5, 2026. Exact pinned path and blob identity are in `source-records.json`.

* Theorem 1, page 1: G is ANY group, 1<=m<=n, w_i in G*F_n, exponent rows independent over Q. Conclusion: the canonical coefficient map to the quotient by their normal closure is injective. No torsion-free, countability or finiteness hypothesis on G; no torsion-free conclusion for the quotient.
* Theorem 2, pages 3--4: r>=0, n>=1, positive k_i, and for each j a finite ordered list of signed occurrences (i,s), repetitions allowed. Let d_j be the sum of occurring k_i and b_ij the signed occurrence counts. If B=(b_ij) has rational row rank r, then for every collection P_j in U(d_j) there is X in product_i U(k_i) with sum_j dim_C ker(diag_(i,s in list j)(X_i^s)P_j-I)>=sum_i k_i. Empty lists and r=0 are allowed as stated. This is an existing simultaneous fixed-space operation; it is not an arbitrary dependent-row theorem.
* Proposition 4.1, pages 10--13: a connected oriented GENUS-ZERO surface formed from a nonempty finite set of signed relator disks, every variable slot paired once by bands with the same variable and opposite signs. The exponent matrix has independent rows. Boundary words have coefficients only. If all but possibly one are trivial in G, all are trivial. Its proof obtains equal signed disk counts from row independence, then uses the fixed-space input and the phase trace. It has no arbitrary variable-bearing outside boundary conclusion.
* Remark 5.1, page 15: arbitrary independent sets of finite-support exponent rows follow by a finite-relation argument. This is already paid.

Actual semantic reading: extracted text lines 1--245 (introduction, Theorems 1--2, beginning of the incidence proof), and 514--846 (entire planar interface/proof and the kernel-relation construction through Remark 5.1); references at the end. The incidence calculation and phase lemmas in intervening lines were not read in full or re-audited. Their truth is stipulated with the theorem. No claim that all retained source proof bodies were read.

**The Kervaire theorem for groups**, September 24, 2026, same pinned family.

Theorem 1.1: every group A and every word in A*<t> of exponent +/-1 have injective coefficient map to the relator quotient. Corollary 1.2: for A!=1 every one-relator quotient A*<t>/<<w>> is nontrivial; the proof uses the cyclic exponent quotient outside +/-1. Neither says that an overgroup is torsion free. Read introduction/context and proof overview, extracted lines 1--135, with the exact two statements and Corollary 1.2 proof. No full proof audit of its spectral or planar sections.

## Primary antecedents

**Klyachko--Mikheenko--Roman'kov**, Equations over solvable groups, arXiv:2303.13240v1, later J. Algebra 638 (2024), 739--750. Main theorem: the class of extensions of a Howie quasivariety by torsion-free abelian groups is again a Howie quasivariety. The introduction explicitly gives the finite-series corollary with EVERY factor torsion-free abelian. Proposition 2: for a quasivariety Q, the groups in Q admitting solutions in Q for all finite nonsingular systems form a quasivariety, equal to its arbitrary-system analogue. Read introduction, Section 1 examples, Section 2 including full Proposition 2 proof, and opening of Section 3 through the split-extension/translated-variable setup (extracted 1--285). Later independence/ring proof not audited. The main theorem is an established input for the paid subclass, not a claimed new proof. No assertion about all torsion-free groups follows by putting Q=TF without first proving the Howie premise.

**Forester--Rourke**, The adjunction problem over torsion-free groups, author PDF `https://math.ou.edu/~forester/papers/multi.pdf`. Theorem 1 requires a LAYERED relative 2-complex with torsion-free initial fundamental group and exponent matrix invertible over Z. It gives coefficient injectivity and the indicated induced-module isomorphism on pi_2. Proposition 1 gives torsion-freeness of the new fundamental group under relative asphericity. The proof propagates along actual one-generator/one-relator layers with exponent +/-1. Read all four pages including proofs and the remark about finite subgroups. No claim that integer matrix reduction constructs a layering.

**Chiodo**, A universal finitely presented torsion-free group, arXiv:1107.1489v3 (the later publication has title On torsion in finitely presented groups and different numbering). Definition 3.9/Proposition 3.10 in THIS v3 are the omega-stage torsion radical and universal quotient. The paragraph after Proposition 3.10 explicitly gives <x,y,z|x^2,y^3,xy=z^6> as an example needing further removal, crediting another primary. Read Section 3 through Proposition 3.10 and its following example, plus Definitions 3.5--3.9 and the surrounding torsion-preserving embedding context (extracted 165--340). No claimed full reading of the algorithm or universality proof; no new credit for the radical or example.

**Nitsche--Thom**, Universal solvability of group equations, arXiv:1811.07737v2, Theorems 1.2--1.3. Coefficient group is Connes-embeddable in the stated theorems; 1.3 assumes a covering of the content presentation complex with vanishing H_2. Remark 2.2 explains extension from finite unrestricted nonsingular solvability, and Lemma 2.3 gives a solution in a permutation wreath product once the covering system is solved. The introduction explicitly anticipates dropping Connes embeddability if the full Howie input is known. Read extracted 1--305, including Lemma 2.1, covering construction, Lemma 2.3 proof, theorem proof, and applications through Example 3.3. The semidirect/wreath solution is not asserted torsion free. The broad covering transfer is screened as already paid rather than selected.

**Ramirez-Cote--Wise**, The Kervaire conjecture via banach fixed points in power series groups, Math. Z. 311 (2025), article 6, DOI 10.1007/s00209-025-03753-3. Definition 1 fixes an EMBEDDING G into 1+K for the specified graded monoid power-series ring. Theorem 9 solves a square system when the exponent matrix is invertible over Z; Theorem 10 solves it when determinant r!=0 after using Z[1/r][[M]]. Read introduction, ring/metric definitions, Definition 1/Theorem 5 setup (extracted 1--145), Remark 8, Theorem 9 and its full displayed contraction proof, and Theorem 10 statement/proof introduction (243--350). The full later alternative proof was not read. No general torsion-free embedding theorem is contained in these interfaces. The integer-degree completion obstruction in our checkpoint is deliberately limited to separated integer-degree Magnus constructions, not every possible positive-real grading.

## Search and retrieval limits

Primary searches covered nonsingular systems with torsion-free solution overgroups, Howie classes/quasivarieties, torsion-free radicals, layered adjunctions and power-series solutions. Search snippets and secondary sites were used only to identify primary papers. No novelty or current-open-status claim is based on a secondary page. The Brodsky--Howie 1997 PDF was identified but the direct download failed with curl error 7 (connection failure); web open also failed. Its abstract/definition and Chiodo's explicit attribution were seen, but its full original small-presentation theorem was NOT read and is not used as a dependency. No absent full theorem is invented from its title. The PMC Forester--Rourke endpoint returned a browser check; the author's actual PDF was obtained and read instead.
