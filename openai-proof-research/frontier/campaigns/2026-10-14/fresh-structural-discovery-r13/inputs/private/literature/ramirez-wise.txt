Abstract
We use the Banach Fixed Point Theorem to solve equations in a group within a Magnus
power series ring.

Keywords Power series · Magnus ring · Group ring

Mathematics Subject Classification 20F06 · 05C38


1 Introduction

The Kervaire conjecture states that for a group G, adding a single generator and a single
relator cannot result in the trivial group. That is Ḡ = G ∗ t/W   = 1. The stronger form
of this conjecture asserts that G → Ḡ is injective when the exponent sum #t (W ) of t in W is
nonzero. Note that Ḡ is obviously nontrivial when #t (W ) = 0 since then Ḡ/im(G) ∼    = Z.
    There are several beautiful partial results towards the Kervaire conjecture, always proving
this strongest form. It is true when G is residually finite [2], when G is locally indicable
[3], and when G is torsion-free [4]. A more recent result gives the conjecture when G is
hyperlinear [5].
    One viewpoint on the Kervaire conjecture, is to view G as lying within a larger group G  ,
and find a solution to W (t) = 1 for some t ∈ G  . We then say that the equation W (t) = 1 is
solved in the overgroup G  .
    For instance, when G is finite, one views G as a subgroup of G  = U (n) for n = |G|. Then a
solution t is found by using that the map G  → G  given by t → W (t) has degree #t (W )  = 0,
and must therefore be surjective, and hence W (t) = 1 for some t. Thus W (t) is solved in the
overgroup U (n).

   In this note, we show that an equation over a right-angled Artin group G can be solved
within the associated Magnus power series G  . More generally, this holds for any subgroup
of the group of units of such a power series ring.
   Subgroups of these groups of units are well-known to be residually finite, torsion-free
and orderable. So this case of the Kervaire conjecture was already a consequence of each
of the theorems referenced earlier. However, the solution within the power series overgroup
is new. More significantly, the mode of proof utilizing the Banach fixed point theorem to
solve group-theoretic equations is of interest here, as it offers a new paradigm and hopefully
there will be further generalizations along this direction. Other approaches to the Kervaire
conjecture using fixed points are given in [1].

2.1 R-graded monoid

The monoid M is R-graded if there is a map deg : M → R such that:

1. deg(m) ≥ 0 with equality iff m = 1
2. deg(mn) = deg(m) + deg(n)

variables (i.e. the generators).
