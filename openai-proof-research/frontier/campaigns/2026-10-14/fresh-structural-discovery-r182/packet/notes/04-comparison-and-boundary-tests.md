## Concrete checks of the exact interfaces

1. **Wild signs do occur.** If T is any linear onto isometry and ε is any even sign function, f=εT satisfies the pair identity and is onto. A discontinuous ε generally makes f discontinuous. Therefore the proof cannot assume f continuous or build the source return map directly from f. The constructed U, not f, is the continuous map.

2. **Evenness is essential for onto classification.** Taking one representative from each antipodal pair and folding both points to it preserves every unordered pair, but is not onto the whole sphere. Surjectivity is used to prove f(-x)=-f(x). Later arbitrary sign changes are not assumed to preserve surjectivity unless their signs are even. The proof's σ is even because f and U are both odd.

3. **Dimension2 has genuine monodromy.** In angular coordinates, a homeomorphism of the quotient circle has lift increment±π. The resulting sphere lift has increment±2π. Treating the sphere circle as simply connected would fail. The proof retains this separate case, and uses the simply connected construction from the inverse map if only the target has dimension≥3.

4. **Nonseparability is harmless at the verified topological step.** Only the image of one loop is approximated, by finitely many vertices in its given norm. The filling uses a finite-dimensional containing subspace, not a separable model for the entire phase map. No global separable reduction or simultaneous selection over all loops is assumed; homotopy invariance makes the endpoint construction path independent.

5. **Flat faces do not force a strict-monotonicity hypothesis.** In ℓ∞², with x=(1,0), y=(1,1), the entire normalized segment is z_t=(1,t). Its two chord values are t and2. In the crossing-edge example x=(1,1), y=(-1,1), both values at y equal2. The proof handles this tie directly, rather than trying to propagate a strict branch choice through it. For arbitrary independent endpoints with A<B, the two-triangle estimate gives the UNIFORM path gap b(t)-a(t)≥B-A>0, even if one or both functions have flat intervals.

6. **The min-only problem is not smuggled into the proof.** From the min metric alone one obtains the quotient homeomorphism and its odd continuous lift. That says nothing by itself about the larger of the two target chord norms. In §5, h(t)∈{a(t),b(t)} uses the complete unordered identity. Thus the displayed argument does not establish an arbitrary-Banach min-phase theorem. A late bounded search located the2022 whole-space min-phase paper (Huang–Tan, Results Math., DOI10.1007/s00025-022-01702-8); only navigation text was read, no proof is claimed or used. It is an additional literature-review lead, not a premise or a quietly ignored sphere theorem.

7. **No complex linearity.** On C considered as a real plane, conjugation is already an ordinary sphere isometry. It satisfies the frozen input but is not complex linear. The conclusion correctly stays real-linear, as does Source322.

8. **Uniqueness has the right normalization.** The identity phase data admit both T and -T with opposite phase functions. The statement only claims uniqueness after prescribing T(x_*)=f(x_*). The proof uses the continuous0-or2 distance between two candidate linear restrictions, not any regularity of their separate phase functions.

9. **No replacement of old full targets.** Nothing here estimates the radial defect of an ε-isometry, gives an approximation rate to a linear map, constructs a coupled AP/no-BAP absorption space, controls arbitrary nonlinear homeomorphisms, or cancels the integral stable summands in r181. All original quantitative, relative, uniformity and Banach-space scopes encountered remain intact.
