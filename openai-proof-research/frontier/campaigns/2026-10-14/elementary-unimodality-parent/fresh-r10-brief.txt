## 2. Exact allowed expansion and all six trace families

Let G have n vertices and let e=uv, f=xy be a GSW C4-expansion input:
u,y in one bipartition class, v,x in the other; all four endpoints distinct
on one facial cycle; deleting e,f from that cycle leaves two odd paths.
The expanded graph G+ adds u',v',x',y', deletes e,f, and adds

    uu', vv', xx', yy', u'v', x'y', u'x', v'y'.

This is precisely the definition preceding GSW Theorem 5.1. Its stated
closure keeps the required graph class. Put F=G-{e,f}; the old vertices
are retained. Write the new square cyclically as

    q0=u', q1=v', q2=y', q3=x',
    r_i=q_i q_(i+1), s_i=a_i q_i,
    (a0,a1,a2,a3)=(u,v,y,x),

with indices modulo four. F is connected: a simple cubic 3-vertex-connected
graph has no cut of at most two edges. To see this, each side of such a cut
has at least three vertices (a singleton has boundary three and two vertices
have boundary at least four); deleting its at most two boundary endpoints
on one side disconnects a nonempty remnant from the other side, a contradiction.
Thus deleting e,f cannot disconnect G.

Every Hamiltonian cycle in G+ has one of the following gadget traces:

- L_i, i=0,1,2,3: the three ring edges other than r_i, and spokes s_i,s_(i+1).
- P: ring edges r1,r3 and all four spokes.
- Q: ring edges r0,r2 and all four spokes.

For completeness, let t be the number of used spokes. Degree two at the
four square vertices gives 8=2*(number of ring edges)+t. The possible
values of t are 0,2,4. Zero would isolate the entire square as a closed
cycle, impossible because old vertices exist. With two spokes the three
ring edges form a spanning path; with four they form a perfect matching.
This exhausts all traces.

All six occur, from the exact pinned P4 corollary. The three-edge path in
the square omitting r_i forces L_i. To force the matching trace containing
r_i, prescribe the three-edge path a_i-q_i-q_(i+1)-a_(i+1). The adjacent
ring edges at its two square endpoints are excluded, forcing the opposite
ring edge and the other two spokes. This gives both P and Q. No printed
path-length typo in GSW Observation 4.11 is used.

Removing the gadget from such a cycle leaves a spanning path in F with
endpoints a_i,a_(i+1) in type L_i. In type P it leaves two spanning paths
pairing (a0,a1),(a2,a3); in type Q it leaves two paths pairing
(a0,a3),(a1,a2). There are no extra cycle components, because the original
cycle was connected. The third pairing of four boundary points crosses

the one different from the internal matching, to obtain a single cycle.
Conversely every such spanning path cover completes to the corresponding

## 3. The exact C4 rank decomposition

Let gamma_i be the eight gadget incidence coordinates (four ring, then
four spokes) in trace i. Explicitly, gamma_i for L_i has ring coordinates
1 except at i and spoke coordinates 1 exactly at i,i+1; gamma_P has ring
(0,1,0,1) and all spokes; gamma_Q has ring (1,0,1,0) and all spokes.

**Lemma 1.** Their affine rank is four, and their sole affine relation,
up to scalar, is

    gamma_0+gamma_2-gamma_1-gamma_3-gamma_P+gamma_Q=0.       (3.1)

*Proof.* The spoke vectors of L0,L1,L2,L3 form a parallelogram of affine
rank two, all with sum two. The all-spoke vector has sum four and adds a
third direction. The difference gamma_P-gamma_Q is nonzero on the ring
and zero on the spokes, adding a fourth direction. All eight-coordinate
vectors satisfy the four independent degree equations at the new vertices;
independence follows since each equation has its own spoke coordinate.
Their affine rank is therefore at most four. Direct coordinate addition
gives (3.1), whose coefficient sum is zero. With six points and affine
rank four, the space of affine relations has dimension one. ∎

For each trace i, let S_i be its complete set of exterior edge vectors on
F, and choose any representative z_i in S_i. Define

    W = sum_i span_R {z-z_i : z in S_i},
    d = z_0+z_2-z_1-z_3-z_P+z_Q.                         (3.2)

Changing representatives changes d only by a vector in W.

**Lemma 2 (exact rank formula).**

    dim aff_R HC(G+) = 4 + dim_R(W+R d).                 (3.3)

Moreover W+R d is a subspace of ker B_F, of dimension at most n/2-1.
Consequently H on this expanded graph is equivalent to

    W+R d = ker B_F.                                    (3.4)

*Proof.* Let D be the Hamiltonian difference space and project it onto
the gadget coordinates. Since all six trace families are nonempty, the
image has dimension four by Lemma 1. Within-trace differences lie in the
kernel and have exterior coordinates generating W.

Consider any vector in the projection kernel. Express it as a linear
combination of full Hamiltonian vectors with coefficient sum zero. Group
coefficients by trace, writing their six sums as b_i. Subtract the
within-trace differences to replace every cycle of type i by its chosen
representative (z_i,gamma_i). The remaining coefficients satisfy
sum b_i=0 and sum b_i gamma_i=0. Lemma 1 says b is a scalar multiple of
(1,-1,1,-1,-1,1). Thus the remaining exterior vector is a multiple of d.
Conversely d, with zero gadget coordinates, is that linear combination
of actual Hamiltonian vectors and belongs to D. This identifies the
projection kernel exactly with W+R d, proving (3.3).

Every full Hamiltonian difference satisfies degree zero. If its gadget
coordinates vanish, its old-vertex equations say B_F z=0, proving the
inclusion. F is connected and bipartite, so rank B_F=n-1 by Section 1.
Since |E(F)|=3n/2-2, dim ker B_F=n/2-1. The expanded graph has n+4 vertices
and degree affine dimension n/2+3=4+(n/2-1). Equality of dimensions inside
A(G+) now proves the final equivalence. ∎

This pays the local gadget rank exactly. It does not pay (3.4). In
particular, source-guaranteed nonempty families pay four dimensions;
the remaining n/2-1 directions require their full exterior variation.
Even W=ker B_F is too strong as a universal replacement: the expanded
cube diagnostic below has dim W=2, dim ker B_F=3, and d adds the missing
direction. Conversely full H forces codim(W in ker B_F) at most one.
No argument proving that necessary bound for every exterior was obtained.

The inherited cycles from G occupy only types L0,L2,Q. Cycles avoiding

## 4. An integer exchange route is impossible for structural reasons

An original Hamiltonian cycle C avoiding e,f is a Hamiltonian cycle of
F. In G+ its union with the new square is a disconnected spanning
2-factor D0 with two components. On gadget coordinates,

    1_square = gamma_0+gamma_2-gamma_P
             = gamma_1+gamma_3-gamma_Q.                 (4.1)

One attempted way to recover missing cycles was to choose actual
Hamiltonian cycles of those types so that the same identity holds on
exterior coordinates, for example

    1_D0 = 1_HL0 + 1_HL2 - 1_HP.                        (4.2)

The following proves that this route cannot work, independently of
finite probing.

**Lemma 3 (Pfaffian parity obstruction).** In a bipartite Pfaffian graph
on N vertices, the incidence vector of a spanning 2-factor with an even
number of components is not an integer affine combination of Hamiltonian
cycle vectors. Any rational affine certificate for it has an even common
denominator.

*Proof.* Fix a Pfaffian orientation. Put epsilon_e=1 when e is directed
from the second bipartition class to the first, and zero otherwise.
For a conformal cycle of length 2k, take an alternating traversal. If b
is the number of its edges oriented from the second class to the first,
the number aligned with the traversal has parity k+b: among its k steps
from the first class to the second, a backward orientation subtracts one,
and among the other k steps it adds one. The Pfaffian condition makes
that aligned number odd. Therefore

    sum_(e in cycle) epsilon_e = k-1  (mod 2).

Each component of a spanning 2-factor is conformal, since the remaining
even cycle components have perfect matchings. Summing gives

    epsilon . 1_D = N/2-c(D)  (mod 2),                 (4.3)

where c(D) is its component count. All Hamiltonian vectors have parity
N/2-1. Integer affine coefficients summing to one preserve that parity,
whereas even c(D) gives the opposite value. For rational coefficients
with odd common denominator a, multiply the proposed identity by a.
Their integer coefficient sum is a, and reducing modulo two produces
the same contradiction. ∎

Planar graphs are Pfaffian by GSW Theorem 4.6, so the lemma applies here.
In particular (4.2) is impossible. This is an integral exchange
obstruction, not a real linear functional constant on all Hamiltonian
cycles. It neither refutes H nor changes its coefficient field.

## 6. Why denominators cannot be repaired by a fixed universal bound

Here is a supporting calculation over a family already paid in r6, not
a replacement goal. In the even prism P_m, let A_i,D_i be the two ring
edges at column gap i and R_i the rungs. The complete Hamiltonian family
has cycles L_i using both rings except A_i,D_i and rungs R_i,R_(i+1),
and two zigzags Z0,Z1 using all rungs and complementary alternating
ring matchings.

To check completeness, the chosen rungs in any Hamiltonian cycle are
nonempty and even in number. If not all rungs are chosen, there is a gap
of at least two steps between consecutive chosen columns. The top and
bottom paths across it are forced, as are its two boundary rungs. They
already form a closed component. Hamiltonicity forces it to contain every
column, so the other gap is one step and there are exactly two rungs:
this is L_i. If all rungs are chosen, each ring contributes one of its
two alternating perfect matchings. Equal choices give disjoint squares;
complementary choices give Z0,Z1. This also verifies that the listed
cycles themselves are Hamiltonian.

They form an affine basis. Here is the weight proof, included to avoid
silently assuming the r6 supporting lemma. Let a_i,b_i,r_i be edge costs
and let all listed cycle costs equal K. Set S=sum(a_i+b_i), R=sum r_i.
Summing the L_i costs and the two zigzag costs gives

    mK=(m-1)S+2R,  2K=S+2R.

Since m>2, subtraction yields S=2R and K=S. Individual L_i equations
then give a_i+b_i=r_i+r_(i+1). Equality of zigzag costs says the alternating
sum of a_i-b_i is zero. The alternating sum of a_i+b_i is also zero by
the preceding equations, so the alternating sum of a_i is zero. Therefore
p_i+p_(i+1)=a_i has a cyclic solution for even m, by recursion
p_(i+1)=a_i-p_i. Put q_i=r_i-p_i; then q_i+q_(i+1)=b_i. These are vertex
potentials, proving affine span by Section 1. There are m+2 listed cycles
and dimension m+1, so they are independent.

Let D_ring be the disconnected factor of the two rings. Coordinatewise,

    D_ring=(sum_i L_i-Z0-Z1)/(m-2).                     (6.1)

Each ring edge has numerator (m-1)-1=m-2, and every rung has numerator
2-2=0. Coefficients sum to one, and the affine basis makes them unique.
In particular their common denominator must be divisible by m-2. As
even m grows, no fixed denominator represents every such integral degree
two vector by affine combinations of the complete Hamiltonian family.
This does not exclude real rank proofs or iterated local rational
identities, whose denominators could grow under composition.

## 1. Original target and exact scope

A *Barnette graph* is a finite simple undirected cubic bipartite planar
3-vertex-connected graph. Write n=|V|, m=|E|=3n/2, let B be its unsigned
vertex-edge incidence matrix, and let H(G) be the set of 0-1 incidence
vectors of all its Hamiltonian cycles. The full target, recorded in
`original-scope.md` before the mathematical attempts, is

> **H.** For every Barnette graph G,
>
>     aff_R H(G) = A(G) := {x in R^E : Bx = 2 1}.

This concerns the entire set of cycles, every graph size, and all real
edge weightings. It asks for real affine span, not binary cycle-space span,
convex-hull equality, or a polynomial algorithm. For a connected bipartite
graph, rank_R B=n-1: a left-kernel vector satisfies a_u+a_v=0 on every
edge, and connectivity propagates one value on one color class and its
negative on the other. Thus dim A(G)=m-n+1=n/2+1. H would imply at least
n/2+2 different Hamiltonian cycles. This implication is part of the same
target and would not be a second campaign result.

An equivalent formulation is:

> If w in R^E has sum_(e in C) w_e independent of the Hamiltonian cycle C,
> then there are vertex potentials a_v with w_uv=a_u+a_v on every edge.

Indeed, H(G) is nonempty by the stipulated source. The annihilator of the
direction space of A(G) is row(B). Equality of the two affine spaces is
equivalent to equality of their annihilators; every row(B) weighting has
cycle cost 2 sum_v a_v. This is standard finite-dimensional linear algebra,
not the proposed discovery.

The bounded screen rejected exponential counting *before* target selection:
even prisms have only linearly many Hamiltonian cycles. The primary
Gibbons working note already treats the linear lower bound as a research
target. Accordingly, H was selected as a real gap requiring new work, not
as a routine consequence of Hamiltonicity. The screen found no exact
primary statement of H, but it is not a worldwide novelty certificate.

## 3. A full proof for even prisms (technical support only)

Let P_m=C_m square K_2, with even m>=4. Name its top, bottom and rung edges
A_i=u_i u_(i+1), D_i=v_i v_(i+1), R_i=u_i v_i (indices modulo m).
There are the following m+2 Hamiltonian cycles:

* L_i uses every top and bottom edge except A_i,D_i, and the two rungs
  R_i,R_(i+1). Its two long ring paths join into one spanning cycle.
* Z_0 uses all rungs, the even A_i and odd D_i. Z_1 uses all rungs and the
  complementary ring edges. Each travels successively through the columns
  in cyclic order, so each is one spanning cycle.

**Proposition 1.** These m+2 vectors are affinely independent and span
A(P_m). Consequently H holds on all even prisms.

*Proof.* Give the three edge types weights a_i,b_i,r_i. Suppose all the
listed cycle costs equal K. Put S=sum_i(a_i+b_i), R=sum_i r_i. Summing the
costs of the L_i gives

    m K = (m-1) S + 2R.

Adding the two zigzag costs gives 2K=S+2R. Subtraction, with m>2, gives
S=2R and K=S. Each L_i equality now gives

    a_i+b_i = r_i+r_(i+1).                              (3.1)

Equality of the zigzag costs gives sum_i (-1)^i(a_i-b_i)=0. By (3.1) the
alternating sum of b is the negative of the alternating sum of a. Hence
sum_i(-1)^i a_i=0. The cyclic equations p_i+p_(i+1)=a_i are soluble:
choose p_0 arbitrarily and recurse p_(i+1)=a_i-p_i; for even m the terminal
condition is exactly the alternating-sum condition. Set q_i=r_i-p_i.
Then (3.1) gives q_i+q_(i+1)=b_i. Thus the weighting is the sum of its
endpoint potentials p on the top and q on the bottom.

The annihilator characterization from Section 1 now proves that the listed
vectors affinely span A(P_m), of dimension m+1. They number m+2, so they
are affinely independent. Every other Hamiltonian vector also lies in
A(P_m), proving the final assertion. ∎

The listed cycles and the exact prism count are classical elementary
material; the fresh Gibbons primary note supplies a complete count proof.
The proposition's real linear-algebra conclusion is proved here and is
not claimed substantial or new to the literature. No exact count theorem
is needed for its proof. The checker separately exhausts perfect matchings
and validates the counts for m=4,6,...,20.

## 4. Splice closure, with the entire affine statement retained

For two disjoint connected simple cubic bipartite graphs G_1,G_2, choose
vertices v_1,v_2 and a bijection between their three incident edges,
labelled 1,2,3. Delete v_1,v_2 and replace each matched pair of incident
edges by one edge joining their other endpoints. Call the result G.
Assume each G_i-v_i is connected. The color classes can be reversed in
one piece to make G bipartite. For planar pieces, matching the boundary
orders gives a planar splice; for three attachment points every bijection
is a rotation or reflection of an order.

**Lemma 2 (finite affine gluing).** Let S_i be finite sets partitioned into three nonempty
classes S_(i,1),S_(i,2),S_(i,3). Suppose an affine map pi_i on aff(S_i)
sends S_(i,s) to the s-th standard basis vector of R^3. Then

    aff { (x,y): x in S_(1,s), y in S_(2,s), some s }
      = { (x,y) in aff(S_1) x aff(S_2): pi_1(x)=pi_2(y) }.       (4.1)

*Proof.* Inclusion from left to right is immediate. For the reverse,
consider any affine functional F(x,y)=f_1(x)+f_2(y) vanishing on the left.
Fixing one variable in each nonempty class shows that f_i is constant on
that class; write the constants a_(i,s). They obey a_(1,s)+a_(2,s)=0.
The affine function g_i(z)=sum_s a_(i,s) z_s agrees with f_i on S_i after
composition with pi_i, hence on aff(S_i). On the right of (4.1), F is
therefore sum_s(a_(1,s)+a_(2,s)) z_s=0. Equality follows because a proper
affine subspace of a finite-dimensional affine space has a separating
affine functional. ∎

**Proposition 3 (splice closure).** Suppose H(G_i) affinely spans A(G_i)
for i=1,2, and for each s each G_i has a Hamiltonian cycle omitting its
s-th edge at v_i. Then H(G) affinely spans A(G).

*Proof.* A Hamiltonian cycle of G uses a positive even number of the three
joining edges, hence exactly two. It restricts to a spanning path in each
piece. Adding back v_i turns these paths into Hamiltonian cycles of G_i
with the same omitted edge s. Conversely any such pair splices to one
Hamiltonian cycle. This is exactly the ordinary splice correspondence,
with all compatible pairs retained.

On A(G_i) set pi_i(x)_s=1-x_(v_i,s). Since the three star coordinates sum
to 2, the three pi coordinates sum to 1; on a Hamiltonian vector pi is
its omitted-edge basis vector. Lemma 2 shows that the affine hull of all
compatible pairs is the fiber product of A(G_1),A(G_2) over pi.

That fiber product is affinely isomorphic to A(G). The map retains the
internal coordinates and uses the common star coordinates on the joining
edges. Its inverse is obtained by duplicating each joining coordinate at
the two removed stars. The only potentially additional equations are the
degree equations at v_i. They follow already from the equations in G:
in G_i the color classes have equal size by cubicity; after removal of
v_i, the piece has one fewer vertex of its color and all three boundary
vertices have the opposite color. Subtracting the degree sum on one color
from the sum on the other cancels all internal edges, and gives sum of the
three joining coordinates equal to 2. This argument is valid for arbitrary
real coordinates, without positivity. Thus the inverse satisfies both
removed-vertex equations, completing the affine identification. ∎

For Barnette graphs, all the omitted-edge classes are nonempty by the
pinned `cor:edge-avoidance`. The cube satisfies Proposition 1. Thus cube
expansions preserve H. Arbitrarily iterated planar splices of even prisms
also satisfy H, with no size bound. This is a supporting family theorem,
not a narrowed campaign nomination. The substantive global issue is the
other generator, C4 expansion. The glue is elementary affine bookkeeping
on top of the GSW/HMM splice, and is not assessed as a nonroutine advance.

## 5. The C4 step and a rigorous loss in the direct lifting route

Use GSW's exact C4 expansion notation. Choose cofacial edges uv,xy with
u,y in one color class, v,x in the other, all distinct, such that deleting
these edges from their facial cycle leaves two odd paths. Replace them
by the chains u-u'-v'-v and x-x'-y'-y, and add u'x',v'y'. The four new
vertices induce the square u'v'y'x'. Call the expanded graph G^+.

An original Hamiltonian cycle using uv or xy has a unique lift with the
following traces. If it uses both, lift through the two chains; if it
uses only uv, replace uv by the long square path

    u-u'-x'-y'-v'-v;

if it uses only xy, use the other long square path. All these are spanning
lifts with the old exterior edges unchanged. If the cycle uses neither,
its exterior already forms a closed spanning cycle on the old vertices;
keeping those exterior edges leaves each old vertex degree two and
precludes attachment of the four new vertices. It therefore has no such
lift. These facts follow directly from degree two and connectedness.
They are the classical local trace issue, not a new obstruction to H.

**Proposition 4 (arbitrarily large rank loss for this route).** In P_m,
choose the two rungs at columns 0 and 1, which meet the cofacial odd-path
conditions. Exactly five original Hamiltonian cycles have spanning lifts
of the above kind. Their lifts have affine dimension at most four,
whereas dim A(P_m^+)=m+3. Thus inheriting only these cycle lifts cannot
prove H for this expansion, with a deficit growing in m.

*Proof.* The exact prism count says every Hamiltonian cycle is one of the
m+2 cycles listed in Section 3. A short independent verification of the
needed classification is as follows. Let R_H be its rung set; it is
nonempty and even. If R_H is proper, any gap of length at least two
between consecutive chosen rungs forces the entire top and bottom path
across that gap into the cycle. At each chosen rung, both adjacent gaps
cannot have length at least two, since each rung endpoint can use just
one ring edge. If there is at least one such long gap, each of its two
chosen boundary rungs has its one ring edge already fixed toward that
gap; the two ring paths and the boundary rungs consequently form a closed
component. Hamiltonicity forces that component to be the whole graph,
so there are exactly two chosen rungs and the other gap is one edge:
the cycle is L_i. This argument also covers runs of consecutive chosen
rungs: any long gap would already close a component before those runs
could be attached. If all rungs are chosen, the ring edges must be
perfect matchings in the two m-cycles. Each cycle has precisely two
perfect matchings; choosing the same one makes disjoint four-cycles,
while complementary ones give Z_0,Z_1. This proves the classification.

The cycles using R_0 or R_1 are L_(m-1),L_0,L_1,Z_0,Z_1, five distinct
cycles even when m=4. Any five vectors have affine dimension at most four.
The expanded graph has 2m+4 vertices and is connected and bipartite, so
its degree affine space has dimension m+3. ∎

The general direct-lifting deficit is not a counterexample. For example,
the exact check at m=10 finds 26 Hamiltonian cycles after expansion and
full affine dimension 13; most needed directions arise outside the five
inherited lifts. No exact formula for all expanded sizes is claimed.
A successful induction needs information on exterior spanning path
covers or some other source of these directions. Neither Proposition 3
nor the original affine rank of P_m supplies that information.

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. 2026-10-16. Original prose CC BY 4.0;

The main new finding is a status result. Already in dimension one, T2 is
exactly equivalent (up to a factor 2 in the constant) to the following
question: does every weighted *one-block* 0-1 matrix have bounded
discrepancy? Cherniavskii, Kupavskii, Moiseev and Shekhovtsov posed this
question as open in arXiv:2610.16079v1, §6 (12 October 2026). It is also

weighted-discrepancy conjecture (in either of its stated forms) implies it.
Bérczi, Liu, Reis and Tarnawski's 2026 prefix-constrained basis conjecture
also implies it. So a proof of T2 would settle a recently posed open problem,
and a counterexample to T2, even at d=1, would refute two published
conjectures. The pinned one-order theorem gives nothing beyond greedy in d=1.
The missing step is therefore the coupling of the two orders, not the
dimension.

## 2. Notation

For d ≥ 1 let B^d be the closed Euclidean unit ball. All suprema below are
taken in [0,∞].

* **Two-order constant.**
  D_2(d) := sup_{N, pi, rho, v ∈ (B^d)^N} min_epsilon max_{sigma,k} ||Σ_{j≤k} epsilon_{sigma(j)} v_{sigma(j)}||.
  T2 says exactly that D_2(d) ≤ C sqrt(d) for all d.
* **Interval-coverage constant.** For finitely many closed bounded intervals
  I_1,…,I_N ⊂ R with vectors v_j ∈ B^d, put
  cov_epsilon(x) := Σ_{j : x ∈ I_j} epsilon_j v_j. Then
  D_I(d) := sup min_epsilon sup_{x∈R} ||cov_epsilon(x)||.
* **One-block constant.** Fix a row order of a 0-1 matrix M. M is *one-block*
  if the ones in every column form one consecutive block of rows. Put
  D_OB(d) := sup over one-block M (m×N) and v ∈ (B^d)^N of
  min_epsilon max_r ||Σ_j M_rj epsilon_j v_j||.

When d=1, put d_j := |v_j| ∈ [0,1] and absorb sign(v_j) into epsilon_j.
Then D_OB(1) is exactly the supremum, over 0-1 one-block matrices A, of
wdisc(A) := sup_{W diagonal in [0,1]} disc(AW). Reis, Chapter 8, uses this
notion. Likewise, D_2(1) is exactly the supremum over n of the "weighted two
permutation discrepancy" C_n in BRS Definition 19, where the first order is
the identity. Relabeling the indices makes this no loss.

## 3. Exact interval reformulation (new to this project; elementary)

**Theorem 1.** For every d ≥ 1,

    D_2(d) ≤ D_OB(d) ≤ 2 D_2(d)    and    D_2(d) ≤ D_I(d) ≤ 2 D_2(d).

So T2 holds iff D_I(d) = O(sqrt d) iff D_OB(d) = O(sqrt d). In particular,
the d=1 case of T2 holds iff weighted one-block 0-1 matrices have uniformly
bounded weighted discrepancy.

*Proof.* (a) *Two orders embed as intervals.* Fix pi, rho and v. Let
a_i := pi^{-1}(i) and b_i := rho^{-1}(i), both in [N]. Put
I_i := [a_i, 2N+1−b_i]. This interval is nonempty because a_i ≤ N < N+1 ≤ 2N+1−b_i.

* For an integer x=k with 1 ≤ k ≤ N, x ∈ I_i iff a_i ≤ k. The upper
  condition k ≤ 2N+1−b_i always holds. So cov_epsilon(k) is the k-th pi-prefix.
* For x = 2N+1−k with 1 ≤ k ≤ N, the condition a_i ≤ x always holds, and
  x ≤ 2N+1−b_i iff b_i ≤ k. So cov_epsilon(x) is the k-th rho-prefix.
* The empty prefixes are zero.

Hence, for every epsilon, the two-order maximum is at most
sup_x ||cov_epsilon(x)||, and the minimum over epsilon gives D_2 ≤ D_I.
Restricting to the integer points 1,…,2N gives the 2N×N matrix whose rows,
in order, are P_1,…,P_N, Q_N,…,Q_1. Here P_k and Q_k are the k-th pi- and
rho-prefix sets. Column i has ones exactly in rows a_i,…,2N+1−b_i, which is
one block. The same signs therefore give D_2 ≤ D_OB.

(b) *Intervals reduce to two orders.* Take closed intervals I_j = [l_j, r_j]
with l_j ≤ r_j. Let sigma_L order the indices by (l_j, j) and sigma_R by (r_j, j).
For each x define L(x) := {j : l_j ≤ x} and R(x) := {j : r_j < x}.

* L(x) is an initial segment of sigma_L, because any index with smaller
  l also lies in L(x).
* R(x) is an initial segment of sigma_R, for the same reason.
* R(x) ⊆ L(x), because l_j ≤ r_j < x.
* {j : x ∈ I_j} = L(x) \ R(x).

Therefore cov_epsilon(x) = S_L(x) − S_R(x), where S_L(x) and S_R(x) are
prefix sums of epsilon_j v_j in the orders sigma_L and sigma_R. Signing this
two-order instance within D_2(d) + eta, for any eta > 0, makes every coverage
value at most 2(D_2(d) + eta). Letting eta → 0 gives D_I ≤ 2 D_2.

For a one-block matrix, the same argument applies to the integer intervals
{s_j,…,t_j} of row indices. An all-zero column is placed last in both orders.
It never lies in L(r) or R(r), so it does not change the coverage. This gives
D_OB ≤ 2 D_2. ∎

Finite exact checks of both directions are in `checks.py` (A1–A3, with a
deliberate off-by-one control in C).

**Proposition 3 (prefix-constrained bases imply the scalar core).** Suppose
Bérczi–Liu–Reis–Tarnawski's Conjecture 5 holds with constant C. That is, for
every matroid M on [n], every w ≥ 0 and every x in the base polytope, some
basis B satisfies |Σ_{i≤t} w_i(1_B(i) − x_i)| ≤ C max w for all t. Then
D_2(1) ≤ 4C.

*Proof.* Take scalars v_1,…,v_N ∈ [−1,1] and orders pi, rho.

* Let the ground set be {i^+, i^- : i ∈ [N]}, ordered as
  pi(1)^+,…,pi(N)^+, rho(1)^-,…,rho(N)^-.
* Let M be the partition matroid that chooses exactly one element of each
  pair {i^+, i^-}. The point x ≡ 1/2 lies in its base polytope.
* Give both copies of i the weight w(i^±) := |v_i| ≤ 1.
* Take the basis B from Conjecture 5 and set delta_i := +1 if i^+ ∈ B and
  −1 otherwise.

Then 1_B(i^+) − 1/2 = delta_i/2 and 1_B(i^-) − 1/2 = −delta_i/2. The t=k
prefix (k ≤ N) equals (1/2) Σ_{j≤k} |v_{pi(j)}| delta_{pi(j)}, so every
pi-prefix has absolute value at most 2C. The t=N+k prefix equals

    (1/2) Σ_all |v_i| delta_i − (1/2) Σ_{j≤k} |v_{rho(j)}| delta_{rho(j)}.

The first term is the full pi-prefix, so every rho-prefix has absolute value
at most 4C. Finally, epsilon_i := delta_i sign(v_i) with sign(0) := 1. ∎

## 4. Where the difficulty lives

**Remark 4 (d=1 one-order is greedy).** For one order and scalars in [−1,1],
choose each sign opposite to the current prefix sum, using +1 at zero. Every
prefix then stays in [−1,1]. The induction step is |s − |v|| ≤ 1 when
s ∈ [0,1] and |v| ≤ 1, and symmetrically. So at d=1 the pinned one-order
theorem `thm:signing` adds nothing beyond this greedy bound. Yet the d=1
two-order problem is exactly the open question of Corollary 2.

This shows where the gap lies. The source's analytic machinery (filtered
Dirichlet energies, adaptive shifts, Gaussian path correlation) is what turns
O(d) into O(sqrt d) for one order. The missing step in T2 is the coupling of
two orders, and that step is already unresolved when d=1. Any proof of T2
must therefore contain a new argument for weighted one-block matrices. This
is a statement about content, not a theorem that excludes particular proof
methods.

**Remark 5 (norm classes are without loss).** Suppose T2 holds with constant
C' for families whose norms all lie in (1/2,1] ∪ {0}. Then T2 holds with 2C'.

*Proof.* Split the indices into U_b := {i : 2^{-b-1} < ||v_i|| ≤ 2^{-b}} for
b ≥ 0. Only finitely many classes are nonempty, and zero vectors get any sign.
Sign the rescaled family 2^b v_i, i ∈ U_b, in the two orders restricted to
U_b. Each prefix of pi or rho meets U_b in a prefix of the restricted order.
So each class contributes at most 2^{-b} C' sqrt d, and the classes sum to at
most 2C' sqrt d. ∎

So making all norms comparable is no restriction. In particular, the scalar
core is equivalent to its case with weights in (1/2,1]. The hard regime is
many distinct but comparable weights. With finitely many distinct weights
a_1 > … > a_K, signing each weight class by the unweighted two-permutation
bound (1, BRS §5 and the Bansal–Nikolov survey §2.3) gives at most Σ a_k.
That bound is not uniform in K.

## 5. Unconditional baseline over the full scope

This is a standard linear-algebraic bound. It adapts Bansal–Nikolov,
*Discrepancy Theory: An Algorithmic and Geometric Perspective*, Theorem 2.8
(k permutations, scalar), to vectors. The proof is self-contained and is
included so that the round has an honest unconditional bound over all
quantifiers. It is not a resolution, because it grows with N.

**Lemma 6.** Let x ∈ [−1,1]^N, let A := {i : |x_i| < 1}, and let
Lambda : R^A → R^q be linear. Put

    Q := {y ∈ [−1,1]^N : y_i = x_i for i ∉ A, Lambda(y_A − x_A) = 0}.

Then Q is a nonempty polytope, and each vertex y of Q has at least
|A| − rank(Lambda) indices i ∈ A with |y_i| = 1.

*Proof.* Q contains x and is bounded. Let K := {z ∈ R^N : z_i = 0 off A,
Lambda z_A = 0}, so dim K = |A| − rank(Lambda) =: k. Let T ⊆ A be the set of
coordinates of A that are tight at y. If some nonzero z ∈ K has z_T = 0, then
y ± tz ∈ Q for small t > 0, since the coordinates of A outside T have slack.
That would contradict y being a vertex. Hence K ∩ {z_T = 0} = {0}, and this
subspace has dimension at least k − |T|. So |T| ≥ k. ∎

**Proposition 7 (baseline).** For all d,N, every v ∈ (B^d)^N and every pi,rho,
some epsilon has every prefix in both orders of norm at most
(8d − 2)(⌊log2 N⌋ + 1).

*Proof.* Start with x^(0) = 0 and put g := 4d. Phase t runs while
A_t := {i : |x^(t−1)_i| < 1} is nonempty. It does the following.

* For each sigma ∈ {pi,rho}, list A_t in sigma-order and cut the list into
  consecutive groups of exactly g indices. Any shorter tail is left unconstrained.
* For each complete group G, impose the d scalar equations
  Σ_{i∈G}(y_i − x_i) v_i = 0.
* There are at most 2⌊|A_t|/g⌋ d ≤ |A_t|/2 equations. Let x^(t) be a vertex
  of the resulting Q.

By Lemma 6, at least |A_t|/2 coordinates of A_t become tight, so
|A_{t+1}| ≤ |A_t|/2. Hence |A_t| ≤ N/2^{t−1}, and the process stops after
T ≤ ⌊log2 N⌋+1 phases. The final x^(T) is a sign vector epsilon.

Now fix a sigma-prefix S. In phase t, only coordinates in A_t move.
S ∩ A_t is an initial segment of the active sigma-list. It is therefore a union
of complete groups, which preserve their weighted sum, together with fewer
than g further indices. Each coordinate moves by at most 2 and each
||v_i|| ≤ 1, so the prefix sum changes by at most 2(g−1) = 8d − 2 per phase.
Summing over the phases, starting from 0, gives the bound. ∎

Check B runs this exact algorithm in rational arithmetic, with d ≤ 3 and
N ≤ 40. It checks the equation count, the halving, the per-phase prefix
change and the final bound. This bound is O(d log N). T2 asks for O(sqrt d)
uniformly in N. CKMS Theorem 4 (statement read, proof not used) gives an
O(log)-type bound for weighted one-block matrices at d=1.

## 6. Mechanisms tried in this round, and where each fails

These are honest attempts at the scalar core. None of them closes the gap.
Each item records exactly where the attempt fails. Proved statements are
marked as proved; anything else is labeled heuristic or numerical.

1. **Bit-level decomposition (failed).** Write |v_i| = Σ_b beta_ib 2^{-b} and
   U_b := {i : beta_ib = 1}. If the signs satisfy |Σ_{i ∈ P ∩ U_b} epsilon_i| ≤ D_b
   for every pi- and rho-prefix P, with Σ_b 2^{-b} D_b ≤ C', then the scalar
   core holds with C'. This step is proved by the triangle inequality. The
   conditions form a system of 2(B+1) restricted prefix chains with one
   common signing. For three or more unrelated chains, unweighted discrepancy
   can be Ω(log n) (Newman–Neiman–Nikolov, as cited by CKMS Theorem 5). The
   level-b chains are not unrelated, since they restrict the same two
   permutations, but no structure was found that removes the coupling across
   levels. The geometric allowances D_b do not help at levels 0 and 1, which
   need constants. The LSV-type bit rounding cannot be used, because it needs
   independent signs at each bit level, while T2 needs one sign per index.
2. **Weighted pairing and merging (failed).** BRS Lemma 17 and survey §2.3
   use consecutive pairs in both orders. Reis's Theorem 141 merges
   weight-sorted pairs. With weights, opposite signs inside a pair leave the
   residual w_a − w_b, and the union of the two matchings ties all residual
   orientations on a cycle to one bit. The residual problem is a signing of
   cycles whose contributions are spread over both orders. It is not a
   smaller instance of the same kind, and no recursion was found in which the
   residuals shrink.
3. **Robust online game (closed in the literature).** BRS Theorem 20 shows
   that a robust constant-bound maker strategy for the one-dimensional game
   would bound D_2(1). BRS Lemma 21, whose proof was read in full, gives
   instances with values in [1/2,1] where the breaker forces
   Ω(log n / log log n). So this sufficient route cannot give a constant.
4. **Iterated rounding (logarithmic loss).** Proposition 7 loses a constant
   in each of log N phases. The Beck–Fiala alternative also fails. That
   alternative would drop a prefix row once at most t of its active
   elements are still fractional. Within one chain, the rows that still
   contain more than t fractional elements, restricted to the fractional set
   F, are |F|−t distinct nested nonempty sets. Such sets are linearly
   independent (proved). With two chains, the kept rows can therefore have
   rank |F| once |F| ≥ 2t, for example whenever the second chain adds a
   single independent row. Then no free direction remains. Some other
   accounting is needed to avoid paying in every phase.
5. **Relaxations (numerical only).** The semidefinite (vector-coloring)
   relaxation was solved with CLARABEL in exploratory scripts. On random and
   bit-reversal two-order instances with N ≤ 128, and in weight-climbing
   searches with N = 12 and 24, its value never exceeded 1. So no SDP-dual
   certificate of a large lower bound appeared at these sizes. This is
   numerical evidence, not a theorem; boundedness of the relaxation was not
   proved. For one order, BRS §6 proves SDP prefix discrepancy at most 1 via
   Banaszczyk. Their argument needs a one-order prefix theorem, and no
   two-order analogue is available.
6. **Small exact values (numerical search; one exact check).** Weight
   hill-climbing and an exact cutting-plane minimax with MILP inner solves
   (HiGHS) never found a two-order scalar instance with value ≥ 2 for N ≤ 21.

   k=3 orders as 5/3, at weights (1,5/6,1,5/6,1,2/3,1). Check E verifies this
   value exactly. That is above Reis's 8/5 for the same N but below his
   limiting lower bound 2. These searches say nothing about large N.
7. **Lifting two orders into one order (not achieved).** A route through the
   pinned theorem would need each rho-prefix to be a bounded combination of
   one-order prefix functionals of lifted vectors in O(d) dimensions with
   O(1) norms. The interval form of Theorem 1 shows what is needed:
   cov(x) = S_L(x) − S_R(x) is one *arrival* prefix minus one *departure*
   prefix, and the departures follow a second, unrelated order. No bounded
   encoding was found. Arbitrary encodings were not proved impossible.
8. **Source filter route (not retried).** The r4 obstructions are retained.

## 4. The live polynomials are centered and parity pure

One must not apply v->v^-1 to an arbitrary one-sided Laurent series.

    bar(v)=v^-1,   bar(X_a)=X_a,   bar(FG)=bar(G)bar(F).

    (-1)^(j+1) / (j*(v^j-v^(-j))).

This changes sign under bar, so bar(D_p)=D_p^-1 as a root-completed
rational identity. In particular it is not a statement about reversing
an infinite energy expansion term by term.

positive group, preserves root supports and each sign subgroup in the
source three-factor decomposition, and preserves the incoming equations.

one; the simple prescribed coefficients are fixed by this operation,

therefore gives tau(g)=g. Three-factor uniqueness then gives

    bar(g_0(h))=g_0(h)^-1

for every wall factor. Consequently each wall adjoint and its inverse

    J_e(v^-1)=J_e(v)                                (4.1)

    Q(a+b)=Q(a)+Q(b)+Omega(a,b) mod 2.
