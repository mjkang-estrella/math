                 A source-relative factor-two approximation
                    for reverse-complement superstrings
                                          Myeongjin Kang

                                  Manuscript r1, October 2026


                                               Abstract
          Assuming the count, periodic-layer, and connection lemmas of the pinned ordinary
      shortest-common-superstring construction of OpenAI, we give a deterministic polynomial-
      bit-time factor-two algorithm for superstrings with reverse complements. The alphabet
      and its involution are explicitly encoded and may vary with the input; ﬁxed letters, reverse-
      complement palindromes, empty requirements, duplicates, and containment are included.
      The extension uses one matched construction: a phase-free 2k + 1 count rule for self-
      reversed periodic texts, adjacent sorted proﬁles sharing both reﬂection axes, and an odd
      core of 2k − 1 layers in a 2k-layer budget unit. Established connection and symmetric
      traversal operations complete the algorithm. The dependence is on the stated source’s
      stronger structural lemmas, not merely on black-box existence of an ordinary factor-two
      algorithm. Sixteen ﬁnite and scalar Lean support lemmas cover speciﬁed interfaces only;
      the full algorithm is not formalized.


1     Statement, dependence, and antecedents
Let Σ be a ﬁnite alphabet with an involution cm : Σ → Σ. For a word s = s1 · · · sh , put
ρ(s) = cm(sh ) · · · cm(s1 ) and ρ(ε) = ε. A word is feasible for a ﬁnite listed family S if, for
every s ∈ S, it contains s or ρ(s) as a contiguous substring. Write OPTRC (S) for its minimum
symbol length. Alphabet labels, the involution table, word symbols, and list boundaries are
explicit; N denotes their total encoded bit length.

Theorem 1.1 (Source-relative approximation). Assume the structural results of [1] at revision
fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb, with the contracts speciﬁed below. There is
a deterministic algorithm, polynomial in N , which for every such (Σ, cm, S) returns a feasible
word T with |T | ≤ 2 OPTRC (S). The assertion includes arbitrary ﬁxed letters and words,
empty words and the empty list, duplicates, and substring redundancy. Neither the alphabet
nor the word lengths are bounded constants.

    The source’s truths are assumptions of this article. We do not audit them or derive an
independent proof of the ordinary superstring theorem. In particular, an unspeciﬁed ordinary
factor-two algorithm would not supply the contracts used here. Yamano–Shibuya’s reduction
in [2, Theorem 2] also explains why an unrestricted reverse-complement result cannot be
advertised as bypassing the ordinary problem.

1.3   Why the matched package is needed
For identity complement on {b, c}, take S = {bbcbbc, bcbbcb, cbbcbb}. The ordinary rules sym-
metrized by orbit maxima and even rounding give one-letter counts 4, 2, hence Wsym = 6. But
OPTRC = 7: a length-six word cannot contain the two distinct length-six orientation orbits,
and bbcbbcb satisﬁes both. This refutes only a connection theorem that would retain those
counts and yield output length at most the same Wsym . It does not refute factor two or all
equivariant methods. The stronger rule below gives W = 12 on this instance.
    A diﬀerent diagnostic, {aaa, aab, abcaa, accc} with a ↔ b and c ﬁxed, has optimum 10.
Minimum constrained-cycle-cover orientation ties can lead to ordinary forced-count certiﬁcates
11 or 9. This shows that arbitrary minimum tie-breaking does not ensure the desired W
certiﬁcate; it does not exclude all orientation-selection methods. These ﬁnite certiﬁcates
motivate the construction and are not used to prove Theorem 1.1.
    Boosting only ﬁxed words is also insuﬃcient for the intended proof: in (abcacb)Z , the
layer z(x) = x + 8 has ﬁxed-axis windows at starts 0, 3, whereas its unique greatest forward
rotation starts at 4. The two windows there are cbabcac and cbabcacb, neither ﬁxed. A count
rule conﬁned to ﬁxed-axis starts would not justify the maximal-rotation cycle argument. The
rule below holds at every phase of a self-reversed text.


2     Symmetric counts and their domination
Remove empty requirements, merge ρ-orbits, and discard an orbit contained in a diﬀerent
larger orbit. If s is contained in t, then ρ(s) is contained in ρ(t), so either orientation of the
larger word satisﬁes an orientation of the smaller. This preprocessing preserves feasibility and
the optimum. If nothing remains, return ε. Otherwise let R include both orientations of the
surviving requirements, and let V consist of all their substrings, including ε. It is substring-
closed and ρ-closed; required words are maximal in V . Let L be the total symbol length of
one representative per surviving orbit, so L ≤ N and |V | = O(L2 ).

3.1   Paired decomposition and the exit convention
At each nonﬁxed vertex choose a matching from incoming to outgoing edge copies and force
its reversed matching at the mate vertex. At a ﬁxed vertex, pair outgoing copies by a ﬁxed-
point-free involution; identifying incoming copies with their reversals gives the transition
matching. This is possible by Section 2.2. The resulting cycles come in distinct reversed pairs.

3.3   Spliced paths for hosting
Let D1 , D2 be a self pair and let U, V be its two shared axes in cyclic order. Form E from
D1 [U, V ] followed by D2 [V, U + (p, p)], and form F from the complementary arcs. These are
monotone period-p paths with p up steps each; their labelled union equals that of D1 , D2 .
Reﬂection exchanges the half-period arcs, so R(E) = F at the level of windows and phase-
labelled edges. Coincident proﬁles use distinct labels. This is not an assertion about the
original base edge-copy involution after sorting. Later rootedness uses shared vertices, and
mirrored added walks receive new paired edge copies. Keep the original sorted proﬁles for all
band geometry; use E, F only as host paths.
    The exchange-of-halves operation has the antecedent in [6, Section 5]. That operation
assumes the two meetings. Lemma 3.1 is what establishes them for the ordered proﬁles here.


4     Geometric connection contracts
A piece is rooted when its underlying undirected support is connected to ε. All edge multisets
are balanced, and all base edges remain, so touching one vertex of a layer connects its entire
support. Incidental connectivity does not change budget marks.
    A record is an actual window on a target layer, together with an alignment in a source text
agreeing throughout that window. The matching-window contract [1, lem:matching-window]
says that the number of blocked recorded occurrences relative to A is BA (s; m), and every
unblocked occurrence is contained in such an actual matching window. The nearest-mismatch
correspondence proves the ﬁrst assertion. For the second, follow the layer from the ﬁrst window
containing the occurrence until its left mismatch is deleted; a right mismatch cannot enter
ﬁrst without recording its bracket.
    We also use [1, lem:agreement]: distinct aligned texts of periods p, q agree on fewer than
p + q letters, and on fewer than p if both have period p. Alignment matters, even within one
translation class. The maximal-rotation contract [1, lem:max-rotation] says that if t starts
the greatest forward rotation of A and every rotation of a diﬀerent aligned period-q text D is
at most that word, they cannot agree on all q positions starting at t. These comparisons use
encoded-symbol order and include opposite orientations as well as diﬀerent phases.

The requesters at j were touched directly, so one unspent pj budget roots the short contact.
This proves the contract, including the case with no later records.


5     Routing requests through paired hosts
Process primitive-text orbits in increasing least period, breaking ties by canonical text and
stable identiﬁers. Requests will go strictly upward in period. All records refer to the ﬁxed
base-layer decomposition. A basic pair reserves its entire 2p budget, and processing never
splits that account.
    For a nonself recipient pair use its two exchanged sorted layers E, F . For a self pair use
the spliced paths of Section 3.3, each with budget q and together representing the recipient’s
2q account. If requests arrive, choose one original request as an anchor and name E so that
its original record lies there. Keep its whole child-orbit batch unﬂipped. Every other child
orbit also uses one record for all its requests. If that record lies only on F , reﬂect the whole
batch, so its record lies on E. Distinct child orbits still give distinct child texts, all of period
smaller than q.

5.1    Reclassiﬁcation before invoking the host lemma
A reﬂected requester may be right-anchored. At its new record R′ = [a′ , b′ ), its reﬂected
actual window is [u, b′ ) ⊆ R′ . Monotonicity gives f (a′ ) ≤ f (u) ≤ b′ . Reapply Section 4.1

vertex of length at most p, and reﬂection preserves lengths. Its second case gives a link to R′
which will be executed after the host is rooted. Its third case gives exactly the left-anchored
inequality in Lemma 4.3. The unchanged anchor batch still supplies at least one genuine
third-case request.
    Apply Lemma 4.3 once on the actual monotone path E to the surviving third-case requests
and mirror the entire construction on F . The one-record-per-child, distinct-text, left-anchor,
nonempty, and available-budget hypotheses have all been re-established; this is not an invo-
cation on a disconnected union of proﬁles. Choose the minimum-period child only among
these surviving groups. One of its requester-pair budgets pays the two short root contacts;
that pair was touched directly by the two host excursions. Total cost is at most 2q plus all
surviving requester-pair budgets.
    Both host paths are rooted. Their union contains all of the original recipient pair. Now
execute the second-case attachments with their own 2p accounts. They are attachments to
rooted material, not new outgoing plans at a noncanonical phase. Thus all original requester
units become rooted. A self child pair is connected at its axes, so touching its selected member

6     Upper groups and the matched odd core
After incoming requests and lower pairs have been handled, the available upper region has
intact pair boundaries. Its budgets are untouched. We now either root its pairs, reserve
outgoing links, or send requests using one chosen record per child text orbit.

6.1   Nonself orbits: the ordinary procedure and its mirror
Use the canonical representative group. If it has n available layers, let t start its greatest
forward rotation and H = fH (t). When H − t ≤ np, an excursion through [t, H) roots them
within np. When a baseline exists with lbase (t) ≥ H − np, a band of width np attaches them.
Mirror the chosen operation; the paired cost is at most 2np.
    In the remaining case H > t + np and the baseline, if present, lies below H − np. Choose
the ﬁrst k with #{i : li (t) ≥ H − kp} ≤ k, counting the whole representative group. It stops
by n. The preceding failed threshold gives the bottom of the top k layers last end at least
H − (k − 1)p; for k = 1 this is immediate. Translation to (t − p, t] gives the wide band ≤ kp at
every start. The ordinary k + 1 rule forces an unblocked occurrence of w = A[t : H − kp) at a
diﬀerent full-text alignment, and hence a record R = [a, b) with a ≤ t, b ≥ H − kp, |R| < p + q.
Only canonical-group layers can carry the identical alignment.

reserve its K budget for the collective link. Otherwise use the individual trichotomy on all
upper layers. If the collective link is made, use the trichotomy on the layers below it. In
either case their ﬁrst ends at a are at most b: when the test fails rH ≤ rL + K < b, and
below a successful block li (t) < H − K ≤ b. Mirror every root, link, and request with its
paired layer budget. This is precisely the ordinary procedure of [1, Section 5], with paired
accounting. A collective block and its mirror form a unit of budget 2kp; band joining each
component costs at most 2(k − 1)p, leaving 2p. An individual pair has the same saving with
k = 1. The canonical orientation does not change with host routing.

7     Opening quotient cycles symmetrically
Make one node for each outgoing unit. Its target pair belongs to another unit or is rooted, so
every nonsink node has one outgoing arc. A nonself unit has two ports, its representative block
and its reﬂected block; each is internally connectable. A self unit is internally connected once
its core is joined, so its conceptual ports coincide. A planned link pair joins corresponding
source and target ports, possibly exchanging them. A quotient path therefore lifts to paths
reaching endpoint ports. A twisted quotient cycle need not lift to two disjoint cycles, and we
do not assume that it does.

For a quotient selﬂoop, source and target have equal period p and diﬀerent aligned text, even

8    Half-Euler extraction and polynomial bit complexity
Lemma 8.1 (Half-Euler walk). Let a ﬁnite nonempty balanced directed multigraph have a
reversing involution on vertices and edge copies, no ﬁxed edge, connected used support, and
a ﬁxed root in that used support. If every ﬁxed vertex has even outdegree, there is a directed
closed walk from the root using exactly one edge from each involution pair. It can be constructed
in polynomial time.
Proof. Quotient vertices and edges by the involution, retaining both incidences of each edge,
including loops. At a nonﬁxed vertex orbit choose a representative v. Label an incidence
positive if its compatible lift is outgoing at v, and negative if incoming there. The numbers
of positive and negative incidences equal outdeg(v) and indeg(v), so they agree. At a ﬁxed
vertex the quotient degree equals its directed outdegree and is even.
     Pair opposite signs at each nonﬁxed orbit and pair arbitrary incidences at ﬁxed vertices.
The resulting transition system decomposes the quotient edges into closed alternating trails.
These lift consistently: at a nonﬁxed orbit an arrival through a positive incidence puts the
lift at the mate of v, where departure requires a negative incidence; the other case is reversed.
At a ﬁxed vertex either state is available. The ﬁnal/initial paired incidences give closure. If
several trails remain, connectedness supplies two sharing a vertex. Cross-switch two incidence
pairs there, aligning opposite signs at a nonﬁxed orbit, to merge the trails. This preserves all
local conditions. Repeating produces a quotient Euler trail, which lifts from the ﬁxed root
and closes there, selecting one copy from each orbit.
     This is the signed-incidence Euler construction underlying the antecedents in Section 1.2,
with ﬁxed vertices handled explicitly. The general lemma allows quotient loops. Our hierar-
chical graph has none, because an edge changes word length and ρ preserves it.

Proof of Theorem 1.1. The empty cases were handled before the construction. Otherwise
apply Lemma 8.1 to D from Theorem 7.2, rooted at ε. Since D has U up and U down edges
and τ exchanges them, it has U edge orbits. The selected closed walk has U edges; half are
up, since word length returns to zero. It visits a representative of every used vertex orbit, and
thus one orientation of every required word. Append the letter of each up step to the output.
At every moment the current vertex is a suﬃx of the output so far: up appends a letter to
both, and down deletes the vertex’s ﬁrst letter. This is the suﬃx invariant of [1, Section 7].
Therefore the word is feasible and

Evaluate blocking by enumerating every bracket word and position. Self-reversal is tested

A.1    What the support does not establish
The frozen package does not formalize words, reverse complements, blocking, hierarchical
graphs, sorted staircase geometry, spliced hosts, quotient cycles, half-Euler extraction, or
polynomial bit complexity. The unblocked and nonﬁxed-bracket arithmetic (k + 1) + k =
2k + 1 is not part of its frozen count support. Removing pairs, re-indexing the sorted list,
and pairing the remainder is not formalized as a composed layer operation. In the removal
statements, “absent pairs” means absent from the phase; both distinct elements must still
belong to the underlying set. The extra 1 < k assumption in odd_core_from_previous_
failure is harmless but unnecessary; the separate ﬁrst-threshold theorem includes k = 1.
Most declarations are elementary ﬁnite or scalar lemmas. None should be described as a
machine proof of the geometric construction.

not formal hypothesis-sensitivity tests of those theorems. The independent review added ﬁfty-
nine declarations that supply the sensitivity evidence and positive controls. We attribute that
evidence to the reviewer, not to the original seven controls.
