# Local null-set propagation, conditional on a smooth nonnegative flow

This author derivation is an interface lemma, not the full uniformization target.

Let E be a smooth finite-rank bundle over a possibly noncompact manifold, with a fixed bundle metric, and D(t) a smooth metric connection on an open time interval I. Let P be a smooth frame bundle (or an open set of nonzero vector tuples), and let u>=0 be smooth on P×I. Suppose on every relatively compact coordinate region the horizontal parabolic inequality of Brendle–Schoen Proposition 2 holds, with a finite constant depending on that region. Then at each t in I, {u(·,t)=0} is invariant under D(t)-parallel transport along every piecewise smooth finite path.

Proof. Work in one relatively compact bundle-coordinate chart and a compact time interval interior to I. Choose a smooth local g(t)-orthonormal frame X_i. In coordinates on P×I the horizontal Laplacian is sum_i Xtilde_i² minus a smooth first-order field. The time derivative is also a first-order field. Coordinate differentiation of Xtilde_i adds only bounded first-order coefficients on the smaller chart. The vertical infimum of the Hessian is bounded below by a constant multiple of min(0,inf over all coordinate-unit directions D²u), up to a bounded multiple of |Du| if covariant Hessians were used. Thus the hypothesized inequality gives exactly the local inequality of Brendle–Schoen Proposition 1, with horizontal fields Xtilde_i having zero time component and one finite enlarged constant. All coefficients are bounded only on this smaller chart; no assertion is made outside it.

Multiply the desired horizontal field by a compactly supported cutoff equal to one on a still smaller chart. Proposition 1 now preserves its zero set for the portion of its flow in that smaller chart. A parallel lift of a fixed finite path is a continuous path in P: the metric connection preserves frame lengths, and its ordinary differential equation exists over the entire compact parameter interval. Its image, together with the fixed time, is compact. Cover it by finitely many such smaller charts and subdivide the path accordingly. Iteration gives forward preservation. Apply the same argument to the reversed path to get equality. Piecewise smooth paths follow by concatenation. QED.

Primary reading: Brendle–Schoen, arXiv:0705.3963v1, extracted lines1–240, includes all of Propositions1–2 and their proofs. Proposition1 is already local on an arbitrary open subset of Euclidean space. We add only the explicit compact-path localization for a noncompact base. This is standard maximum-principle localization and earns no standalone substantial claim.

Application boundary. Cao–Chen–Zhu's full Theorem2.8 proof, read at extracted lines689–1047, gives the needed reaction inequality for the bisectional-curvature function, with a coefficient controlled by a curvature bound. On compact spacetime subsets of a smooth flow that coefficient is finite. Thus once a smooth flow with nonnegative bisectional curvature is actually constructed, global unboundedness does not by itself obstruct null-set parallel propagation. This does not establish existence, preservation, completeness, a common de Rham splitting through time, or strict positivity of each irreducible factor. Those dependencies remain separate. In particular it would be circular to use this lemma to assert curvature nonnegativity: u>=0 is a hypothesis.

## All-pair versus orthonormal-pair convention

The survey defines P as all ordered vector pairs, not just orthonormal two-frames (extracted lines750–783). Its symbols ei,ej in the reaction estimate are two fixed input vectors: the separately summed Ep basis diagonalizes the Hermitian form H_X(Z,W)=R(X,bar X,Z,bar W). The perturbation (X+εEq,Y+εΣCpEp) is unconstrained in the pair fiber. Neither orthogonality nor linear independence of X,Y enters this diagonalization or the vertical Hessian calculation. Thus the estimate also applies to collinear pairs, in particular (X,X). For clarity, the bounded coefficient is taken on compact sets of nonzero pairs with lengths bounded above and away from zero; no uniform bound over unbounded fibers is asserted. The subsequent two-plane rotation argument uses an orthonormal pair only after the stabilizer averaging has reduced to that case. This distinguishes the survey's full-pair interface from Gu's orthonormal-pair presentation.
