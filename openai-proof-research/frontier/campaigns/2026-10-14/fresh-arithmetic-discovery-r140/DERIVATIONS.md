## 3. Quantitative filling from the complete source data

This is a fully carried-out consequence of the source's two-summand proof, retained to make the best available degree estimate explicit. It is not a claim of a new substantial theorem.

Let P be a fixed full-dimensional compact convex polytope in R^n, n>=2, and F_k be additive finite supports inside kP. Suppose, for k>=k0,

    b_k := #(kP intersect Z^n) − #F_k <= B k^(n−1),  B>=0.       (3.1)

For the source P_*, this follows from its exact h0 cardinality: the Hilbert polynomial for ample L has leading term vol(P_*) k^n and error bounded by a constant times k^(n−1). A fixed real polytope, rational or irrational, has lattice discrepancy bounded by a constant times k^(n−1). Indeed tile by unit cubes; only cubes meeting a fixed-width neighborhood of the finitely many dilated facets contribute to the difference between volume and count. The total volume of this neighborhood is bounded by a constant times k^(n−1). The constant B and k0 depend on P_* and the Hilbert data; this is not an effective presentation of them from a bare variety oracle.

Set alpha=1−1/n and

    C=6 sqrt(n) (2B+1)^(1/n).

For k>=max(2k0+2,3), EVERY lattice point y in kP with Euclidean distance at least C k^alpha from the boundary belongs to F_k.

To prove this, put k1=floor(k/2), k2=k−k1, z=y/k and d=dist(y,boundary(kP)). The ball of radius d/k about z lies in P. Each lattice y1 in the cube centered at k1 z with coordinate half-width d/(3 sqrt(n)) gives y2=y−y1, and both y_i lie in k_i P: each displacement has Euclidean norm <=d/3<=k_i d/k. Once d>=6 sqrt(n), each coordinate interval has at least d/(3 sqrt(n)) integers. There are consequently at least

    (d/(3 sqrt(n)))^n

candidate decompositions. At most b_k1 choices have the first summand missing and at most b_k2 have the second missing, since y1 -> y−y1 is injective. Their total is at most 2B k^(n−1). Our C makes the number of candidates strictly larger. One decomposition survives and additivity gives y in F_k. All constants are uniform over y; no fixed compact interior subset is being selected after y.

The proof is exactly the source counting mechanism with its Hilbert-polynomial remainder retained. No new geometric transfer has been introduced.

## 4. Exact polynomial-cardinality countermodel and exact jet loss

The next elementary construction diagnoses what the abstract support hypotheses omit. It is NOT a counterexample to any stipulated source theorem and is not asserted to be a transformed source section ring. It strengthens the general non-finite-generation warning with an exact count and exact jet calculation.

In N^2 write s=a+b and define

    R(a,b)=s(s+1)/2+a.

R enumerates lattice points bijectively by increasing total degree and then increasing a: degree s occupies the consecutive integers T_s,...,T_(s+1)−1, T_s=s(s+1)/2. For k>=1 put

    G_k={(a,b) in N^2 : a+b<=k, R(a,b)>=k}.                   (4.1)

For x,y of total degrees s,t respectively,

    R(x+y)−R(x)−R(y)=st>=0.                                  (4.2)

Consequently G_k+G_l is contained in G_(k+l). The first k ranks are all inside the degree-k triangle, since R(a,b)>=a+b for a+b>=1. Precisely k points are omitted, and

    #G_k=binom(k+2,2)−k=(k^2+k+2)/2                          (4.3)

for EVERY k>=1. The family has full density in Delta(1,1), defect exactly k, and an exact polynomial cardinality, not merely a leading asymptotic. No geometric realization of this polynomial is needed or used.

For k>=36, put q=floor(sqrt(k)/3). Then R(q,q)=2q^2+2q<k, so (q,q) is omitted. Its distance to the triangle boundary is

    min(q,(k−2q)/sqrt(2))=q,

which grows on the order of sqrt(k). Thus even the exact polynomial count plus gradedness cannot improve the dimension-two conclusion of section3 to a constant-width filling statement. This is a limitation of these abstract data, not a failure of any geometric jet map.

There is also an EXACT jet calculation, valid over every field at every torus point. Let

    t_k = min{t>=0 : (t+1)(t+2)/2−1 >= k}
        = ceil((sqrt(8k+9)−3)/2).

The smallest total degree represented in G_k is t_k, and (t_k,0) belongs to G_k. R is coordinatewise nondecreasing; hence

    (t_k,0)+{(a,b)>=0:a+b<=k−t_k} subset G_k.

The corresponding polynomial space contains x^t_k times every polynomial of total degree <=k−t_k. Since x is a unit on the torus, this subspace separates all ordinary jets through order k−t_k at ANY torus point.

For the reverse bound, restrict to the smooth diagonal one-parameter subtorus through a torus point (u,v): lambda -> (lambda u,lambda v). Every supported monomial restricts to a scalar multiple of lambda^s with t_k<=s<=k. Thus the restriction space has dimension at most k−t_k+1. A surjection onto ambient jets through order m would, after quotienting by the ideal of this smooth curve, surject onto its m-jets, of dimension m+1. This is impossible when m>k−t_k. We therefore obtain the exact identity

    largest jet order separated by M(G_k) at a torus point = k−t_k. (4.4)

The jet deficit is asymptotic to sqrt(2k). Neither ordinary derivatives nor characteristic-zero Vandermonde inversion enters this argument.

The semigroup generated by (k,G_k) has full ambient group: (1,1,0),(1,0,1),(2,1,0) occur, and their differences generate Z^3. Its closed convex cone is the full closed cone over Delta(1,1), by the full interior density. It is not finitely generated. A finite generating set would have a positive minimum of (a+b)/k, while points of G_k with a+b=t_k have that ratio tending to zero. This is exactly why Kaveh–Khovanskii's finite-generation conductor cannot be applied. Their general interior-cone theorem and Example1.8 already explain the broader phenomenon; (4.1)–(4.4) are a concrete compatible calculation, not an assertion of worldwide novelty.

## 5. Stronger geometric payment: bounded deficit in every characteristic

## 6. Finite-degree endpoints and the actual remaining distinction

This is nef and big when k>a and t<=w, because the pullback term is nef and big and the remaining term nef. The displayed class is integral even if its decomposition uses real numbers. This is the standard adjoint argument applied at the source constant; it gives an r-independent form of the additive error n+a w once (X,L) and a are fixed.
