The proposed remaining operation is this: the source's singular radial equality gives only the **length** of the normal component, with no normal vector, no smooth support and no preselected finite-dimensional ambient tangent. At one regular point, two-sided scalar derivatives and contraction of log force the entire relevant logarithmic image into the parallel set of its two-plane. Collapse its transverse factor by distance from the base point. The resulting global contraction to R³ maps all boundary support to one round sphere and exactly preserves distance from the chosen pole. This last identity removes remote preimages in the local oriented-current blowup, proving degree ±1. Applying paid filling rigidity then completes the full endpoint.

The construction deliberately does not prove alignment of the original transverse directions, which would reintroduce the unavailable smooth mean-curvature vector. It first constructs a contraction and uses the equal filling mass to force the full geometry. The question for the contribution reviewer is whether this scalar-normal contraction/degree construction is a substantial new source-supported deduction or a routine robust version of the smooth equality strategy. The author submits the full unchanged theorem and evidence, with no favorable presumption. If classified technical, no lower-dimensional or quantitative side result is proposed as a substitute.
