# A joint-approximation diagnostic

This elementary calculation is prefreeze feasibility, not a nominee. It does not refute the stipulated source328 theorem or a left-amenable/common-fixed-point theorem.

Let H=l2, let X=H direct-sum-infinity R with norm max(||x||_2,|t|), and let C=B_H times [0,1]. X is reflexive, and C is nonempty, bounded, closed and convex. Define A e_j=(1−1/(j+1))e_j, and

    T(x,t)=(x,||x||_2),       S(x,t)=(Ax,1).

Both maps take C to C. The reverse triangle inequality gives

    ||T(x,t)−T(y,s)|| = ||x−y||_2
                      <= max(||x−y||_2,|t−s|).

The same inequality for S follows from ||A||=1. Thus both are nonexpansive for the given reflexive norm. Fix(T) is the norm graph; Fix(S)={(0,1)}, since every diagonal entry of A is strictly below one. T(0,1)=(0,0), so the fixed-point sets are disjoint.

Nevertheless z_j=(e_j,1) satisfies Tz_j=z_j and

    ||Sz_j−z_j|| = 1/(j+1) -> 0.

This is one sequence with vanishing defects for both maps. Its weak limit is (0,1), which is not T-fixed. In particular norm continuity alone cannot justify the proposed weak-limit inference. T is not weakly continuous: Tz_j=z_j converges weakly to (0,1), whereas T(0,1)=(0,0).

For 0<lambda<1, form U_lambda=lambda T+(1−lambda)S. Its first fixed-point equation is x=lambda x+(1−lambda)Ax, hence x=0; its second is t=1−lambda. Therefore it has the unique fixed point (0,1−lambda). At this point the T and S defects are respectively 1−lambda and lambda. No choice of lambda makes both less than 1/2. This checks the strongest literal convex-averaging shortcut, not merely one unlucky weight.

There is also a precise minimality issue. C0={0} times [0,1] is jointly invariant. Any nonempty jointly invariant convex subset of C0 contains both (0,0) and (0,1), so C0 is minimal for this property. For its points the two defects have maximum at least 1/2. The common approximate sequence in C does not persist in that minimal set. Thus passing to a minimal common convex invariant set cannot be justified by the existence of the original approximate sequence.

The semigroup generated by T,S, with multiplication composition, is not left reversible: every map in the principal right ideal T Sigma sends (0,0) to (0,0), and every map in S Sigma sends it to (0,1). The ideals are disjoint. It is also not left amenable as a discrete semigroup: a left-invariant mean would give mass one to each principal right ideal (left translation by its generator pulls its indicator back to one), impossible for two disjoint such sets. Nor is the action norm-distal: S^k(e_1,0) and S^k(0,0) approach each other. These failures are part of the diagnostic, not hypotheses to suppress.

The closest inspected primary is [Wiśnicki, 1903.12123v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.12123v2), Theorem3.1. Its dynamical-system convention includes continuity in the weak topology. Its proof first obtains an invariant measure on a minimal distal set, uses fragmentability and a positive-measure small norm ball to obtain norm compactness, and applies a center argument. Our example fails both weak continuity and norm distality. The primary cannot be silently enlarged to all norm-nonexpansive actions. Conversely its actual compatible hypotheses already supply its conclusion; no new result at that scope is proposed.

Source328 uses a single-map minimal set and compact detector, a convexified tail-semigroup idempotent/anchor, adaptive selection with thresholds assigned before weights, and a terminal resolvent sequence on which every fixed finite family of generated maps acts almost identically. The tree predicts all possible earlier weights before choosing its separating functional. These are stipulated paid operations. Replacing the single defect by the maximum of several defects requires a compatible detector and a minimality/terminal-sequence construction, not just a joint approximate sequence somewhere in C. That replacement is not established here.

The actual structural r90 working body already explores near-commuting approximations for asymptotically nonexpansive powers, graded-word error bounds and a nonlinear averaging obstruction. It leaves degree synchronization and the actual-fixed-point step unpaid. This packet does not reopen or rename that frozen target. The present example is recorded only to rule out the attempted shortcuts, with no worldwide novelty claim.
