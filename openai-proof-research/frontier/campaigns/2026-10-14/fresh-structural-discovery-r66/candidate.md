# T314C: a commutator-rank obstruction to short cyclic series

Myeongjin Kang; no affiliation. Original prose CC BY4.0. Author proof; no independent review or acceptance claimed. The exact frozen scope is original-scope.md, SHA256 676eed9172e85a2c99a7ff52dbe911cae036c104dba0bb409a2b158e4791f29e. All its quantifiers and claims are retained.

For every fixed odd prime p there are finite powerful groups of exponent p² and nilpotency class two, of arbitrarily large odd sectional rank d, whose optimal chromatic fixed-point loss at every height is at least (3d−1)/2. The spectrum statement uses the stipulated source314 theorem; the new group-theoretic step bounds arbitrary cyclic subnormal series by the minimum rank of a scalar commutator form.

## 1. Group from an alternating algebra

Let p be odd, d>=1, L a d-dimensional F_p-vector space, and beta:L×L→L alternating bilinear. Fix a basis, put R=(Z/p²Z)^d, and write bar:R→L for reduction. Let i:L→pR be the additive isomorphism sending a vector to p times any lift. Define

    x*y = x+y+i(beta(bar x,bar y)/2).                         (1)

The additive correction c(x,y) depends only on the residues, lies in pR, and satisfies
c(x,y)+c(x+y,z)=c(y,z)+c(x,y+z) by bilinearity. Corrections disappear under bar, so this identity proves associativity of (1). The identity is0 and inverse of x is−x. Induction using beta(bar x,bar x)=0 gives the mth group power x^m=mx for every positive integer m. In particular x^p=px=i(bar x). Thus the group G_beta has order p^(2d), exponent exactly p², and distinguished central subgroup Z0=pR of order p^d; its elements of order dividing p are precisely Z0.

With commutator convention xyx^(-1)y^(-1), direct expansion of (1) gives

    [x,y]=i(beta(bar x,bar y)).                              (2)

The other common convention changes only the sign. Consequently G has class at most2 and [G,G]⊆Z0=G^p. It is powerful, in the standard odd-prime sense. Its Frattini subgroup is Z0, since Phi(G)=G^p[G,G] for finite p-groups. The case beta=0 is abelian and is included. We do not assert Z(G)=Z0 for arbitrary beta.

The passage between powerful groups of type (2,…,2) and alternating algebras is prior work: de las Heras–Traustason §6. Formula(1) gives a concrete realization and independently verifies consistency; the correspondence itself is not claimed new.

## 2. Every subgroup and section has generator rank at most d

For any H≤G define U=bar H⊆L and identify H∩Z0 with W⊆L through i. These are vector subspaces. The exact sequence for restriction of bar gives

    |H|=p^(dim U+dim W).                                    (3)

The pth-power map on G is x↦i(bar x), a homomorphism into Z0. Its image on H is i(U), so the subgroup H^p generated by powers equals i(U). In particular U⊆W and

    |H/H^p|=p^(dim W).                                      (4)

The classical finite-p-group Frattini formula and Burnside basis theorem give

    d(H)=log_p |H/Phi(H)| ≤ log_p |H/H^p| = dim W ≤ d.

No claim that H/H^p is abelian is needed. Any quotient H/N needs no more generators than H. Conversely Z0 itself is elementary abelian of dimension d. Hence the sectional rank, max d(H/N) over N normal in H≤G, is exactly d. This also shows d(G)=d.

## 3. The last order-p² factor controls the entire cyclic series

Put

    rho=(1/2) min_{0!=f∈L*} rank B_f,
    B_f(u,v)=f(beta(u,v)).

Every B_f is alternating, so rho is an integer between0 and floor(d/2). We prove ell(G,1)≥d+rho.

Take any strict subnormal chain 1=H_0◁H_1◁…◁H_k=G whose factors are cyclic. Normality is only required in the next subgroup. Since G has exponent p², each factor has order p or p². Let s count order-p² factors and t count order-p factors. Multiplication of orders gives 2s+t=2d and k=2d−s.

At an order-p² step K◁H, choose g∈H generating H/K. Then H=⟨K,g⟩ and g^p∉K. If bar g belonged to U_K, choose k∈K with bar k=bar g. Then g=kz for z∈Z0, and g^p=k^p because z is central of exponent p, contradicting g^p∉K. Thus U_H=U_K+F_p bar g has dimension dim U_K+1. Formula(3) and the index p² show dim W_H=dim W_K+1 as well. At every other step U and W can only increase. This counts distinct projected directions without assuming any subgroup is graded or globally normal.

If s=0 then k=2d≥d+rho. Otherwise let K◁H be the last order-p² step. Since W_H grows from W_K by one, W_K is proper in L. Choose a nonzero f vanishing on W_K (indeed one may choose f(bar g)≠0 since g^p∉K). Because H/K is cyclic, [H,H]⊆K. By(2) these commutators also lie in Z0, and hence

    beta(U_H,U_H)⊆W_K.

Thus U_H is totally isotropic for B_f. By this last step every order-p² factor has occurred, and each has increased dim U by one, so dim U_H≥s. An alternating form of rank2a on a d-dimensional space has isotropic subspaces of dimension at most d−a: quotient by its (d−2a)-dimensional radical and use the symplectic dimension bound. Since rank B_f≥2rho,

    s≤dim U_H≤d−rho,
    k=2d−s≥d+rho.                                           (5)

This proves the bound for every cyclic subnormal chain, not merely a selected family of central or powerful chains. It is the proposed new interface. Sections1–2 and the alternating-form dimension bound are established machinery.

## 4. Constant-rank finite-field family

For any odd prime p and odd d≥3 take L=F_(p^d) as an F_p-space and

    beta(x,y)=x y^p−x^p y.

The scalar forms are exactly the classical Gow–Quinlan family. Their Lemmas1–3 and Theorem1 give rank d−1 for every nonzero functional. For completeness, here is the full finite-field calculation, credited to that construction.

The trace pairing is nondegenerate, so every functional is uniquely f_a(z)=Tr(a z), with a!=0 for a nonzero functional. Trace invariance under Frobenius gives

    B_a(x,y)=Tr(((a x)^(p^(d−1))−a x^p)y).

Its radical is therefore the solution set of (a x)^(p^(d−1))=a x^p, equivalently, after taking pth powers,

    a x=a^p x^(p²).

For x!=0 this is x^(p²−1)=a^(1−p). Let N=p^d−1. Since d is odd, gcd(p²−1,N)=p−1. The image of the power map on the cyclic group F_(p^d)^* is the subgroup of elements b satisfying b^(N/(p−1))=1. The element a^(1−p) lies in this image because its indicated power is a^(−N)=1. There are exactly p−1 nonzero solutions, and adding0 gives a radical of size p, hence dimension1. Thus every nonzero B_a has rank d−1 and rho=(d−1)/2. No restriction p∤d is imposed or needed: the field trace pairing remains nondegenerate even when Tr(1)=0.

The common radical of beta is0. For x!=0 write y=xz with z outside F_p; then beta(x,xz)=x^(p+1)(z^p−z)!=0. Hence Z(G)=Z0. The F_p-span of beta(L,L) is all L: otherwise a nonzero functional annihilating this span would give the zero form, contrary to rank d−1>0. Equation(2) gives [G,G]=Z0. Together with §1,

    Z(G)=G^p=Phi(G)=[G,G]=Z0≅(C_p)^d.

These groups have class exactly2, order p^(2d), exponent p² and sectional rank exactly d. From(5),

    ell(G_(p,d),1)≥(3d−1)/2.                               (6)

For each fixed odd p the gap over sectional rank is at least(d−1)/2 and diverges along odd d. No matching upper bound or optimality assertion is made.

Apply it to P=G_(p,d) and H=1. Equation(6) gives r_h(P,1)≥(3d−1)/2 at every h≥0. For each integer0≤t<(3d−1)/2, t<ell(P,1); the source's sharpness statement supplies a finite p-local genuine P-spectrum X with

    K(h+t)_*(Phi^1 X)=0,       K(h)_*(Phi^P X)!=0.

This is an existential finite-spectrum witness at each loss below our lower bound. It is not an explicit cell construction, a claim that r_h equals the displayed lower bound, or a new proof of source314.
