## Mathematical failures and counterexample checks

1. Entrywise Betti domination alone does not prove consecutive cancellation. Arbitrary nonnegative k-rank differences cannot just be divided by dim_k Delta. The successful route works over Delta and obtains integer cancellation coefficients before division.
2. The pure-powers cancellation theorem was already present as Mermin–Murai Theorem 9.2. C226 only transfers it through accepted filtration machinery; it is not a substantial nomination.
3. Scalar coordinate-change/Gröbner reduction fails on `(x^2+yz,y^2+xz,z^2+xy)`. Both regularity and absence of a linear square were proved by coefficient equations, without computation. Conca supplies direct earlier context.
4. The initial auxiliary-size bound from a matrix corner, rho<=2r-1, is paid but superseded by the exponential Clifford bound. No numerical rank or parameter scan was run.
5. For the broader Q226, centrality of the B_j cannot be assumed. It follows from the absence of linear syzygies among a regular quadratic sequence. The coefficient field can be noncentral in Delta, but centralizes the algebra generated by the coefficients of t. The proof is written in that algebra.
6. The diagonal Clifford argument does not require generic independent B_j. It uses the actual rank of their combination after passing to their finite coefficient field. This avoids an invalid specialization of the generic division theorem.
7. Division is necessary: a nonzero nilpotent linear matrix has zero square. Regularity is necessary for the centrality argument. Pure-square systems exclude universal lower bounds on all inputs. These checks are included in the proof file.
8. The final source inspection showed that the actual pinned all-quadratic multipliers already lie in A_0=k. Consequently G226's application is a standard scalar Clifford bound. Q226's broader lemma does not fix that contribution problem.
