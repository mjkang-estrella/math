## 1. Exact theorem and dependency boundary

For L>0 and a real 2 by 2 matrix A with tr A=0 and d=det A>0, define c_L(A) as the infimum of |F|/covol(Gamma) over bounded Lebesgue-measurable F and full-rank lattices Gamma such that

    F+(I+sA)Gamma = R² for every s in [-L,L].

**Theorem 1.** For all such A,L,

    c_L(A)=1+L²d,

and a compact nondegenerate parallelogram attains this value.

If L=0 is allowed, the value is one by an ordinary fundamental parallelogram; the explicit formulas below are for k>0.

The density lower bound is paid I201 material. The new step is an explicit pair (F,Gamma) that covers the entire height interval for arbitrary k=L sqrt(d). It uses the accepted deformed-square lemma `b:lattice` from *Finite cylinder approximation of ruled sets*. That lemma also follows from classical notched-square identities. Neither that pointwise lemma, the density argument, nor the finite-retention operation is nominated anew.

Unlike a similarity transformation of A, the construction uses different coordinate matrices for the intercept shape and the label lattice. This converts the full path to square-covering matrices without changing the elliptic eigenvalues of A or asserting that elliptic and hyperbolic differentials are similar.

## 2. The construction and its first pointwise proof

Let J=[[0,-1],[1,0]], fix k>0, put D_k=1+k², and define

    B_k = [[k,2k],[-1,0]],
    G_k = [[0,2k/D_k],[-1,2k²/D_k]],
    Q   = [-1/2,1/2]²,
    F_k = B_k Q,                 Gamma_k = G_k Z².                 (1)

Both matrices are invertible:

    det B_k=2k,                  det G_k=2k/D_k.                   (2)

Thus F_k is bounded, closed, convex, measurable and has nonempty interior, while Gamma_k is a full-rank lattice. Its vertices are the four points

    ±(k,-1)/2 ±(2k,0)/2.

**The identity that covers all heights.** The inverse of B_k is [[0,-1],[1/(2k),1/2]]. Direct multiplication gives, for every real t,

    B_k^(-1)(I+tJ)G_k
       = [[1, -2k(t+k)/D_k],[(t-k)/(2k),1]].                       (3)

For |t|<=k set

    p_t=-2k(t+k)/D_k,            r_t=(t-k)/(2k).

Then p_t,r_t<=0 and

    0 <= p_t r_t = (k²-t²)/D_k <= k²/D_k < 1.                      (4)

The accepted lemma `b:lattice` says exactly that Q+[[1,p],[r,1]]Z² covers R² whenever 0<=pr<1. Applying it to (3), then multiplying by B_k, proves

    F_k+(I+tJ)Gamma_k=R²         for every |t|<=k.                 (5)

This is one fixed shape and one fixed lattice for the whole continuum. It is not a choice of different tiles at different heights. Equations (3)-(4) include t=±k, where one off-diagonal entry is zero, and all irrational t. No limiting or almost-everywhere step is involved in (5).

From (2),

    |F_k|/covol(Gamma_k)=D_k.                                     (6)

At t=±k the transformed lattice in (3) is a shear lattice of covolume one, so Q tiles almost everywhere; the closed squares also cover their edges. At intermediate t the relative covolume is (1+t²)/D_k<1, so overlaps are permitted and expected.

### Transfer to an arbitrary elliptic A

Cayley-Hamilton gives A²=-dI. Write omega=sqrt(d). For any nonzero e the columns e,Ae/omega are independent; otherwise A would have a real eigenvalue. Let S be the matrix with those columns. Then AS=S omega J. Define

    k=L omega,
    F=S F_k,                   Gamma=S Gamma_k.

For t=omega s,

    F+(I+sA)Gamma = S[F_k+(I+tJ)Gamma_k]=R².

Both area and covolume multiply by |det S|, proving the claimed ratio and all regularity requirements for arbitrary nonnormal A.

### Lower bound over the full admissible class

For any admissible bounded measurable F and any Gamma, set Lambda=(I+LA)Gamma. Periodize 1_F over Lambda and integrate over a fundamental parallelepiped P of Lambda. Since actual coverage implies N(x)=sum_(lambda in Lambda)1_F(x-lambda)>=1 everywhere, Tonelli gives

    |F| = integral_P N(x) dx >= |P|
         = (1+L²d)covol(Gamma).

Boundedness ensures local finite multiplicity; Tonelli would also suffice without it. This lower bound does not assume convexity, a fundamental-domain structure, or attainment. Together with (1)-(6) it proves Theorem 1.

## 3. Independent pointwise validation: strips and a staircase

Here is a second proof of the covering part, without relying on a Fourier tiling identity or the accepted rounding lemma.

**Lemma 2.** Let alpha,beta in [0,1], alpha+beta=1, b<=1, and delta=alpha b+beta>0. The lattice generated by

    u=(b,beta),                  v=(-1,alpha)

meets every closed axis-aligned unit square. Equivalently, Q+Zu+Zv covers R².

**Proof.** Fix any y0. For every integer m set

    n_m=ceil(y0-m alpha),        z_m=m v+n_m(u+v).

The second coordinate of z_m belongs to [y0,y0+1), since u+v has second coordinate one. Its first coordinate is

    x_m=-m+(b-1)n_m.

The difference n_(m+1)-n_m is either 0 or -1, also for alpha=0 or 1. Hence x_(m+1)-x_m is either -1 or -b, and in particular is at least -1. Since n_m=y0-m alpha+O(1),

    x_m=-delta m+O(1).

Therefore x_m tends to +infinity as m tends to -infinity, and to -infinity as m tends to +infinity. For any x0, there is a least integer m with x_m<=x0+1. Its predecessor exceeds x0+1, and the downward jump is at most one; consequently x0<x_m<=x0+1. Thus z_m lies in (x0,x0+1] times [y0,y0+1), contained in the desired closed square. Taking that square to be x-Q proves the covering. QED.

Apply this lemma as follows. Put beta=(t+k)/(2k), alpha=1-beta, q=4k²/D_k and b=1-q beta. The matrix in (3) generates the same lattice as

    [[1-q beta,-q beta],[beta,1]],

because adding its second column to its first recovers this matrix from (3). Its first column u=(b,beta) and the difference of its second and first columns v=(-1,alpha) are another unimodular basis. Here

    b<=1,       delta=1-q alpha beta=(1+t²)/D_k>0.

Lemma 2 proves (5) again. In particular, b may be negative: the staircase can backtrack horizontally, but all downward jumps remain at most one. Dropping this observation would wrongly restrict the construction to part of the k range. The strip proof includes arbitrary real alpha and all square boundaries.

An earlier partial construction used the companion matrix [[a,-1],[1,0]] with a=2(1-k²)/D_k and initially suggested k>=1/sqrt(3). Replacing it by [[a-1,a-2],[1,1]], whose endpoint lattice is also a shear tiling, gives (3) and removes that restriction. The final formulas above supersede the partial candidate written during discovery.

## 4. A finite nonlinear consequence, including small determinants

Write K for a compact label set in R², V for a C¹ field on a neighborhood of K, and

    E(K,V,L)={(s,p+sV(p)): p in K, |s|<=L},
    F_h(s,x,y)=(s,x,hy),
    J_h(g)=(1+g_1²+h²g_2²)^(-1/2).

**Theorem 3.** Suppose L,h>0 and, on K,

    tr DV=0,                    det DV>-L^(-2).

For every epsilon>0, F_h(E(K,V,L)) has a finite cylinder cover with compact nondegenerate parallelogram perpendicular bases and total base area at most

    h integral_K [1+L²(det DV(p))_+] J_h(V(p)) dp + epsilon.       (7)

The correction in (7) improves I201's 1+2L²(det DV)_+. The determinant multiplier is optimal for the local periodic affine class by Theorem 1. This is not an optimality assertion for arbitrary finite cylinder covers.

### Uniform affine templates away from determinant zero

Fix a derivative norm bound M and a positive cutoff d_*. For elliptic A with d_*<=det A<=M², the templates in Theorem 1 may be rescaled to make covol(Gamma_A)=1. Their intercept radii and the radii of a centered fundamental parallelogram of Gamma_A are then bounded uniformly in A.

For completeness, in fixed coordinates write A=[[a,b],[c,-a]]. Since -bc=det A+a²>=d_* and |b|<=M, |c|>=d_*/M. The choice S=[e_1,Ae_1/sqrt(det A)] has uniformly bounded norm and inverse; its determinant is c/sqrt(det A), bounded away from zero. Also k ranges in the compact interval [L sqrt(d_*),LM]. The matrices B_k,G_k and their inverses vary continuously there. The area-normalizing scalar is bounded above and below. This proves the uniform template assertion without a continuous choice of eigenvectors.

If 0<det A<d_*, use I201's extended-square template of covolume one and area 1+2L²det A, split into its at most three rectangles. If det A<=0, use the accepted square template of area one. All these templates have bounded intercept and fundamental-cell radii, for fixed M,L,d_*. If Psi(A) is their total intercept area, then

    Psi(A)<=Phi(A)+L²d_*,        Phi(A)=1+L²(det A)_+.             (8)

The selected template need not depend continuously on A; the continuous upper envelope in (8) is sufficient.

### Finite retention of actual nonlinear targets

Choose a compact neighborhood K_a of K inside the domain of V, a bound M on ||DV|| there, and a common modulus omega. Put

    sigma=min(1,min_K[1+L²det DV])>0,
    C=(1+LM)/sigma.

For every frozen A=DV(p), p in K, and |s|<=L, Cayley-Hamilton implies ||(I+sA)^(-1)||<=C. Let rho be a uniform intercept radius and rho_G a uniform fundamental-cell radius for the chosen templates.

Take all closed grid squares S of side delta meeting K and choose p_S in K intersect S. Freeze

    A_S=DV(p_S),
    W_S(p)=V(p_S)+A_S(p-p_S).

For small delta,

    |V(p)-W_S(p)|<=eta_delta delta on S,
    eta_delta=sqrt(2) omega(sqrt(2)delta)->0.

Use fine scale ell=delta² and centers l in p_S+ell Gamma_(A_S), with intercepts l+ell F_(A_S) and velocities W_S(l). Their infinite affine family covers the whole transverse plane at every allowed height. Apply it to the actual point p+sV(p), for p in K intersect S, obtaining

    |(I+sA_S)(l-p)|<=rho ell+L eta_delta delta.

Retain all centers within distance

    R_delta=C(rho ell+L eta_delta delta)=o(delta)

of S. They form a finite list independent of p,s and contain a cylinder covering every actual target. Thus boundary labels and all heights are retained before any integration.

Let N_S count retained centers. The disjoint fundamental cells of the scaled lattice have area ell² and lie in the (R_delta+rho_G ell)-neighborhood of S. Therefore

    N_S ell² <= [delta+2R_delta+2rho_G ell]²=delta²(1+o(1)).

All retained velocities differ from V(p_S) by O(delta), uniformly. Projection of a cylinder gives the accepted exact area h ell²|F|J_h(W_S(l)); a linear image of a parallelogram remains a nondegenerate parallelogram in the perpendicular plane. Splitting the small-determinant template into rectangles does not change total area. Hence the patch cost is at most

    h delta²[Phi(A_S)+L²d_*]J_h(V(p_S))+o(delta²).

The errors are uniform, with constants allowed to depend on d_*. Summing over O(delta^(-2)) squares and using continuity of Phi(DV)J_h(V) gives a limsup at most

    h integral_K Phi(DV)J_h(V) + hL²d_*|K|.

Indeed, the union U_delta of these squares lies between K and its sqrt(2)delta neighborhood; |U_delta minus K| tends to zero by continuity from above, including when the boundary has positive area or |K|=0. First choose d_* small enough to make the extra term below epsilon/2 (for instance using |K|+1 in the denominator), then delta small enough for the remaining error. This proves (7). No count uniform as d_* decreases to zero is asserted.

The cutoff is necessary for this proof's uniform estimates: the optimal area-one elliptic templates may degenerate as d tends to zero. It does not restrict the theorem's allowed fields. Count and mesh sizes can depend on K,V,L,h, the negative spectral margin, the modulus of DV, epsilon and the selected cutoff.

### Specified C¹ stability

If W is also trace free on K, ||W-V||_infinity,||DW-DV||_infinity<=gamma, and L²(2M gamma+gamma²)<sigma/2, then W remains in the admitted spectral range. With H=max(1,h), the two integral bounds in (7), divided by h, differ by at most

    |K|[L²(2M gamma+gamma²)+(1+L²M²)H gamma].

This follows from |det DW-det DV|<=2M gamma+gamma² and the Lipschitz bounds for positive part and J_h. It is stability of the proved upper functional, not an assertion about optimal arbitrary-cylinder costs.

## 5. Route A before the exact construction: approximation and its precise gap

The initial construction route tried bounded common tiles at finitely many heights followed by thickening. The following elementary statement records exactly the missing uniformity; it remains useful but is not nominated.

For a fixed normalized lattice Gamma and finite H subset [-k,k], let P be a bounded set covering (I+uJ)Gamma at each u in H. Suppose P is contained in a radius-R ball centered at zero and H has covering radius eta in [-k,k]. For any t choose u with |t-u|<=eta. Given x, put z=(I+tJ)^(-1)x. At height u choose gamma with (I+uJ)(z-gamma) in P. Since ||(I+uJ)^(-1)||<=1, |z-gamma|<=R. Hence

    (I+tJ)(z-gamma) in P+B(0,eta R).

Thus P+B(0,eta R) covers all heights. If P is a union of N compact convex polygons of total perimeter T, its thickening has area at most

    |P|+eta R T+N pi(eta R)².                                    (9)

A near-optimal finite-height scheme would suffice if its area excess, eta R T and N(eta R)² all tended to zero. Merely taking a finer height net gives no such estimates. Existence theorems whose tile diameter and complexity can grow with the number of heights do not fill this gap. The explicit construction in §2 bypasses it, giving zero excess immediately; if a literal positive-error family is wanted, homothetically scaling both F_k and Gamma_k leaves (5)-(6) unchanged, so the error is identically zero.

Outer regularity also implies that any actual bounded measurable all-height cover can be replaced, with arbitrarily small extra area, by a finite union of compact rectangles that still covers the same interval. To see this, choose bounded open O containing F with |O|<|F|+epsilon. Over the compact parameter set [-k,k] times a closed fundamental parallelogram of Gamma, residuals (I+tJ)(z-gamma) lying in O give an open cover. A finite subcover has residuals a positive distance inside O after shrinking its parameter neighborhoods. Their compact union is contained in a finite union of closed rectangles lying in O. That union is an actual all-height cover. This regularization alone does not manufacture a density-optimal cover; using it as such would be circular.

## 6. Route B: Fourier, fractional covers and finite-height obstructions

This was pursued independently before the exact construction. It explains why the contemplated obstruction methods did not force a gap.

### Fourier defect, not continuum Fourier zeros

Normalize covol(Gamma)=1, let |F|=D_k+epsilon, and put Lambda_t=(I+tJ)Gamma, D_t=1+t². For xi in Lambda_t^* minus {0}, periodizing on Lambda_t and subtracting the constant one gives

    |hat(1_F)(xi)| <= |F|-D_t = epsilon+k²-t².                   (10)

Indeed the periodized surplus is nonnegative and its integral over a fundamental domain is |F|-D_t; its xi Fourier coefficient is hat(1_F)(xi). At equality only the two endpoint dual lattices force zeros. Interior heights have strictly positive slack. Analyticity of a bounded-support Fourier transform therefore does not supply the circles of zeros used in bounded Steinhaus obstructions. Theorem 1 demonstrates that no universal positive gap for this problem can be recovered by strengthening that mistaken identification.

### The fractional optimum for compact lattice paths

Let Lambda_t=M_t Z² for a continuous nonsingular matrix path on a compact parameter interval, with maximal covolume D. Its centered fundamental parallelograms P_t are contained in a common radius-rho ball. For R>rho define

    f_R = D/[pi(R-rho)²] * 1_(closed ball of radius R).

For every x,t, the cells lambda+P_t that meet B_(R-rho)(x) have their centers in B_R(x), so

    #(Lambda_t intersect B_R(x)) >= pi(R-rho)²/covol(Lambda_t).

Therefore sum_(lambda in Lambda_t) f_R(x-lambda)>=1 at every x,t, and

    integral f_R = D R²/(R-rho)² -> D.

For large R, 0<=f_R<=1. Integration at a parameter attaining the maximal covolume gives the matching lower bound D. Thus the bounded fractional covering relaxation has infimum D for any such path, independently of its geometry. This statement uses weights, not indicators. It neither proves set-cover optimality in general nor supplies an indicator-rounding method. Linear dual bounds that remain valid for all these fractional covers cannot exceed D.

### A real finite-family integrality obstruction, but not in this path

For comparison, let

    L1=Z times 2Z,    L2=2Z times Z,
    L3={(m,n) in Z²: m+n even}.

Each has covolume two. If a measurable F covers all three, look, for each x in [0,1)², at residues modulo 2Z² represented by F intersect (x+Z²). To meet both cosets for all three index-two subgroups of (Z/2Z)² requires at least three residues: any two distinct residues have difference in one of the three subgroups and fail that quotient. Thus sum_(z in Z²)1_F(x+z)>=3 and |F|>=3. The union of three distinct unit cells attains three, after closure. This is the elementary parity obstruction underlying the classical three-lattice counterexample; it is not new and it cannot be imported into our path, which has only two maximum-covolume heights. It also shows why arbitrary fractional-to-indicator rounding is false.

The 2025 two-lattice packing theorem does have a two-height covering consequence. If E tiles the denser lattice and packs the sparser one, extend E by the unrepresented points in a bounded fundamental parallelogram of the sparser lattice. The resulting bounded measurable tile of the sparser lattice contains E and covers the denser lattice. For polytopal E, closure gives the closed version without area loss. The source explicitly notes this containing-tile interpretation. It solves two heights, not a continuum; repeated extension need not preserve previous tilings. The June 2026 three-subgroup theorem concerns equal-volume lattices with discrete sum and supplies no contrary conclusion to Theorem 1.

## 7. Significance and prior-art boundary

Theorem 1 solves the exact unpaid optimization problem for all k, with attainment and a convex one-piece tile. At every k>0 outside the three paid resonances it strictly improves the previously stated minimum of 1+2k², q_4(k), q_6(k). It is not another isolated resonance. It resolves the issue that an arbitrary common endpoint tile need not cover the path by choosing a specific pair for which (3)-(4) verify the path pointwise.

The core square-covering mechanism is old and accepted: the matrix in (3) lies in `b:lattice`, and the latter is related to Kolountzakis's 1998 notched-square identity. The advance is the pair of explicit matrices B_k,G_k that put every elliptic path in that regime at the exact endpoint cost. The source's extended-square construction and its two-rectangle union do not perform this optimization over independent shape and lattice coordinates. No claim of worldwide priority is inferred from failed keyword searches. The bounded common-domain, soft multi-lattice and three-subgroup papers read here do not state the varying-covolume all-height theorem. The new-to-project exact solution is nominated with those limits.

The finite geometric consequence (7) is stronger than the paid transfer applied to I201's cost, but uses that transfer rather than counting it again. On the old hyperbolic/nilpotent regime it agrees with the accepted theorem. The angular, slope-field and radial companions' finite counts, tetrahedral savings, arbitrary-tag treatment and freezing operations remain paid. No new tetrahedral coefficient or convex-body lower bound is claimed. The rotation volume/chord example is respected: the periodic factor D_k differs from the arbitrary-axis lower factor 1+k²/3; the present theorem does not equate those admissible classes. Covering a nonconvex ruled sweep also does not cover its convex hull.
