## Attempt sequence and resolved failures

Early records were written after the initial source reading and literature check. The analytic record then developed two routes and was updated with exact proofs.

1. **Tail-only replacement.** Positivity excludes replacing a nonzero tail below 1/B while holding the prefix fixed. A carry from 1/n to 1/(n+1) creates positive mass but leaves an uncontrolled exact denominator. Gap: a global bounded short completion.
2. **Distinctness.** Initial concern: iterative Takenouchi cleanup might enlarge denominators too much. Resolved conditionally by a divisor-rich multiplier and one two-term split per input term. This proves equivalence of the polynomial-cap target with and without repetitions, up to absolute constants. It does not supply the multiset expansion and is not a nomination.
3. **Polynomializing the source rational reservoir.** Full t_i sizes were included. The same bounded number of integer prime blocks cannot work with polynomial K, by the proved divisor-output count. Independent rational t_i with nonintegral partial sums are not covered; this is the remaining supply problem.
4. **Separate reservoirs.** Choosing K_s after discovering each s does not automatically fit a grouping scheme in which X=RK and q are chosen first. No consistent size-bounded ordering of those choices was found.
5. **Modular reciprocal blocks.** The exact prime-removal identity is valid, but positivity needs a separate mass reserve. A distinct example at p=5 gives residue matching with negative real remainder. A bound on each emitted denominator does not bound the remainder lcm.
6. **Contracting the next lcm.** Union over all M<=p^{1-epsilon} still misses almost all residues with bounded h. The quantitative extension for small multiples of loglog p is proved. This is a limitation of the attempted step, not of arbitrary polynomial-cap expansions.
7. **Absorption literature.** Conlon et al. supplies a genuine method using small individual denominators and a changing lcm. Its fixed-delta count is too large for this target. Letting delta depend on the growing modulus, or following only the largest introduced prime, does not establish uniform thresholds or the full accumulated length.
