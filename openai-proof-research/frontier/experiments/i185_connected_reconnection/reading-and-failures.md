## 3. Written routes and their disposition

### Route A: use an arbitrary alternating ear and either arc

**Failure.** The endpoint matching edge is already on $Z$, so the
ear has to start and end with nonmatching edges. It has odd
length and opposite-color endpoints. Only the arc of $Z$ that
also starts and ends with nonmatching edges can be removed.
The other arc must be retained, and must contain $e_2$.

**Retained result.** Lemma 1 states and proves the exact conditions.
A degree-two continuation terminal cannot be an endpoint.
Length-one chords are included; existing cycle edges and
parallel-edge two-cycles are excluded.

### Route B: a valid splice automatically improves connectivity

**Failure.** Validity ensures a single simple alternating
$\widehat Z$ and a spanning degree-two complement, not fewer
components. The complement change is a switch on $I\cup Q$.

**Retained result.** With $L=T_I\cup R_Q$, $N=R_I\cup T_Q$,
and $S$ the retained-path pairing of $F-L$, the change is
$b-a$, where $a=\#\operatorname{comp}(S\cup L)$ and
$b=\#\operatorname{comp}(S\cup N)$. A common refinement of the
two $H_0$ decompositions retains the actual singleton paths at
the ear endpoints, as well as loops and parallel edge identities.
No substitution of a removed-edge pairing for a retained-path
pairing is made.

### Route C: different endpoint components imply a joining ear

**Failure as a local pairing inference.** The six-incidence
pattern in the analytic attempt has different old components
at the two prospective endpoints but $a=b=2$. One old component
is cut twice; joining is accompanied by fragmentation. The old
and new endpoint component traces are written out explicitly.
The arc and ear both have length three.

**Limit.** This is a formal incidence calculation, not a new
graph, an actual admissible ear, or a counterexample under the
full hypotheses. It supplies no full $H_0$, retained arc $J$,
fixed exterior, brace proof or complete fibres. No exclusion of
every allowed completion is claimed.

**Positive conditional statement.** If all edges of $L$ lie in
distinct old components, replacing $L$ by $N$ merges those
components into one. Existence of such an eligible ear is unpaid.

**Quantifier protection.** The minimum is taken over all
completions, not just an arbitrary one. The attempted splice
