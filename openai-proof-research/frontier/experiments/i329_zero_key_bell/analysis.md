# I329 — Proof and exact validation

2026-10-13. This is supporting analysis for the single main obstruction Q329 in `frontier/iterations/i329-zero-key-bell.md`. It supplies no Bell-nonlocal member of `C`.

## S329: the needed Schmidt-number lemma

**Lemma S329.** Every positive matrix `X` on `C^3 ⊗ C^N` satisfying `(T_3⊗id_N)X=X` is a finite sum of outer products of vectors of Schmidt rank at most two. For `N≤2` the assertion is immediate. The following proof handles arbitrary `N≥3`, including singular blocks.

The general bound is already known: [Huber–Lami–Lancien–Müller-Hermes, Theorem V.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.04975), also [Christandl–Müller-Hermes–Wolf, Theorem A.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.01266). Here is an independent reconstruction of only the qutrit case.

First, a positive matrix on `C^2⊗C^N` invariant under transposition of its first factor has block form

\[
Y=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&C\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=B^*.
\]

Let `C^+` be the Moore–Penrose inverse and `Π` its support projection. Positivity gives `B=BΠ=ΠB` and `A−BC^+B≥0`: test a block vector `(x,−C^+Bx)` for the latter, and vectors in `ker C` for the former. Set `D=C^{+1/2}BC^{+1/2}` on `ran C`, and diagonalize `D=Σ_α λ_α u_αu_α^*` there. With `w_α=C^{1/2}u_α`,

\[
Y=|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes(A-BC^+B)
+\sum_\alpha
(\lambda_\alpha|0\rangle+|1\rangle)
(\lambda_\alpha\langle0|+\langle1|)
\otimes w_\alpha w_\alpha^*.
\]

All coefficients and factors are positive as displayed. Thus `Y` is separable. This uses no invertibility assumption or limiting argument.

Next, let `P:M_3→M_N` be any 2-positive complex-linear map. Define

\[
A=P(E_{33}),\quad B_i=P(E_{i3}),\quad B_3=A,
\quad R(E_{ij})=B_iA^+B_j^*.
\]

`R` is CP, since its Choi blocks form a Gram matrix. For `u∈span{e_1,e_2}`, 2-positivity gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}P(uu^*)&P(ue_3^*)\\P(e_3u^*)&A\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

It follows that `B_i=B_iΠ_A` and

\[
P(uu^*)-B(u)A^+B(u)^*\succeq0,
\qquad B(u)=\sum_{i=1}^2u_iB_i.
\]

Consequently `Q=P−R` vanishes on all matrix units in row or column 3 and restricts to a positive map on the first `M_2` corner. The principal `2×N` corner of `X` is PT-invariant and hence separable by the preceding decomposition. Therefore `(Q⊗id_N)X≥0`; also `(R⊗id_N)X≥0`, since `R` is CP. Hence `(P⊗id_N)X≥0` for every 2-positive `P`.

For completeness this implies Schmidt number at most two without assuming a duality theorem. The cone generated by `vv^*` with Schmidt rank `≤2` is closed: its trace-one slice is the convex hull of a compact set in finite dimension. If `X` were outside it, separation would give a Hermitian `W`, nonnegative on every such `v`, with `tr(WX)<0`. Write `W=ΣE_ij⊗W_ij` and set `P(E_ij)=W_ji^T`. Its Choi matrix is `W^T`. Nonnegativity on Schmidt-rank-two vectors is equivalent to 2-positivity of `P`: evaluate `(id_2⊗P)(ψψ^*)` against arbitrary vectors and expand the two Schmidt terms; conversely use such expansions to test its Choi matrix. Full transpose preserves this nonnegativity. Finally, for the unnormalized `Ω_N=Σ_h e_h⊗e_h`,

\[
\langle\Omega_N,(P\otimes\mathrm{id}_N)(X)\Omega_N\rangle
=\sum_{ij}\operatorname{tr}(W_{ji}X_{ij})
=\operatorname{tr}(WX)<0,
\]

contradicting the positivity just proved. This proves S329. The proof reconstructs known machinery; it is not claimed as a new lemma in the literature.

## Q329 proof audit

The symmetrization is on the **output** of the first representing map. It is permissible because `ω^Γ_A=ω`; it would change a general represented state. Four CP checks are kept distinct:

* `L` and `L∘T_r` are hypotheses.
* `T_3∘L` is CP by PPT input/output equivalence.
* `T_3∘L∘T_r` is CP by simultaneous transposition of `L`.

Thus the averaged map and its input-transposed map are CP. S329 applies to its swapped Choi matrix, not directly to the output state. A decomposition of that Choi matrix with Schmidt rank at most two gives Kraus matrices of rank at most two by vectorization. The last application of `M` preserves PPT on Bob and leaves Alice in a two-dimensional support. The theorem's bound `b≤3` is exactly what permits the classical `2×b` PPT separability theorem. No bound on `r,s` is introduced.

The external dependency is [Horodecki–Horodecki–Horodecki, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9605038), for `2×2` and `2×3`; zero positive matrices and `1×b` terms need no theorem. We use the established theorem, not a new claim that arbitrary `2×N` PPT matrices are separable. Its underlying positive-map classification is not a new deduction in I329.

The local-filter assertion needs only product CP maps and a positive success trace. Rescaling `χ` absorbs normalization; postcomposition preserves both CP conditions. This proves membership of the filtered marginal. A purifying Eve can hold both an initial purification and additional local-environment purifications; discarding such information cannot invalidate the source's zero-key obstruction. The Bell test's settings must be chosen after any heralded, setting-independent filtering. None of the proof's representing maps or vectors is used as an extra shared physical resource.

## Exact verification of the excluded predecessor

Use the four vectors and weights from [Vértesi–Brunner, Section II](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.4502v2). The following checks reconstruct the listed matrices rather than fitting any parameters. All entries are evaluated in the real multiquadratic field generated by square roots of `2,3,7,131`, using Python `Fraction`. The representation `Σ_m q_m√m` has squarefree `m`; multiplication uses `g=gcd(m,n)` and `√m√n=g√(mn/g²)`. No floating-point arithmetic, optimization, or measurement search occurs.

The vectors are orthonormal and all four weights are positive with sum one. Both partial transposes equal the positive matrix itself. Alice's three success vectors have norm one; Bob's first two vectors are orthonormal, making their orthogonal-complement outcome positive. All remaining binary effects are identity minus a projector. These establish positivity, normalization, and finite outcome validity.

For the paper's Bell expression, the 48 deterministic strategies consist of `a_0,a_1,a_2∈{0,1}`, `b_0∈{0,1,2}`, `b_1∈{0,1}`. Their scores have multiplicities

\[
\{-2:6,\;-1:24,\;0:18\}.
\]

Thus every local behavior has score at most zero by convexity. Exact evaluation on the listed quantum effects gives the numerator and denominator in the report. The strict rational lower bound follows from

\[
\sqrt{42}>64807/10000,\quad
\sqrt{131}<114456/10000,\quad
\sqrt{5502}>74175/1000.
\]

Each inequality is checked by squaring positive rationals. Substitution gives `28249/107562500>0`. This is a certificate for the **published excluded state**, not for any member of `C`. The paper's numerical method was not run.

This standard-library replay is retained as authored validation code, not an external source body:

```python
from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import gcd
from itertools import product

class S:
    def __init__(self, x=0):
        self.d = x if isinstance(x, dict) else ({1: F(x)} if x else {})
    def __add__(self, o):
        o = o if isinstance(o, S) else S(o)
        z = self.d.copy()
        for k, v in o.d.items(): z[k] = z.get(k, F(0)) + v
        return S({k: v for k, v in z.items() if v})
    __radd__ = __add__
    def __neg__(self): return S({k: -v for k, v in self.d.items()})
    def __sub__(self, o): return self + -(o if isinstance(o, S) else S(o))
    def __mul__(self, o):
        o = o if isinstance(o, S) else S(o)
        z = {}
        for m, a in self.d.items():
            for n, b in o.d.items():
                g = gcd(m, n); k = m*n//(g*g)
                z[k] = z.get(k, F(0)) + a*b*g
        return S({k: v for k, v in z.items() if v})
    __rmul__ = __mul__
    def __eq__(self, o): return self.d == (o.d if isinstance(o, S) else S(o).d)

def identity(n): return [[S(int(i == j)) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
def mul(A, B):

             for j in range(len(B[0]))] for i in range(len(A))]

def kron(A, B): return [[x*y for x in ar for y in br] for ar in A for br in B]
def trace(A): return sum((A[i][i] for i in range(len(A))), S())
def e(i, j): return [S(int(k == 3*i+j)) for k in range(9)]
def add(*v): return [sum(x, S()) for x in zip(*v)]
def scale(c, v): return [c*x for x in v]

v = [scale(rt(2)*F(1,2), add(e(0,0), e(1,1))),
     add(scale(rt(262)*F(1,24), add(e(0,1), e(1,0))),
         scale(F(1,60), e(0,2)), scale(F(-3,10), e(2,1))),
     add(scale(rt(262)*F(1,24), add(e(0,0), scale(-1, e(1,1)))),
         scale(F(1,60), e(1,2)), scale(F(3,10), e(2,0))),

rho = [[sum((l*z[i]*z[j] for l,z in zip(lam,v)), S())
        for j in range(9)] for i in range(9)]
assert all(dot(v[i],v[j]) == int(i == j) for i,j in product(range(4),repeat=2))
assert all(l > 0 for l in lam) and sum(lam) == 1 and trace(rho) == 1
assert all(rho[3*i+j][3*k+l] == rho[3*k+j][3*i+l]
           for i,j,k,l in product(range(3),repeat=4))
assert all(rho[3*i+j][3*k+l] == rho[3*i+l][3*k+j]
           for i,j,k,l in product(range(3),repeat=4))

     [S(F(2,5)),S(),rt(21)*F(1,5)],
     [S(F(-1,5)),rt(3)*F(-1,5),rt(21)*F(1,5)]]

     [rt(2)*F(-1,2),rt(6)*F(-1,6),rt(3)*F(1,3)]]

        vals.append(-int(aa[2] == 0)-2*int(b1 == 0)-q(0,0,1)-q(1,0,0)
```

## Independent audit of the parent's block model

Write `Π_q=Σ_{i∈I_q}|i><i|`. The source formula gives `H_ij=0` for distinct `i,j` in the same context and `H_ii=P_i`. The parent's selected basis measurements therefore have both-pass probabilities `tr(P_iP_a)/(Q²D)`. Their four output types are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
p(i,a\mid q,r)&=\operatorname{tr}(P_iP_a)/(Q^2D),\\
p(i,\perp\mid q,r)&=(Q-1)\operatorname{tr}(P_i)/(Q^2D),\\
p(\perp,a\mid q,r)&=(Q-1)\operatorname{tr}(P_a)/(Q^2D),\\
p(\perp,\perp\mid q,r)&=(Q-1)^2/Q^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

All match the parent's hidden variable `(q_0,r_0,i_0,a_0)`: uniform independent contexts, then joint label law `tr(P_i0 P_a0)/D` in those contexts. Completeness normalizes that label law. Responses pass only when the requested context matches the hidden context. This also covers `Q=1` and zero projections. Success conditional on setting-dependent filtering is not a Bell certificate.

There is a stronger diagnostic for the same attempted measurement route. Let `D_A(X)=Σ_q Π_qXΠ_q` and `T_i=[C_(i,a),(i,b)]_ab=Ψ†(P_i)≥0`. Then

\[
(D_A\otimes\mathrm{id})(\rho)
=\frac1{Q^2D}\sum_i |i\rangle\langle i|\otimes T_i,
\quad\operatorname{tr}T_i=Q\operatorname{tr}P_i.
\]

This is a normalized separable matrix. For any Alice effects satisfying `D_A(E_{a|x})=E_{a|x}`, and **arbitrary** Bob POVMs, its statistics equal those on `ρ`. An explicit model has weights `w_i=tr(P_i)/(QD)`, Alice response `<i|E_{a|x}|i>`, and Bob response `tr(T_i F_{b|y})/tr T_i`; omit zero-weight indices. For tensor powers the same argument uses the product pinching and weights over label tuples, and permits arbitrary joint effects within context-tuple blocks.

For a preliminary setting-independent product filter `F⊗G`, it suffices that every pulled-back Alice effect `F†E_{a|x}F` be fixed by the input context pinching. The pass effect `F†F` is then fixed as well, since it is their sum. The accepted correlations are exactly those obtained by filtering the separable pinned state, so remain local; this includes filters that coherently combine labels **within** each context. It makes no assertion for filters erasing the distinction between different contexts. Analogous statements hold with the parties interchanged. This is a proved restriction on the proposal, not a theorem that the projection state is Bell local for all POVMs.

Finally, `C^Γ_A=C^Γ_B=C` follows directly from `H_ij=H_ji`. The pinned nonseparability criterion therefore supplies an entangled PT-invariant member of `C` in large dimensions. This audit enforces the explicit dimension bounds in Q329.
