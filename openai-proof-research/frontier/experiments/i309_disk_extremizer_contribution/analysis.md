## 2. An actual older theorem covers all of E307

The decisive primary source is Hwa-Long Gau and Pei Yuan Wu, *Inner functions of numerical contractions*, Linear Algebra and its Applications 430 (2009), 2182–2191, DOI 10.1016/j.laa.2008.11.020. The institutional journal PDF was retrieved in memory and its whole substantive argument read, including the previously truncated portions of the necessity proof on pp. 2186–2190. No new copy was saved.

Theorem 8 applies to every f in the unit ball of H∞ and every numerical contraction T without a singular unitary part, when ||f(T)x||=2 for a unit vector x. It proves f inner and f(0)=0, and identifies a reducing summand similar to S(φ), φ=zf. Its proof first uses Drury's norm refinement to force f(0)=0. Equality in the condition-two similarity chain then gives endpoint metric eigenvectors. On the cyclic subspace generated by x, it proves Cf(C)=0, identifies the minimal inner function as zf, identifies C with S(zf), determines the endpoint metric, and proves the subspace reducing by the adjoint construction. Thus the older result is not merely the assertion f(0)=0, or the existence of one zero at one eigenvalue.

For the proposed finite matrix A with σ(A) strictly inside the disk, there is no unitary summand, hence no singular unitary part. Its Taylor calculus agrees with the old H∞ calculus: on disk polynomials the calculi agree, and interior spectrum permits norm convergence under radial approximation. Its operator norm is attained. Therefore Theorem 8 applies without changing hypotheses or imposing disk-algebra boundary regularity.

Write M for the nonzero reducing subspace supplied by the theorem. M is finite-dimensional. A|M is similar to S(zf), so the model space H²⊖zf H² is finite-dimensional. An inner function with finite-dimensional model space is a finite Blaschke product. This is the model-space fact explicitly used by Gau–Wu, and also follows from their finite-dimensional Corollary 3. Hence

    f(z)=η z^r ∏_(α≠0) ((z−α)/(1−bar(α)z))^kα,
    |η|=1, r≥1.

To verify the multiplicities without silently upgrading a total-degree bound, put

    φ(z)=zf(z)=η n(z)/d(z),
    n(z)=z^(r+1)∏_(α≠0)(z−α)^kα,
    d(z)=∏_(α≠0)(1−bar(α)z)^kα.

The compressed shift S(φ) is cyclic. Its dimension is deg n, its minimal inner function is φ, and d(S(φ)) is invertible because its poles are outside the closed disk. Consequently n(S(φ))=0. Cyclicity makes the polynomial minimal degree equal to its dimension, so its monic minimal polynomial is exactly n. Equivalently one may use the usual finite-model triangular representation in Gau–Wu's Corollary 3. Similarity preserves this minimal polynomial, and a restriction to a reducing summand has minimal polynomial dividing μ_A. Thus

    n | μ_A.

Every α is therefore an eigenvalue of A, kα≤ν_α, and r+1≤ν_0. These are precisely all the E307 restrictions, including the extra zero multiplicity at zero. The poles follow from the uncancelled poles of a finite Blaschke product. The theorem is necessary; no sufficiency for arbitrary A is inferred. The old theorem actually retains more information, namely the reducing endpoint-scaled model summand.

If A^L=0, μ_A has only a zero at zero. The same divisibility forces f=ηz^r with 1≤r<L. This covers **each H∞ extremizer**, not just one selected finite interpolation lift. It also covers L given as a nilpotence bound rather than assumed minimal: the actual index only strengthens the restriction.

## 3. Supremum and the R019 consequence

For fixed nilpotent A, evaluation uses only f_0,…,f_(L−1). The Schur class is compact for coefficientwise subsequential convergence, and the limit remains Schur by local uniform convergence. Evaluation is continuous in these finitely many coefficients. Consequently a scalar supremum equal to two is attained by a Schur function. By Section 2 this is a monomial, giving

    C_scalar(A)=2 iff ||A^r||=2 for some 1≤r<L.

The scalar polynomial supremum agrees with the Schur supremum: radially contract a Schur function, approximate on the closed disk by its Taylor polynomials, normalize their suprema, then pass to the radial limit in the finite evaluation. This is routine compactness/approximation, not a new exclusion theorem. Alternatively the usual finite Schur algorithm gives an inner lift with identical jets, but arbitrary Taylor truncation alone would not preserve the disk bound.

For R019's N, I306 and the original I287 Section 10 give

    ||N||²=(71+3sqrt(105))/32<4,
    ||N²||²=25/8<4, ||N³||²=7/4<4, N⁴=0.

Thus the old theorem plus jet compactness gives C_scalar(N)<2. The accepted R019 matrix polynomial and the stipulated complete bound give C_complete(N)=2. This is a stronger logical statement than failure of every constant coefficient compression of the specified polynomial P, and I do not identify them. But the new scalar statement on this already constructed witness is an immediate application of the old all-H∞ equality classification. The LSS matrix, its disk geometry, the matrix-inner delay realization, the entangled norming lines, and the coefficient obstruction remain spent ingredients. No second finding is awarded for the corollary.

## 4. Required alternative-source comparisons

### ACV v3 and Crouzeix

I read ACV arXiv:2608.27346v3 Section 10 and all of Section 11, plus Section 9's equality and stability machinery. Proposition 10.1 supplies attainment and finite Blaschke/conformal form. Proposition 11.1 uses it to classify polynomial equality as a monomial and a reducing Crabb–Choi–Crouzeix block. Lemma 11.6 and Theorem 11.7 handle algebra-generating equality; Proposition 11.9 gives an existential zero-order/block relation m<r<3m. These are credited at their actual scope. An existential zero and a total Blaschke degree do not entail that **all** zeros are eigenvalues or bound each multiplicity. Nor does algebra generation hold for arbitrary higher-order nilpotent equality functions.

Crouzeix's 2004 finite-dimensional extremal-function theorem is credited through the explicit formulation and use in ACV Proposition 10.1 and Crouzeix's own 2016 Section 2, equation (4). I read those substantive statements and proofs/discussion. The original 2004 full proof was not obtained; no claim about an unstated operation in it is made. Its finite Blaschke conclusion is valid for the nontrivial norm-two extremum relevant here, but by itself does not give E307's support restrictions. Crouzeix 2016 Section 10 was also read: its size-three equality proof does locate a second Blaschke zero at the internal eigenvalue. Its dimension-three classification is not falsely extended to arbitrary size. Gau–Wu is the actual all-dimensional antecedent resolving that comparison.

### KMR, Berger–Stampfli, and Drury

The parent note was read as a reading lead, not adopted as a verdict. I independently read KMR arXiv:1510.08132v1 Sections 2–5, including the full partial-fraction proof, the local norm inequalities, Drury's quadratic-operator proof, and the Clark-measure discussion. I tested equality, rather than stopping at the absence of an E307 statement.

For a finite Blaschke B with B(0)=0, KMR gives the operator identity

    (I−bar(γ)B(A))^(−1)=Σ_j c_j(I−bar(ζ_j)A)^(−1),
    c_j>0, B(ζ_j)=γ.

Taking the real part yields a positive-kernel sum. If S=B(A) has the norm-two numerical-radius equality block, choose a kernel vector u_γ of

    H_S(γ)=(I−bar(γ)S)^(−1)+(I−γS*)^(−1).

Then positivity forces H_A(ζ_j)u_γ=0 for **every** preimage ζ_j, where H_A(ζ) is the corresponding original resolvent kernel. By its congruence this also forces

    (2I−bar(ζ_j)A−ζ_j A*) (I−bar(ζ_j)A)^(−1)u_γ=0.

That is a real equality restriction, stronger than merely retaining f(0)=0. It is not yet the all-zero/multiplicity divisibility conclusion: the partial-fraction denominators in KMR are circle preimages of γ, not the eigenvalues of A, and equality kernels alone do not assert μ_model|μ_A. In particular the mapping theorem already assumes f(0)=0; it cannot be cited as proving that part for arbitrary norm-two f. I do not claim to have completed all of E307 from the KMR proof **alone**. The complete older equality extraction is supplied by Gau–Wu's model-minimal-function argument, which explicitly uses Berger–Stampfli and Drury in its proof. This settles the task's ordinary-disk-equality comparison without pretending that a KMR search miss is novelty evidence.

Classical Schur interpolation and rational-inner factorization are likewise credited. They provide finite lifts or degree control, but not spectral support without an equality/minimal-function condition. Crabb's power blocks, Choi/Crouzeix examples, and matrix-inner realization theory receive no new credit.

## 5. Quantitative extension: exact scope, and a concrete routine reduction

Q310 is separately assessed; its correctness is not presumed from I308. For A^L=0, w(A)≤1, L≥2, its input is the gap δ below two of the maximum of 1 and the first L−1 power norms. Its claim is dimension-independent and concerns **all** Schur functions, not only polynomials or exact maximizers. Neither old exact rigidity nor qualitative compactness by itself gives the displayed modulus.

The closest quantitative results actually read are ACV Section 9, especially Theorems 9.7–9.8 and Corollary 9.9, and Section 10 Theorem 10.4. They control the second iterate on norming vectors, an approximate reducing block for f(A), and Fourier moments of a pushed-forward extremal measure. These are different outputs from closeness of f itself to a monomial, or a bound in terms of the original powers of A. I do not claim that citing those results directly proves Q310. Gaaya arXiv:1202.3962v1 Theorem 4.3 and its proof were also read: the nilpotent Schwarz–Pick refinement there assumes an operator-norm contraction. A numerical contraction may have norm greater than one. Rescaling or a condition-two similarity does not transfer that theorem into the asserted original power-gap estimate without additional work.

The concrete routine reduction is instead from the **actually read pinned direct proof's lower operations**. In the disk replace the Faber coefficient estimate by exact Hardy Parseval, retaining the same scalar singular pair and original positive resolvent compression. Let γ=||f(A)||≥sqrt(3), ε=4−γ²∈[0,1], and use the candidate's m,p,q,l moments, a²=Σ|m_k|². To avoid collision with the polynomial below, write p^x and p^y for the two diagonal sequences. The source's ordered tests and positive-block comparison give

    γ²≤a²≤2γ²/(γ²−2)≤6,
    Σ_(k≥1)|p^x_k|², Σ_(k≥1)|p^y_k|²≤ε,
    b=3|m_0|²+Σ_(k≥1)|l_k|²≤ε.

The first upper bound is simply 2a²≤4+4a²/γ². Subtracting the constant diagonal coefficient gives the second line. In the third, the exact cross Parseval identity is a²+b, and

    b≤2+a²(2/γ²−1)≤4−γ².

The last inequality uses a²≥γ² and the negative sign of 2/γ²−1. This is retention of the existing slack, not an invocation of an unproved quantitative exact-rigidity theorem.

For D_x,D_y the two real diagonal fields and C=M+bar(L), Parseval and positivity now imply

    ||D_x−2||₂, ||D_y−2||₂≤sqrt(2ε), ||L||₂≤sqrt(ε),
    ||D_xD_y−4||₁≤4sqrt(2ε)+2ε,
    0≤∫(D_xD_y−|C|²)≤3ε,
    |||C|²−|M|²||₁≤2sqrt(6ε)+ε.

For example the third line follows from ∫D_xD_y≤4+2ε and ||C||₂²≥a²≥4−ε. Each estimate uses only Cauchy–Schwarz, the preceding three scalar bounds, and the same original kernel positivity. Define the degree-at-most-L−1 polynomial

    q(z)=Σ_(k=0)^(L−1) bar(m_k)z^k/2.

The displayed bounds give

    ||q||₂≤sqrt(3/2),
    η=|||q|²−1||₁≤[4sqrt(2)+2sqrt(6)+6]sqrt(ε)/4
                         ≤(17/4)sqrt(ε).

The coefficient pairing gives Re⟨f,q⟩=γ/2, hence

    ||f−q||₂²≤1+γ²/[2(γ²−2)]−γ
             =ε/[2(2−ε)]+ε/(2+γ)≤ε.

No ambient dimension occurs. Nilpotence is used only to truncate the **resolvent moment polynomial**, not to assert that truncating f preserves its disk bound.

The remaining scalar-polynomial stability is elementary and explicit. If deg q≤d and ||q||₂≤2, the highest autocorrelation is c_d bar(c_0), so |c_dc_0|≤η. Delete the smaller endpoint, of modulus at most sqrt(η); when deleting c_0, divide by z after deletion. This reduces the degree by one, changes the H² norm by at most sqrt(η), and changes η to at most η+4sqrt(η)+η≤6sqrt(η) when 0<η<1. The trivial distance bound 3 handles η≥1. Degree zero has ||q−ζ||₂≤η, so the convenient base constant C_0=3 works. Thus

    dist_H²(q,{ζz^j})≤C_d η^(1/2^d),
    C_d=1+6C_(d−1)=(16·6^d−1)/5≤4·6^d.

At η=0 this is the standard polynomial-inner endpoint argument. The exponent 1/2^d is produced by d repeated square roots; it is not a new optimized stability exponent. Combining this recurrence with the two preceding q estimates yields exactly

    ||f−ζz^j||₂≤(1+17·6^(L−1)) ε^(1/2^L), 0≤j<L.

Finally evaluation of the first L coefficients has the elementary norm bound

    ||Σ_(k=0)^(L−1) c_k A^k||≤2Σ|c_k|≤2sqrt(L)||c||₂.

Using ||A^j||≤2−δ also for j=0, and 2−γ≤ε^(1/2^L), gives

    δ≤[1+2sqrt(L)(1+17·6^(L−1))] ε^(1/2^L)=D_L ε^(1/2^L).

Since 2−γ=ε/(2+γ)≥ε/4, this is Q310 with the exact proposed constants. The range γ<sqrt(3) is automatically covered because the asserted upper bound is at least 7/4>sqrt(3). This reduction addresses every stated quantitative parameter and avoids treating an existential compactness modulus as the explicit one requested.

This scope calculation is why I judge the quantitative contribution **TECHNICAL**, rather than dismissing it merely because the exact result is old. It extracts small residuals from the existing scalar disk block proof, iterates an elementary endpoint deletion, and evaluates finitely many coefficients. There is no new geometric-to-channel implication, no new equality alternative resolved beyond Gau–Wu, and no sharp rate or constants established relative to an earlier optimum. It is a useful explicit bound and may be new as a written formula; useful or explicit does not automatically meet the project's substantiality threshold. Conversely I make no assertion that some unread publication already contains these exact constants. This is a routine-reduction ground for the negative verdict, not a worldwide nonnovelty claim.
