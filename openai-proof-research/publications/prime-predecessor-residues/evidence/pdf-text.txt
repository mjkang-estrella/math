                                                Abstract
        Let p range over the primes in a ﬁxed reduced arithmetic progression. We give a proof, condi-
    tional on speciﬁed analytic results in the OpenAI mathematics repository, that Ω(p − 1) modulo
    any ﬁxed positive integer is asymptotically uniform and independent of the ranked logarithmic
    sizes of the prime factors of p − 1. The limiting factor-size distribution is PD(1). For parity, the
    joint limit also includes the squarefree indicator, with its explicit progression-dependent density.
    This implies the Moree–Hommersom parity conjecture under the same assumptions. The proof
    treats the Liouville sign by an uncentered marked correlation. For nonreal roots of unity, it
    constructs a common principal term from the full small-prime part of each integer. This term
    respects shared prime labels and can be summed through the allocation identities before its ab-
    solute value is estimated. The assumptions refer to an immutable source revision and are listed
    by statement in the paper. The draft was developed with AI assistance and has not undergone
    external human peer review or formal proof veriﬁcation.

1.1   Related work and the proof’s scope
The ordinary-integer distribution results of Pintz [11] and Granville and Shao [8] supply progression
estimates used below. They do not restrict the averaging variable to prime predecessors. Lichtman
[9] proves cancellation averaged over a growing range of shifts. Carella [6, Theorems 1.1–1.2] states
shifted-prime cancellation subject to a separate distribution hypothesis. These statements must be
distinguished from the ﬁxed-shift joint law in (1.1). We make no claim to an exhaustive priority
search.
    The factor-count residue is recovered by ﬁnite Fourier inversion. The analytic problem is to
prove cancellation for fz (n) = z Ω(n) , for each nontrivial root of unity z. For z = −1, the ordinary
mean has arbitrary logarithmic cancellation. For nonreal z, the Selberg–Delange expansion has a
nonzero principal term. Sections 4 and 5 construct and propagate that term. Section 6 derives the
joint laws from the resulting interior factor estimates.
