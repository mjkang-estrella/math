# W032: scalar substitution before N-adic extraction

Parent construction, 2026-10-08 local. **Verified complete factoring improvement:
NONE.** This changes the computation from emitting W031's coefficient grid
to evaluating one input-dependent scalar. Raw canonical digits are distinct
from subtracting known rational lower coefficients; their carries are retained.
No experiment has been run by the parent.

## 1. An actual scalar computation, without a polynomial jet

Let N=2n+1>=3 be odd and choose an integer 0<=a<N. Compute gcd(a,N).
A proper gcd is an ordinary initial factor; if a=0 skip its normalization.
Otherwise a is a unit. Let R>=1 be the highest digit requested and set

    z=N*a,  Delta=z^2-4,  M_big=N^(2R+3),  M_out=N^(2R+1).

In the quadratic algebra (Z/M_big Z)[w]/(w^2-Delta), compute

    (z+w)^N = A+B*w

by binary powering of coefficient pairs. This is O(log N) pair
multiplications, on O((R+1)log N)-bit residues. It needs no square root,
no factor of N, and no polynomial coefficient list of length N or R.
The companion-matrix identity from W031 says

    A=z*J_N(z^2)=N*a*J_N(N^2*a^2).

The integer A is divisible by N^2: J_N's constant term is N*(-4)^n,
and every other term is multiplied by N^2 before the outer N*a. Since
M_big is divisible by N^2, the canonical residue of A is divisible by
N^2 too. Define the canonical residue

    V_R(a) = (A/N^2) * (a*(-4)^n)^(-1) mod M_out,
    d_r(a) = floor(V_R(a)/N^(2r)) mod N,   1<=r<=R.

All divisions by N^2 and N^(2r) here are exact quotient/digit operations
on specified integer representatives, not modular inverses of N. The
normalizing inverse is legal because a and 4 are units. The discarded
odd base-N digits are still present while extracting later digits.
Computing at R and reducing to smaller precisions is consistent with
computing those smaller precisions directly.

The complete supplied-a computation costs soft O((R+1)*poly(log N)) bit
operations with fast arithmetic; binary-powering stages, inverses and
the growing operand widths are paid. O((R+1)log N) bits describes the
constant number of resident residues; the arithmetic routine's workspace
is additional. The proposal tests gcd(d_r(a),N), retaining gcd=N outcomes
as trivial hits, not successful splits. Returning an initial gcd(a,N) is
reported separately so it cannot be credited to the new digits.

## 2. Formal diagonal and the subtraction cost it avoids

Write the accepted W031 coefficient polynomial as

    A_j(Y)=product_(i=1..j)((2i-1)^2-Y)/(4^j*(2j+1)!)
          =sum_(h=0..j)c_(j,h)*Y^h.

When the least prime factor exceeds 2R+1, all denominators needed below
are units. The scalar satisfies

    V_R(a) = sum_(k=0..R) N^(2k)*G_k(a^2) mod N^(2R+1),
    G_k(x) = sum_(j=ceil(k/2)..k) c_(j,k-j)*x^j.

Here G_0=1 and each G_k has degree k. Terms j>R in J_N contribute
N^(2j-1) after normalization and therefore vanish at the required precision,
even without asserting that those distant raw coefficients are divisible
by N. For j<=R, the unit-denominator hypothesis justifies the displayed
rational substitutions. Every residue on the right is taken at enough
precision to include its carries.

Subtracting all lower rational terms and dividing by N^(2R) would return
G_R(a^2) modulo N. That operation must pay for generating/evaluating the
lower G_k; merely reading a base-N digit does not perform that subtraction.
W032 instead tests the canonical digits d_r, preserving the carries.
This escapes the fixed public-coefficient gcd family of W031. It does not
automatically escape a root-count or full-work obstruction of its own.

## 3. The first two digits have an explicit elementary description

Assume gcd(N,30)=1 and 0<=a<N is a unit. The first formal terms are

    G_1(a^2)=a^2/24,
    G_2(a^2)=3*a^4/640-a^2/24.

Since V_1=1+N^2*G_1 modulo N^3,

    d_1(a)=a^2/24 mod N.

It is a unit and its gcd cannot split N. Higher precision does not change
this first digit.

For the second digit let r=(-a^2 mod 24), the representative in [0,23].
The proposed exact identity is

    d_2(a) = 3*a^4/640-(a^2+r)/24 mod N.                 (1)

The second quotient is an ordinary integer; 640 is a checked unit.
To see the carry, write the canonical lift of a^2/24 modulo N^3 as
(a^2+k*N^3)/24 with 0<=k<24. Because N^2=1 modulo 24,
k*N mod24=r. Since a^2<N^2,

    floor((a^2+k*N^3)/(24*N^2)) = (k*N-r)/24.

Its reduction modulo N is -r/24. Adding this carry to G_2 yields (1).
The leading constant 1 in V does not create another carry at this digit.

Thus, in each residue class of a modulo 24, d_2 is a quartic polynomial
over any prime p|N with p>5. For fixed N=pq, the count of zeros modulo p
over all a in [0,N) is at most

    4*24*ceil(q/24) <= 4*q+96.

Indeed, each class modulo 24 has at most four roots modulo p, and a fixed
pair of residues occupies at most ceil(N/(24p))=ceil(q/24) integers.
This is an upper bound before conditioning on units. It gives no lower
success bound, and both-component zeros need not give a proper factor.
It excludes a constant-density generic gain from digit two as p grows.

For later digits, lower terms such as G_2(a^2)/N^2 can have a substantial
integer quotient as a ranges up to N. The argument above does not make
all those carries constant on a small set of residue classes. Applying
a degree-2R polynomial root bound directly to d_R would be unjustified.
Their exact carry structure, and the probability of a proper gcd per
paid computation, are the remaining questions for this scalar route.

## 4. Decisive bounded assay, not a complexity experiment

The selected diagnostic computes all digits 1..4 from a single highest-
precision power per supplied unit. Exhaust all a in [0,143) for N=143,
then use the fixed list 1,2,3,5,7,11,13,17,23,31,47,61 for N=323.
These inputs have least primes greater than 2R+1. Their factors are for
reference classification only; the candidate receives N,a,R. There is
no parameter tuning, adaptive case selection or fallback factor solver.

Preregister and freeze exact source and cases before the one permitted
run. Cap the complete assay at 60 seconds and 20,000 declared arithmetic
events; retain unstarted cases and failures if a cap is reached. Count
candidate and reference work separately, with all powered exponents,
gcds/inverses and operand widths recorded. Event counts are not bit costs.

Check: N^2 divisibility of A; V=1 modulo N^2; the exact d_1 and d_2
identities; consistency with a direct 2-by-2 matrix power on a small
fixed reference subset. Record separate counts of initial proper gcds,
unit branches, proper digit gcds, gcd=N and gcd=1 for every digit. Record
the actual a and output residues for replay. No favorable output rate
will be promoted to an asymptotic success law. In particular, digit two's
quartic roots are a control, not a new factoring speedup.

This test can falsify the implementation or the carry identity and can
show what the higher digits do on the fixed small domain. It cannot
settle whether growing R gives a useful general root density. A separate
symbolic audit should address that exact point while the main thread
continues deriving the later-digit computation.
