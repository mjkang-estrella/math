## A Integer-valued approximants with a common powered base

W013's short-denominator obstruction concerns integer coefficients. Instead
let A(h) be a rational polynomial which is integer-valued at every integer,
for example a sum of integer multiples of binomial(h,j). Let M>0 clear its
coefficients, so P=M*A is in Z[h]. Use one common M for all certificates;
otherwise a different powered base can destroy sharing. For degrees at most
d, M=d! suffices in the binomial basis. Its bit length is O(d log d).

For a certificate |A(h)-q*N/(X+h)|<=E, q>=1 integer, and a covered prime
p=X+h, define

    e=A(p-X)-q*(N/p),       D=A(1-X)-q*N.

Both are integers, including A at the negative offset 1-X. Polynomial
congruence for P gives M*(D-e)=0 modulo p-1. Therefore with beta=alpha^M,

    beta^D=beta^e (mod p).

This changes the computation: search an E-sized error interval using beta,
rather than the M*E-sized numerator error using alpha. Full collisions have
the integer recovery polynomial

    x*(P(x-X)-M*e)-M*q*N,

which is nonzero at x=0. It eliminates a denominator-sized search factor
if useful certificates and a usable common beta can be acquired cheaply.

The hidden costs are substantial. Large ord_N(alpha) does not imply large
ord_N(alpha^M). Order tests, useful gcd failures, and any repair of this loss
are paid. Growing degree makes root isolation and coefficient/exponent bit
length part of the bound; the fixed-degree W013 root routine cannot silently
be reused with a degree-independent constant. Extrapolating A to 1-X must
also use polynomially many bits. A factorial exponent is cheap to write for
polylogarithmic d, but that alone does not settle these costs.

### A rigidity test for the proposed representation

Here is a parent proof to audit, using only the classical Markov derivative
inequality. It does not assume small power-basis coefficients or a bound on
the denominator M.

Let X>0, L>=2 be integers; d>=2; L>=2*d^2. Let A be a real polynomial of
degree at most d, with A(j) integer for j=0,...,L. Suppose

    |A(j)-q*N/(X+j)|<=E       for j=0,...,L,
    8*d^4*(E/L^2 + q*N/X^3) < 1,                  (A1)

where q,N>0 and E>=0. Then A is affine.

Proof. Subtract the exact tangent line

    T(h)=q*N/X - q*N*h/X^2.

The reciprocal remainder is q*N*h^2/(X^2*(X+h)), so at all grid points
|A-T|<=U=E+q*N*L^2/X^3. Set R=A-T and S=||R||_[0,L]. Every real point
is within 1/2 of an integer grid point. Markov on [0,L] gives
||R'||<=2*d^2*S/L. Thus S<=U+d^2*S/L, hence S<=2U. Applying Markov a
second time gives ||R''||<=4*d^4*S/L^2<=8*d^4*U/L^2. Since T is affine,

    Delta^2 A(j)=integral_0^1 integral_0^1 A''(j+s+t) ds dt

has absolute value <1 for j=0,...,L-2. It is an integer, so it is zero.
The polynomial Delta^2 A has degree at most d-2 and L-1>d-2 zeros; it
vanishes identically. Thus A is affine. Its values at 0 and 1 also force
integer intercept and slope. This is a rigidity statement for a uniform
integer grid approximation, not a lower bound on factoring.

For X~sqrt(N), L~X^(3/5), q=O(X^(1/5)), E=O(X^(2/5)), and any fixed
polylogarithmic d, (A1) holds eventually. Thus this purported upgrade of
the W013 linear budget is actually affine, even allowing arbitrary rational
coefficients. This blocks that particular repair more directly than a d!
coefficient-spacing calculation. It does not cover larger q, much shorter
blocks, nonuniform/sparse domains, high degree violating (A1), or piecewise
functions. These are possible changed computations, not automatic gains.

Primary context: Coppersmith--Rivlin, SIAM J. Math. Anal. 23 (1992),
https://doi.org/10.1137/0523054, studies grid-to-interval polynomial bounds;
only its abstract was read. The proof above uses its own elementary dense-grid
argument, not the stronger theorem. Adcock--Platte--Shadrin's research paper
https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04769 is a further source for an auditor to check
the Markov normalization and nearby approximation theory. No novelty for
Markov or the grid-norm technique is claimed.

## B Pay for quotient carries through their image rather than their range

A materially different escape from A1 is an integer rounding circuit

    A(h)=floor(P(h)/M),       r(h)=P(h)-M*A(h),

where P is an integer polynomial, M>=1, and 0<=r(h)<M even for negative
P(h). Suppose |A(h)-q*N/(X+h)|<=E on a block. At a prime in the block,
e=A(p-X)-q*N/p is an integer in [-E,E], and

    D=P(1-X)-M*q*N = M*e+r(p-X) (mod p-1).        (B1)

Unlike A, this rounding circuit need not be a single low-degree polynomial.
The discarded carry is explicit rather than silently erased. Suppose a
PAID list R_i contains the entire remainder image for block i, or for a
certified retained domain containing its factor. One can consume this image
instead of scanning the full M*E range.

Take a common M, beta=alpha^M, and table beta^j, 0<=j<B, with ord_N(beta)>=B.
First test gcd(beta^j-1,N) as in W013. When these tests do not split N,
ord_p(beta)>=B for every component. Generate, for every r in R_i,

    gamma_(i,r,k)=alpha^(D_i-r+M*E_i-M*k*B),
    0<=k<ceil((2*E_i+1)/B).

The true r and e yield a component collision. For a global match j, recover
the actual candidate e'=kB+j-E_i by integer roots of

    x*(P_i(x-X_i)-r-M*e')-M*q_i*N=0.             (B2)

Its constant term is nonzero. Fixed-degree root checks followed by the same
non-global batch-product consumer in W013 handle full collisions. A correct
true collision cannot be discarded unsuccessfully because the baby index is
unique modulo its component. Incorrect r/e matches remain charged.

For fixed degree and polynomial operand widths, the conditional list cost is

    B + sum_i |R_i|*ceil((2*E_i+1)/B) + K,

up to polynomial logarithms, plus all certificate/image/base acquisition.
Equivalently it includes sum_i |R_i| and the balanced term
sqrt(sum_i |R_i|*(2*E_i+1)). This is a concrete replacement for denominator-
scaled error enumeration if the remainder image is genuinely sparse.

Cheap falsification: for prime M, a nonconstant degree-d reduction of P has
at most d preimages per value. If the retained offsets cover all M residue
classes, |R|>=ceil(M/d). Removing offsets whose X+h is zero modulo M leaves
at least ceil((M-1)/d) values. Thus ordinary fixed-degree polynomials on a
full residue block do not compress the carries by a power of M. This says
nothing about every composite M, an incomplete domain or a constant reduction.
Those cases need an actual image construction and a complete cost analysis.

Fermat compression P(h)=(X+h)^(M-1), with prime M and known unit inputs,
gives image {1}. But it has degree M-1; evaluating the resulting integer
exponent has roughly M*log(X+L) bits, and (B2) is no longer fixed degree.
A small modular arithmetic circuit for P mod M does not compute alpha^D
mod N for free: reducing D modulo M or N is not reducing it modulo ord_N(alpha).
This is a concrete failed implementation, not a general circuit lower bound.

The remaining constructive question is to generate sparse carry images and
cheap exact recovery together, for example through a deliberately composite
denominator and a restricted domain whose certification is paid. The current
note supplies the consumer and its acquisition test, not such a generator.

## C Encode the factor sum and signed difference in two explicit groups

This branch bypasses small-reciprocal-error certificates entirely. For now
assume N=p*q with distinct odd primes. It is a scoped semiprime construction,
not an all-composite reduction. Choose d with gcd(d,N)=1 and Jacobi(d,N)=-1.
One local sign is -1 and the other +1; name them so (d/p)=-1, (d/q)=+1.
No algorithm knows that naming. It must allow either sign of p-q.

Work in R=(Z/NZ)[t]/(t^2-d). Choose a with gcd(a^2-d,N)=1 and set

    z=(a+t)/(a-t).

This uses an ordinary inverse of a^2-d modulo N; a proper gcd is already
a factor. Norm(z)=1. Modulo p its order divides p+1; modulo q it divides
q-1. Consequently

    z^(N-1)=z^(p-q),

because N-1-(p-q)=(p+1)*(q-1). With an ordinary unit u modulo N,

    u^(N+1)=u^(p+q),

because N+1-(p+q)=(p-1)*(q-1). Both equalities are GLOBAL modulo N
(in the indicated groups), with publicly computable left sides.

Let S=p+q and D=p-q. Then

    S^2-D^2=4*N,
    (u,z)^p * (u,z^(-1))^q = (u^(N+1), z^(N-1)).  (C1)

The changed computation is a coupled bounded-exponent search constrained by
this quadratic relation. A correct D alone recovers S=isqrt(D^2+4N) if it
is an exact square; a correct S alone recovers D similarly. Every candidate
is verified by its integer quadratic equation and proper divisor checks.
Repeated discrete logs from small orders cannot be silently discarded.

Acquiring Jacobi -1 is expected polynomial work in this semiprime scope:
it is a nontrivial character with half the unit inputs negative; sample
integers uniformly, charge gcd/Jacobi/rejections, and stop on a factor.
The unit norm denominator must also be checked; gcd=N is a failed sample.
Group elements are pairs of residues and a group operation costs poly(log N).
This does not prove either group generator has large order.

For balanced factors, ordinary interval baby-step/giant-step in either
coordinate already costs about N^(1/4), subject to complete collision/output
handling. Two encodings alone do not square-root that cost again. A proposed
gain must share the constrained search, rather than enumerate all S or all D
or call a bounded-discrete-log oracle. Near-diagonal D is a special family.

A concrete first joint search partitions p into blocks, approximates q=N/p
linearly and rewrites (C1) as a two-dimensional short-exponent equation in
the direct product group. For an integer line q=q0+c*h+e,

    T*(g^X*hbase^q0)^(-1)=(g*hbase^c)^h * hbase^e,

where g=(u,z), hbase=(u,z^(-1)), T is the right side of (C1). This permits
an ordinary meet-in-the-middle rectangle search over h and e, with costs
depending on both side lengths; different slopes change the first base.
It eliminates a pointwise test for each (h,e) pair, but a gain across ALL
blocks would require sharing despite the changing bases and paying for the
line errors. No such sharing theorem is currently supplied. Rational slopes
require denominator-cleared group powers, with order loss paid as in A.

The decisive audit should first test these exact identities and then compare
the paired search with primary Williams p+1/Lucas and constrained discrete-log
literature. A pointer to Williams (1982) was found, but the attempted AMS PDF
fetch returned 403; no theorem from its unread text is adopted. The elementary
identities above have their displayed derivations. No novelty is asserted.
