 ON THE CONVEX HULL OF INTEGER POINTS ABOVE
               THE HYPERBOLA

       DAVID ALCÁNTARA, MÓNICA BLANCO, FRANCISCO CRIADO,
                      AND FRANCISCO SANTOS




       Abstract. We show that the polyhedron defined as the convex hull of
       the lattice points above the hyperbola {xy = n} has between Ω(n1/3 )
       and O(n1/3 log n) vertices. The same bounds apply to any hyperbola
       with rational slopes except that instead of n we have n/∆ in the lower
       bound and by max {∆, n/∆} in the upper bound, where ∆ ∈ Z>0 is the
       discriminant.
          We also give an algorithm that enumerates the vertices of these con-
       vex hulls in logarithmic time per vertex. One motivation for such an
       algorithm is the deterministic factorization of integers.

   Our complexity bounds are in the arithmetic model, or RAM model of
computation [6]. In this model we assume that comparisons, additions,
substractions, multiplications, integer divisions, and integer square roots

bit size of the input (via the parameters n and ∆) which comes from the
computation of gcd’s.

  To simplify the complexity analysis of our algorithm, in Section 3.1 we
again first reduce the case of a general hyperbola to that of the standard
hyperbola by changing the lattice, except now we assume the new lattice
Λ to be a sublattice of Z2 rather than a superlattice. We also introduce
the concept of standard basis, the computation of which for a given Λ we
consider a preprocessing.
  Once this is done, our algorithm is based on the observation that if p and
q are consecutive vertices of Hn ∩ (p + Λ) with px < qx , then the slope of
q − p is the minimum slope among all the vectors in
                 (Hn − p) ∩ Λ ∩ (x, y) ∈ R2 : x ≥ 0 ⊂ Λ.
                                 

   Hence, we devise an algorithm to compute that minimum slope for any
p ∈ Hn and any lattice Λ (Algorithm 2). This algorithm maintains a lattice
basis that is guaranteed to contain the slope we are seeking, iteratively
narrowing the cone spanned by that basis in a way that parallels the steps
taken by the Euclidean algorithm for computing a gcd. See the statement
of Theorem 3.10, and its proof in Section 3.3.
   As said at the beginning of the introduction, the algorithm of Corollary 1.4
gives, in particular, a deterministic factorization algorithm for n with run-
ning time O(n1/3 log2 n). Faster algorithms, running in time O(n1/4+ε ),
were obtained by Strassen [9], and slightly improved by Bostan, Gaudry
and Schost [5]. Yet, our algorithm is quite flexible in that it can start look-
ing for integer vertices in the hyperbola (hence for factorizations of n) at
any particular place, not necessarily at a vertex, at a cost of a logarithmic
factor in the complexity:
Proposition 1.7 (Corollary 3.13). There is an algorithm that, with input
n ∈ N and an xstart ∈ [0, ∞), finds the next vertex p = (px , py ) ∈ Hn ∩ Z2
using at most O(log2 n) arithmetic operations.
  Here by “next” we mean that
        px = min x ≥ xstart : (x, y) ∈ vert Hn ∩ Z2 for some y .

   This is useful in several ways: for example, it allows for an easy paralleliza-

algorithm and its variants is of the same order O(n1/4 ) as its time com-
plexity, while ours has space complexity in O(1) (in the arithmetic model,

the following definition:
Definition 3.1. A sublattice Λ ⊂ Z2 is called reduced if it satisfies the
following (equivalent) conditions:
    (1) π1 (Λ) = π2 (Λ) = Z, where πi : R2 → R are the coordinate projec-
        tions.
