## B. A different route: distinguish Frobenius multiplicities

Let N=pq, with distinct odd primes p=3 mod4, q=1 mod4, and q<2p.
Set n=(N-1)/2=2m+1, m=(N-3)/4, and

    H_n(X)=sum_(j=0)^n binom(n,j)^2 X^j.

The integer identities H_(2m+1)(-1)=0 and

    H_(2m+1)'(-1)=(-1)^m*(2m+1)*binom(2m,m)

give an explicit scalar separator. Lucas decomposition gives, modulo p,

    H_n(X)=H_((p-1)/2)(X)*H_((q-1)/2)(X^p).

Differentiating kills the derivative of the X^p factor. At -1 the first
factor has nonzero derivative; the second has nonzero value because its
index is even and less than p. Modulo q, the X^q factor instead has an
odd index and vanishes at -1, so the derivative is zero. Since n is a unit
modulo N, the proposed consequence is

    gcd(binomial((N-3)/2,(N-3)/4), N)=q.

The conditions label congruence classes, not necessarily the smaller factor.
This covers close semiprimes N=3 mod4; it is not an all-input theorem. For
unbalanced q, further base-p carries can make the gcd N. The exact required
condition is p not dividing binomial((q-1)/2,(q-1)/4).

This replaces a long list of collision tests by one specified integer
coefficient, with a direct proposed correctness proof. It does NOT make
that coefficient cheap. Its integer has Theta(N) bits; direct construction
is expensive. Modular numerator/denominator cancellation cannot invert
nonunits modulo N, and already encounters factors of N along the way.
A recurrence that detects its first nonunit denominator reduces to a paid
small-factor search; its batched product comparator already exists.

The precise research question is whether this particular index/modulus
coupling admits a fast residue computation or compressed cancellation whose
cost does not presuppose factoring. No such algorithm is supplied. The
cheapest test is a fixed certificate of the derivative, Lucas and gcd
identities, with unbalanced/full-gcd and N=1 mod4 negative controls. Primary
prior-art and actual binomial-mod-composite algorithms require review.

This is distinct from the W021 cyclic folding selector: it preserves a
specific local multiplicity instead of summing aliased coefficient positions.
It is also a concrete arithmetic barrier to investigate, not a claim that
the binomial residue is a free oracle.
