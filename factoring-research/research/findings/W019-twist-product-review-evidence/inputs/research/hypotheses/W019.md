## A. An N-only cover of weighted sums, with an explicit ceiling cost

This is the leading direction. W016 supplied progressions but did not generate
them from N. Instead generate progressions for S=u*p+v*q, where N=p*q, using a
public auxiliary prime m. The factors are used only in the coverage proof.

First consider a dyadic target p in [X,2X], q=N/p, gcd(m,N)=1, and integers
1<=U<m, V=ceil(m/U)<m. Sort the U+1 residues j*(p/q) mod m, 0<=j<=U.
Two adjacent residues have difference at most (m-1)/U. Subtract their indices
and change both signs if necessary. This proves existence of

    1<=u<=U,  1<=|v|<=V,  u*p-v*q = 0 mod m.            (A1)

Nonzero v follows because u<m and p/q is a nonzero residue. The algorithm
does NOT compute that ordering: it enumerates the public O(UV)=O(m) pairs.
Signed v is essential; positive small u,v do not cover every residue ratio.

For the covered pair, the exact identity gives

    S^2-4uvN = (u*p-v*q)^2 = 0 mod m^2.                (A2)

For every public pair compute the zero or two roots s of 4uvN mod m^2, using
roots modulo the known odd prime m and Hensel lifting. Since u,v are nonzero
mod m, roots are simple. Tonelli--Shanks with an acquired nonresidue is a
paid expected-polylog(m) procedure; m-1 need not be fully factored. An m-sized
sieve can acquire a prime in a constant-factor interval in O~(m), within the
explicit-record budget. Any gcd(m,N)>1 is checked as a factor branch.

The true S is in [-W,W] for W=ceil(2UX+VN/X). Form the index intervals

    S=s+m^2*t in [-W,W].

There are K=O(m) root/progression records and at most

    T = O(m + W/m)                                      (A3)

candidate occurrences, with all ceilings retained. When U and V are balanced
against the target factor sizes, U~sqrt(mN)/X and V~sqrt(m)*X/sqrt(N),
W=O(sqrt(mN)) and T=O(m+sqrt(N/m)). These choices require the integer boundary
guards U<m and V>=1; they hold away from a constant-factor lower endpoint
X~sqrt(N/m). Handle that endpoint with small-factor preprocessing.

### A collision consumer, rather than checking every candidate sum

Obtain alpha and beta=alpha^(m^2) with ord_N(beta)>B using W018, or obtain a
factor. Then run gcd(beta^j-1,N), 1<=j<B. A nonunit is proper by the global
order bound; absent a factor, every component order is at least B.

Because p=1 mod (p-1) and q=N mod (p-1),

    alpha^(u+vN-s) = beta^t mod p                       (A4)

at the true t. Split each index interval into blocks of at most B entries.
A block starting t0 has giant alpha^(u+vN-s)*beta^(-t0); its matching baby
beta^j corresponds to S=s+m^2*(t0+j). Negative powers are legal only after
the unit check and their binary widths are paid.

Use the W013 monic multipoint collision consumer with every (u,v,s,t0) tag.
For a full-modulus match solve exactly

    u*P^2 - S*P + v*N = 0                              (A5)

and check all integral roots as proper divisors. The polynomial is nonzero
since u>=1. If the covered p is in that block, component uniqueness makes
the full match's j the true j, so this recovery must find p. A full match
which yields no factor may then be removed with its tag processed. Repeated
tags and padded last blocks must not bypass bounds or recovery. Remaining
proper-component collisions are handled by the accepted product consumer.

The proposed total cost for a dyadic block is

    O~(m + B + sqrt(N/m)/B),                            (A6)

including K, all ceilings, square roots, exponent setup and fixed-degree
recovery; choose B~(N/m)^(1/4). The O~ hides polynomial logarithms of N,
not powers of m or a free scan through all candidate t. The order-preparation
cost O~(log(m)+sqrt(B)) and O(B) component checks fit this envelope.

Small-factor product evaluation through c*sqrt(N/m) costs O~((N/m)^(1/4)).
With m~N^(1/5), remaining p lie in O(log N) target dyadic intervals where
the U,V construction is feasible, and (A6) has scale N^(1/5). At this cutoff
an unfactored composite cannot have three prime factors with multiplicity;
perfect squares can also be removed directly. Constants and all boundaries
remain for audit. This is a proposed route to an ALREADY KNOWN exponent,
not a claimed improvement or priority result.

Known primary comparator: Harvey, [arXiv:2010.05450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05450),
and Harvey--Hittmeir, [arXiv:2105.11105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11105).
Parent read their abstracts in this wave; full algorithm/prior-art comparison
is delegated. Also inspect the later rank-3-lattice paper
[arXiv:2512.19076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19076), without assuming its claims
from metadata. N^(1/5) remains exponential in the input bit length and is not
a competitor to heuristic GNFS on general large instances.

### What could actually reduce the exponent

The m records are now the bottleneck, not factor coverage or base acquisition.
Eliminating them would require a new computation of the collision product
over a structured two-dimensional family, including full-gcd localization.
Dropping K or replacing ceil(length/B) by length/B is INVALID. If an actual
implicit evaluator reduced this stage to O~(sqrt(m)), with no lost coverage
and all other work paid at that scale, balancing with (N/m)^(1/4) would give
the formal target m=N^(1/3), cost N^(1/6). No such evaluator is supplied.
An m-sized prime sieve or per-pair root construction would already erase it.

A concrete structure to investigate: separate u,v into their two quadratic
character classes modulo m. Select a public nonresidue eta, and acquire
A_u^2=u/eta^i and B_v^2=v/eta^j modulo m^2 in their respective classes.
When C^2=N*eta^(i+j) exists, the roots of 4uvN are

    s = +/-2*C*A_u*B_v mod m^2.                        (A7)

Thus only O(U+V) roots need be acquired for this representation. This is
real shared work in the balanced U,V~sqrt(m) region; it is NOT yet a shared
collision computation. If r=2*C*A_u*B_v is an unreduced integer and
s=r-m^2*c, then alpha^s=alpha^r*beta^(-c). The carry c varies with the pair;
discarding it changes (A4). Keep this exact carry or price its computation.

The row product can be written formally using factors

    alpha^u*(alpha^N)^v - (alpha^(2*C*A_u))^(B_v)

with appropriate beta corrections and allowed t. Sparse notation does not
make multipoint evaluation cheap: exponents B_v may approach m^2, and
ordinary dense polynomial degree can be much larger than m. Fourier tricks
requiring alpha^(m^2)=1 destroy the deliberately acquired large beta order.
The decisive next task is to exploit (A7) WITHOUT expanding m pairs or
replacing the bounded root representative by an unbounded exponent interval.
This auxiliary-modulus structure differs from W008's unfiltered random
four-list sums, although both require paying the actual implicit detector.

## B. Cancel a dominant unhelpful involution across class-group samples

**Post-review correction:** the original argument below omitted the effect of
swapping a larger-order sample into the pivot. Its per-sample >=1/2 claim is
withdrawn. The completed [B review](../findings/W019-torsion-cancellation-review.md)
proves that success is equivalent to the generated subgroup being noncyclic.
A maximal-order pivot gives the half bound; retaining upgraded pivots gives
at most four expected fresh uniform samples from an arbitrary pivot. Odd
annihilator exponents preserve the relevant cyclic subgroups, so they do not
themselves bias this event; sampling/selection still can. The mechanism is
already present in Schoof's class-group factoring algorithm. No novelty or
exponent improvement is retained. The submitted text follows for provenance.

W017 extraction needs useful 2-torsion, not merely some even-order sample.
An explicit group operation can repair a skewed squaring-chain distribution.
Let G be an abelian 2-group, J a known involution, A an element of exact
order 2^s with A^(2^(s-1))=J, and B have order at most 2^s. Compute its exact
2-power order 2^t by repeated squaring. If B=1, stop. Otherwise its top
involution is Z=B^(2^(t-1)). If Z differs from J, return Z. If Z=J, replace

    B <- B * A^(-2^(s-t)).                             (B1)

Now B's order divides 2^(t-1). Iterate, so at most s cancellations occur.
With explicit square tables, even a conservative O(s^2) group-operation
bound is polynomial in log|G|. If initial B has larger order than A, swap
them first after checking its top involution. If no useful involution occurs,
the original B is in <A>; if it was outside <A>, cancellation cannot reach 1.

With uniform B in a noncyclic G, every cyclic <A> is proper and has index
at least two. Hence the procedure finds an involution outside {1,J} with
probability at least one half per fresh uniform sample, regardless of the
relative sizes of invariant factors. This is a conditional sampling theorem:
it does not make uniform class generation or odd-part annihilation free.
Individual known annihilators E_i suffice to project samples into G_2 by
raising to their odd parts, but how those E_i were selected can bias samples.
Uniformity of the resulting 2-parts must be proved for the actual generator.

Potential factoring interface for squarefree odd semiprime N: when N=1 mod4,
the fundamental discriminant -4N has the known ambiguous form represented
by (2,2,(N+1)/2). Reduced ambiguous forms have b=0, |b|=a, or a=c; the
identities ac=N, (a/2)(2c-a/2)=N, or (a-b/2)(a+b/2)=N respectively suggest
that all nontrivial ambiguous classes except this known J split N. At N=3
mod4 use discriminant -N instead; the proposed interface needs separate
genus/order/reduction proof. Do not infer these class-group claims merely
from the abstract cancellation lemma. Audit primary prior art for standard
2-group basis algorithms and existing class-group factoring extraction.

Saved work: avoid discarding an expensive class-order interval search just
because its squaring chain repeatedly lands on the same unhelpful J. Hidden
costs: annihilator acquisition, selection-biased samples, form composition,
uniform/generating coverage, and any prime-discriminant advice. This alone
does not share interval searches across different discriminants or improve
their asymptotic cost. A decisive cheap check is exact enumeration of small
C(2^s) x C(2^t) groups with the unhelpful J in the long cyclic factor, plus
a symbolic audit of the quadratic-form extraction interface.

## C. Twist products really cancel Euler factors, but magnify target height

Instead of averaging estimates in unrelated groups, let chi be the quadratic
character of a negative discriminant and psi a public quadratic twist. Away
from ramification, the paired Euler factor is

    [(1-chi(ell)/ell)(1-chi(ell)*psi(ell)/ell)]^(-1).

When psi(ell)=-1 this is (1-1/ell^2)^(-1), removing the first-order unknown
sign. When psi(ell)=1 it is (1-chi(ell)/ell)^(-2), doubling it. A full group
of T=2^r twists cancels first-order terms except at primes where every twist
is +1; their first-order coefficient is T*chi(ell)/ell.

Under an explicitly HEURISTIC independent-sign/equidistribution model, the
surviving prime density is 1/T and the variance coefficient is therefore T,
not 1/T. The normalized geometric mean has variance 1/T, but is not an
integer annihilator. The integer product of T class numbers annihilates
each constituent group and has scale N^(T/2), up to discriminant multipliers
and L-values. Even granting relative error of scale sqrt(T/B), its absolute
interval width has that large height; ordinary BSGS costs N^(T/4)B^(-1/4)
times parameter factors. For fixed T this relaxed model balances against B
at N^(T/5), worse than the T=1 route. Varying T/conductors must be charged.

The materially different attempted computation is exact local cancellation
followed by an annihilator product, not uncorrelated averaging of square
conductors. It currently appears to lose. A useful replacement would need
to extract torsion from a rational/product relation without searching an
interval at its full height. No such primitive is assumed. Decisive review:
exact Euler identities, dependence and conductor exceptions, distinction
between actual tail theorems and the variance model, and whether known norm
maps genuinely provide a smaller searchable integer annihilator.
