This is the first in a series of papers in which we study the n n -Selmer group with the aim of representing its elements as genus one normal curves C ⊂ ℙ n − 1 C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1} (when n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 ). A further application is to the study of explicit counter-examples to the Hasse Principle. In this introduction we discuss our approach to the problem and set out the goals of our work. Following some historical remarks, we will outline the contents of this first paper, and then briefly that of the remaining papers in the series. The method of descent, for explicitly determining the solutions of Diophantine Equations, has a long and distinguished history going back (at least) to Fermat. As a tool for the determination of the Mordell-Weil groups of elliptic curves over number fields, descent has been used since the very first applications of computing to number theory. Until the 1990s, the only methods which had been implemented for general elliptic curves were based on 2 2 -descent and could be applied only to elliptic curves defined over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} , though of course individual examples had been worked out over other fields. The advent of higher-level computer algebra software and the development of efficient algorithms for handling the arithmetic of more general number fields has meant that 2 2 -descent can now be carried out over general number fields (of moderate degree and discriminant, for practical reasons). We may cite both Simon’s gp program ell.gp (see [ 18 ] ), and the MAGMA package written primarily by Bruin, as examples of this which are widely used. The situation regarding so-called higher descents, meaning n n -descent for n > 2 n>2 , has until now been much more fragmentary and less satisfactory. Some 3 3 -descents (for twists of Fermat curves) and certain other descents via isogeny have been studied systematically, but these apply only to special families and not to general elliptic curves (at least, not without an extension of the ground field, which introduces further complexities and complicates implementation significantly). Higher 2 2 -power descents (also known as second and third 2 2 -descents) have been studied and implemented by Siksek [ 10 ] and Womack [ 20 ] for 4 4 -descent, and by Stamminger [ 19 ] for 8 8 -descent. In the case of 2 2 -descent, the map C → ℙ 1 C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1} is a double cover rather than an embedding, and the elements of Sel ( 2 ) ⁡ ( E / K ) \operatorname{Sel}^{(2)}(E/K) are represented as curves of the form Y 2 = g ⁡ ( X ) Y^{2}=g(X) where g g is a quartic. Our goal is to be equally explicit for all n > 2 n>2 . The methods we present are fully worked out for all odd prime n n , and have been implemented in MAGMA [ 9 ] in the case n = 3 n=3 for elliptic curves over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} . This implementation will be included in MAGMA version 2.13, to be released later this year.
