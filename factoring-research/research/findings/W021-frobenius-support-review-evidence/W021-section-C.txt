## C. A separate algebraic starting point: Frobenius support

The polynomial (1+X)^N over Z/NZ has, modulo p|N with N=p*q, support only
on exponents divisible by p: (1+X)^(pq)=(1+X^p)^q over F_p. If p<q are
primes, its first coefficient not divisible by N after the constant term
is at degree p, where gcd(binomial(N,p),N)=q. This uses divisibility of
coefficients, rather than smooth relations or power-collision density.

A concrete attempted selector is to reduce the polynomial modulo X^r-1
using binary polynomial powering in O~(r log N) ring work, then inspect
coefficient gcds. This aggregates coefficient positions cheaply, but it
also folds p-multiples onto all residue classes when gcd(p,r)=1. Thus the
first-support argument does not transfer to those aggregate coefficients.
No success probability is claimed. The cheap decisive test is a frozen
small-prime r menu and full coefficient-gcd outcomes on fixed semiprimes,
including controls for r sharing a factor with N. A theorem about the
unfolded polynomial alone cannot certify the folded selector.

Keep this direction separate from A: A is a promising supplied-product
primitive; C asks whether coefficient aggregation can expose a factor at
all. Neither currently gives a complete factoring bound.
