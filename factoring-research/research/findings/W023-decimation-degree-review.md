## 0. Verdict

* **Accepted, with repairs and a strengthening.** For every a ≥ 1,
  E_a(x)=Σ_n C(2an,an) x^n has algebraic degree exactly 2^a over C(x) and over
  Q(x). The parent's outline is correct. Three steps needed to be written
  properly: the formal-branch embedding, the lift of t↦ζt to the radical
  field, and the descent. All three are done in §2.
* **Strengthening 1 (every binary path).** The same exact degree 2^a holds
  for every residue path, not just the all-even path. The series
  G_{a,c}(x)=Σ_n C(2(an+c),an+c) x^n with 0 ≤ c < a also has degree 2^a. So
  the actual decimation path for m=(N−3)/4 has degree 2^(2^k) at depth k.
* **Strengthening 2 (characteristic p).** The degree statement also holds over
  F_p(x) for every odd prime p, with the same proof. Consequently, over Z/N for
  odd N, *every nonzero polynomial annihilator* P(x,Y) has Y-degree at least
  2^a (§4). The characteristic-zero statement alone would not have given this.
* **Scope.** The result rules out exactly one thing: carrying an *exact
  polynomial equation* (dense or sparse in Y) through the decimation. Each step
  squares the degree, so the generic resultant bound d→d² is attained at every
  step. It does **not** bound:
  * linear-differential/recurrence representations, which have size Θ(a), not 2^a;
  * truncated relations;
  * arithmetic circuits;
  * single-coefficient computation, either in general or modulo a known prime.

  Each of these is priced in §5. None of them yields a sub-baseline residue
  computation.
* **Prior art.** I found no exact statement of this degree in primary
  sources. The ingredients are classical: the root-of-unity filter, Kummer
  theory, and Beukers–Heckman finite-monodromy theory. **No novelty
  conclusion.**

## 1. Conventions and index check

* N ≡ 3 (mod 4), since p ≡ 3 and q ≡ 1 (mod 4). So m=(N−3)/4 is an integer,
  and C((N−3)/2,(N−3)/4)=C(2m,m)=[x^m]F, where F(x)=(1−4x)^(−1/2).
* LSB-first binary section: S_r(Σu_n x^n)=Σu_{2n+r} x^n. After k steps
  along the digits of m, the series is G_{2^k,c_k}, where c_k=m mod 2^k.
  The wanted value is the constant term of G_{2^k,m} once 2^k > m, which
  means k = bitlen(m) ≈ log₂N − 2.
* The all-even path gives E_a=G_{a,0} with a=2^k. Depth 0 is F, with degree
  2=2^1. Depth 1 is E_2, with degree 4, matching the parent's identity
  E_2² = 1/[2(1−16x)] + 1/[2√(1−16x)], which I checked by squaring the
  filter. The parent's statement "degree 2^(2^k) at depth k" uses this
  convention and is correct.

## 2. Proof (repaired), over any field K₀ ∈ {Q, F_p}, p odd

Let K be an algebraically closed field containing K₀. Its characteristic is 0
or an odd prime. Let a ≥ 1 and 0 ≤ c < a. Let ζ ∈ K be a primitive a-th root
of unity. It exists because char K ∤ a whenever a is a power of 2. For
general a, this proof needs char K ∤ 2a.

**Step 1 (formal branch and filter).** In Z[1/2][[t]] define
y_j(t) = (1−4ζ^j t)^{1/2} by the binomial series with constant term 1. Then
y_j² = 1−4ζ^j t, and u_j := y_j^{-1} = Σ_n C(2n,n) ζ^{jn} t^n. The filter
identity Σ_j ζ^{j(n−c)} = a·[n ≡ c mod a] holds in any ring with 1/a. It
gives, in K((t)),

    t^c · G_{a,c}(t^a) = (1/a) Σ_{j=0}^{a−1} ζ^{−jc} u_j(t).        (★)

The coefficients C(2n,n) are integers. So for K₀=F_p, (★) is the reduction
mod p of the characteristic-zero identity. The series in (★) is the actual
formal power series, not a choice of analytic branch.

**Step 2 (Kummer).** The elements ℓ_j = 1−4ζ^j t, for 0 ≤ j < a, are pairwise
non-associate primes of K[t]. Their roots 1/(4ζ^j) are distinct because
4 ≠ 0 and the ζ^j are distinct. For a nonempty J, ∏_{j∈J} ℓ_j has valuation
1 at each root in J, so it is not a square in K(t). Since char ≠ 2, Kummer
theory gives an abstract field L̂ = K(t)(√ℓ_0,…,√ℓ_{a−1}) with
[L̂:K(t)] = 2^a. Its Galois group is {σ_s : s ∈ {±1}^a}, with σ_s(√ℓ_j)=s_j√ℓ_j.
The monomials ∏_{j∈J} √ℓ_j form a K(t)-basis of L̂.

*Branch compatibility.* The map √ℓ_j ↦ y_j defines a K(t)-algebra map
L̂ → K((t)). Its image is the subfield L generated by the y_j. A ring
homomorphism out of a field is injective, so L ≅ L̂. All degree statements
can therefore be made for the actual series inside K((t)).

**Step 3 (orbit and stabilizer over K(t)).** Put
S = Σ_j ζ^{−jc} u_j = Σ_j ζ^{−jc} ℓ_j^{−1} y_j. This is a combination of
distinct basis elements y_j, and every coefficient is nonzero. So
σ_s(S) = Σ_j s_j ζ^{−jc} ℓ_j^{−1} y_j, and these 2^a values are pairwise
distinct. The stabilizer is trivial, and S has degree 2^a over K(t).
By (★), t^c G_{a,c}(t^a) = S/a. Multiplying by the unit t^{−c} ∈ K(t) does
not change the degree, so G_{a,c}(t^a) has degree 2^a over K(t).

**Step 4 (lower bound over K(x), x=t^a).** The substitution x ↦ t^a is an
injective map K((x)) → K((t)), and K(x) ⊂ K(t). Hence deg_{K(x)} G_{a,c} ≥
deg_{K(t)} G_{a,c}(t^a) = 2^a.

**Step 5 (upper bound over K(x)).** The parent's step "invariant under
t→ζt by permutation of the radicals" needs an explicit lift. Here it is.
Define τ on K((t)) by f(t) ↦ f(ζt). This is a field automorphism with fixed
field K((x)). The series (1−4ζ^j·ζt)^{1/2} has constant term 1 and squares
to ℓ_{j+1}, so it equals the unique such series y_{j+1}. Indices are taken
mod a, using ζ^a=1. Hence τ(y_j)=y_{j+1}, and τ restricts to an automorphism
of L.

Put P(Y) = ∏_s (Y − σ_s(S)/a), which lies in K(t)[Y]. Then

    τ(t^{−c} σ_s(S)) = t^{−c} Σ_j s_j ζ^{−(j+1)c} ℓ_{j+1}^{−1} y_{j+1} = t^{−c} σ_{s'}(S),

where s' is a cyclic shift of s. Thus τ permutes the 2^a roots of
P̃(Y) = ∏_s (Y − t^{−c}σ_s(S)/a), and its coefficients lie in
K(t)^{⟨τ⟩} = K(x) by Artin's theorem. P̃(G_{a,c}(t^a)) = 0 in K((t)). Since
x ↦ t^a is injective, P̃(G_{a,c}(x)) = 0 in K((x)). So the degree over K(x)
is exactly 2^a.

The group L/K(x) is Galois with group {σ_s τ^i}, of order a·2^a. This is
C₂≀C_a, the wreath product of C₂ by C_a. For every c, the displayed
computation shows τ fixes t^{−c}S/a = G_{a,c}(t^a) ∈ L. No σ_s with s ≠ (1,…,1)
composed with a power of τ fixes it, because the coefficient vector is
cyclically shifted and then signed. So its stabilizer is exactly ⟨τ⟩, of index
2^a.

**Step 6 (descent to K₀(x)).** G_{a,c} has coefficients in K₀. The
condition "Σ_{i≤d, l≤e} λ_{il} x^l Y^i annihilates G_{a,c}" is an infinite
homogeneous linear system in finitely many unknowns λ_{il}, with
coefficients in K₀. Its solution space over K is K ⊗ (its solution space
over K₀), because rank is decided by finitely many equations. Hence a degree-2^a
annihilator exists over K₀(x). The degree over K₀(x) is at least the degree
over K(x). So it is exactly 2^a.

This replaces the parent's "permutations of ζ" descent. That route also
works: σ_b: ζ ↦ ζ^b with b odd sends u_j ↦ u_{bj}, which is a permutation.
It does, however, need a Galois action on K((t)) coefficientwise, plus a separate
Q(ζ)(x) ∩ fixed-field argument. Linear algebra is shorter.

**Theorem.** For a ≥ 1, 0 ≤ c < a, and K₀ = Q or F_p with p ∤ 2a, G_{a,c} is
algebraic of degree exactly 2^a over K₀(x). At binary depth k along any digit
path, the degree is 2^(2^k). For the target path, the final depth has
2^k > m, so the degree there is at least 2^(m+1) ≈ 2^(N/4).

## 3. Minimal-polynomial denominators and height (c=0)

Let Z = a·Y and w_j = u_j² = ℓ_j^{−1}. ∏_s (Z − Σ_j s_j u_j) is invariant under
each sign flip, so it is a polynomial in Z and the w_j with integer
coefficients. The coefficient of Z^{2^a−2i} is homogeneous of degree i ≤
2^{a−1} in the w_j. Since ∏_j ℓ_j = 1 − 4^a x, multiplying by
(1−4^a x)^{2^{a−1}} gives

    R(x,Z) ∈ Z[x][Z],  Z-degree 2^a,  x-degree ≤ 2^{a−1}

after descent: the invariants of Z[ζ][t] are Z[x]. The minimal polynomial
over Q(x) is R/(content in Z[x]). Its only finite x-poles in monic form are
at x = 4^{−a}. That is the radius of convergence, so the leading coefficient
really vanishes there to positive order. I did not determine the exact order,
because content may cancel powers of (1−4^a x).

In R as constructed, the factor (1−4^a x)^{2^{a−1}} alone has top coefficient
4^{a·2^{a−1}}. That is a·2^a bits, unless content cancels it. This is an upper-bound
construction, not a proved height lower bound. In total, a dense equation has
≥ 2^a Y-coefficients. Both the degree and the coefficient count are doubly
exponential in k at depth k.

## 4. Exact consequences for Z/N (N odd)

* **Annihilator degree is a modular invariant.** Let N be odd and let P be a
  nonzero element of (Z/N)[x][Y] with P(x, G_{a,c} mod N) = 0. Pick a prime
  power p^e ∥ N with P ≢ 0 mod p^e. Write P ≡ p^s P′ with P′ ≢ 0 mod p and
  s < e. Then P′(G) ≡ 0 mod p^{e−s}, and in particular mod p. By the
  theorem over F_p, P′ mod p has Y-degree at least 2^a. Its top nonzero mod-p
  coefficient survives in p^s P′ mod p^e, so P has Y-degree at least 2^a.
  Therefore no exact algebraic-equation representation over Z/N, however its
  coefficients are stored, has Y-degree below 2^(2^k) at depth k.
* **What this does not say.**
  * Nothing about the *size* of a non-dense representation of such a P, such
    as a circuit for P.
  * Nothing about representations that are not polynomial equations.
  * Nothing about the cost of the single residue C(2m,m) mod N.

  For a known prime p, the residue is O(log m) digit operations by Lucas. That
  is exactly the excluded known-characteristic route. It shows that the degree
  obstruction is not a coefficient lower bound.

## 5. Representations the degree does not bound, priced

1. **Hypergeometric / D-finite data (size Θ(a), not 2^a).** The term ratio is
   u(n+1)/u(n) = 4^a ∏_{i odd<2a}(n+i/(2a)) / ∏_{i=1}^{a}(n+i/a). Hence

       E_a = ₐF_{a−1}(1/(2a),3/(2a),…,(2a−1)/(2a); 1/a,…,(a−1)/a; 4^a x).

   This is an order-a linear ODE and an order-1 recurrence with degree-~2a
   polynomial coefficients. For a=1 it is ₁F₀(1/2;;4x)=F. G_{a,c} is
   analogous, with parameters shifted by c/a.

   This is the concrete example that polynomial degree does not imply every
   representation is huge. It is still Θ(a)=Θ(2^k), so at the final depth it
   is Θ(m)=Θ(N). Its coefficients, cleared to integers, are
   ∏_{i≤2a}(2an+2c+i) and ∏_{i≤a}(an+c+i)². Once a ≥ p these contain
   multiples of p in *every* step. That is the W022 nonunit-denominator
   obstruction again, now in every step instead of after index p. BSGS on the
   remaining index m/a costs Õ(√(m/a)) matrix steps of degree a, so Õ(√(ma))
   ≥ Õ(√m). That is worse than the existing Õ(N^{1/4}) factorial-gcd baseline,
   and it also fails on units.
2. **Truncated relations.** Only [x^{⌊m/2^k⌋}] of the depth-k series is ever
   needed. So an equation valid modulo x^{M_k+1}, with M_k = ⌊m/2^k⌋, would
   suffice, and such low-degree Hermite–Padé relations always exist. But the
   section map needs G_{k−1} mod x^{2M_k+O(1)}. Unwinding to depth 0 needs F
   mod x^{m+1}: Θ(m) coefficients, the direct computation. Truncation is
   circular, not obstructed.
3. **Root-of-unity trace (★) itself.**
   * At depth k it lives in (Z/N)[z]/(z^{a/2}+1) with a=2^k. Each element has
     a/2 residues.
   * All a conjugates appear in the sum.
   * Its constant term is [t^c](1/a)Σ_j ζ^{−jc}u_j = [t^c]F. That is literally
     the wanted coefficient C(2c,c) with c=m once a > m.

   Cost: a > m roots of unity, a ring of rank a/2 > N/8, and series precision
   m. That is Θ(N) residues before any multiplication. Reducing the cyclotomic
   ring modulo a small modulus aliases the indices (the W021 C failure mode).
   The trace is a re-encoding, not a recursion.
4. **Frobenius / Cartier sections.** In F_p, the p-sections preserve
   bounded-degree data (Christol). Bostan–Christol–Dumas get O(log N)
   operations, with cost quasi-linear in p; this is abstract-level, via the
   W022 review. Binary sections are not p-sections, and p is unknown. This is
   the excluded known-characteristic route.
5. **Arithmetic circuits.** There is no bound in either direction. A circuit
   for G_{a,c} with poly(k) gates that also supports cheap constant-term
   extraction would be a new idea. Nothing in this section rules it out, and
   nothing proposed so far supplies it.

## 7. Disposition and next computation

* **B's bounded-degree recursion: rejected, now on an exact proof.** Degree
  2^(2^k) on every digit path. Modulo any odd N, every nonzero polynomial
  annihilator has Y-degree at least 2^(2^k).
* **B's broader goal (a single-residue recursion): open, but not supplied.**
  Every representation priced in §5 is either Θ(a)-sized with nonunit
  denominators, or circular in the needed precision. Any proposed state must
  meet three requirements:
  1. Declare its size at depth k, and keep it sub-exponential in k. Θ(2^k)
     already costs Θ(N) at the end.
  2. Compute the depth-k → depth-(k+1) transition with divisions only by 2
     and by provably unit quantities, not by integers growing with 2^k.
  3. Compute the final constant term without materializing [x^m]F by another
     route.

  A cheap decisive falsifier for any such proposal: write the state for
  a=2 and a=4 explicitly, then check whether its size or its denominator set
  grows with a. No numerical certificate is needed for the degree claim itself.
* Verified complete factoring improvement: **NONE.** This review proves a
  representation obstruction for one method class. It is not a lower bound for
  circuits, single coefficients, modular reductions, or factoring.
