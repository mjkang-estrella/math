# W022: prime-power root charts and a coefficient-multiplicity separator

2026-10-08 local. Parent constructions, pending independent review.
Verified new complete factoring improvement: **NONE**. No numerical run yet.

## A. Repair the prime-power cover by retaining valuation strata

Fix a small ODD prime ell (for example 3), M=ell^k, and gcd(N,ell)=1.
As in W019, N=pq, 1<=U<M, V=ceil(M/U)<M. The sorted-residue proof works
for this composite M too: p/q is a unit and j*p/q, 0<=j<=U, are distinct.
It supplies 1<=u<=U, 1<=|v|<=V with up-vq divisible by M.

The new issue is that u,v need not be units modulo ell. Their ell-adic
valuations are equal: if one is t<k, the congruence forces the other to
have that same valuation. Let r=ell^t and divide BOTH weights by r. The
resulting public stratum has

    M_t=M/r, U_t=floor(U/r), V_t=floor(V/r),
    1<=u'<=U_t, 1<=|v'|<=V_t, ell does not divide u'v',
    u'p-v'q = 0 mod M_t.

Enumerate every nonempty t stratum, including t=0. One contains the divided
witness, without knowing it. For an original bound |up+vq|<=W, put
W_t=ceil(W/r). Then S=u'p+v'q satisfies |S|<=W_t and

    S^2=4u'v'N mod Q_t,       Q_t=M_t^2.

The radicand is now a unit, so over the odd prime power Q_t it has exactly
zero or two simple roots. This repairs the unit-only counterexample in the
W021 continuation, rather than assuming it away.

There are O(UV/r^2) pairs in stratum t, at most two roots per pair, and at
most 1+2W_t/Q_t integer candidates per root. Since M<=UV<2M,

    total pairs = O(M),
    total canonical-root occurrences = O(M+W/M).

The sums use sum_t ell^(-2t)=O(1) and sum_t ell^(-t)=O(1). Thus the explicit
prime-power cover has the same count as the prime cover. Pair enumeration
is still expensive; this is a representation repair, not an exponent gain.

## A2. Explicit low-degree charts over a prime power

For each stratum write M_t=ell^h. Choose a=ceil(2h/5) and H=ell^a. Partition
the UNIT u',v' values into residue charts u'=u0+H*x, v'=v0+H*y, with u0,v0
unit representatives modulo H. Signed v is retained via its actual integer
y interval; negative division uses mathematical floor and ceiling. Empty
charts are absent. If H exceeds a coordinate range, that coordinate simply
has singleton charts. All chart acquisition costs are counted.

For a chart with a root r0^2=4N*u0*v0 mod Q_t, take the truncated series

    R(x,y)= r0 * sum_(i+j<=4) binom(1/2,i) binom(1/2,j)
                     * (H*x/u0)^i * (H*y/v0)^j  mod Q_t,

interpreted as a polynomial with integer coefficient representatives. All
denominators are units: u0,v0 are units and these binomial coefficients have
only powers of 2 in their denominators. Choose coefficients in [0,Q_t).
Every omitted term has total degree at least 5 and a coefficient divisible
by H^5, which is divisible by Q_t. Therefore

    R(x,y)^2 = 4N(u0+H*x)(v0+H*y) mod Q_t.

Use both R and -R. Their values give the two roots for every point in that
chart, because each remains a unit modulo ell. The algorithm never needs to
choose a bounded root independently at every pair. For fixed ell, each chart
needs only a fixed number of modular inverses, small coefficients and a
Hensel root, at polynomial logarithmic bit cost.

Let Z bound 1, |x| and |y| on a chart, C4=15, and Rmax=C4*Q_t*Z^4. Then
|R(x,y)|<=Rmax. Instead of calculating pair-dependent carries, use a common
integer t interval

    |t| <= ceil((W_t+Rmax)/Q_t).

It includes every covered S=sign*R(x,y)+Q_t*t. The candidate exponent is

    E(x,y,t)=u0+H*x + N*(v0+H*y) - sign*R(x,y) - Q_t*t.

This is an explicit polynomial of degree at most 4 in x,y, linear in t,
on rectangular integer domains. There is no modular-root or floor oracle
inside E. Extra candidates are intentional and paid. A sufficient nonzero
guard for all of them is

    U_t + W_t + 2*Rmax + Q_t < N.

Indeed every represented S' has |S'|<=W_t+2Rmax+Q_t, while |v'|>=1.
With this guard, W020 can consume a localized nonunit leaf 1-alpha^E using
the original uniform alpha, including any full-modulus collision. A finite
small-N fallback, all ranges/strata and all acquisition must still be paid.

## A3. Balanced size account and the unresolved computation

For balanced-factor ranges and the top stratum, U,V=Theta(sqrt(M)),
W=O(sqrt(MN)). The quartic charts have

    H=Theta(M^(2/5)),  charts=O(M^(4/5)),
    Z=O(1+M^(1/10)),  Rmax/Q=O(1+M^(2/5)).

Consequently their uncentered search length is
T=O(1+sqrt(N)/M^(3/2)+M^(2/5)). The enlargement is within the original
scale when M<=N^(5/19), up to fixed constants and additive terms. The extra
occurrence term is O(M^(7/5)), against O(sqrt(N/M)) from the genuine width.
The corresponding lower-stratum terms decrease geometrically (r=ell^t):
pair counts M/r^2, chart-coordinate size O(1+M^(1/10)/r^(3/5)), and extra
occurrences O(M/r^2+M^(7/5)/r^(22/5)). Thus they do not change these powers.

This is a genuine bridge to polynomial exponents, but not a fast splitter.
At M~N^(1/4), for example, charts cost up to N^(1/5), the ordinary t width
has exponent 1/8, and the carry term exponent 1/10. The explicit pair count
is still N^(1/4). Even chart acquisition already saturates the one-fifth
target here, before any collision computation. Smaller M can reduce that
acquisition term, but the remaining product costs must actually improve.

A direct application of W021's multivariate shift-block idea is too costly.
On one quartic chart, a b-by-b microblock needs variables for monomials of
total degree 0..3 of the outer shifts: 10 variables, whose individual degrees
scale as b^(j+2) for each degree-j monomial. Their rectangular box has b^40
slots; outer records have order Z^2*T/b^2 if t is enumerated. This is far
from a square-root-in-occurrences evaluator. If coefficients vary across
charts, a universal block polynomial must also encode degree-4 monomials,
making 15 variables and b^70 slots. Sharing preprocessing is not free.
Blocking the linear t dimension enlarges those coefficient degrees too.

Disposition: promising explicit chart/coverage representation; current
evaluation composition rejected as a speedup. The next constructive question
is a computation exploiting the square-root series' special coefficients,
or a smaller exact product representation, not generic dense evaluation.
These balanced estimates do not cover the unbalanced U+V acquisition issue;
any complete exponent claim must cost that range separately.

## B. A different route: distinguish Frobenius multiplicities

Let N=pq, with distinct odd primes p=3 mod4, q=1 mod4, and q<2p.
Set n=(N-1)/2=2m+1, m=(N-3)/4, and

    H_n(X)=sum_(j=0)^n binom(n,j)^2 X^j.

The integer identities H_(2m+1)(-1)=0 and

    H_(2m+1)'(-1)=(-1)^m*(2m+1)*binom(2m,m)

give an explicit scalar separator. Lucas decomposition gives, modulo p,

    H_n(X)=H_((p-1)/2)(X)*H_((q-1)/2)(X^p).

Differentiating kills the derivative of the X^p factor. At -1 the first
factor has nonzero derivative; the second has nonzero value because its
index is even and less than p. Modulo q, the X^q factor instead has an
odd index and vanishes at -1, so the derivative is zero. Since n is a unit
modulo N, the proposed consequence is

    gcd(binomial((N-3)/2,(N-3)/4), N)=q.

The conditions label congruence classes, not necessarily the smaller factor.
This covers close semiprimes N=3 mod4; it is not an all-input theorem. For
unbalanced q, further base-p carries can make the gcd N. The exact required
condition is p not dividing binomial((q-1)/2,(q-1)/4).

This replaces a long list of collision tests by one specified integer
coefficient, with a direct proposed correctness proof. It does NOT make
that coefficient cheap. Its integer has Theta(N) bits; direct construction
is expensive. Modular numerator/denominator cancellation cannot invert
nonunits modulo N, and already encounters factors of N along the way.
A recurrence that detects its first nonunit denominator reduces to a paid
small-factor search; its batched product comparator already exists.

The precise research question is whether this particular index/modulus
coupling admits a fast residue computation or compressed cancellation whose
cost does not presuppose factoring. No such algorithm is supplied. The
cheapest test is a fixed certificate of the derivative, Lucas and gcd
identities, with unbalanced/full-gcd and N=1 mod4 negative controls. Primary
prior-art and actual binomial-mod-composite algorithms require review.

This is distinct from the W021 cyclic folding selector: it preserves a
specific local multiplicity instead of summing aliased coefficient positions.
It is also a concrete arithmetic barrier to investigate, not a claim that
the binomial residue is a free oracle.
