## B. Binary coefficient extraction: proposed exact degree-growth audit

The intended computation is to repeatedly select the even/odd subsequence of
F(x)=(1-4x)^(-1/2), using the binary digits of m=(N-3)/4, and keep an algebraic
equation for the selected series. All binary divisions would be by units
when N is odd, avoiding the factorial recurrence's first nonunit denominator.
If these equations had bounded degree/height, this would replace Theta(N)
coefficient work by logarithmically many algebraic operations.

The following parent derivation predicts that the advertised bounded-degree
representation fails already along the all-even path. For every integer a>=1,
let

    E_a(x)=sum_(n>=0) binom(2an,an) x^n.

**Conjectured exact scoped result (pending review):** its algebraic degree
over C(x), hence over Q(x), is 2^a. At binary depth k, a=2^k, so representing
the series by its dense minimal polynomial has degree 2^(2^k). This says
nothing about all arithmetic circuits, one coefficient, or reduction modulo
a particular composite N.

Proposed proof: put x=t^a and let zeta be a primitive a-th root of unity.
The root-of-unity filter gives

    E_a(t^a)=(1/a)*sum_(j=0..a-1) (1-4*zeta^j*t)^(-1/2).

The a distinct linear factors have independent square classes in C(t):
their valuations at the a different roots force every nonempty subset
product to have an odd valuation. Adjoining their square roots therefore
gives a multiquadratic extension of degree 2^a. Their inverse roots are
linearly independent over C(t), as distinct basis characters, so all 2^a
independent signed sums are different. The orbit of their sum has size 2^a.
The product of the 2^a conjugate linear factors has coefficients in C(t)
and is invariant under t -> zeta*t by permutation of the radicals; hence
its coefficients lie in C(t^a). This gives the matching upper bound over
C(x). Rational descent/permutation of zeta should give the same bound over
Q(x), but the exact descent and formal branch must be checked independently.

For a=2 this matches the previously derived identity

    E_2(x)^2 = 1/[2(1-16x)] + 1/[2 sqrt(1-16x)],

which generically has degree four. Merely retaining this as a nested circuit
does not imply a cheap next coefficient extraction. On the other hand,
minimal-polynomial degree does not lower-bound the size of every such circuit.
The precise next constructive question is whether a *single requested residue*
can be propagated through a succinct trace/circuit without materializing the
whole algebraic equation. An explicit recurrence and operation count, including
every growing truncation, is needed; a small description is insufficient.

**Selected validation:** audit the multiquadratic-orbit argument, descent,
and its exact computational scope; check relevant primary prior art. No
large symbolic resultant or parameter sweep is warranted. An optional tiny
certificate must be preregistered and frozen before one run, <=60 seconds and
20,000 charged events, with failures retained. It would check identities only.
