## A. Quadratic exponents: a concrete smaller product computation

Given a unit alpha modulo N, binary integers A,D,C, and L>=1, compute

    P = product_(0<=i<L) (1-alpha^(A*i^2+D*i+C)) mod N.

Signed powers use a checked modular inverse. Write f(i)=A*i^2+D*i+C.
Choose block length b; write i=b*k+j. Form the SINGLE polynomial

    F(X,Y) = product_(0<=j<b) (1-alpha^(A*j^2+D*j)*X^j*Y).

For each full block k evaluate F at

    X_k = alpha^(2*A*b*k),
    Y_k = alpha^(A*b^2*k^2+D*b*k+C).

Then F(X_k,Y_k)=product_(0<=j<b)(1-alpha^f(b*k+j)), exactly in Z/NZ.
Multiply these block values and the fewer than b remaining leaf factors.
No modular root, root/carry chart, or high-order base is needed for this
identity. Repeated evaluation points and zero product values are permitted.

Actual degrees are deg_X F<=b(b-1)/2 and deg_Y F<=b. Its rectangular dense
coefficient size is O(b^3), not O(b) and not exponential in b. A balanced
product tree with degree-aware Kronecker multiplication should construct
it in O~(b^3) ring work: at a level with factor groups of size s, each node
has X-degree O(bs), Y-degree O(s), hence O(bs^2) slots, and b/s nodes have
O(b^2*s) slots. Summing the doubling levels costs O~(b^3). Modular coefficient
widths and the binary exponent widths are paid.

To use an equal-partial-degree evaluation theorem without padding to b^4,
replace each monomial X^e Y^h by X0^(e mod b) X1^floor(e/b) Y^h. This is
coefficient relabelling AFTER multiplication, not a ring homomorphism used
inside the product tree. Evaluate the resulting three-variable polynomial
at (X_k,X_k^b,Y_k). All three degrees are <=b, and the box has O(b^3) slots.

**Cost proposed for audit:** a nearly linear multivariate evaluation theorem
over Z/NZ, with fixed three variables and arbitrary composite N, gives for
every fixed epsilon>0 a bound of the shape

    (b^3 + L/b + 1)^(1+epsilon) * poly(log N, input exponent bits, log L),

including constructing the points, coefficient array, products and tail.
With b approximately L^(1/4), this is L^(3/4+epsilon) times the stated bit
factors (rescale epsilon). This is a bit algorithm using a nonalgebraic
multimodular evaluation theorem, not a claim of that many ring operations.
The constants and practical crossover can be very poor.

Primary lead: Kedlaya--Umans, *Fast Polynomial Factorization and Modular
Composition*, Corollary 4.3, and van der Hoeven--Lecerf, *Fast multivariate
multi-point evaluation revisited*, introduction and Turing-machine treatment.
The latter explicitly describes the former bound for polynomials over Z/rZ,
not only fields. The parent has read those introductory statements; the
precise original theorem, uniform parameter choice, model and prior-art
overlap are assigned to independent review, not silently assumed checked.

- https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~umans/papers/KU08-final.pdf
- https://www.texmacs.org/joris/kucomp/kucomp.pdf

The saved work is the explicit L-factor loop. The new work is a b^3-sized
coefficient array plus L/b point records. Ordinary row-by-row univariate
evaluation costs b*(b^2+L/b)=b^3+L and DOES NOT supply the claimed saving.
An algebraic-RAM theorem over fields is not a substitute for the required
composite-ring bit theorem. No novelty for this primitive is claimed.

**Localization:** shifting an interval [a,a+ell) only changes D to D+2Aa
and C to C+Da+Aa^2. Rebuild the same algorithm on balanced subintervals.
At a full product gcd, compute a child product and descend into a nonunit
child, stopping on a proper gcd. A geometric sum of ell^(3/4+epsilon)
costs supports localization at the same soft envelope; charge actual bit
growth and choose a fixed epsilon<1/4. A singleton reveals f(i).

For the W020 annihilator consumer, every retained f(i) must be nonzero.
For a nonzero quadratic this can be checked by solving its at most two
integer zero locations and partitioning around them, at binary arithmetic
cost. A zero polynomial has no admissible leaves. Removing zeros requires
coverage to be proved on the retained domain. There is currently NO proof
that such a quadratic family of cheap length supplies factoring coverage.
The new primitive would not evaluate W019's arbitrary modular root table.

Cheapest falsifier: verify the block identity, digit relabelling, truncated
last block, signed exponents and shifted-domain product against direct
products for one fixed preregistered small composite suite. Independently
audit the b^3 construction and the exact ring evaluation theorem. A toy
implementation will not implement that fast theorem or prove the exponent.

## B. Square weights remove root calls but lose coverage density

Try u=r^2, v=s^2 in S=u*p+v*q, taking a public prime m with N square mod m
and a lifted R^2=N mod m^2. Then 2*R*r*s is a known root of 4*u*v*N mod m^2.
The collision exponent before interval corrections is the quadratic form

    r^2 + N*s^2 - 2*R*r*s - m^2*t.

The proposed gain is a polynomial exponent in place of per-pair square
roots. But for balanced p,q, covering an unknown root of p/q with short
r,s has RS of order m; squared weights have size m, so S has size m*sqrt(N).
Even granting centered intervals, the t length is of order sqrt(N)/m and
the occurrence count is of order sqrt(N). This is worse than the ordinary
short-weight cover's sqrt(N/m). A square-root-in-occurrences comparator
already has N^(1/4) scale. Moreover an unreduced 2Rrs moves the t interval
by pair-dependent carries; a common uncentered interval is larger still.

Disposition: do not infer an N^(1/6) algorithm from A plus this replacement.
This is a cost account for the displayed rectangle, not a lower bound on
all square-weight covers. The constructive question is whether coverage
with polynomial exponents can retain the ordinary cover's smaller total
occurrence count, rather than merely eliminating root acquisition.

## C. A separate algebraic starting point: Frobenius support

The polynomial (1+X)^N over Z/NZ has, modulo p|N with N=p*q, support only
on exponents divisible by p: (1+X)^(pq)=(1+X^p)^q over F_p. If p<q are
primes, its first coefficient not divisible by N after the constant term
is at degree p, where gcd(binomial(N,p),N)=q. This uses divisibility of
coefficients, rather than smooth relations or power-collision density.

A concrete attempted selector is to reduce the polynomial modulo X^r-1
using binary polynomial powering in O~(r log N) ring work, then inspect
coefficient gcds. This aggregates coefficient positions cheaply, but it
also folds p-multiples onto all residue classes when gcd(p,r)=1. Thus the
first-support argument does not transfer to those aggregate coefficients.
No success probability is claimed. The cheap decisive test is a frozen
small-prime r menu and full coefficient-gcd outcomes on fixed semiprimes,
including controls for r sharing a factor with N. A theorem about the
unfolded polynomial alone cannot certify the folded selector.

Keep this direction separate from A: A is a promising supplied-product
primitive; C asks whether coefficient aggregation can expose a factor at
all. Neither currently gives a complete factoring bound.
