# W016 Sparse progression certificates: a concrete transfer and its price

Parent algebraic development, 2026-10-08. Verified complete factoring
improvement: NONE. This is a separate construction from factorial routing.
It changes the full consecutive-grid assumption in W014, while explicitly
charging the congruence information needed to cover a factor. No experiment
or solver implementation is authorized by this note alone.

## Operation replacing dense-grid approximation

Suppose a prime factor p of N obeys p=1+m*t, with public m>=1 and unknown
positive integer t in a supplied interval. Approximate q*N/(1+m*t) by an
integer polynomial A(t), only on this t-grid. Let alpha be a unit modulo N,
beta=alpha^m, q>=1, Q=N/p, and

    D=A(0)-q*N,              e=A(t)-q*Q.

Then beta^t=alpha^(p-1)=1 mod p, and A(t)-A(0) is divisible by t. Also
N-Q=(p-1)*Q. Therefore

    beta^D = beta^e (mod p).                              (1)

The search can use the W013 shared-baby consumer in beta, with true integer
error radius E. The integer recovery polynomial, in the candidate t variable, is

    (1+m*T)*(A(T)-e')-q*N.                               (2)

For q>=1 it cannot be identically zero: if A-e' is zero, its constant is
-q*N; otherwise multiplying its nonzero leading term by m*T increases degree.
Integer roots give candidates 1+m*T, each checked for proper divisibility of N.

This representation uses alpha^m irrespective of the degree of A. It avoids
the naive alpha^(m^d) clearing needed to express a general degree-d polynomial
in (p-a)/m at arbitrary residue a. It is a concrete conditional primitive,
not an assertion of a new factoring algorithm or new prior-art theorem.

## Costs that can erase the benefit

The same relation causing (1) also bounds ord_p(beta)<=t=(p-1)/m. A large
order for alpha is not a large order guarantee for beta. Component checks,
reacquisition, all errors, all integer roots and coefficient widths are paid.
Different moduli m generally have different beta and cannot silently share
the same baby table.

The sparse grid permits curvature of size q*N*m^2/X^3 near p~X, rather than
q*N/X^3 per original integer step. Thus W014's dense-grid theorem must be
rescaled to the t-domain before applying it. This removes a specific premise;
it does not supply useful approximants or prime-factor coverage.

There is already an elementary comparator once the progression is supplied.
Compute the product of the candidates 1+m*T in the allowed interval using
monic block evaluation. If the interval has L candidates, this costs
O~(sqrt(L)) modular operations, with gcd=N localized by product bisection.
Discard candidates outside (1,N). When L=O(sqrt(N)/m), the scale is

    O~(N^(1/4)/sqrt(m)).                                 (3)

This is just the arithmetic-progression product method with additional input
information, not a new general bound. Any certificate method must beat (3)
AFTER paying for that same information. In particular m~N^(1/10) already
brings this elementary conditional comparator to N^(1/5) scale.

## The constructive question left open

Can a paid family of progression restrictions, chosen from N alone, cover at
least one prime factor while enabling shared work that neither (3) nor
separate beta tables already provide? Safe-prime-shaped p-1=2*r prevents
treating a large easily known divisor m of p-1 as universal. Enumerating
divisors of a public smooth number does not guarantee a large such m for p-1.

The next mathematical test is explicit: for a proposed family, write its
coverage proof, acquisition cost, total candidate lengths and actual common
base construction. Compare its proposed consumer with applying the arithmetic-
progression product evaluator to the same family. Reject a claimed gain that
comes solely from supplying p's residue for free. No parameter sweep is needed
until a family and its coverage rule are written down.

W013 consumer is reused only under its complete contract; (1) does not replace
its order, extraction, acquisition or bit-complexity obligations.
