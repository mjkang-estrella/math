# H012 — Accepted finite evidence; uniform cover premise closed

2026-10-08. Both T3 reviews completed and were consumed. Verified factoring
complexity improvement: **NONE**.

The frozen 96-case experiment remains a pass of its finite continuation
criterion. Its first independent audit checked 9,727 certificates and 314,101
coverage decisions, with all registered edge checks passing. Those observations
do not establish asymptotic cover compression.

The new analytic obstruction from that first review has now passed a second
independent review. For infinitely many actual distinct-prime semiprimes, the
exact primorial-then-doubling modulus rule and fixed slack require
Omega(phi(m)*bit_length(N)) certificates counted separately per residue to
cover the entire real factor interval. A positive fraction of unit residues
each need Omega(bit_length(N)) certificates. The quantified theorem, uniform
error estimate, semiprime construction, and regular logarithmic retargeting
are in [the second review](H012-obstruction-review.md).

The coordinator checked the congruence average, error terms, hard-point
charging, and semiprime transfer. The apparent Brun–Titchmarsh citation concern
is resolved: the reviewer checked the original short-interval theorem, which
supports the stated constant 4. A weaker cumulative fallback would also suffice
for existence. Broad low-scale coverage by axis certificates is genuine and
does not contradict the hard interior-band count.

Close the uniform fixed-slack, per-residue, full-real-interval compression
premise, including the explicit regular logarithmic retargeting in the review.
This is a representation bound, not a runtime or distinct modular-value bound.
It says nothing universal about early stopping, integer-only coverage, adaptive
moduli, cross-residue sharing, or factoring hardness. The existing Lean lemmas
prove extraction algebra, not this analytic result.

Reopen for a concrete costed primitive changing one of those premises, with
correct coverage, construction, metadata and recovery. More samples of the
same finite reuse statistic cannot overturn the quantified obstruction.
