# H016 — Batch smoothness processing after exact enumeration

Status: proposed analytic construction, 2026-10-08.

Claim class: paid subroutine, not a new factoring bound. H014's provisional
coefficient-line enumerator produces S integer pairs with polynomial bit width.
Can a concrete product/remainder-tree algorithm identify their B-smooth parts
and recover the required prime exponents in (B+S)*poly(n) bit work, including
all rejected candidates? Trial division's S*B cost is a baseline, not a lower
bound. Bernstein's primary smooth-parts and small-factors algorithms are the
starting point; ordinary integer smoothness must remain distinct from ideal
decomposition and square-class information.

Read H014-resultant-enumerator.md and W001-relations.md Card C. Its stronger
eta=1/10 model has an optimistic 1.89 envelope only if supply and processing
obligations hold. This task assesses processing, without assuming supply,
rank, square roots or proper splitting. No novelty is claimed for batch gcd.

Charge base construction, aggregate bit lengths, memory, prime multiplicities,
zero and signed values, exceptional primes, and every failed candidate. State
any extra assumptions needed to attach ideal labels and valuations. The cheap
falsifier is a hidden multiplicative B or superpolynomial-width cost, or an
unresolved algebraic step being mislabeled as integer factorization.

No numerical experiment is registered here. If a finite exact check is useful,
the owner must first write a complete protocol, source hashes, fixed inputs,
reference and rejection criterion, then run once within 60 seconds and retain
every outcome. A full siever or large benchmark is outside this question.
Independent review is required before accepting a new positive cost claim.
