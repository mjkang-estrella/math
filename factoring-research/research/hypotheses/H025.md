# H025 — Input-density bound for very small lattice residuals

2026-10-08. Parent analytic proposal; independent review required.
Depends on H024's independently reviewed-or-to-be-reviewed diagonal count.
Verified full factoring complexity improvement: **NONE**.

## Question

Can the missing lower residual-size premise in H024 be replaced by a rigorous
statement about the fraction of inputs, without assuming random-integer
smoothness? For a fixed bit length, the diagonal low-energy universe and
prime list are independent of N. Each vector fixes u,v, and |u-Nv|<=W
holds for at most floor(2W/v)+1 integers N. Union counting may bound the
exceptional inputs even for an arbitrary adaptive choice of vector after N.

Proposed theorem: with f_j=ceil(j/2), fixed first k=ell primes and any
N-dependent accepted subsets of q(e)<=E_bar(ell), the number of ell-bit
inputs having any residual <=W is at most

    min(2^(ell-1), exp(11 E_bar^(1/3))*(floor(2W)+1)).

H024's cutoff should satisfy E_star<=40ell² explicitly, making the exceptional
fraction vanish exponentially for W=N^(1-epsilon), fixed epsilon>0.
More generally E_bar=o(ell³) gives exp(o(ell)) vectors and the same conclusion.
This is only a density statement about small absolute residuals. It neither
bounds smoothness at larger residuals nor proves factoring hard.

## Method and falsifier

Symbolic proof and independent review only. Freeze no empirical trial because
none runs here. Check constants, floors, domain independence, N-dependent
rounding, variable W and the probability normalization. Show why arbitrary
selection from the fixed universe is covered. Reject any extension to changed
N-dependent prime lists/weights without a costed count of their universes.

Do not infer a balanced-semiprime density theorem without an input-set size
bound, or a random-smoothness law. A high-energy or changed generator may lie
outside the theorem. No executable, benchmark, package or Lean change.

Next useful question, if accepted: a costed selector achieving unusually small
residuals at larger energy, or a demonstrated useful distribution at the
larger typical residual sizes. Optimizing a low-energy objective alone is
insufficient. Any positive factoring claim requires independent review.
