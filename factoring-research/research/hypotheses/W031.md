# W031: implicit chart products and reversed characteristic jets

Parent constructive work, 2026-10-08 local. Symbolic derivation only.
**Verified complete factoring improvement: NONE.** Two actual computations
are developed below. Their failures concern the displayed implementations,
not all product algorithms or all characteristic-extraction methods.

## A. Joint quadratic-chart and carry blocks

W030's unbalanced frontier places a bottleneck at p on the scale N^(2/5).
There the unscaled cover has M on the scale N^(1/5), U on the scale M,
and V bounded (use the strict-rectangle repair, not literally U=M,V=1).
The genuine root width is W on the scale M^3, while Q=M^2 and the carry
length is on the scale M. Since V is bounded, consider one signed v.

Replace individual u-roots by quadratic Hensel charts. Choose a public
ell-power H with H^3 divisible by Q and H on the scale M^(2/3), up to
polylogarithmic rounding. On each unit residue u0 modulo H,
u=u0+Hx. The degree-two truncation of

    sqrt(4*N*u0*v) * (1+H*x/u0)^(1/2)

is an exact root modulo Q. Its reduced coefficients are public after one
base root lift. There are O(H) charts and X=O(M/H)=O(M^(1/3)) coordinates
per chart. A common carry envelope is O(W/Q+1+X^2), hence O(M) here.
Actual coefficients may give smaller envelopes; this is the proposed
uniform implementation. Valuation strata, the W024 whole-chart residue
filter, and signed endpoints remain necessary. Focus on the top-stratum
cost: failure there already prevents this particular all-stratum saving.

After freezing a chart and sign, its actual exponent is

    f(x,t)=A*x^2+D*x+C-Q*t.

For block lengths b<=X and c<=T, write x=b*k+i, t=c*l+j. Let beta=alpha^(-Q)
for a checked unit alpha and construct

    F_(b,c)(Z,Y)=product_(0<=i<b,0<=j<c)
        (1-alpha^(A*i^2+D*i) * Z^i * Y * beta^j).

At the public points

    Z_k=alpha^(2*A*b*k),
    Y_(k,l)=alpha^(A*b^2*k^2+D*b*k+C-Q*c*l),

this is exactly the block product. Ordinary shifted coordinates merely
change D,C. Use exact smaller tail blocks, with at most four block shapes,
rather than silently changing an already frozen family. All labels are
acquired by finite differences and geometric recurrences; exponent widths
and the number of emitted evaluation points are paid.

For b>=2 the dense coefficient rectangle has

    deg_Z=c*b*(b-1)/2,  deg_Y=b*c,
    rectangle size Theta(b^3*c^2).

A balanced product tree constructs that rectangle in soft O(b^3*c^2)
ring operations. Even granting essentially linear multivariate evaluation
in this rectangle plus the number of points, the displayed per-chart
work measure is

    D(b,c)=b^3*c^2+X*T/(b*c).

This is a priced dense implementation, not a lower bound on the true
coefficient support after cancellation or on all resultant methods. A
full application of the W021 evaluation theorem also pays a fixed epsilon
power, coefficient layout and all setup. Those can only increase this
optimistic work measure. There is no need to assume that theorem to
reject the present optimistic count.

Put z=b*c. Then D=b*z^2+X*T/z. If D<X, the point term forces z>T;
the dense term then exceeds b*T^2. Consequently, whenever T>=sqrt(X),
this dense implementation cannot have D<X. At the transition T is on
the scale M and X on the scale M^(1/3), well inside this unfavorable
regime. The b=1 boundary is different: Z disappears and the polynomial
has only c+1 coefficients, but there are X*ceil(T/c)>=X evaluation
points. Thus it also visits every root coordinate. Multiplication by the
number of charts restores at least the explicit-coordinate work scale.

What failed is the attempt to replace root-table traffic by an ordinary
dense block polynomial. The carry dimension itself makes this price
unfavorable. A successful joint consumer would need a different
representation or computation, rather than a smaller choice of b,c in
this template. This obstruction does not cover a merged-support circuit,
an implicit norm algorithm, or a different factoring construction.

## B. Reverse a compact circuit before extracting characteristic information

This starts from a different object, W029's Lucas-type polynomial, rather
than a table of modular roots. For odd N>=3, write N=2n+1 and set

    F_N(X)=((1+a)^N+(1-a)^N)/2,  a^2=1-4X,
    J_N(t)=t^n*F_N(1/t).

Both are integer polynomials, and J_N has degree n. For distinct odd
primes p<q with N=pq, W029's accepted degree computation implies

    ord_t(J_N mod p)=(p-1)/2,
    ord_t(J_N mod q)=(q-1)/2.

Reversal changes the target from a high forward coefficient into the
first nonzero coefficient of a low-degree truncation. Crucially, one can
compute that truncation without constructing the full degree-n polynomial.

Define the polynomial matrix

    D(z) = [[2*z, -4], [1, 0]].

Its eigenvalue identity, equivalently its formal recurrence, gives

    trace(D(z)^N) = 2*z*J_N(z^2).

Compute D(z)^N by binary powering in

    (Z/N^2 Z)[z] / (z^(2*K+3)).

The trace has only odd powers for odd N. Shift the coefficient indices
by one and multiply by the inverse of 2 modulo N^2, then read through
t^K. Do not halve canonical residues as integers or invert z. This takes
O(log N) polynomial-matrix
multiplications of degree O(K), not degree O(N). It is an actual finite
circuit algorithm. With fast polynomial arithmetic its bit cost is
soft O((K+1)*log N*poly(log N)); a direct implementation must charge its
chosen multiplication routine. Resident coefficient arrays use
O((K+1)*log N) bits; total storage also includes that routine's workspace.
Truncation retains exactly the requested coefficients, including modulo
N^2; no unknown characteristic or field inversion is used.

For each coefficient a_j=[t^j]J_N, first compute gcd(a_j mod N,N). A proper
gcd is a factor. If the gcd is N, its canonical residue modulo N^2 is
divisible by N, and division by N gives a well-defined residue modulo N.
Scale this residue by the unit (-4)^(-n). This normalization must NOT be
performed by inverting N, or applied when the coefficient is not divisible
by N. Computing modulo N^2 is what makes the permitted exact division
meaningful.

## C. Exact coefficient calculation: where the signal begins

The proposed closed formula, for 0<=j<=n, is

    a_j = N*(-4)^n * (-1)^j * (n+j)! / ((n-j)!*(2j+1)!).

This is an equality of rational expressions with the integral coefficient;
it does NOT assert that a_j/N is always an integer. Pairing the 2j factors
from n-j+1 through n+j gives

    a_j/(N*(-4)^n)
       = product_(i=1..j)((2*i-1)^2-N^2)
           / (4^j*(2j+1)!).

If gcd((2j+1)!,N)=1, the denominator is a unit modulo N. The coefficient
is then divisible by N and its legal normalization reduces to

    a_j/(N*(-4)^n)
       = binom(2j,j) / (16^j*(2j+1)) modulo N.

Thus every such low coefficient is a universal central-binomial expression
independent of the unknown factorization. As a formal rational series,
these coefficients are those of 2*asin(sqrt(t)/2)/sqrt(t), but no real
analytic computation or transcendental oracle is part of the algorithm.

For the RAW-coefficient gcd test on a squarefree semiprime, the first
position yielding a proper factor is j=(p-1)/2: all earlier coefficients
vanish modulo both p and q, whereas
this coefficient is nonzero modulo p and zero modulo q. Its gcd with N
is q. Consequently an expanding truncated-jet search genuinely finds a
factor, but the displayed implementation needs Theta(p) coefficients.
It does not beat the known small-factor product comparator on the scale
sqrt(p), let alone a matched complete factoring baseline. Squarefree
semiprime assumptions are essential to this exact onset statement;
prime powers and general N are not silently covered by it.

What failed is the hope that reversal plus legal N-adic normalization
exposes a factor in a polylogarithmic jet. Reversal really does make the
desired end of the polynomial accessible by binary matrix powering, but
the first non-universal coefficient still occurs at an index proportional
to the smallest prime factor. The coefficient formula identifies the
specific obstruction: denominators first meet the unknown characteristic.
This is not a lower bound for implicit valuation detection or a single
large-index coefficient algorithm. W028's block-product coefficient-or-factor
baseline is the required comparator for any such proposed replacement.
