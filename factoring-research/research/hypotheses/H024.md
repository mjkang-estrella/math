## Question and scope

For the accepted H021 N-only generator with ell=bit_length(N), k=ell,
C the least power of ten >=ell, a_j=round_star_C(p_j), T=round_star_C(N),
and f_j=ceil(j/2), can a cheap feasible vector lower the sufficient global
optimizer cutoff to O(ell²)? What exact upper bound on the number of
low-energy vectors follows from the diagonal energy, and what does it
actually say about obtaining enough useful relations?

Parent candidate: take r the exact nearest integer to T/a_1, choose e=(r,0,...),
and E_star=r²+(a_1 r-T)². Then E_star=O(ell²). The finite theta-product
bound may give number of F(e)<=E at most exp(11 E^(1/3)), uniformly in k,
for E>=1 and this weight sequence. This is an upper count, not a lower
population, a uniform sampler guarantee or a smoothness law.

The parent report derives costs with accepted H021 arithmetic and separates
indexed memory from its conservative multitape realization. Original H021
prime setup is still quartic; a separately specified elementary sieve may
reduce setup, but this is standard arithmetic, not a factoring innovation.

- Show every global minimizer is inside E_star without relying on a heuristic.
- Derive the diagonal ellipsoid count by an elementary theta-product argument;

- Charge prime/log generation, all bit widths and memory access, and output.

  integer smoothness into structured residuals. Large-residual assumptions

- Give a concrete next useful-population question, not an implementation
