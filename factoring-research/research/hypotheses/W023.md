## A. Projective rational roots: fewer coefficients, paid denominators

Replace a Taylor polynomial by a rational approximant. In one chart put
D=u0*v0, w=u*v, T=w-D. The integer identity

    D*(D+3w)^2 - w*(3D+w)^2 = -(w-D)^3

shows that if r0^2=4ND modulo Q=M^2 and T^3=0 modulo Q, then

    r0*(D+3w)/(3D+w)

is a root of 4Nw modulo Q. With an odd fixed prime ell, u0,v0 units and
u=u0+Hx, v=v0+Hy, the denominator reduces to 4D modulo ell and is a unit.
Choosing H=ell^ceil(2h/3) for M=ell^h ensures T^3 is zero modulo Q.
For the true weighted sum S, retain both signs and clear the denominator:

    (3D+w)*S - sigma*r0*(D+3w) = Q*tau.

Then the explicit exponent

    E=(3D+w)*(u+Nv)-sigma*r0*(D+3w)-Q*tau

is divisible by p-1 at the covered leaf. This changes root acquisition into
a low-degree integer exponent without a modular-inverse-in-the-group step.
It uses real operations: public Hensel root acquisition, polynomial arithmetic,
a common integer tau interval, exponentiation and product localization.

The cost rejects this first version. H~M^(2/3) exceeds both side lengths of
a balanced sqrt(M) rectangle, so chart compression disappears. Worse, an
integer bound |3D+w|<=Bden multiplies the S range W: a safe common interval
must pay at least the envelope

    |tau| <= (Bden*W + |r0|*Bnum)/Q,

where Bnum bounds |D+3w|. A modularly invertible denominator is not a small
integer denominator. With canonical chart residues, the basic bound is
Bden,Bnum=O(H^2+UV), which is O(M^(4/3)+M), before signed endpoints.
This bound is conservative, not a lower bound on every possible lift scheme.
Higher Padé order can reduce H but introduces larger denominator polynomials;
their values, sign, possible integer zero leaves and interval enlargement
must be paid. Neither division by the denominator modulo an unknown group
order nor replacing it by a unit-cost symbol is permitted.

**What could change the conclusion:** a concrete low-height projective chart
whose denominator values stay small on enough of the covering rectangle,
or an algorithm using rational exponents without those divisions. This wave
does not posit either as available. The degree-one Padé construction itself
is rejected, not queued for another numerical root certificate.

## B. Binary coefficient extraction: proposed exact degree-growth audit

The intended computation is to repeatedly select the even/odd subsequence of
F(x)=(1-4x)^(-1/2), using the binary digits of m=(N-3)/4, and keep an algebraic
equation for the selected series. All binary divisions would be by units
when N is odd, avoiding the factorial recurrence's first nonunit denominator.
If these equations had bounded degree/height, this would replace Theta(N)
coefficient work by logarithmically many algebraic operations.

The following parent derivation predicts that the advertised bounded-degree
representation fails already along the all-even path. For every integer a>=1,
let

    E_a(x)=sum_(n>=0) binom(2an,an) x^n.

**Conjectured exact scoped result (pending review):** its algebraic degree
over C(x), hence over Q(x), is 2^a. At binary depth k, a=2^k, so representing
the series by its dense minimal polynomial has degree 2^(2^k). This says
nothing about all arithmetic circuits, one coefficient, or reduction modulo
a particular composite N.

Proposed proof: put x=t^a and let zeta be a primitive a-th root of unity.
The root-of-unity filter gives

    E_a(t^a)=(1/a)*sum_(j=0..a-1) (1-4*zeta^j*t)^(-1/2).

The a distinct linear factors have independent square classes in C(t):
their valuations at the a different roots force every nonempty subset
product to have an odd valuation. Adjoining their square roots therefore
gives a multiquadratic extension of degree 2^a. Their inverse roots are
linearly independent over C(t), as distinct basis characters, so all 2^a
independent signed sums are different. The orbit of their sum has size 2^a.
The product of the 2^a conjugate linear factors has coefficients in C(t)
and is invariant under t -> zeta*t by permutation of the radicals; hence
its coefficients lie in C(t^a). This gives the matching upper bound over
C(x). Rational descent/permutation of zeta should give the same bound over
Q(x), but the exact descent and formal branch must be checked independently.

For a=2 this matches the previously derived identity

    E_2(x)^2 = 1/[2(1-16x)] + 1/[2 sqrt(1-16x)],

which generically has degree four. Merely retaining this as a nested circuit
does not imply a cheap next coefficient extraction. On the other hand,
minimal-polynomial degree does not lower-bound the size of every such circuit.
The precise next constructive question is whether a *single requested residue*
can be propagated through a succinct trace/circuit without materializing the
whole algebraic equation. An explicit recurrence and operation count, including
every growing truncation, is needed; a small description is insufficient.

**Selected validation:** audit the multiquadratic-orbit argument, descent,
and its exact computational scope; check relevant primary prior art. No
large symbolic resultant or parameter sweep is warranted. An optional tiny
certificate must be preregistered and frozen before one run, <=60 seconds and
20,000 charged events, with failures retained. It would check identities only.

## C. A changed root-chart degree, with the full budget visible

The fixed quartic is not an obligatory degree. For fixed d>=4 use the
degree-d total Taylor truncation and H~M^(2/(d+1)). In a balanced top stratum,

    charts C=O(M^(4/(d+1))),
    coordinate width Z=O(1+M^(1/2-2/(d+1))),
    common carry length T=O(1+sqrt(N)/M^(3/2)+Z^d).

The extra carry term fits within the usual ordinary-width envelope when

    M <= N^((d+1)/(d^2+3)).

For d=5, the endpoint is M=N^(3/14), C=N^(1/7), Z=N^(1/28),
T=N^(5/28), and total leaf occurrences M*T=N^(11/28). These exponents are
parent bookkeeping, not an audited all-strata/all-input factoring bound.
They show that raising degree reduces chart acquisition while making the
polynomial evaluator and carry envelope harder; it is not a free improvement.

Even an imagined square-root evaluator run INDEPENDENTLY on each chart costs
C*sqrt(Z^2*T)=N^(15/56) at that endpoint, above one fifth. A useful algorithm
must share work across the charts, not merely improve each chart in isolation.
Unbalanced rectangles, small-factor preprocessing, all valuation strata,
zero-exponent handling, and localization remain paid obligations. This is a
changed candidate family to investigate, not an assumed aggregate evaluator.
