## A. Central coefficient splitting, with an actual compressed computation

For odd primes p<q<2p and N=pq, put m=(N-1)/2. The proposed exact identity is

    gcd(binomial(N-1,m),N)=p.                              (A1)

Parent proof: modulo q the base-q digits of N-1 are (p-1,q-1), and of m
are ((p-1)/2,(q-1)/2), so Lucas gives a nonzero coefficient. Modulo p the
digits of N-1 are (1,q-p-1,p-1), while m<p^2 has digits
((q-1)/2,(p-1)/2). The middle lower digit exceeds q-p-1, so the coefficient
vanishes. More exactly Legendre's formula gives v_p=1: the p-level term
cancels, and floor((N-1)/p^2)-2floor(m/p^2)=1. No q valuation occurs.
This is a proposed elementary selector, not a claim that its central
coefficient is cheap or novel. Verify against primary prior art.

**What work would be eliminated:** this one coefficient has deterministic
factor separation on the balanced promise, so it avoids searching rare
square-root signs, smooth relations or collisions. Its exact value has
Theta(N) bits, and a huge binomial call is not a fast modular evaluator.
The acquisition computation is the entire question.

**Concrete compression attempt:** for a specified small L, compute
F_L(X)=(1+X)^(N-1) mod (N, X^L-1) by binary exponentiation of length-L
coefficient vectors and schoolbook cyclic convolution. Inspect the L
coefficient gcds with N in increasing index order. Each coefficient is
the sum of binomial coefficients whose indices coincide modulo L.
This actual algorithm costs O(L^2 log N) ring operations with O(log N)-bit
reduced operands and O(L log N) working bits. No formal derivative, hidden
prime, coefficient extraction oracle or factor library is supplied.
If L were polylogarithmic and the coefficient zero survived this folding
often enough, this would be a polynomial trial. That survival is UNPROVED,
and adding aliases may completely erase the carry-based selector.

The exact control computes (A1) only in the tiny evaluator, never supplies
its factor to the folded candidate, and never treats its cost as modular
candidate work. The fixed small screen below can reject coverage by the
particular degree menu, not all compressed binomial algorithms.

**Already visible obstruction:** L=2 gives the two unit coefficients
2^(N-2) for every odd N, so it never splits. For the conditional prime
family q=2p-1, p=1 mod 4, the parent proposes that L=4 also never splits:
over F_p the fourth-root evaluations are 1,0,1,1; over F_q they are
1,0,a,a with a=+/-1, since q=1 mod 8. The inverse four-point transforms
have numerators among 1,-1,3, hence are nonzero for p,q>3.
This uses the field fourth roots for proof only. Check the value at -1,
the transforms, and all signs. It does not assert infinitely many such
prime pairs. N=37*73=2701 is a fixed admissible member.

**Decisive next computation:** audit (A1), the folding algorithm, and the
explicit L=4 obstruction; run only the frozen primitive screen. If folding
fails, identify exactly which aliases cancel the selector. A replacement must
actually isolate the middle block or compute an equivalent quantity, with
charged vector dimension, modulus precision and time. Calling it a Hasse
coefficient, a trace or a holonomic recurrence does not provide that algorithm.

## B. Direct splitting: two rational reflections instead of an exact shell scan

The earlier short-direction test used a single integral reflection on the
orthogonal norm-N shell. A real change would allow rational intermediate
points and require integrality only at the end. Here is its actual formula.

Given primitive integer A of odd norm N, take B0=i*A. For nonzero integer
directions C,D, set
c=Norm(C), d=Norm(D), b=<B0,C>, e=<B0,D>, h=<C,D>. Then

    B2 = B0 + [-2*b*d*C+(4*b*h-2*c*e)*D]/(c*d).           (B1)

It always has norm N. Exact coordinate divisibility by c*d decides final
integrality. With a=<A,C>, f=<A,D>, final orthogonality is exactly

    a*b*d+c*e*f-2*b*h*f=0.                               (B2)

Each candidate and its content-gcd extraction have polynomial bit cost in
input/direction heights. Direction generation, solving (B2), divisibility
and output probability remain paid work, not free lattice operations.

**A new parent obstruction to the easy generator:** taking both directions
in A-perp makes (B2) automatic, but apparently cannot work if c,d are units
modulo N, even with rational intermediate points. Quaternion reflection is

    R_C(V)=-C*bar(V)*C/c.

For C perpendicular to A, C*bar(A)=-A*bar(C) and
bar(A)*C=-bar(C)*A. Therefore reflection alternates the forms U*A and A*U,
where U has norm one and denominators supported on the direction norms.
Starting from i*A, an even word is U*A and an odd word is A*U with U
integral in Z_(N). This preserves respectively A's right kernel or image
at every prime power. If the final vector is integer and has norm N,
the accepted primitive gcd/content formula should force it to be an
integer unit associate; orthogonality then leaves only the twelve exceptions.
This extends beyond intermediate integrality and needs independent audit,
especially integrality of the final norm-one multiplier and repeated primes.

The apparent escape must therefore allow directions that individually move A
and solve the final equation (B2). A single such reflection cannot help:
orthogonality factors into <C,A>*<C,B0>=0, leaving the old case or no change.
For two it is a genuine quadratic equation in the second direction.
It has known rational points that return B0, so parametrizing its quadric is
possible, but final integrality and useful local component choice may still
encode factoring. Do not claim a useful generator merely from rationality.

This branch is distinct from field packets and the coefficient experiment.
It seeks to replace exact-shell enumeration with constrained rational
transformations. A specified parameterization, height control and retained
useful mass are needed before numerical search or complexity claims.
