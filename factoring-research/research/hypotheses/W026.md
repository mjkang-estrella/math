# W026: coordinate-dependent multipliers with a paid exceptional branch

Parent construction, 2026-10-08 local. No numerical run. Verified complete
factoring improvement: **NONE**. The generic coefficient bound below is already
uncompetitive; the surviving object is a concrete admissibility mechanism for
structured multipliers, not a claim that affine multipliers improve factoring.

## 1. Replace a constant multiplier by an acquired affine polynomial

On a supplied one-coordinate chart, R(x)=sum_(i=0)^4 d_i*x^i modulo
Q=ell^L represents a root of the covered S, with |x|<=Z and |S|<=W.
The W024 root SIGN has already been kept, so its exponent residue epsilon
modulo ell is nonzero for every x on this chart.

Choose integer caps C0,C1>=0, not both zero, each strictly less than Q.
Seek c(x)=a+b*x, with |a|<=C0, |b|<=C1, (a,b)!=(0,0), and a polynomial
P whose selected high coefficients satisfy

    |a*d_i+b*d_(i-1)-Q*k_i| <= floor((Q-1)/K_i),

where d_-1=d_5=0, 0<=i<=5, and K_i=ceil(Z^i/T) for active weights Z^i>T.
The number of variables is fixed. Four linear feasibility systems enforcing
a>=1, a<=-1, b>=1, or b<=-1 exclude the zero polynomial. Overlaps are harmless.

Existence has a direct sufficient condition. Map every (a,b) in the integer
rectangle [0,C0] x [0,C1] to its active residue vector divided by Q. If

    (C0+1)*(C1+1) > product_i K_i,

two vectors share a half-open residue box. Their difference is a nonzero
affine polynomial satisfying all bounds. As in W025, fixed-dimensional
integer feasibility computes a point in polynomial bit time. This is not a
claim about finding a shortest vector with unpriced data acquisition.

Strip the common ell-adic coefficient valuation: r is the largest ell-power
dividing BOTH a and b, with the valuation of a zero coefficient ignored.
Since a,b are not both zero and their caps are below Q, r<Q. Put
c'=c/r, Q'=Q/r>=ell. Divide the active coefficient errors by r; center the
remaining coefficients modulo Q'. Then P=c'R modulo Q'. On the whole box,

    |c(x)| <= C0+C1*Z,
    |P(x)| <= 6*Q'*T,
    |tau| <= ceil((C0+C1*Z)*W/Q + 6*T).

The true scaled sum c'(x)*S is represented by sigma*P(x)+Q'*tau. The exponent

    E'=c'(x)*(u+Nv)-sigma*P(x)-Q'*tau

equals c'(x)*(p-1)*(vq-u) at the covered leaf. Every ingredient is public and
independent of alpha. Its polynomial degree and binary width remain bounded
by constants and polynomial logarithms, respectively. Signed multipliers are
allowed; modular negative powers require the usual checked unit alpha.

## 2. A multiplier zero modulo ell need not kill coverage

The primitive affine c' modulo ell is not the zero polynomial. If its linear
coefficient vanishes, its constant is nonzero and it is safe on the whole box.
Otherwise there is exactly one exceptional residue x=a0 modulo ell.

Retain the other residue classes separately: each is an ordinary interval
after x=j+ell*y, and E' has the constant nonzero residue epsilon*c'(j).
Only the exceptional class needs further work. Reindex that class as
x=a0+ell*y and keep its ORIGINAL unscaled root congruence R(a0+ell*y) modulo
Q. Acquire another affine multiplier on the now smaller interval, or use
c=1. Do not repeatedly divide Q until it becomes 1. At a singleton, use the
constant original root representative and c=1 directly.

There is at most one exceptional child per level, and the number of integer
points in that child falls to at most ceil(previous_length/ell). Thus at
most O(log_ell(length)+1) levels produce O(ell*log(length)+1) retained ordinary
intervals. The new multiplier on a child may be unrelated to the old one;
it is still acquired from public data. A covered x either lies in a retained
class or continues down the unique exceptional path and reaches the safe
singleton fallback. This explicitly preserves coverage rather than assuming
an unknown witness avoids multiplier zeros.

The number of intervals is small, but their PRODUCT COST need not be.
Charge each retained interval's carry width, coefficient setup and evaluator.
Localization inside a retained interval can keep the same polynomial and
residue restriction. The extra residue branching is a polynomial-logarithmic
acquisition overhead for the public small ell schedule. There is no claim
that it automatically preserves a previous evaluator's shared coefficients.

## 3. Cheap falsification of the generic hoped-for gain

The first generic size calculation LOSES even to the original constant
multiplier, before the stronger precision-truncation baseline found by Opus.
Take C0~C, C1~C/Z, so the coefficient rectangle has about C^2/Z points and
the value bound is O(C). Balance C*A~T, where A=max(1,ceil(W/Q)).
In the regime A=Z^s, 0<=s<=2, the exponent-only pigeonhole frontier is

    T=Z^((13+2s)/5),   0<=s<=1  (degrees 3,4,5 active);
    T=Z^((10+2s)/4),   1<=s<=2  (degrees 4,5 active).

These follow from C^2/Z exceeding the respective box counts Z^12/T^3
or Z^9/T^2. The coordinate cap C/Z is at least 1 in these regimes.
Both exponents exceed the constant-multiplier envelope (7+s)/3. This is a
failure of this sufficient generic construction, not a lower bound for all
affine multipliers or for the special root coefficients.

Exact coefficient cancellation also has an immediate comparator. Suppose
Q=H^5 and R's cubic coefficient is H^3 times an ell-unit. Choosing
c(x)=1+H*b1*x can cancel the quartic term modulo Q by solving one linear
congruence for b1 modulo H; the fifth-degree term vanishes automatically.
But |H*b1*x| can cost H^2*Z in the true scaled width. Simply reducing the
precision to H^4 already removes that quartic term with cost H in W/Q.
The cancellation is real; a speedup does not follow. Preserve actual public
coefficient correlations and measured caps before preferring this option.

## 4. What remains worth validating

The residue-branch construction removes a concrete restriction: useful
polynomial multipliers no longer have to be units at EVERY coordinate.
It allows a structured approximation to use nonconstant denominators while
paying and recovering its exceptional set. It supplies no cheap approximation
by itself, and the generic affine attempt above is rejected as a gain.

Audit existence, the fixed-dimensional acquisition, normalization when one
coefficient is zero, degree changes, interval endpoints, exceptional-path
coverage, polynomial bit widths and the full W024/W020 interface. Check the
generic exponent comparison and exact cancellation comparator. A symbolic
review suffices; no new experiment is requested unless a concrete ambiguity
needs one frozen <=60-second/20000-event certificate. No reruns or sweeps.

The main thread will also return to a different starting point: the single
binomial coefficient / Frobenius multiplicity branch. W023's exact-series
degree barrier applies only to those exact representations, and its Newton
circuit still exists. A candidate there must actually compute a requested
coefficient or a separating invariant, rather than invoke a coefficient
oracle or demand another audit of the same root chart.
