# W018 Randomized preparation of a common powered base

Parent constructive result, 2026-10-08. Provisional until independent review.
Verified new complete factoring improvement: NONE. This addresses an actual
shared-consumer input cost: construct beta=alpha^M of sufficiently large order,
or factor N, even when M destroys much of the original unit-group order.
It does not construct useful approximation certificates. No novelty is claimed
for bounded order search or extraction from an annihilating exponent.

## Proposed factor-or-powered-order primitive

Input: an odd composite N which is not a prime power, a positive integer M
in binary, and an integer B>=1. Standard elementary preprocessing can remove
even inputs and recognize perfect powers; the primitive's domain is explicit.
Let lambda_M be the BIT LENGTH of M; M need not be factored or polynomially
bounded in N.

Repeat independently:

1. Choose a uniformly from {1,...,N-1}. Compute g=gcd(a,N). A nonunit gives
   a proper factor immediately, since 0<a<N.
2. For a unit, compute beta=a^M mod N by binary modular powering.
3. Decide whether ord_N(beta)<=B using a deterministic bounded baby/giant
   order search costing O~sqrt(B) group operations. If not, return a,beta
   with ord_N(beta)>B.
4. If its order r<=B is found, put E=M*r. Then a^E=1 mod N. Write
   E=2^s*u with u odd. Compute x=a^u mod N and follow its squaring chain,
   testing gcd(x-1,N) (and/or the conventional nontrivial square-root branch)
   at each step before x becomes 1. Return any proper gcd. If none appears,
   restart with an independent a.

Retain only an actual verified factor or a completed bounded-order decision.
No global group-order factorization, primality oracle or hidden prime is used.

## Bounded order search is explicit

Take b=ceil(sqrt(B)). Compute babies beta^i, 0<=i<b. A repeated baby yields
the order: find the first repetition of 1, or equivalently the least positive
index difference. If there is a repetition, ord(beta)<b.

If all babies are distinct, store their indices. Compute giants beta^(k*b)
for 1<=k<=ceil(B/b). A match with baby i gives a positive annihilator
r'=k*b-i; ignore r'>B and choose the least positive matched r'. If ord<=B,
its Euclidean representation r=k*b-i with 0<=i<b occurs in this menu, so
the least matched value is exactly the order. If no allowed match exists,
the order is greater than B. The B=1 case uses the baby 1 and giant beta.

Hash tables can be replaced by sorted lists for deterministic comparison work.
There are O(sqrt(B)) stored elements and giants. Early baby repetition is
handled before giant matching, so duplicate babies do not create a hidden
quadratic list of matches. The argument works in the actual unit group modulo
N without knowing its size or decomposition.

## Why restarting is expected constant work

Write N=product_j p_j^e_j with at least two distinct odd primes. This
factorization is used only in the proof. Conditioned on gcd(a,N)=1, CRT makes
the components of a independent uniform units. Put

    v_j = v2(ord_(p_j^e_j)(a)).

If these valuations are not all equal and step 3 returned a small order,
the annihilating exponent E makes the squaring chain in step 4 reach 1 at
different times in different prime components. At one stage gcd(x-1,N)
contains some but not all prime-power factors, so it is proper. Extra odd
or even factors in E do not destroy this separation; they only change the
initial exponent or insert redundant stages. This is the usual annihilator
extraction argument, applied to E=M*ord(a^M).

For a uniform unit at an odd prime power with s_j=v2(p_j-1)>=1, the
distribution is

    Pr(v_j=0)=2^(-s_j),
    Pr(v_j=t)=2^(t-1-s_j), 1<=t<=s_j.

For two distinct components let a0=min(s_1,s_2). Independence gives

    Pr(v_1=v_2) = (4^a0+2)/(3*2^(s_1+s_2)) <= 1/2.       (1)

Thus with conditional probability at least 1/2 the valuations differ. If
step 3 instead returned large order, the trial already succeeded. Hence
failure implies equal valuations in these two components, whether or not
the small-order event is correlated with them. No unjustified independence
between order size and 2-adic separation is assumed. Nonunit a succeeds
immediately, so unconditional trial success is also at least 1/2.

Independent retries have expected count at most 2. This is an expected-time
Las Vegas primitive, NOT a deterministic worst-case replacement. Prime powers
need preprocessing: a single prime component would invalidate (1).

## Paid complexity

Let nbit=ceil(log2 N) and bbit=ceil(log2(B+1)). With standard arithmetic,
one trial costs, conservatively,

    O~((lambda_M + sqrt(B) + bbit + 1)*poly(nbit)) bits.   (2)

Both a^M and a^u are paid; E has at most lambda_M+bbit bits and at most
that many squaring-chain stages. Products M*r, finding its 2-adic valuation,
gcds, input reading, sampling and list comparison are included. Storage is
O~(lambda_M+sqrt(B)*nbit+bbit). The bound is polynomial in the actual input
width, not unit cost for an enormous M. Expected costs multiply by at most 2.

If beta has global order>B, the ordinary W013/W014 consumer's component checks
gcd(beta^j-1,N), 1<=j<B, cannot equal N. Any nonunit check is therefore a
proper factor; otherwise every component has order at least B. Their O(B)
straightforward cost is already included in the consumer's baby-table budget.
No faster component-checking primitive is needed for this claim.

## Application: common bases for different progression moduli

Suppose K W016 certificates have public moduli m_i>=1, with potential covered
primes p=1+m_i*t and integer-polynomial errors e_i. Take

    M=product_i m_i.

An lcm is optional; the product avoids pretending factorization is free.
If the total binary input length of these m_i is L_m, a product tree constructs
M in O~L_m bit work, and lambda_M<=L_m. Apply the primitive to construct the
SAME beta=alpha^M for every certificate, or return a factor.

For any covered prime, beta^t=alpha^((M/m_i)(p-1))=1 mod p, so its transfer
beta^(A_i(0)-q_i*N)=beta^e_i is valid, independently of degree. Different
m_i no longer require separate baby tables. Complete expected cost for the
shared consumer is therefore of the form

    acquisition + O~(L_m + K + B + S/B),
    S=sum_i(2E_i+1),                                    (3)

with all integer recovery/operand widths and consumer guards from W013 paid.
The sqrt(B) order preparation is absorbed by B. If each modulus has O(log N)
bits, L_m=O(K log N), even though M's numeric value may be enormous. M is
used in the one-time base preparation; do not multiply every certificate's
searched error by M. Coefficients and exponents D_i still have their actual
widths charged. Formula (3) is conditional on useful certificate supply and
coverage and is expected, not a new deterministic all-input bound.

The same primitive may discharge the powered-base acquisition obligation in
W014 A/B when their denominator M is supplied. It does not fix dense-grid
rigidity, the full carry-image cost, or certificate usefulness.

## A stronger equal-information comparator

Varying progression strides also admit shared PRODUCT evaluation. For unit
m_i modulo N,

    product_(j=0)^(ell-1)(a_i+m_i*j)
      = m_i^ell * product_(j=0)^(ell-1)(a_i/m_i+j) mod N.  (4)

Nonunit strides are first gcd-checked and yield a proper factor when
1<=m_i<N; degenerate wider strides require explicit handling. The right-hand
side uses the same universal rising-factorial block polynomials at different
normalized starts. Dyadically split lengths and apply W015's monic evaluator.
For K supplied progressions with total occurrence length L, this gives
O~(K+sqrt(L)) modular work, including stride inversions, setup and full-gcd
localization. Candidate leaves must be checked/clipped to (1,N), so a nonunit
leaf is proper; overlapping candidates retain their occurrence costs.

Thus (3) must beat the SHARED comparator (4), not merely sum_i sqrt(L_i).
The remaining genuine target is a paid family with S substantially below L,
not merely moduli allowing base sharing. Common-base preparation removes one
obligation but does not manufacture the required useful approximation family.
